# Weekly Digest — 2026-W41 (Oct 5–11)

## Regulatory/Legal Watch

- **Ontario's resale cap leaves primary-market fees untouched.** A Backstreet Boys fan's viral complaint over C$111 in service fees exposed that Ontario's Ticket Sales Act (effective Apr. 24, 2026) caps resale price at the original total purchase price but imposes no limit on original-sale fees, which Ticketmaster (keeps ~5–7%) and venues set freely. Ontario has fined SeatGeek Canada (C$25,000) and StubHub (C$20,000) for resale-cap violations — active secondary-market enforcement — while holding unused cabinet authority to cap primary fees directly, a lever that could be pulled with little notice (Q2, Q6, Q9, Q11). → [[articles/2026-10-09-backstreet-boys-fan-complains-over-111-service-fees-while-ontario-cracks-down-on|Oct 9]]
- **Massachusetts revives a resale/fee cap bill as a $11B academic estimate comes under fire.** NATP's Oct. 6 rebuttal of Vanderbilt's "$11 Billion" study (ban above-face resale, cap platform fees at 10%) warns strict caps can shrink seller participation and raise average prices, while Vanderbilt's methodology (resting partly on 2004/2010–2014 data) remains contested. The fight is directly live: Gov. Healey's Great Divide Act (H.5679) — a 110%-of-face resale cap plus 10% marketplace-fee cap for non-sporting events — is pending in a supplemental Massachusetts budget bill (Q6, Q9, Q11). → [[articles/2026-10-08-natp-challenges-vanderbilt-s-11b-ticket-study-warns-resale-caps-could-backfire|Oct 8]]
- **A documented $168 resale fee gap keeps the FTC's live case in the spotlight.** A viral Ticketmaster resale listing showed a seller netting $254.15 on a $299 listing while buyers saw $422.45 for the same seat — landing one week after a federal judge denied Live Nation/Ticketmaster's motion to dismiss the FTC's case alleging $986M in resale fees and $187M in markups collected 2019–2024 (Q2, Q6, Q9, Q10). → [[articles/2026-10-07-viral-bruno-mars-ticket-listing-shows-168-gap-between-ticketmaster-buyer-price-a|Oct 7]]
- **California centralizes consumer-protection enforcement under Rohit Chopra.** Newsom appointed the former CFPB Director to lead the state's new Business and Consumer Services Agency, overseeing DFPI alongside the state AG — a second active enforcer, with a track record of aggressive, systemic (not case-by-case) actions, that Etix should watch for California-specific junk-fee/all-in-pricing guidance (Q9, Q11). → [[articles/2026-10-07-inside-california-s-bcsa-rohit-chopra-s-expanding-role-in-consumer-protection|Oct 7]]
- **FTC extends its "no safe harbor" pricing theory outside ticketing.** Warning letters to 24 major healthcare companies state that compliance with sector-specific disclosure rules (CMS price-transparency) does not shield against FTC Act Section 5 liability for incomplete/inaccurate pricing — the same theory underlying the ticketing Junk Fees Rule, applied via a lower-friction warning-letter tool rather than litigation (Q9). → [[articles/2026-10-05-ftc-issues-letters-warning-hospitals-against-deceptive-pricing-practices|Oct 5]]
