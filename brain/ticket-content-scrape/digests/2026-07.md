## Q8 — Where's the next big opportunity?

Three converging signals point past pure ticketing-transaction competition and toward two adjacent growth vectors: **non-traditional content formats** and **AI-native discovery/concierge layers**.

On format: a top-tier promoter is directly testing whether "creator culture" content can anchor a ticketed event on its own ([[articles/2026-07-08-c3-presents-announces-creator-led-for-you-fest-nov-14-15-in-irvine-ca|C3 Presents' creator-led For You Fest]]), an industry Q&A frames non-concert formats as one of live's most exciting growth areas ([[articles/2026-07-07-non-concert-formats-are-one-of-the-most-exciting-developments-in-live-q-s-with-s|Semmel Concerts Q&A]]), and a longer-form piece frames sports/venues explicitly as attention-economy assets needing year-round programming beyond single-purpose game days ([[articles/2026-07-22-sportainment-how-sports-became-part-of-the-attention-economy-and-what-that-means|Sportainment]]). Read together, these argue the next inventory category isn't a new artist tier — it's new *formats* (residencies, creator events, hybrid programming) that a flexible ticketing/CRM platform is well positioned to serve.

On AI: three independent deployments this month report outsized, *measured* (not projected) commercial impact from conversational/AI concierge layers — a 70% clickthrough-to-checkout lift and ~$285K incremental revenue in 30 days ([[articles/2026-07-23-napster-ai-agent-guides-f1-fans-through-belgian-grand-prix-buying-purchase|Napster's F1 agent]]), 2.5x weekly in-chat revenue growth over 8 weeks ([[articles/2026-07-15-satisfi-labs-launches-ai-ticketing-agent-for-tourism|Satisfi Labs/Ventrata]]), and a fresh $3.75m raise for a pure discovery-layer AI startup with no ticketing/production ambitions of its own ([[articles/2026-07-10-live-entertainment-discovery-platform-for-india-thumpn-raises-3-75m|thumpN]]). This is no longer speculative — it's a validated pattern worth a real Etix Assistant investment, not just a monitor posture.

A third, smaller thread: bundling ticket purchases into paid membership/content relationships ([[articles/2026-07-27-wwe-launches-99-club-wwe-membership-with-ticket-presales-exclusive-merch|WWE's $99 Club]], [[articles/2026-07-30-world-supercross-expands-revenue-opportunities-with-recast|World Supercross/Recast digital passes]]) is a lower-conviction but recurring pattern worth continued monitoring rather than immediate build.

## Q19 — What are clients asking for that no vendor serves well? (whitespace)

Cross-referencing this month's Q1 (client pain) and Q12 (competitor feature) notes surfaces several gaps no vendor — including the ones shipping adjacent features — has actually closed:

- **Per-person (not per-account) purchase-limit enforcement.** Multiple bot/tout stories this month ([[articles/2026-07-29-who-now-has-the-power-and-the-responsibility-to-stop-ticket-scalping|Who now has the power to stop scalping?]], [[articles/2026-07-17-manchester-united-fans-face-potential-season-ticket-ban|Man United's season-ticket bans]], [[articles/2026-07-22-man-united-criticised-by-fan-group-as-supporters-made-to-feel-like-suspects|fan-group criticism]]) confirm every vendor is still enforcing limits at the account/device level, which both lets coordinated (increasingly AI-driven) buyers through and produces false positives against legitimate long-tenure fans. No one has shipped verified-identity purchase limiting that solves both sides.
- **Pre-agreed, fast cancellation/refund-liability terms between promoter and agent.** The Heritage Live saga ([[articles/2026-07-15-row-erupts-over-heritage-live-cancellation-following-refund-offers|refund row]], [[articles/2026-07-28-not-worth-the-risk-uk-independent-festivals-facing-dip-in-ticketer-confidence|ticketer-confidence piece]]) shows agents currently improvise goodwill refunds under reputational pressure rather than operating from a contractually clear framework — and the [[articles/2026-07-16-formula-1-las-vegas-grand-prix-reach-3-05m-settlement-over-2023-ticket-mess|F1 Las Vegas settlement]] shows it can take *years of litigation* to reach a standardized partial-refund model most vendors still lack out of the box.
- **Face-value attribution for bundled/discounted season and package tickets.** As resale price caps spread, [[articles/2026-07-29-ontario-resale-cap-leaves-season-ticket-holders-in-limbo-as-renewals-arrive|Ontario's cap]] shows regulators and vendors alike have no working method to compute "face value" for season tickets, hospitality packages, or corporate allocations — a gap that will only get more urgent as more jurisdictions copy the DC/Massachusetts template.
- **Mid-tier flexible commitment packaging.** The [[articles/2026-07-17-seattle-kraken-launches-new-flexible-ticket-option|Seattle Kraken flexible-pack launch]] is a rare example of a vendor actually building the middle ground between full-season and single-game — most clients still only have those two options.
- **Standardized, trigger-based event-disruption refunds.** [[articles/2026-07-16-wildfire-smoke-disrupts-events-across-midwest-northeast-ahead-of-busy-weekend|The wildfire-smoke weekend]] shows ticket-validity handling for weather/force-majeure disruption is still fully ad hoc across operators, with no shared platform tooling tying refund/credit logic to an external hazard signal (AQI, NWS alerts, etc.).
- **Payment-cost/treasury visibility as a first-class feature**, not just transaction processing — even an NFL franchise is shopping for this separately ([[articles/2026-07-17-steelers-partner-with-priority-commerce-for-ticketing-payments|Steelers/Priority Commerce]]).

## Q20 — Why do clients churn or choose competitors? (win/loss)

The clearest pattern: clients switch on **perceived technology/checkout modernity**, often with no specific complaint against the incumbent. Warrington Wolves named faster checkout and a "modern platform" as the explicit reason for leaving its prior vendor for Ticketmaster Sport ([[articles/2026-07-08-ticketmaster-sport-partners-with-miami-warrington|Ticketmaster Sport/Warrington]]), and AXS took Sydney's Hordern Pavilion from the long-dominant incumbent Ticketek on a "fan-first technology" pitch alone ([[articles/2026-07-15-axs-expands-apac-presence-with-hordern-pavilion-tie-up|AXS/Hordern Pavilion]], [[articles/2026-07-15-axs-named-official-ticketing-partner-for-sydneys-hordern-pavilion|AXS Mobile ID feature detail]]) — worth noting that deal also leaned on AEG's ownership stake in the venue operator, a lock-in advantage independents can't replicate and should instead route around by targeting non-affiliated venues.

Fee-model friction is a second, quieter churn driver on the *patron* side that shows up as club/team attrition: [[articles/2026-07-16-charlotte-fc-ends-sale-of-permanent-seat-licenses|Charlotte FC]] reversed its permanent-seat-license fee entirely after it coincided with a 15–25% season-ticket sales decline — the fix (dropping the fee, refunding it as credit) came only after fan focus-group data made the cost clear.

On the retention side, [[articles/2026-07-08-ticketco-renews-partnership-with-wsl2-s-durham-women-fc|TicketCo's multi-year Durham Women FC renewal]] is the month's best positive counter-case: a smaller independent club stayed with its vendor on relationship quality and a stated commitment to keep evolving the supporter journey together, not price — suggesting client-success investment, not just feature parity, is what keeps accounts from bidding out.
