# Weekly Digest — 2026-W40 (Sep 28–Oct 4)

## Regulatory/Legal Watch

- **BOTS Act survives dismissal twice this week.** A federal judge denied Ticketmaster/Live Nation's motion to dismiss the FTC's BOTS Act + all-in-pricing suit, holding the Act reaches platforms (not just individual resellers) and that FTC adequately alleged Ticketmaster knew brokers were evading ticket limits via multi-account setups and profited from the resulting resale (Q9, Q10, Q11). A second ruling the same week revived bait-and-switch pricing allegations predating Ticketmaster's 2023 voluntary all-in-pricing switch, citing an internal "turn a blind eye" email and a 2021 rejection of identity verification because it was "too effective." Case (joined by 7 state AGs) now moves to discovery. → [[articles/2026-09-29-judge-rejects-ticketmaster-bid-to-escape-ftc-bots-act-lawsuit|Sep 29]], [[articles/2026-10-01-bots-act-applies-to-ticketmaster-judge-rules-allowing-prosecution-to-continue|Oct 1]]
- **California adds a second new enforcement track against Ticketmaster/Live Nation.** Gov. Newsom signed the COMPETE Act (AB 1776, effective Jan. 1, 2027), giving AG Bonta a standalone state monopolization claim (no two-party-conduct requirement) explicitly justified by April's federal antitrust verdict; AG-and-DA enforcement only, no private right of action (Q10). → [[articles/2026-10-01-california-gives-bonta-new-monopoly-enforcement-tool-after-ticketmaster-verdict|Oct 1]]
- **California also enacted AB 1349** (speculative-ticket ban), effective immediately upon signing Sep 28 — bars advertising/selling tickets a seller doesn't hold, requires resale marketplaces to take "reasonable measures" against such listings, and expands non-delivery liability. Newsom called the carve-outs (season-ticket exemption, pro-sports safe harbor) "uneven" and invited 2027 amendments; original backer NIVA reversed to urging a veto over those carve-outs (Q11). → [[articles/2026-09-28-california-enacts-ab-1349-speculative-ticket-ban-as-newsom-calls-for-more-work|Sep 28]]
- **UK resale-cap bill gains a dedicated drafting team**; a government minister told an industry-organized Labour-conference panel it hopes to move faster than planned (no firm date), while officials flag a still-unaddressed risk that touts/marketplaces relocate offshore to evade the cap once enacted (penalties up to 10% of global turnover) (Q11, Q6). → [[articles/2026-09-28-uk-steps-up-work-on-ticket-resale-cap-bill-as-officials-consider-black-market-ri|Sep 28]], [[articles/2026-10-01-uk-minister-promises-faster-resale-crackdown-at-industry-backed-labour-conferenc|Oct 1]]
- **UMAW's Sep. 4 Tunney Act comment** (surfacing in trade press this week) asks DOJ to reject the Live Nation/Ticketmaster settlement outright and pursue a full breakup, going further than other commenters (SeatGeek, AEG/Messina, NIVA) by centering musician-specific remedies (artist grant funds, forced separation from artist management, venue-ownership caps) (Q10). → [[articles/2026-09-28-musicians-group-urges-doj-to-reject-live-nation-settlement-seeks-breakup-and-art|Sep 28]]
- **New York AG sues Evolutions Festival promoters** for allegedly diverting $243K+ in advance ticket/fee revenue instead of escrowing it under NY's Arts and Cultural Affairs Law, then failing to refund after a 2025 cancellation (only 11 of 521 buyers recovered anything, via chargebacks) — seeking restitution, per-violation penalties (1,185 alleged violations), and a $500K performance bond before the promoters can run another NY event (Q2, Q11). → [[articles/2026-09-30-new-york-ag-sues-evolutions-festival-promoters-for-allegedly-scamming-ticket-buy|Sep 30]]
