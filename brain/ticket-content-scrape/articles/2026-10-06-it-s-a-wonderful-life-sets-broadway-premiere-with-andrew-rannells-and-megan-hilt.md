# ‘It’s a Wonderful Life’ Sets Broadway Premiere With Andrew Rannells and Megan Hilty

## Summary

"It's a Wonderful Life: A Live Radio Play" will make its Broadway debut at the Broadhurst Theatre this holiday season (previews Nov. 4, opening Nov. 18, through Jan. 3, 2027), starring Andrew Rannells, Megan Hilty, Tituss Burgess, Harry Hamlin and Lisa Rinna, directed by Jerry Zaks. The production recreates the Capra classic as a 1940s radio broadcast, marking the film's 80th anniversary.

### Key Points
- Limited Broadway engagement at the Broadhurst Theatre: previews Nov. 4, opens Nov. 18, closes Jan. 3, 2027.
- Cast: Andrew Rannells (George Bailey), Megan Hilty (Mary Bailey), Tituss Burgess, Harry Hamlin, Lisa Rinna; directed by Jerry Zaks.
- Staged as a live 1940s radio broadcast with onstage foley effects; coincides with the film's 80th anniversary.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Its-A-Wonderful-Life.webp)

“It’s a Wonderful Life: A Live Radio Play” will make its Broadway debut this holiday season, with Andrew Rannells, Megan Hilty, Tituss Burgess, Harry Hamlin and Lisa Rinna starring in a limited engagement at the Broadhurst Theatre.

Previews begin November 4 ahead of an official opening November 18. Directed by four-time Tony Award winner Jerry Zaks, the production will run through January 3, 2027.

Adapted by Joe Landry, the play presents the familiar holiday story through the setting of a 1940s radio broadcast. Audience members become the studio audience as the performers bring Bedford Falls to life, taking on multiple characters while a foley artist supplies sound effects onstage. Holiday carols will also be part of the production.

Rannells will play George Bailey, with Hilty appearing opposite him as Mary Bailey. Burgess will portray Clarence and other characters, while Hamlin takes on Mr. Potter and additional roles. Rinna will play Violet and others.

The story follows George Bailey, whose years of sacrifice for his community take on new meaning when a crisis forces him to reconsider his life. The radio-play format allows the company to portray dozens of characters while creating the sounds of the story in front of the audience.

The Broadway premiere coincides with the 80th anniversary of Frank Capra’s 1946 film, which received five Academy Award nominations, including best picture. The film was based on Philip Van Doren Stern’s 1943 short story “The Greatest Gift.”

Landry’s adaptation premiered at Connecticut’s Stamford Center for the Arts in 1996 before being staged by theaters around the country. Landry said the project began after a friend asked him to create a version she could direct at Fairfield High School.
