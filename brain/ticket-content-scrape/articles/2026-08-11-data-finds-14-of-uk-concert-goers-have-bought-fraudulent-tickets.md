# Data finds 14% of UK concert-goers have bought fraudulent tickets

## Summary

A new UK Home Office survey finds that 14% of concert-goers have bought a fraudulent ticket, costing fans a combined £150m (€175.4m) per year, with 77% of fraudulent purchases made via online platforms. The finding underpins the joint Home Office / Virgin Media O2 "Stop! Think Fraud" campaign, launched as a follow-up to O2's earlier "Let's Stamp it Out" effort. The campaign lands against a backdrop of continued delay to the UK's promised anti-touting legislation, which O2 estimates could still be up to three years away and cost fans a further £400,000 a day in the meantime.

### Key Points

- 14% of UK concert-goers surveyed have bought a fraudulent ticket; fraud costs fans an estimated £150m per year.
- 77% of fraudulent ticket purchases happened via online platforms; 18-24 year-olds face the highest risk (31% scammed) versus 4% for over-55s.
- The Home Office and Virgin Media O2 (which sponsors 21 UK music venues) launched "Stop! Think Fraud" to raise awareness, building on O2's prior "Let's Stamp it Out" campaign.
- The UK's promised legislative crackdown on ticket touting remains only a draft bill (as of May 2026, under then-PM Keir Starmer), with O2 estimating up to a further three-year delay before laws take effect.
- O2 pegs the ongoing cost of legislative delay at roughly £400,000 per day to concertgoers, driven by bot competition, illegal speculative selling, and inflated resale prices.

### Highlights

- Quantified patron fraud exposure: 14% incidence, £150m/year aggregate loss, 77% occurring on online platforms — concrete, citable figures for patron-pain-point tracking.
- Sharp age skew (31% for 18-24s vs. 4% for 55+) indicating fraud risk concentrates among younger, likely lower-income buyers.
- UK anti-touting legislation remains in draft form with an estimated multi-year delay, keeping the regulatory backdrop unresolved (relevant to resale/transferability policy tracking).

### Industry Problem

Patrons continue to be defrauded at scale when buying outside verified primary channels, and the underlying policy fix (UK anti-touting/resale legislation) keeps stalling, leaving fans exposed to speculative selling and fake listings with no near-term regulatory resolution.

### Proposed Solution

The item describes an awareness campaign rather than a technical fix; for Etix this reinforces the case for visible primary-market fraud-prevention messaging and verified-ticket guarantees at checkout, plus continued monitoring of UK anti-touting legislation timing as a trigger point — if the draft bill advances, transferability/resale features may need to adapt ahead of enforcement.

Polling also finds fans lose £150m a year to fake tickets, as O2 Virgin Media and the Home Office launch the 'Stop! Think Fraud' campaign

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Radiohead_The-O2.jpg)

The O2 Arena

A new UK government survey has found 14% of concert-goers have bought fraudulent tickets, adding up to a loss of £150 million (€175.4m) per year.

The polling comes as part of a campaign launched jointly by the Home Office and O2 Virgin Media, known as *Stop! Think Fraud*, which builds on the company’s previous campaign *Let’s Stamp it Out.*

O2 Virgin Media sponsors 21 music venues in the UK. The poll defines ticket fraud as occurring when fans buy tickets from a website or social media platform, but the tickets “either don’t arrive or turn out to be fake”.

“The data shows that more than one in ten (14%) gig-goers surveyed have bought a fake ticket with over three quarters (77%) of the tickets bought via online platforms,” the Home Office said, adding: “The polling found that no age group was immune to these scams, but those aged 18 to 24 faced the highest risk with 31% having been sold a fake ticket compared to the lowest of 4% of those aged over 55.”

“Ticketing scams are a blight on the UK’s live music industry, and it’s heartbreaking to see so many fans being targeted,” said Gareth Griffiths, director of partnerships and sponsorship at Virgin Media O2. “Seeing your favourite artist live should be an exciting experience, not one that leaves you out of pocket or turned away at the door.”

> “Ticketing scams are a blight on the UK’s live music industry”

In May, the UK industry reacted with disappointment as a long-promised legislative crackdown on ticket touting emerged only as a draft bill under previous prime minister Keir Starmer.

Referencing this at the announcement of the campaign, the Home Office said “the government is taking the time to get this right by bringing forward draft legislation to ensure that our measures are truly effective, enforceable, and future-proof and fans get the protection and security they have always deserved”.

Despite teaming up with the Home Office for the campaign, O2 Virgin Media said following the May delay “that despite promises from ministers to fix the issue ‘as soon as possible’, it could now be as many as three years before laws come into effect – a delay which could cost concertgoers up to half a billion pounds”.

“The cost of setbacks to music fans is real – £400,000 every day, according to research from O2. The government delays come at a time when households are already under financial pressure and every pound matters. The impact is not only financial – from racing against bots for tickets to avoiding illegal but widespread ‘speculative selling’, the current resale market creates confusion, stress and inflated prices for fans – truly serving ticket touts alone,” the company said.
