# Australia launches first industry-led festival guide for organisers

## Summary

The Australian Festival Association launched Festival Blueprint, Australia's first industry-led best-practices guide for festival organizers, funded by the NSW government's Sound NSW and covering accessibility, crowd management, First Nations engagement, police engagement and the NSW Music Festivals Act.

### Key Points
- The guide was built with input from more than 70 contributors; it is free and positioned as a "living resource."
- It targets festival suppliers, leaders, contractors and other NSW industry stakeholders.
- It arrives alongside a wave of Australian festival announcements (Spilt Milk, Beyond The Valley, Wildlands, CMC Rocks) and a prior study finding some Australian states get up to five times fewer international tour stops per capita than east-coast markets.

### Highlights
- The guide covers accessibility, crowd management, medical/harm reduction, environmental impact, and compliance with the NSW Music Festivals Act.
- It was developed with more than 70 industry contributors and funded by the NSW government (Sound NSW).
- It comes amid evidence of an Australian regional touring gap (up to five times fewer international shows per capita outside the east coast).

### Industry Problem
Festival organizers, especially smaller or regional ones, have historically lacked a centralized, credible source for navigating safety regulation, accessibility requirements and crowd-management practice -- a structural client pain point that a fragmented, festival-by-festival approach to compliance makes worse.

### Proposed Solution
The Blueprint's response is a free, living best-practices resource built collaboratively with the sector. For Etix, this is grounding material (AI-context) for client-facing guidance to organizer clients on compliance and accessibility questions, and a signal to watch whether similar centralized guides emerge in other markets Etix serves.

NSW's Splendour in the Grass

The Australian Festival Association (AFA) has launched the country’s first industry-led best practices guide for music festivals to provide organisers with a centralised resource for delivering large-scale events.

Funded by the NSW state government through its Sound NSW arm, the free-to-access Festival Blueprint is a living resource intended to provide organisers with practical operational guidance, regulatory information, and industry expertise.

It is specifically created for festival suppliers, leaders, contractors, agencies, and industry stakeholders in Australia’s most populous state, New South Wales (NSW), helping support safety and consistency of festivals in the market.

“Festival Blueprint consolidates the festival industry’s collective knowledge, guiding organisers of every scale to deliver safe, well-run events that communities love,” says Adelle Robinson, AFA chair and CEO of Fuzzy Operations.

More than 70 contributors helped shape the Festival Blueprint, giving practical guidance to navigate planning, risk management, and requirements with up-to-date insight.

Its leading chapters break down the topics of accessibility, crowd management, First Nations engagement, police engagement, and the legislation of the Music Festivals Act (NSW). Additional topics of coverage include medical and harm reduction, emergencies, environmental impact, and ethics and access, helping give a comprehensive view of the sector.

Of the new guidance, Sound NSW head Emily Collins says: “Festival Blueprint marks a significant milestone for the NSW festival industry and sets a strong foundation for the sector’s future.”

It arrives as more Australian events, including Spilt Milk, Beyond The Valley, Wildlands, and CMC Rocks, [roll out details](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/festival-news-spilt-milk-leads-flurry-of-australian-announcements/) of their next events ahead of the country’s festival season.

Yet last month, a study revealed the scale of Australia’s regional [touring divide](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/new-australian-pension-laws-blamed-for-festival-cancellations/), with some states receiving five times fewer international concerts per capita than their east‑coast neighbours.
