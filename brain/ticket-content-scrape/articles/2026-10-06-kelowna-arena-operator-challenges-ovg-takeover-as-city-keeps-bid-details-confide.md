# Kelowna Arena Operator Challenges OVG Takeover as City Keeps Bid Details Confidential

## Summary

A year after Kelowna, B.C. named Oak View Group Canada the "top-ranked operator" to take over Prospera Place and MNP Place arena operations in 2029, the incumbent operator GSL Group is publicly fighting the decision — releasing its own competing commercial proposal and a commissioned poll showing 76% local opposition to the switch — while the city continues withholding OVG's bid, scoring and evaluation criteria, even after a contract is signed. The dispute also raises an unresolved ticketing question: Prospera Place's in-house ticketing company (GSL-owned Select Your Tickets) could be displaced if OVG becomes operator, but neither the city nor OVG has said whether a ticketing-platform change is part of the deal.

### Key Points
- Kelowna selected OVG Canada as top-ranked operator in Oct. 2025; contract negotiations/procurement still "in progress" a year later.
- City says even post-contract, individual proposals, scoring and evaluation detail will stay confidential permanently.
- GSL's public counter-proposal: $75K annual management fee, 97% of profit above threshold reinvested into the venue, ~$11.6M projected 10-year reinvestment.
- GSL-commissioned poll (n=301, ±5.2pp MOE) found 76% opposed the OVG switch, framed partly around Canada–U.S. trade-dispute sentiment.
- GSL flags a conflict-of-interest concern: OVG already operates nearby Penticton's South Okanagan Events Centre, competing for the same regional bookings.
- Current ticketing is handled in-house by GSL's own Select Your Tickets; city and OVG won't say if that changes under new operatorship.
- Part of OVG's continuing Canadian/U.S. municipal-venue expansion (also recently won Greater Sudbury's new arena and Noblesville's Riverview Health Arena).

### Industry Problem
Venue-management consolidation by firms like OVG increasingly bundles operations, booking AND ticketing-platform decisions into one contract, meaning an operator switch can silently displace an incumbent ticketing provider — a structural risk to any primary platform whose venue relationships run through an operating contract rather than a direct platform choice.

### Proposed Solution
This is a monitor/evolve situation, not a build trigger today: the item signals that OVG's continued venue-management wins are a channel through which ticketing-platform incumbency can change hands. The trigger to watch is whether OVG's Canadian/U.S. contracts start explicitly naming a preferred ticketing platform — that would be the moment an independent platform like Etix needs to compete for (or defend) venue-operator relationships directly, not just venue relationships.

Nearly a year later, however, the city tells TicketNews that the procurement process remains in progress.

“With the anticipated transfer of Prospera Place in 2029, the City completed a full review and conducted a request for proposals to select an appropriate operator of the arena under the City’s ownership,” the city said in response to questions from TicketNews. “The procurement process is currently still in progress.”

## GSL Makes Its Prospera Place Offer Public

GSL, which has operated Prospera Place since the arena opened in 1999, has responded by [publishing its own commercial proposal](https://keepprosperacanadian.ca/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) and calling for the city to disclose the evaluation criteria, weightings and scores used in the procurement.

“The City has not provided any information about the evaluation criteria, weightings or scores to justify their decision,” GSL Group President and CEO Graham Lee said in a statement to TicketNews. “What we’re seeking is the City provide that information to voters, so they know what is at stake before October 17.”

Lee said GSL has not filed a freedom-of-information request and has not initiated a procurement challenge or legal proceeding over the decision.

GSL’s proposal calls for a $75,000 annual management fee for Prospera Place and a structure under which most operating income above specified thresholds would be reinvested into the venue.

Lee characterized the offer as providing for “a minimum of 97% of profits returned to the arena,” but the proposal itself lays out the allocation more specifically.

Under the proposed arrangement, up to $800,000 in annual net operating income would be allocated to the Kelowna Rockets, followed by GSL’s $75,000 annual management fee. Of net operating income above $875,000, 97% would go into a Prospera Place Capital & Community Fund and the remaining 3% would be paid to GSL as an incentive fee.

If annual net operating income fell below $800,000, GSL would not collect its management fee for that year. The proposal also includes attendance-based guarantees supporting certain payments to the Rockets.

GSL projects the structure would generate $11.6 million for reinvestment into Prospera Place over 10 years. That figure is projected future operating income under the proposed model rather than an upfront $11.6 million capital contribution from GSL.

The proposal does not eliminate all city financial exposure. It says Kelowna would fund an annual operating deficit if one occurred outside GSL’s performance guarantee, with the city able to recover that amount from money held in the proposed capital fund or future operating income. Kelowna would also continue to be responsible for capital repairs and maintenance.

## GSL-Commissioned Poll Shows Opposition to OVG Change

GSL has also released a Mustel Research Group survey showing strong opposition to the planned operator change, although the wording and sequence of the questions are important to interpreting the result.

The hybrid telephone and online survey questioned 301 Kelowna residents from Sept. 8 through Sept. 17. Mustel says respondents were randomly selected, the sample was weighted to Statistics Canada demographics and the survey carries a margin of error of plus or minus 5.2 percentage points at the 95% confidence level.

The poll found 76% opposed and 8% supported the operator change when respondents were told: “The City of Kelowna is considering changing the company responsible for managing Prospera Place from the current Canadian operator to a subsidiary of Oak View Group, a US based company.”

The question followed one that explicitly referenced the “current trade dispute between Canada and the United States” and asked residents how important it was for Kelowna to do business with Canadian companies over American companies. Eighty-eight percent said doing so was at least somewhat important.

A separate question found 88% considered it important that Prospera Place be run by a Canadian company, including 68% who called it extremely or very important.

The poll therefore provides evidence of substantial opposition when the operator decision is presented through the lens of Canadian versus U.S. ownership. It does not, in the published results, give respondents a side-by-side comparison of GSL and OVG’s financial or operational proposals before asking them to choose between the companies.

GSL commissioned and paid for the research, but Lee said Mustel drew the sample independently and that GSL did not recruit respondents.

The polling and bid disclosure push come ahead of Kelowna’s Oct. 17 municipal election.

## Booking and Ticketing Questions Remain

GSL’s commercial proposal also raises a competitive issue that has received less attention in the public debate.

OVG currently manages the South Okanagan Events Centre in Penticton, about 65 kilometers from Prospera Place. GSL argues that putting both similarly sized venues under the same operator could create conflicts when they compete for concerts and other events, giving one company influence over which Okanagan venue receives a booking.

Kelowna cited OVG’s Penticton operation as one of the company’s qualifications when announcing the selection last year.

OVG has continued expanding its Canadian municipal venue business. [TicketNews reported in August](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/08/oak-view-group-to-operate-new-5800-capacity-sudbury-event-centre/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) that Greater Sudbury selected the company to operate its new 5,800-capacity event center, including event booking, ticketing, premium seating, food and beverage and sponsorship operations.

The change in Kelowna could also have implications for ticketing.

[Prospera Place currently says all of its event ticketing is handled by Select Your Tickets](https://prosperaplace.com/box-office/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), an in-house ticketing company owned by GSL. Neither Kelowna nor OVG has publicly detailed whether the operator change would result in a different primary ticketing platform.

TicketNews asked the city whether the proposed OVG agreement gives the operator authority to change the ticketing provider or whether a platform has already been proposed. The city did not answer that question.

OVG has not responded to emailed questions regarding the dispute as of Tuesday evening. This story will be updated if a statement is received.

For now, the public comparison remains one-sided. GSL has disclosed the financial and operating terms it says Kelowna rejected, while OVG’s proposal and the detailed evaluation that placed it ahead of GSL remain confidential – and the city says the scoring itself will remain confidential even after the procurement is finished.
