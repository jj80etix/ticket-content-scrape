# FTC Seeks Comment on Enforcement Policy Statement Regarding Personalized Pricing

## Summary

The FTC issued a draft enforcement policy statement on "personalized pricing" — using personal data to set prices based on individual willingness-to-pay — and opened a 30-day public comment period once it's published in the Federal Register. The statement doesn't ban the practice outright but puts businesses on notice that failing to disclose data-driven price variation can violate the FTC Act's unfair-or-deceptive-practices standard.

### Key Points
- Draft policy statement targets undisclosed use of personal data to vary prices per individual consumer.
- FTC Chairman Ferguson: FTC "does not have the legal authority to ban personalized pricing in all circumstances" but will enforce disclosure failures.
- 30-day public comment window opens once published in the Federal Register; comments submitted via regulations.gov docket FTC-2026-1057.
- Commission vote to authorize the Federal Register notice was 2-0.
- Part of a continuing Trump-Vance FTC enforcement series on hidden fees and surprise charges.

### Highlights
- 30-day comment window (docket FTC-2026-1057) opens once published — a near-term deadline for industry input.
- Signals FTC intent to police any pricing model using browsing/purchase-history data to vary displayed prices, without an outright ban.

### Industry Problem
Any platform using demand-based or personalized dynamic pricing risks running afoul of disclosure expectations if consumers aren't told the price they see is individualized rather than the price everyone sees — a compliance question directly relevant to ticketing's dynamic-pricing practices.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor the final policy statement's disclosure standard and, if Etix or any pricing partner ever explores personalization, build an explicit "this price may vary by shopper" disclosure into the checkout UI ahead of any FTC action; for now, watch the published Federal Register notice and comment period as the trigger to revisit posture.

## Transcript

The Federal Trade Commission today announced it is seeking public comment on [an enforcement policy statement](/legal-library/browse/federal-trade-commissions-proposed-enforcement-policy-statement-regarding-personalized-pricing) regarding personalized pricing, which is the use of personal data to set prices according to the amount that a company believes an individual consumer is willing to spend.

“When consumers see a listed price, they expect it to be same price that everyone else sees, not the retailer’s estimate of how much they are willing to pay based on their personal data,” said FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson. “The FTC does not have the legal authority to ban personalized pricing in all circumstances, but businesses that fail to tell consumers how their personal data is being used to set a price may be in violation of the FTC Act and other laws we enforce. We are seeking public input on this draft statement, which would put businesses engaged in or considering personalized pricing on notice that the Trump-Vance FTC will not hesitate to enforce the law in this space.”

This is the latest in a series of actions by the Commission, under President Donald Trump’s leadership, against businesses that mislead consumers with hidden fees and surprise charges.

Consumers expect prices for products and services to change based upon supply and demand, not their web surfing habits or buying history, the statement notes. Retailers who represent or imply that a price is static when it in fact varies by individual are at risk of misleading customers. Informed consumers might take measures to avoid higher personalized prices, such as using a virtual private network or private browsing session, or simply avoiding retailers engaged in personalized pricing altogether.

The statement goes on to note that the undisclosed collection or use of personal data for the purpose of personalized pricing could violate the FTC Act, which prohibits unfair or deceptive practices in the marketplace.

Once the statement has been published in the Federal Register, the public will have 30 days to [submit comments electronically](https://www.regulations.gov/docket/FTC-2026-1057). The Commission vote authorizing the Federal Register notice was 2-0.
