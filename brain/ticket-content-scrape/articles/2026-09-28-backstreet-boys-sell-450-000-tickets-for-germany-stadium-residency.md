# Backstreet Boys Sell 450,000 Tickets for Germany Stadium Residency

## Summary

The Backstreet Boys sold approximately 450,000 tickets for their 10-show stadium residency at Merkur Spiel-Arena in Düsseldorf, Germany — their only European performances of 2026 — with demand prompting four added dates.

### Key Points
- Residency: Sept. 25 - Oct. 7, 10 shows at Merkur Spiel-Arena, Düsseldorf.
- Originally announced as six shows ("Into the Millennium – Homecoming: Live in Germany"); expanded to 10 due to demand.
- Fans reported traveling from across Europe, the US, South America, and Australia per Düsseldorf's tourism body.
- Opening show set included "Larger Than Life" and closed with "Everybody" and fireworks.

## Transcript

![Backstreet Boys | Photo via Brian Ziff, Live Nation](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/backstreet-boys.webp)

Backstreet Boys | Photo via Brian Ziff, Live Nation

The Backstreet Boys have sold about 450,000 tickets for their 10-show residency in Düsseldorf, Germany. The run opened September 25 at Merkur Spiel-Arena and continues through October 7. It is the group’s only set of European performances in 2026.

The band initially announced six performances under the title “Into the Millennium – Homecoming: Live in Germany.” Demand prompted four additions, including a final show on October 7. All 10 concerts are scheduled within 13 days at the same stadium.

At the September 25 opening show, the group began with “Larger Than Life.” Kevin Richardson addressed the crowd during the performance, saying, “Germany, you were our first love,” according to German broadcaster WDR. The show closed with “Everybody” and fireworks after the stadium roof opened.

The ticket sales are also bringing visitors to Düsseldorf. The city’s tourism organization said fans were traveling from elsewhere in Europe, the United States, South America and Australia. In a September 22 release, Visit Düsseldorf reported more than 400,000 tickets sold; WDR reported the figure had reached 450,000 by the opening concert.

Hotel bookings point to the scale of the influx. A Düsseldorf hotel survey conducted September 16 found that 94% of available rooms were booked for each of the first two concert dates. For the corresponding dates a year earlier, the figures were 57% and 56%, according to Visit Düsseldorf. The city has also organized events around the residency, including 1990s-themed parties, exhibits and a temporary merchandise store.

The German run follows the Backstreet Boys’ “Into the Millennium” residency at Sphere in Las Vegas.
