# Live Nation Names New General Counsel Amid Post-Verdict Antitrust Fight

## Summary

Live Nation Entertainment is replacing its general counsel for the first time in two decades, hiring Chewy legal chief Da-Wai Hu effective Nov. 2, as the company continues fighting the fallout from a landmark antitrust jury verdict and seeks court approval of a separate DOJ settlement. Outgoing GC Michael Rowles, in the role since 2006, is not leaving but will transition to senior legal adviser through 2029 under an amended, reduced-compensation agreement.

### Highlights
- Da-Wai Hu (from Chewy) joins as EVP, General Counsel and Secretary starting Nov. 2, per a Sept. 25 SEC filing.
- Rowles (GC since March 2006) shifts to modified responsibilities Nov. 1, then formally to senior legal adviser Jan. 1, 2028, through Dec. 31, 2029; base salary drops to $500,000 from 2028 with no contractual bonus.
- Transition lands months after a federal jury verdict against Live Nation and amid the company's pursuit of a separate DOJ settlement approval.
- Filing does not state a reason for the change or link it explicitly to the antitrust litigation.

### Industry Problem
Live Nation/Ticketmaster's antitrust exposure (jury verdict plus a pending DOJ settlement) is an active, unresolved legal risk that shapes the competitive and regulatory environment independents like Etix operate in.

### Proposed Solution
No product response implied by the article itself; posture is Monitor — track the DOJ settlement's terms once approved and any resulting structural or conduct remedies, since they could reshape competitive dynamics for independent primary-ticketing platforms.

Live Nation Entertainment is changing general counsel for the first time in two decades, hiring Chewy legal chief Da-Wai Hu as the company continues fighting the fallout from a landmark antitrust verdict and seeks approval for a separate settlement with the Justice Department.

Hu will join Live Nation as executive vice president, general counsel and secretary beginning Nov. 2, [according to an SEC filing Friday](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1335258/000133525826000038/lyv-20260925.htm). He replaces Michael G. Rowles, who has served as Live Nation’s general counsel since March 2006.

Rowles is not leaving the company as part of the transition. Live Nation simultaneously [amended his employment agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1335258/000133525826000038/ex101-rowlesfirstamendment.htm) to extend his tenure through Dec. 31, 2029, establishing a lengthy handoff period between the two lawyers.

Beginning Nov. 1, Rowles will report to the company’s newly named general counsel and take on modified responsibilities to be defined by that office through the end of 2027. Effective Jan. 1, 2028, his title will formally change to senior legal adviser, with Rowles continuing to provide legal advice to Live Nation and its general counsel through the end of 2029. His base salary will fall to $500,000 beginning in 2028, with no contractual bonus opportunity.

The filing does not give a reason for the leadership change or tie it to Live Nation’s ongoing antitrust litigation. But Hu will take over the company’s top in-house legal job at an unusually consequential point in Live Nation’s regulatory history, months after a federal jury sided with state attorneys general on the antitrust claims they continued pursuing after the Justice Department settled its portion of the case.

Hu has served as general counsel and secretary at online pet retailer Chewy since December 2023. Before joining Chewy, he was general counsel of payments company Checkout.com from May 2022 to December 2023 and spent more than a decade at Amazon, including as vice president and associate general counsel supporting the company’s international and cross-border consumer businesses.

Earlier in his career, Hu practiced corporate law at Sullivan & Cromwell and Paul Weiss and served as a federal appellate law clerk. He holds a law degree from the University of Chicago and an undergraduate degree from Northwestern University.

Rowles, meanwhile, joined Live Nation in March 2006, meaning his tenure as general counsel spans nearly the company’s entire history as an independent public company as well as the 2010 merger with Ticketmaster and the years of antitrust scrutiny that followed. Live Nation originally described his responsibilities as including transactional, securities, compliance and corporate finance matters when announcing his appointment 20 years ago.

Hu will inherit a legal landscape that has become considerably more complicated during 2026.

A federal jury in April [found Live Nation and Ticketmaster liable on the antitrust claims](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/04/live-nation-and-ticketmaster-operate-as-an-illegal-monopoly-jury-finds-in-landmark-antitrust-trial/) brought to trial by a coalition of state attorneys general, including monopolization claims involving primary ticketing and large amphitheaters. The verdict came after the Justice Department reached a separate settlement with the company in March rather than continue to trial alongside the remaining states.

The states that prevailed at trial have asked U.S. District Judge Arun Subramanian for [potentially sweeping remedies](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/states-seek-ticketmaster-breakup-live-nation-venue-selloffs-after-monopoly-verdict/), including the divestiture of Ticketmaster, sales of Live Nation-owned amphitheaters and restrictions on Ticketmaster exclusivity agreements. Live Nation and Ticketmaster are contesting the verdict and have [asked the court either to enter judgment in their favor despite the jury’s findings or grant a new trial](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/live-nation-asks-judge-to-undo-monopoly-verdict-claiming-jury-followed-emotional-arguments-over-law/).

At the same time, the Justice Department’s proposed settlement remains subject to federal court review under the Tunney Act. The agreement would impose new restrictions on ticketing exclusivity and require Ticketmaster to create infrastructure allowing qualifying competing marketplaces to distribute some primary tickets while continuing to use Ticketmaster’s back-end technology. The court cannot enter the proposed final judgment until the statutory public-interest review process is completed.

That review has itself generated opposition from state attorneys general, ticketing competitors and musician advocates, several of which have argued that the proposed settlement does not go far enough to address Ticketmaster’s market position. TicketNews has reported extensively on those comments in recent weeks.

Hu therefore arrives with two major antitrust tracks still unresolved: Live Nation’s effort to undo or retry the states’ jury verdict and the court’s consideration of whether the Justice Department settlement satisfies the Tunney Act’s public-interest requirements.

Rowles will remain inside the company throughout much of that process, first under the new general counsel and later as senior legal adviser, giving Live Nation an extended transition rather than an immediate break with the lawyer who has led its legal department since 2006.
