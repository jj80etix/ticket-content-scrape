# Tommy Lefroy Unveils New Tour, Accompanying Debut Album, ‘The Precariat’

## Summary

Rising rock duo Tommy Lefroy announced their "Save The Precariat" tour alongside debut album *The Precariat*, both arriving October 23. The four-date run starts in Queens, NY and ends November 4 in Los Angeles.

### Key Points
- Tour opens Oct. 23 at Stone Circle Theatre (Queens, NY) and closes Nov. 4 at El Cid (Los Angeles), with stops in Toronto and Chicago.
- Debut album *The Precariat*, written over six years across multiple cities, releases the same day as the tour opener.
- Presale begins Aug. 6; general on-sale opens Aug. 7.
- New single "Thief," with an accompanying music video, is out now in support of the album.

NEW 3x4 Save the Precariat All Dates

Tommy Lefroy, the rising rock duo, have announced both a new tour and an accompanying debut album, *The Precariat.* ‘Save The Precariat’ Tour kicks off in Queens, NY at the Stone Circle Theatre on Oct. 23 and runs for three more dates before ending in Los Angeles at the El Cid on Nov. 4. See full tour routing below.

Presale begins tomorrow, Aug. 6, at 10 a.m. local (presale code: delicate), and tickets are on sale Aug. 7 at 10 a.m. local. Please see below for announced dates and visit https://www.tommylefroy.com/ for more information.

The band’s debut album, *The Precariat*, is scheduled to release on Oct. 23, as Tommy Lefroy gears up for their show in NYC. *The Precariat* was written over the course of six years as the duo traversed through Nashville, Los Angeles, Vancouver, Michigan, and London. Tommy Lefroy’s Wynter Bethel and Tessa Mouzourakis released a moody new single, “Thief,” in support of the forthcoming LP, along with a music video, directed by Brendon Burton, to boot.

In describing “Thief,” the band explains how the song came to be, noting that Wynter first conceived it while in Nashville.

“The title refers to things lost along the way, how they sometimes feel taken, by time, or by people who’ve left us. The chorus came to Wynter in a parking lot, while driving for Postmates in Nashville. The song always felt special to us, like a thesis of how we felt in our early twenties,” says the band. “We held on to this song for years knowing we wanted it to be on our first album, and the feelings the song expresses, of perpetual strife and inadequacy, felt more relevant than ever in the context of *The Precariat*.”
