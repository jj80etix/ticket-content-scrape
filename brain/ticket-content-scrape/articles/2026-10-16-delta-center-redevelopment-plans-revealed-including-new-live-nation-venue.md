# Delta Center Redevelopment Plans Revealed, Including New Live Nation Venue

## Summary

Smith Entertainment Group unveiled detailed plans for a multibillion-dollar redevelopment surrounding Salt Lake City's Delta Center, including a 6,000-capacity Live Nation concert venue, a rebuilt Salt Palace Convention Center, hotels, residential space and a pedestrian-connected entertainment district, timed to the city's 2034 Winter Olympics. The plans add detail to the Live Nation venue partnership first announced in August 2025, as rival promoter AEG also continues expanding its Salt Lake City venue footprint.

### Key Points
- Live Nation venue: 2,000-6,000 capacity, projected at 80-120 concerts annually; specific ticketing arrangements not announced.
- Salt Palace Convention Center to be rebuilt with added meeting rooms, exhibition halls and two ballrooms; closes ~3 years starting fall 2027.
- Public financing includes a 0.5% citywide sales tax increase (~$1.2B over 30 years) and an $1.8B infrastructure financing mechanism; SEG plans ~$3B in private investment.
- A community-benefit fund is partly supported by revenue from Delta Center ticket fees.
- AEG separately acquired The Complex (downtown SLC venue) in 2025, continuing a competing venue expansion in the same market.
- Demolition/construction expected to begin 2027, ahead of SLC's 2034 Winter Olympics.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Delta-Center-rendering-1200.webp)

Smith Entertainment Group (SEG) has unveiled detailed plans for a sweeping redevelopment of downtown Salt Lake City surrounding the Delta Center, including a 6,000-capacity Live Nation concert venue, a rebuilt convention center, new hotels, residential development and an entertainment district designed to connect several blocks of the city’s downtown core.

The plans, [revealed in a video presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uj2zoT-oGys&utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) on October 14, offer the most comprehensive look yet at the proposed Sports, Entertainment, Culture and Convention District, a multibillion-dollar development that city officials hope to complete ahead of the 2034 Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games.

While the Live Nation venue was originally announced in August 2025, the newly released renderings provide additional details about how the concert hall will fit into a larger redevelopment surrounding the Delta Center, home of the NBA’s Utah Jazz and NHL’s Utah Mammoth.

Plans call for a pedestrian walkway connecting the arena to the Salt Palace Convention Center and City Creek Center, creating a continuous stretch of public gathering spaces, restaurants, retail and entertainment facilities.

The arena is already undergoing extensive renovations to better accommodate both professional basketball and hockey, including changes to seating configurations, sightlines and premium hospitality areas.

The planned Live Nation venue represents one of the development’s most significant additions to Salt Lake City’s live entertainment infrastructure.

SEG and Live Nation [announced their partnership in August 2025](https://www.deltacenter.com/news/seg-livenation-venue/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), outlining plans for a purpose-built indoor concert venue capable of accommodating between 2,000 and 6,000 attendees.

At the time, the companies said the venue would host approximately 200 events annually, including 100 concerts, along with corporate gatherings, conferences and community events.

The latest presentation projects between 80 and 120 concerts annually.

The new venue is intended to serve touring artists whose audience sizes fall between smaller clubs and theaters and the much larger Delta Center, which accommodates approximately 12,000 to 19,000 people for concerts and other entertainment events.

Live Nation CEO Michael Rapino previously described the partnership as an opportunity to expand the company’s venue presence in Utah and provide additional options for artists touring through the market.

The development also comes as rival promoter AEG Presents expands its Salt Lake City operations.

As [TicketNews reported in May 2025](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/05/aeg-presents-acquires-salt-lake-city-venue-the-complex/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), AEG acquired The Complex, a downtown music venue featuring several performance spaces, including the 2,500-capacity Rockwell indoor hall and an outdoor venue accommodating up to 4,000 people.

The new Live Nation facility would add another significant option for mid-sized concerts, strengthening the company’s local venue footprint while expanding the range of spaces available to touring acts.

Live Nation is also the parent company of Ticketmaster, although specific ticketing arrangements for the planned concert hall have not been announced.

## Convention Center Rebuild and Public Investment

Beyond the new concert venue, the redevelopment includes a substantial overhaul of the Salt Palace Convention Center.

Salt Lake County plans to replace the existing facility with a redesigned convention center featuring additional meeting rooms, exhibition halls and two ballrooms, enabling it to accommodate multiple major conventions simultaneously.

The Salt Palace is expected to close for approximately three years beginning in fall 2027 as construction progresses, [according to reporting from KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51636072/smith-entertainment-group-salt-lake-leaders-unveil-updated-vision-for-downtown-plans?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

Other planned additions include a new Utah Museum of Contemporary Art building, renovations to Abravanel Hall, a hotel, residential buildings, offices and new dining and retail establishments.

Salt Lake City Mayor Erin Mendenhall praised the proposed pedestrian connections, describing the existing convention center layout as a barrier separating portions of downtown.

“The new district breaks down that wall,” Mendenhall said.

The development is supported by significant public investment alongside SEG’s planned private spending.

In October 2024, the Salt Lake City Council [approved a 0.5% citywide sales tax increase](https://www.slc.gov/council/salt-lake-city-council-approves-sports-entertainment-culture-and-convention-district/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) projected to generate approximately $1.2 billion over 30 years. Under the agreement, up to $900 million will support Delta Center renovations and improvements within the surrounding district.

In April 2025, the council also approved a separate public infrastructure financing mechanism intended to support an estimated $1.8 billion in upgrades to downtown facilities, including the Salt Palace and Delta Center.

SEG has separately indicated plans to invest approximately $3 billion in the broader redevelopment.

The public financing arrangements also include commitments to affordable housing, neighborhood improvements, public art and other community benefits, supported in part by a fund receiving revenue from Delta Center ticket fees.

Salt Lake City officials project that the completed district could attract approximately five million visitors annually.

Demolition and preliminary construction are expected to begin in 2027, with the broader redevelopment scheduled for completion before Salt Lake City hosts the 2034 Winter Olympics. A specific opening date for the new Live Nation concert venue has not yet been announced.
