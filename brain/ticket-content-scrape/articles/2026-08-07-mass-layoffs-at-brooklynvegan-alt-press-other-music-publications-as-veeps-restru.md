# Mass Layoffs At BrooklynVegan, Alt Press & Other Music Publications As Veeps Restructures

## Summary

Veeps — the Live Nation-majority-owned ticketed livestream platform — has reportedly gutted editorial staff at the four music publications it owns (BrooklynVegan, Alternative Press, Revolver, Goldmine), per an Aug. 5 Pitchfork report, drawing criticism as a blow to independent music journalism.

### Key Points
- Veeps (majority-owned by Live Nation since 2021) purchased BrooklynVegan, Alternative Press, Revolver and Goldmine from Project M Group in 2024/2025.
- Emails to affected staff bounced back; only BrooklynVegan founder Dan Levine's email remained active, per Pitchfork.
- Publications span decades of music coverage (Alternative Press since 1985, Goldmine since 1974, BrooklynVegan since 2004).

## Transcript

Veeps, a livestreaming platform majority-owned by Live Nation and also owner of notable music publications, has reportedly gutted editorial staff at *BrooklynVegan*, *Alternative Press*, *Revolver* and *Goldmine*.

[*Pitchfork* reported Aug. 5](https://pitchfork.com/story/brooklynvegan-alt-press-hit-with-mass-layoffs/) that emails to BrooklynVegan staffers were bounced back.

Veeps has mainly operated as a ticketed livestream platform and was founded by Good Charlotte members Benji and Joel Madden in 2017. The company purchased the four music publications from Project M Group sometime in 2024 or 2025, and Live Nation acquired a majority stake in Veeps in 2021 amid the coronavirus pandemic.

BrooklynVegan was founded in 2004 by Dan Levine, whose email was the only one to not bounce back, per [*Pitchfork*](https://pitchfork.com/story/brooklynvegan-alt-press-hit-with-mass-layoffs/). The New York-based site was an influential independent voice in music, as was Alternative Press, which has featured some of rock’s biggest voices since 1985. Goldmine was established in 1974 and catered to collectors and music-related memorabilia, and Revolver, which was founded in 2000, covered the hard rock and metal scenes.

The layoffs are a devastating blow to independent music journalism and the entire music ecosystem, with many artists using these outlets for exposure and to connect with audiences.
