# Milan to gain 71,500-seat San Siro replacement stadium

## Summary

AC Milan and Inter Milan unveiled plans for Generali Stadium, a 71,500-seat replacement for the 100-year-old San Siro, slated to open in 2031 ahead of Euro 2032. The naming-rights deal with insurer Generali is valued at €650 million over 20 years.

### Key Points
- Designed by Foster + Partners and MANICA (also behind Wembley); features a continuous 360° bowl and solar-capable double-curved roof.
- Construction begins 2028; San Siro will be demolished once the clubs relocate.
- Part of a broader 281,000-square-meter urban regeneration project around the site.
- Comes as Milan's live-music infrastructure also grows, with CTS Eventim's 16,000-cap Unipol Dome opening this year.

## Transcript

Generali Stadium will be built next to the famed 100-year-old stadium and is expected to open in 2031, according to AC Milan and Inter Milan

![Generali Stadium rendering](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Generali-Stadium-rendering-©-Inter-Milan.png)

Generali Stadium rendering © Inter Milan

Italian football clubs AC Milan and Inter Milan have announced details of San Siro’s replacement venue: a 71,500-seat stadium on the same grounds, slated to open in 2031.

Generali Stadium will “carry the spirit” of the iconic [100-year-old venue](https://www.iqmagazine.com/featured-list/stadium-stars-2025/san-siro/), a frequent touring stop for the world’s biggest tours, according to the clubs.

The next-generation stadium will feature a continuous 360° bowl with two seating tiers to create “exceptional sightlines” for attendees. San Siro is currently Italy’s largest stadium, with a capacity of over 80,000, and the new venue is set to rival that figure.

It will also have a “double-curved roof” that supports solar-powered energy collection and acoustic optimisation. At either end of the stadium, seating inclinations of up to 37° will “amplify both atmosphere and visual impact”, the clubs say.

The naming rights deal with Italian insurance company Generali is valued at €650 million over 20 years, according to [*ESPN*](https://www.espn.co.uk/football/story/_/id/50317859/milan-inter-new-stadium-generali-naming-rights-new-san-siro), in what the clubs say is “one of the largest stadium naming rights agreements in global sport”.

Beyond the anchor venue, developers are creating a 281,000-square-meter urban regeneration project in the surrounding campus to create “one of the city’s most important new destinations.”

To be utilised year-round, the space will integrate public spaces, green areas, leisure and retail destinations.

Sustainability, accessibility, and enhanced hospitality options are to be embedded into the venue and surrounding areas to provide Milan with a “stadium built for the future”, say the clubs.

> “A destination designed to contribute to the cultural, economic, and urban development of the city”

The project is being led by architecture firms Foster + Partners and MANICA, both of which also helped design London’s Wembley Stadium.

Construction is expected to begin in 2028, with the aim of opening in 2031 ahead of the UEFA Euro 2032 tournament, to be co-hosted by Italy and Türkiye.

The price tag of the new venue was not disclosed.

“More than a football stadium, the project represents a long-term investment in the future of two of world football’s most historic Clubs, a new home for generations of supporters, and a destination designed to contribute to the cultural, economic, and urban development of the city,” the clubs say.

Following the clubs’ transition to the new venue, San Siro is set to be [demolished](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/10/italys-iconic-san-siro-to-be-demolished/). Next year, it is slated to host names like U2, Karol G, Zucchero, and Ultimo.

Originally opening its doors in 1926, the venue had not been modernised since the 1990 World Cup, despite repeated efforts.

Milan is on a [music mission](https://www.iqmagazine.com/magazine/milan-the-engine-of-italys-live-music-boom/) at present, with the CTS Eventim-owned Unipol Dome (cap. 16,000) coming online this year.
