# NFL Ticketing Overhaul Targets Fan Data, Leaves Door Open to League-Owned Platform

## Summary

The NFL is planning a broad ticketing overhaul beyond its October resale-access deal with Ticketmaster, aiming to build a more personalized, data-connected buying experience via NFL Ticket HQ — and potentially laying groundwork for an NFL-owned ticketing platform — ahead of the 2027 season.

### Key Points
- Builds on the NFL's October deal letting competing authorized resale marketplaces access verified season-ticket inventory from all 32 teams.
- League wants to unify fan purchase/attendance data across commercial partners for personalization (execs compare it to "luxury watch" vs. "T.J. Maxx" shopping).
- Officials left open the possibility of an eventual NFL-owned ticketing platform.
- Target launch is before the 2027 schedule announcement in May.

### Highlights
- NFL aims to launch the revamped Ticket HQ system before its May 2027 schedule announcement.
- Builds directly on the October 2026 NFL-Ticketmaster open-resale-distribution agreement covering all 32 teams.
- League explicitly floated building its own ticketing platform rather than relying solely on Ticketmaster.

### Industry Problem
A top-tier primary-ticketing client (the NFL) is signaling dissatisfaction with being a pure pass-through ticketing customer — it wants direct control of fan data and a more tailored buying experience, which is both a warning sign for incumbent primary platforms and a model other large rights-holders may follow.

### Proposed Solution
The NFL's response (unify data, personalize buying, consider owning the platform) is a build-vs-buy signal. An independent primary platform like Etix should watch for similar data-ownership and platform-control asks from its own large clients, and differentiate by offering deeper first-party data access/integration and personalization tooling rather than losing clients to in-house build decisions.

The NFL is planning a sweeping transformation of its ticketing operations that extends well beyond expanding access to resale inventory, with league officials outlining ambitions to build a more personalized ticket-buying experience, connect customer data across commercial partners and potentially establish the foundation for an NFL-owned ticketing platform.

The broader strategy was detailed in interviews with Sports Business Journal published Thursday, November 19, as the league prepares to implement its new ticketing agreement with Ticketmaster ahead of the 2027 season.

Schuyler Simpson, the NFL’s senior director of club business development, and Bobby Gallo, senior vice president of club business development, described a system designed to make NFL Ticket HQ the preferred destination for fans purchasing tickets while creating new opportunities to share information and generate value across the league’s network of business partners.

The NFL hopes to launch the revamped system before its 2027 schedule announcement in May, according to SBJ.

The plans build on the [NFL’s October agreement with Ticketmaster](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/ticketmaster-nfl-resale-competitors-access/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), which established a new open-distribution model allowing competing authorized resale marketplaces to access verified season-ticket inventory from all 32 teams.

## Ticketing Data at the Center of NFL Strategy

A central component of the NFL’s plans involves using ticket purchases and attendance information to create more personalized experiences for fans while connecting the league’s business partners more closely.

Simpson compared the current online ticket-buying experience to shopping at T.J. Maxx, where customers must search through large amounts of merchandise to find what they want. The league wants its premium football product to be sold through a more tailored experience, similar to shopping for a luxury watch.

That approach would rely on information about fans’ interests, purchasing habits and previous interactions with the NFL to help surface more relevant ticket options.

But the league’s ambitions extend beyond ticket recommendations.

According to SBJ, the NFL wants to move away from its existing hub-and-spoke approach to sharing data with commercial partners, in which information flows primarily through the league, toward a more interconnected structure that allows partners to benefit from relationships with one another.

Simpson summarized the objective by saying, “We want to create more value for being an NFL partner.”

The executives described potential connections involving companies such as Uber, Marriott, Amazon, Fanatics and American Express, illustrating how information generated through ticket purchases and event attendance could support additional commercial opportunities.

For example, information about a fan attending a game could potentially help coordinate or personalize offers involving transportation, merchandise, hospitality and other services.

The proposed approach could give the NFL and its partners a more comprehensive understanding of customer behavior while making ticketing an increasingly important source of commercial data.

However, the league has not publicly detailed how information would be shared among participating companies, what limits would apply to its use or how customer consent and privacy preferences would be handled under the new system.

Another important element of the strategy involves changing where consumers begin shopping for NFL tickets.

Rather than having fans search Google for tickets to a particular game and then select among competing marketplaces, the league wants to encourage them to start directly with NFL Ticket HQ.

Simpson suggested that a sufficiently personalized experience, supported by a fan’s existing NFL identity and purchasing history, could make the league’s own website a more attractive destination than searching independently across the internet.

That shift could also benefit Ticketmaster, which will continue powering the NFL’s ticketing infrastructure under the new agreement.

According to Simpson, attracting more consumers directly through the official NFL ecosystem could reduce Ticketmaster’s reliance on paid search advertising to acquire customers.

The strategy illustrates how the NFL’s expanded distribution model could simultaneously open more inventory to competing marketplaces while strengthening the league’s influence over the overall customer relationship.

Under the [agreement announced October 21](https://media.nfl.com/news-and-releases/corporate-communications/nfl-and-ticketmaster-transform-longstanding-partnership-to-usher?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), Ticketmaster will remain the NFL’s exclusive top-tier secondary ticketing partner and continue operating the NFL Ticket Exchange. Other authorized marketplaces will gain broader access to verified season-ticket resale inventory that previously was available primarily through Ticketmaster.

That expanded access could allow competitors to offer a more comprehensive selection of NFL tickets and compete for customers through pricing, fees and service.

At the same time, a stronger NFL Ticket HQ could create a more centralized point of entry for consumers, even as transactions remain available through multiple marketplaces.

How those competing interests are balanced will depend partly on the eventual distribution arrangements, participating marketplaces’ access to customer information and the degree of independence they retain in marketing and servicing their customers.

## League-Owned Ticketing Platform Remains an Option

The NFL also has not ruled out eventually taking direct ownership of its ticketing infrastructure.

As [TicketNews reported in October 2025](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/10/nfl-reportedly-considering-sweeping-ticketing-changes-for-27/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), league officials had been exploring potentially significant changes to their ticketing operations ahead of the expiration of existing agreements in 2027.

Among the possibilities under consideration were developing or acquiring a ticketing platform, consolidating more control over customer information and reducing the league’s dependence on outside ticketing companies.

The subsequent Ticketmaster agreement appeared to provide a less disruptive path forward, retaining the company’s central technological role while broadening secondary-market distribution.

However, Gallo told SBJ that the new model could also move the NFL closer to eventually owning its own platform.

He emphasized that no decision has been made, while declining to rule out the possibility.

Gallo characterized the league’s position by observing that “no is just the first two letters of not now.”

The possibility remains significant because the NFL’s growing control over distribution, customer relationships and data infrastructure could make a future transition to a league-owned operation more feasible, even if Ticketmaster continues providing much of the underlying technology for years to come.

SBJ described the new Ticketmaster agreement as a seven-year contract with a four-year league option, although the precise mechanics of that option were not detailed.

The league also expects to bring additional resale marketplaces into the new distribution system. SeatGeek had previously reached an agreement in principle to participate at a new secondary partnership tier, but SBJ reported that no further progress on that arrangement was available to announce.

The next major milestone will be the anticipated rollout ahead of the 2027 schedule release, when the NFL’s plans for a more personalized purchasing experience and broader verified-ticket distribution are expected to begin taking shape.
