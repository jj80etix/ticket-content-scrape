# Avatar concerts firm Unit1 Studio raise $20m in global touring bid

## Summary

Avatar-concert producer Unit1 Studio raised $20 million to make virtual-performer touring commercially viable at scale, after demonstrating it could relocate its KT Tunstall avatar concert between two London venues — a step the company frames as overcoming the single-location economics that have limited the format since ABBA Voyage.

### Key Points
- $20m (€17.4m) raise backed by investors including Balderton.
- The KT Tunstall avatar show's relocation between venues is cited as proof of "touring" viability for avatar concerts.
- The company describes its ambition as "time travel tourism" — deployable virtual-performer concerts beyond one fixed venue.

### Highlights
- $20m raise is earmarked specifically for making avatar concerts multi-venue/touring-capable, not one-off installations.
- Technical milestone: relocating an avatar show between venues, previously a barrier since ABBA Voyage's single-site model.

### Industry Problem
Avatar and virtual-performer concerts have so far been locked into single, purpose-built venues (the ABBA Voyage model), limiting the format to a handful of markets and shutting most promoters and secondary/tertiary markets out of this new content category.

### Proposed Solution
Unit1 is building the underlying production and logistics stack to make avatar concerts portable across venues. For Etix, this is an early forward-bet signal worth monitoring as a potential new event category — and, if it scales, a new type of client (avatar-concert producers and promoters) needing standard ticketing and box-office infrastructure.
