# Aftershock Tops List of America’s Fastest-Rising Festival Prices

## Summary

A new Hard Rock Bet analysis of 31 major U.S. festivals found Aftershock's single-day GA price rose 264% since 2015 (from $65 to $235), the steepest increase in the study, with festival prices broadly outpacing inflation industry-wide — though a handful (Governors Ball, Seven Stars, Hulaween) held increases to single digits.

### Key Points
- Aftershock (+264%, $65→$235) tops the list; Hinterland Music Festival (+244%) and Louder Than Life (+228%) round out the top three.
- Coachella rose 73% ($125→$216) and Lollapalooza 68% ($110→$185) over the same decade — both well below the leaders.
- Governors Ball recorded the smallest increase (+3%) among the 31 festivals studied.
- Outside Lands is the most expensive on a per-hour basis ($21.50/hr); Summerfest the cheapest ($2.75/hr) despite a 74% price rise.
- Hard Rock Bet attributes the trend to inflation, demand growth, and expanded fan-experience offerings.

### Highlights
- Aftershock's 2026 $235 average GA price equals 18.22% of an average American's weekly salary.
- Ten-year price growth at the top festivals (200%+ for the top 3) is running far ahead of general inflation, a concrete data point on patron sticker-shock.

### Industry Problem
Rising festival GA prices are a visible, quantified example of the affordability pain point patrons already voice about primary-market pricing broadly — relevant context for how fee/price perception shapes checkout abandonment and brand trust, even though festivals set their own base prices independent of the ticketing platform.

### Proposed Solution
Use this kind of price-transparency data as grounding for Etix Assistant/patron-facing messaging around what a ticket price actually includes, and consider surfacing historical or comparative pricing context at checkout to build trust rather than let sticker shock be the patron's only signal — a differentiator against platforms with opaque, all-in-at-the-end pricing.

Aftershock Festival has posted the biggest ticket-price increase among major U.S. music festivals over the past decade, according to a new analysis from [Hard Rock Bet.](https://www.hardrock.bet/news/the-cost-of-americas-biggest-events/)

The California rock festival topped a study of 31 popular U.S. festivals, with its average single-day general admission price climbing from $65 in 2015 to $235 in 2026 — a 264% increase.

That puts Aftershock comfortably ahead of the rest of the field as festival ticket prices continue to rise across the country. Hard Rock Bet attributed the broader trend to factors including inflation, growing demand and expanded fan experiences.

Aftershock, which is expected to draw more than 160,000 festivalgoers in October, now costs an average of $19.58 per hour to attend, according to the study. Its $235 average single-day GA ticket also represents 18.22% of the average American’s weekly salary.

Kentucky’s Louder Than Life rounded out the top three. Its average GA price surged from $70 to $228, representing a 228% increase.

Sea.Hear.Now in New Jersey followed with a 161% jump, while Ohio’s Secret Dreams was fifth at 158%. BottleRock Napa Valley, Austin City Limits, Electric Forest, Stagecoach and Bonnaroo completed the study’s top 10.

Several of the country’s best-known festivals have also become substantially more expensive, although their increases fell well short of Aftershock’s.

Coachella’s average ticket price rose from $125 in 2015 to $216 in 2026, a 73% increase, according to the analysis. Lollapalooza climbed 68%, from $110 to $185, while Electric Daisy Carnival increased 103%, from $116 to $236.

Outside Lands ranked among the more expensive festivals on a per-hour basis. Its $258 average ticket worked out to $21.50 per hour, the highest figure listed in the study, followed by BottleRock at $21 per hour.

At the other end of the price-growth spectrum, Governors Ball recorded the smallest increase of the festivals analyzed. Its average price edged up just 3%, from $135 in 2015 to $139 in 2026.

Seven Stars Festival posted an 8% increase, while Hulaween rose 10%.

Ultra Music Festival also stood out for keeping its increase relatively modest. The Miami electronic music festival’s average GA price increased 20%, from $150 to $180. Hard Rock Bet calculated its 2026 cost at just under $15 per hour.

Summerfest remained the least expensive festival in the study on a per-hour basis despite seeing its average ticket price climb 74%. At $33 for 2026, the Milwaukee festival works out to just $2.75 per hour.

The findings offer another snapshot of how dramatically the economics of attending major live music events have shifted. While some marquee festivals have kept increases comparatively restrained, fans heading to others are now paying two or even three times what they would have a decade ago.

Hard Rock Bet compiled the study using average single-day general admission prices from official festival websites and secondary sources. When single-day tickets were unavailable, the price of a GA pass was divided by the number of days included.
