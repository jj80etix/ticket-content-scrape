# A Look Ahead to the 2026 State AG Elections From RAGA Executive Director Adam Piper

## Summary

A Troutman Pepper Locke "Regulatory Oversight" podcast episode features RAGA Executive Director Adam Piper previewing the 2026 state attorney general elections, which he calls the biggest AG election cycle in 30+ years. The discussion covers resource-heavy races (Iowa, Kansas, Ohio, Georgia, Michigan, Minnesota), expected turnover (~25% new AGs), and how AG races perform relative to top-of-ticket contests — general political analysis with no ticketing-specific content.

### Key Points
- RAGA investing more in 2026 AG races than its prior three election cycles combined
- ~25% of state AGs expected to be newly elected
- Episode also touches on how a lame-duck Trump administration might shape RAGA's 2027 priorities and AGs' growing role as bipartisan policy "main attractions"

In this episode of [*Regulatory Oversight*](https://www.troutman.com/podcast/regulatory-oversight/), [Ashley Taylor](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/ashley-l-taylor-jr/) and [Clayton Friedman](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/clayton-s-friedman/), co-leaders of Troutman Pepper Locke’s [State Attorneys General (AG)](https://www.troutman.com/services/practices/state-attorneys-general/) team, welcome Republican Attorneys General Association (RAGA) Executive Director Adam Piper for a wide-ranging look at the 2026 state AG races and the landscape heading into Election Day. Piper calls this the biggest AG election cycle in more than 30 years, with RAGA investing more directly in AG races than in the previous three years of political spend combined. He discusses the states drawing the most resources, including Iowa, Kansas, Ohio, Georgia, Michigan, and Minnesota. Piper also explains how AG candidates can outperform the top of the ticket when governor and Senate races stay close, but struggle when those races are not competitive.

With roughly 25% of AGs expected to be new, the conversation also turns to the caliber of candidates, shifting leadership, and what a possible lame-duck Trump administration could mean for RAGA’s 2027 priorities.

The group wraps up by discussing why AGs have become “main attractions” with broad tools to shape national policy, and how they often work across party lines.
