# Colombia’s live industry springs into action following devastating earthquake

## Summary

Colombia's live-events industry mobilized in the wake of a 7.4-magnitude earthquake that killed over 250 people and injured thousands west of Bogotá: OCESA Colombia's Vive Claro complex became a national aid point through Aug. 31, band Morat is running a donation drive and directing proceeds from its six-show Bogotá residency to school reconstruction, and several Colombian superstars (Shakira, Karol G, Maluma) urged fans to donate. The Petronio Alvarez Pacific Music Festival was suspended due to the disaster.

### Key Points
- 7.4-magnitude earthquake hit ~240km west of Bogotá on Aug. 10, Colombia's strongest this century, killing 250+ and injuring thousands.
- OCESA Colombia's Vive Claro complex is serving as a national aid/donation collection point through Aug. 31.
- Morat is running box-office and merch-stall donation drives during its six-show Movistar Arena residency (Aug 14-23), with proceeds also funding school reconstruction.
- The Petronio Alvarez Pacific Music Festival (30th edition) was suspended by local authorities.
- Follows two deadly Venezuela earthquakes in June, which prompted LA and Miami benefit concerts.

## Colombia’s live industry springs into action following devastating earthquake

Morat will set up a donation drive at their upcoming Bogotá residency, while the Vive Claro complex is being utilised as an aid station

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/30926061271_dcb61abab5_c.jpg)

Colombia © Niek van Son

Colombia’s live industry has sprung into action following a 7.4-magnitude earthquake that ripped through the western portion of the country earlier this week, killing over 250 people and injuring thousands more.

On Monday (10 August), the South American country’s strongest earthquake this century hit roughly 240 kilometres (150 miles) west of Bogotá, slamming major cities including Cali and Pereira. In addition to the human toll, thousands of buildings were severely damaged.

In the capital, OCESA Colombia-operated Vive Claro complex will serve as a national aid point until 31 August, compiling donations that will be distributed by the national government to those affected by the disaster.

Colombian rock band Morat is also setting up a donation drive in line with their six-show stint at Movistar Arena in Bogotá, set to run from 14-23 August. Attendees can also make donations at the box office and merchandise stalls that will be channelled through UNICEF.

In addition, the band says it will allocate a portion of proceeds from these shows to the recovery and reconstruction of schools in the regions most affected by the earthquake.

“We know this is just our way of contributing a little to this whole cause,” Morat said. “So we also invite all of you to join us, to keep donating, and to keep raising awareness of all the problems we’re facing. We’re sending you lots of strength, lots of hugs, and we’ll see you soon.”

> “In times like these, Colombians know how to come together and support one another”

Other homegrown superstars, including Shakira, Karol G, J Balvin, Maluma, and Feid, have also spoken out about the tragedy, urging donations to a variety of local charities working to support victims.

“Dear Colombia, my heart is with everyone who felt this earthquake today and with the families who are going through moments of anguish,” Shakira wrote online. “In times like these, Colombians know how to come together and support one another.

“All my strength and love to my homeland. Let’s embrace each other tightly and stay alert to the instructions so we can help as soon as possible,” she added.

In the aftermath of the disaster, a cultural live event, the Petronio Alvarez Pacific Music Festival, was suspended by local authorities ahead of its 30th edition. It was scheduled to take place from today (12 August) to Monday (17) and bring thousands of people to the country’s Pacific region.

Colombia’s natural disaster follows closely behind a pair of earthquakes in Venezuela on 24 June that killed over 3,500 people and injured thousands.

In response, the live industry lined up to support victims, with two benefit concerts in [Los Angeles](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/live-music-industry-rallies-for-venezuela-earthquake-relief/) and [Miami](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/latin-stars-line-up-for-venezuela-benefit-concert/) scheduled in the coming two weeks.
