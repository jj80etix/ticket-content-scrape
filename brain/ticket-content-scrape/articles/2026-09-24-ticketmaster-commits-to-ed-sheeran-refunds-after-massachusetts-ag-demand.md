# Ticketmaster Commits to Ed Sheeran Refunds After Massachusetts AG Demand

## Summary

Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell announced that Ticketmaster agreed to a formal refund process for Ed Sheeran's Sept. 25-26 Gillette Stadium shows after the AG's office intervened over nearly 200 consumer complaints. The AG argued that the near-total lineup overhaul — Macklemore, Lukas Graham, Aaron Rowe and Finneas all dropping off after Macklemore's removal over pro-Palestine remarks — made Ticketmaster's standard no-refund-for-opening-act-changes policy "unfair" under the state's Consumer Protection Act. Ticketmaster must accept refund requests up to 24 hours before each show via phone or online customer service, with the event organizer (promoter) authorizing the refunds that Ticketmaster processes.

### Highlights
- Complaints grew from ~25 (as of last Friday) to "more than 170" formal complaints / "nearly 200" affected consumers by Thursday.
- Refund deadline is 24 hours before showtime: 8pm Thursday for the Sept. 25 show, 8pm Friday for Sept. 26.
- The AG's letter invokes the Massachusetts Consumer Protection Act, arguing the scale of lineup change — not a single opening-act swap — makes the standard no-refund terms unfair.
- Ticketmaster says it is processing refunds on the event organizer's authorization, not unilaterally.

### Industry Problem
Standard ticketing terms treat any opening-act change as non-refundable, but state regulators are signaling a threshold exists: when changes are sweeping enough, that blanket policy itself becomes a deceptive/unfair practice, exposing primary platforms to state consumer-protection action whenever multiple support acts drop off a marketed bill at once.

### Proposed Solution
The resolution here was reactive — AG pressure forced an ad hoc refund window — rather than a standing policy. A primary platform could pre-empt this by defining and publishing an internal threshold for "material lineup change" that auto-triggers refund eligibility and clear consumer communication, rather than improvising a deadline after a regulator letter arrives.

Ticketmaster has committed to allowing refunds for Ed Sheeran’s two upcoming Gillette Stadium concerts after the Massachusetts Attorney General’s Office demanded that consumers be given a clear way to get their money back following sweeping changes to the tour lineup.

Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell announced the agreement Thursday, one day after [TicketNews reported that some customers were successfully obtaining refunds for the Sept. 25 and 26 shows despite there being no public announcement telling ticketholders that option was available](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/ed-sheeran-gillette-refunds-policy/).

The new announcement puts an official process — and a tight deadline — around those refunds. The Attorney General’s Office says consumers who purchased through Ticketmaster can request a refund by contacting Ticketmaster Customer Service at 1-800-653-8000 or [through the company’s online customer-service channels](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/9605825450129-How-to-Contact-Us).

Ticketmaster is asking that refund requests be submitted at least 24 hours before the ticketed show begins. Both Gillette concerts are currently listed by Ticketmaster for 8 p.m., meaning customers seeking refunds for Friday’s concert should make their requests by 8 p.m. Thursday, while the corresponding deadline for Saturday’s show would be 8 p.m. Friday.

The Attorney General’s Office [told TicketNews last Friday that it had received at least 25 complaints](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/ed-sheeran-refunds-ticket-market-softens/) from consumers seeking refunds for the Gillette dates. Thursday’s release says that number has now grown to **more than 170 consumer complaints**, while Campbell characterized the number of affected consumers contacting her office as nearly 200.

After receiving those complaints, the AGO sent Ticketmaster a letter challenging the application of its standard policy covering changes to opening acts.

Ticketing terms generally allow an event’s supporting lineup to change without automatically triggering refunds. The Massachusetts office did not argue that every opening-act change requires reimbursement. Instead, it said the scale of the changes surrounding the Loop Tour made this situation different.

“In this case, changing nearly all of the acts and performers at the same time constitutes such a dramatic change in the service that Ticketmaster’s reliance on the purchase policy appears unfair,” the AGO wrote.

The letter also reminded Ticketmaster that unfair or deceptive commercial practices can violate the Massachusetts Consumer Protection Act.

According to the Attorney General’s Office, Ticketmaster responded by informing the state that the event organizer will allow refunds for tickets purchased through Ticketmaster for both Gillette Stadium concerts.

That wording is notable: Ticketmaster is processing the requests, while the AGO describes the underlying refund authorization as coming from the event organizer.

## Refund Question Followed Major Loop Tour Changes

The dispute emerged after the lineup originally marketed for Sheeran’s Gillette Stadium concerts largely fell apart.

Gillette’s 2026 concert schedule had initially advertised Macklemore, Lukas Graham and Aaron Rowe alongside Sheeran for the Sept. 25 and 26 dates.

[Macklemore was subsequently removed from the remaining stadium dates](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/macklemore-ed-sheeran-stadiums-cancel-shows/) after multiple venues objected to his continued participation following his pro-Palestinian remarks and performance of “Hind’s Hall” during Sheeran’s MetLife Stadium shows. Sheeran later confirmed that venues had threatened upcoming concerts if Macklemore remained on the bill, while saying the ultimate decision to remove him was made by promoter Messina Touring Group.

Aaron Rowe and Lukas Graham subsequently withdrew from scheduled appearances, while Finneas also pulled out of planned support dates and Sheeran collaborators Beoga left the tour.

The resulting uncertainty over refunds first surfaced ahead of Sheeran’s Sept. 19 Philadelphia concert, where TicketNews found customers receiving refunds upon request even though the concert remained scheduled. Similar reports subsequently emerged from Massachusetts customers this week, but as of Wednesday there still had been no broad public announcement establishing that Gillette buyers could obtain refunds.

Thursday’s AGO announcement changes that.

It does not, however, say whether Ticketmaster or the event organizer is separately contacting every purchaser to notify them of the accommodation. The release also specifically addresses tickets purchased through Ticketmaster and does not establish a refund process for tickets purchased through other resale marketplaces.

Consumers who encounter problems obtaining a refund can also file a complaint directly with the Massachusetts Attorney General’s Office online or call its consumer hotline at 617-727-8400.

**Questions Remain for Later Loop Tour Dates**

The Massachusetts agreement also leaves unresolved whether similar refund accommodations will be offered for Sheeran’s remaining U.S. concerts. Following the Gillette dates, the Loop Tour is scheduled to visit Atlanta, Indianapolis, Charlotte, Arlington, Texas, Hollywood, Florida, and Tampa through Nov. 7.

Ticketmaster is the official primary seller for each of those markets except Arlington, where SeatGeek handles primary ticketing. The tour’s official FAQ continues to state that sales are final and that customers who change their minds are not entitled to refunds.

There are already indications that the issue may extend beyond Massachusetts. A Charlotte ticketholder reported being denied a refund earlier this month and said a complaint had been filed with the North Carolina Attorney General’s Office, while multiple subsequent commenters reported successfully obtaining refunds for the Oct. 17 show this week. In Atlanta, at least one customer reported being denied a refund as recently as Sept. 18. Those accounts are anecdotal, and TicketNews has not found a public announcement establishing a refund policy for the remaining tour dates.
