# Ticketmaster Names Jim Freeman CTO, Expands AI and Product Leadership

## Summary

Ticketmaster has appointed Jim Freeman — a 30-year engineering veteran who helped build Amazon Prime Video before serving as CTO of Zalando and an adviser at Permira — as its new chief technology officer, with a mandate to lead an "AI-first" push across the company. The move, part of a broader slate that also adds Rachel Smith as first SVP of data science/ML and Chuck Mortimore as SVP of product (plus the newly acquired Ragie.ai team), is framed by global president Saumil Mehta as central to delivering "reliable, secure and personalized experiences." Freeman's brief explicitly includes strengthening defenses against ticket-buying bots alongside building new agentic AI tools for fans and clients.

### Key Points
- Jim Freeman named CTO; prior roles: Amazon (Prime Video, scaled to 30M+ monthly active users), Zalando CTO (29 markets, 62M+ customers), Permira senior adviser.
- Freeman's mandate: global tech operations, agentic AI tools, and strengthening bot/unauthorized-activity defenses.
- Appointment is part of a three-person leadership slate (with Smith, Mortimore) plus the Ragie.ai acquisition team.
- Framed explicitly as moving Ticketmaster toward an "AI-first" organizational approach.

### Highlights
- CTO mandate explicitly pairs "agentic AI tools" with strengthened bot defense — fraud mitigation isn't separated from the AI/product push.
- Hire brings direct large-scale marketplace/streaming-platform engineering experience (Amazon, Zalando) to ticketing.

### Industry Problem
Ticketmaster is signaling that bot/fraud defense and AI-driven personalization are now one unified technical mandate under its CTO, rather than separate initiatives — raising the bar for what an integrated AI + anti-bot architecture looks like at scale.

### Proposed Solution
Track Freeman's early technical roadmap and hires as a leading indicator of where Ticketmaster will next ship agentic AI features; Etix's own bot-mitigation and AI-assistant investments should be benchmarked against this unified approach rather than treated as separate workstreams.

Ticketmaster has appointed former Amazon executive Jim Freeman as chief technology officer, alongside two additional senior leadership hires focused on data science and product development, as the company expands its investment in artificial intelligence and ticketing technology.

The company [announced the appointments Monday, Oct. 19](https://news.livenationentertainment.com/news/investing-in-the-teams-and-technology-behind-global-ticketing?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), naming Rachel Smith as its first senior vice president of data science and machine learning and Chuck Mortimore as senior vice president of product. Ticketmaster is also expanding its AI capabilities through the addition of the team behind startup Ragie.ai.

The appointments are part of a broader effort to strengthen Ticketmaster’s engineering operations, expand its use of machine learning and develop new technology for fans, artists, venues and other ticketing clients.

“Reliable, secure and personalized experiences depend on how effectively we use technology and data,” said Saumil Mehta, Ticketmaster’s global president.

His responsibilities include advancing the company’s use of agentic AI tools, integrating artificial intelligence across its operations and strengthening defenses against ticket-buying bots and other unauthorized activity.

Ticketmaster says it intends to make AI a central component of its operations, with Freeman leading the transition toward an AI-first approach across the organization.

Freeman previously held senior engineering leadership roles at Amazon, where he helped build and launch Prime Video and subsequently expand the streaming service to more than 30 million monthly active users globally. His work also included expanding the platform’s distribution and device partnerships and launching Prime Video Direct and Amazon Channels.

He later served as chief technology officer at European fashion marketplace Zalando, which operates across 29 markets and serves more than 62 million active customers. Most recently, Freeman was a senior adviser at investment firm Permira.

Freeman fills a position that had been vacant since February, when former Ticketmaster CTO Carlos Alvarez-Coch departed the company, [according to Variety](https://variety.com/2026/music/news/ticketmaster-all-in-ai-amazon-exec-chief-technology-officer-1236914016/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

**Smith** will lead Ticketmaster’s data science and machine learning efforts in a newly established senior leadership position.

Her responsibilities include building a centralized data science function, expanding the company’s existing analytics capabilities and incorporating data-driven decision-making more extensively into product development and business operations.

Smith joins Ticketmaster from Block, where she helped develop and expand data science teams supporting Square, Cash App and TIDAL. Ticketmaster said her experience building analytical teams across large consumer technology platforms will support its efforts to make greater use of data throughout the ticketing business.

**Mortimore**, meanwhile, will oversee product strategy related to identity, account access and trust, with a focus on improving how customers securely access and manage their accounts and tickets.

His responsibilities include strengthening protections against unauthorized account access and other security threats while developing products intended to improve the ticket-management experience.

Mortimore brings more than 20 years of product leadership experience, including positions at Disney, Stripe, Visa and Salesforce. Earlier in his career, he held engineering roles at Netscape and Sun Microsystems.

Most recently, Mortimore led identity technology initiatives at Disney, overseeing efforts to consolidate identity systems across the company’s global operations, including its theme parks, Disney+ and Hulu.

Beyond the executive appointments, Ticketmaster is incorporating the team behind **Ragie.ai**, a company specializing in AI infrastructure and information retrieval technology.

[Ragie announced in July](https://www.ragie.ai/blog/ragie-joins-ticketmaster?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) that it was joining Ticketmaster. The startup developed technology designed to help AI applications access, retrieve and use information from business data and documents.

Ticketmaster said the Ragie team will support the development and adoption of AI tools across its operations, contributing expertise in infrastructure and retrieval systems as the company expands its use of artificial intelligence.

The latest investments build on technology initiatives already underway at Ticketmaster, which has [outlined existing uses of AI](https://business.ticketmaster.com/how-ticketmaster-uses-ai-to-enhance-event-discovery-fan-support-and-experiences/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) in event discovery, search, customer support and ticket purchasing.

The company has also been developing integrations designed to make event information and ticket inventory accessible through conversational search and AI-powered platforms.

The appointments come nearly a year after [Mehta was named Ticketmaster’s global president](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/10/ticketmaster-names-saumil-mehta-global-president/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), when the company identified accelerating its AI transformation as a priority under his leadership.

Ticketmaster’s latest announcement did not provide a timeline for the introduction of new consumer-facing agentic AI tools or identify specific products expected to emerge from the expanded technology leadership team.
