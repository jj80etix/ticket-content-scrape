# Live Nation Blames Venue Location, Disputes Concert Obligations in £1 Million Cornwall Lawsuit

## Summary

Live Nation has filed its defense against a £1 million breach-of-contract lawsuit from WV Leisure, operator of The Wyldes concert venue in Cornwall, England, arguing the venue's remote location limited ticket demand rather than any failure on Live Nation's part. Live Nation also filed a roughly £575,000 counterclaim. At issue is whether a 2023 five-year agreement obligated Live Nation to produce at least eight concerts annually (it produced six in 2024 before ending the deal), plus disputes over artist-booking choices and marketing effectiveness.

### Key Points
- WV Leisure seeks £1M+ in damages; Live Nation counterclaims for ~£575,000 (including a £150,000 advance repayment).
- Central dispute: did the contract guarantee a minimum of eight concerts/year, or merely permit them?
- Live Nation argues the venue's remote location was the real constraint on ticket sales, not its promotion or booking choices.
- WV Leisure disputes Live Nation's artist-pairing choices (e.g., mismatched acts on consecutive nights) and defends its own marketing.
- The Wyldes has since scaled back to smaller "Rewylding" events; neither side's claims have been adjudicated yet.

## Transcript

Live Nation is pushing back against a £1 million breach-of-contract lawsuit brought by the operator of an independent concert venue in Cornwall, England, arguing that the property’s remote location limited ticket demand and that the promoter was under no obligation to produce a minimum number of concerts. The company has also filed a counterclaim seeking approximately £575,000.

WV Leisure, which operates The Wyldes outdoor concert venue near Bude, alleges that Live Nation Music UK failed to fulfill its obligations under a five-year agreement signed in 2023. [As reported in October](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/live-nation-wyldes-cornwall-concert-contract-lawsuit/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the venue operator is seeking more than £1 million in damages, citing shortcomings in concert production, artist booking and marketing.

Live Nation had not filed its defense when the lawsuit was initially reported. In court documents [detailed Wednesday by The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/music/news/live-nation-wyldes-bude-high-court-b3091480.html?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the promoter disputes both WV Leisure’s interpretation of the contract and its assessment of why the concerts were financially unsuccessful.

“Its remote and relatively inaccessible location operates as a material constraint on ticket demand and sales for events held at the site,” Richard Gillis KC, representing Live Nation, said of the Cornwall venue.

At the center of the dispute is whether the agreement required Live Nation to produce at least eight concerts annually beginning in 2024 or merely granted the promoter the right to do so. Live Nation produced six concerts at The Wyldes in 2024 before terminating the agreement in 2025.

Live Nation contends that it warned WV Leisure during the planning of the 2024 concert season that staging additional events would lead to further financial losses. The promoter maintains that both parties subsequently agreed not to proceed with additional concerts beyond the six already planned.

The company’s counterclaim seeks approximately £425,000 under what it contends are the contract’s provisions for sharing losses, along with repayment of a £150,000 advance made in 2023.

## Disagreement Over Booking and Ticket Sales

WV Leisure’s lawsuit also challenges Live Nation’s approach to artist selection and concert promotion, including its decision to book artists with substantially different audiences on consecutive nights.

Simple Minds and Anne-Marie performed on successive nights in June 2024, as did Jess Glynne and James Arthur the following weekend. The venue operator argues that booking artists with greater audience overlap would have encouraged fans to purchase tickets to multiple performances.

Live Nation maintains that its booking decisions reflected artist availability and budget constraints and that, to the best of its recollection, WV Leisure agreed to the bookings without raising significant concerns.

The promoter also denies that its marketing efforts were inadequate, arguing that it employed promotional strategies that had proven successful elsewhere. It instead points to the venue’s location and shortcomings in WV Leisure’s own marketing and sales strategy as factors limiting the commercial prospects of the concerts.

WV Leisure claims the 2024 performances generated approximately £850,000 in losses and would have produced more than £1.1 million in profit had Live Nation fulfilled its contractual obligations. The promoter disputes that projection, questioning whether the concerts would have been profitable at all.

The competing claims highlight the importance of how financial risk is allocated in agreements between independent venues and major concert promoters, particularly when ticket sales fall short of expectations. Whether the agreement guaranteed a minimum number of performances or gave Live Nation discretion to reduce its concert schedule is a central point of contention.

The Wyldes, which describes itself as a 10,000-capacity outdoor venue, has previously hosted performances by Paul Weller, Fatboy Slim and McFly. It suspended its traditional large-scale concert series in 2025 in favor of smaller events under its Rewylding banner.

The venue has not permanently ceased operations. Its [official website](https://www.thewyldescornwall.com/whats-on?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) indicates that its Cornwall Forever event, originally scheduled for 2026, has been postponed until summer 2027.

Neither party’s claims have been adjudicated, and a hearing in the case has yet to take place.
