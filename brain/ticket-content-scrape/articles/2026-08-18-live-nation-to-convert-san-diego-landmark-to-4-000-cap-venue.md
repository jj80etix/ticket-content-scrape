# Live Nation to convert San Diego landmark to 4,000-cap venue

## Summary

Live Nation will restore San Diego's historic Wonder Bread Building into a new 4,000-capacity live venue, opening in 2028, aiming to fill a gap between the city's small clubs and large arenas.

### Key Points
- Live Nation to convert the 1924 Wonder Bread Building (Petco Park area) into a ~4,000-cap venue, opening 2028; venue name TBD.
- Design by Blueprint Studio (Live Nation in-house) and Gensler, preserving the historic façade and silo.
- Positioned to attract mid-sized touring acts San Diego has "largely missed out on" due to lacking a venue between small clubs and large arenas.
- Organic venue-development/expansion news rather than a ticketing product, M&A, or regulatory story — no clear substantive match to tracked research questions.

## Transcript

The company said the city has been missing a venue of this size and that the project "fills a gap in the San Diego music scene"

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/San-Diego-Live-Nation.jpg)

San Diego Wonderbread Building (impression) © Live Nation

Live Nation is to restore the famed San Diego Wonder Bread building into a new 4,000-capacity live venue, which it says “fills a gap in the San Diego music scene”.

The plans for the site in Petco Park include restoration and preservation of the building’s historic façade, renovating its historic silo and the building of modern production capacity into the existing structure, with opening date set for 2028. The title of the venue is not yet confirmed.

Blueprint Studio, Live Nation’s in-house design group, and Gensler are designing the venue around artist and fan experience: acoustics, sightlines, and backstage amenities.

Live Nation said the new space “will give artists a purpose-built downtown stage at a capacity currently missing from the market”.

> “This project represents an investment in San Diego’s future as a destination for live music”

The move aims to attract a broader range of touring acts to San Diego, with Live Nation saying the city has “largely missed out on” growing acts at the mid-sized venue level.

The company added that San Diego “currently has no purpose-built venue between its small clubs and its large arenas”.

“This project represents an investment in San Diego’s future as a destination for live music and entertainment. We’ve been missing a venue of this size, and this project will help attract a broader range of touring artists, bring more shows to the region, and contribute to the continued growth and energy of downtown,” said Live Nation vice president of the San Diego market John Schalk.

Built in 1924 and having ceased being the home of the Southern California Baking Company 1952, the building has since been home to other businesses including brewers, and was threatened by a planned stadium development in 2007.

Building owners Megan Heine and Torrey Lee said: “We have always believed in the importance of honouring the history of this building. We agreed to support this vision because it respects what makes this place special while giving it a new purpose that will serve the community for generations to come.”
