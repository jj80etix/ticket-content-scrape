# Market Focus Seattle: From Emerald City To Silicon Forest

## Summary

Pollstar's monthly Market Focus feature profiles Seattle/Tacoma's live-music scene and history, from its jazz and grunge legacy to its current diversified independent club and touring-venue ecosystem.

### Key Points
- Seattle/Tacoma concert market ranked No. 11 nationally by live revenue in 2025: $265.8M, 2.69M tickets sold (per Pollstar Boxoffice data).
- Traces the market's musical history from 1940s jazz through the grunge era (Nirvana, Pearl Jam, Soundgarden) to current acts (Brandi Carlile, Macklemore, Fleet Foxes).
- Describes the city's current strengths: independent club culture, major touring venues, and arts infrastructure.

### Seattle Continues To Grow As Tech And Live Entertainment Mecca

Seattle’s music scene is much more than the grunge story that made it famous. The market, which includes Tacoma, has one of the deepest and most distinctive music cultures in the country with the Seattle Sound spanning generations and styles of music.

*This article is part of Pollstar’s monthly Market Focus feature, the print version of which includes additional charts and information.*

Before rock, Seattle had an important jazz legacy with artists such as Ray Charles and Quincy Jones meeting as young musicians in the late 1940s in Seattle’s Jackson Street jazz scene. Jimi Hendrix grew up in Seattle’s Central District and remains one of the city’s most important musical figures.

In the 1980s and early ’90s, the city became synonymous with Nirvana, Pearl Jam, Soundgarden, Alice in Chains and Mudhoney, making “Seattle” almost shorthand for flannel and hair that could use a brushing. Post-grunge, the city and immediate vicinity continued producing nationally significant acts including Death Cab for Cutie, Modest Mouse, Sleater-Kinney, The Shins, Band of Horses, Fleet Foxes, Brandi Carlile, Macklemore and Ryan Lewis.

Today, Seattle has moved from being famous for one particular sound to being a highly diversified music ecosystem built around a strong independent club culture, major touring venues, a sophisticated arts infrastructure, an active DIY independent movement and a talented pool of homegrown artists.

The Seattle/Tacoma concert market ranks No. 11 with live revenue in 2025 of $265,849,524 and 2,685,620 tickets sold, according to Pollstar Boxoffice reports.

The musical landscape is remarkably diverse with indie rock, punk, metal, hip-hop, electronic, jazz, Americana, R&B, experimental, global/world music coexisting across a network of clubs and theaters including The Showbox, one of Seattle’s iconic midsize rooms, dating back to 1939; McMenamins Spanish Ballroom, a 700-capacity Tacoma venue in a restored 1916 Elks Temple; Dimitriou’s Jazz Alley, which has been open in Seattle since 1980; and The Neptune, which according to Pollstar data, hosted the largest club show in June 2025 with a three-night run by They Might Be Giants. The band grossed $120,000 off 3,000 tickets sold.

Despite its robust club scene, the market lacked options in the 2,000- to 6,000-capacity range.

LIGHT AND LOUD: Lumen Field, home to the NFL’s Seattle Seahwaks and one of the FIFA World Cup 2026 host venues this summer, recently completed a $20 million modernization project. Photo by Steph Chambers / Getty Images

In 2006, WAMU Theater opened inside the Lumen Field Event Center as a 7,200-capacity general-admission concert venue expandable to 9,000, with a 4,700-seat reserved configuration. But there was still a gap between 1,500-cap music halls and WAMU or even 18,000-capacity Climate Pledge Arena, which explains why a new 5,500-capacity Live Nation venue is getting so much attention.

“Seattle has great clubs, theaters and arenas,” says Jeff Trisler, Regional President, Pacific Northwest, Live Nation. “At 5,500 capacity, this project will fill an important space between them. It will be a purpose-built room that offers artists another step as they grow, and more opportunities for fans to see them. The location in the epicenter of the city’s vibrant stadium district makes it an even better addition. It complements the places Seattle already loves and gives more tours a reason to play in our city.”

The proposed SoDo Ballroom is a partnership between Live Nation and Seattle’s First Avenue Entertainment, the local ownership/development group associated with the Mariners and The Boxyard. The venue is planned for The Boxyard, directly across from Lumen Field and WAMU Theater and next to T-Mobile Park, with a target opening in 2029.

“I’ve spent most of my life working in the music business here, and Seattle is special,” says Trisler. “The fans are great: we love to support artists, especially local ones, and are always willing to discover something new. Artists love playing this city because they can feel that connection with audiences. Our goal is to build a space that is true to Seattle. One that puts the music first, treats artists well, and gives fans a great experience from the moment they walk in.”

The new venue integrates with Victory Hall at The Boxyard, with an indoor capacity of up to 1,200 and an additional 1,000 outdoor capacity, effectively turning the SoDo (South of Downtown) area into a sports and entertainment district with multiple venues clustered together.

Festivals are the market’s other stronghold.

Bumbershoot is the big legacy event. The multimedia fest celebrated its 53rd year at Seattle Center over Labor Day weekend (Sept. 5–6) with a music lineup that illustrated how broad Seattle’s cultural identity has become and included Death Cab for Cutie, Turnstile, Bikini Kill, Japanese Breakfast, Blood Orange, De La Soul, Orville Peck and Yves Tumor.

Making its debut Nov. 5-8 is Baba Obscura, a four-day festival spread across six stages in Pioneer Square. With a heavy focus on rock, punk, psychedelia, garage and alternative music, the lineup features 58 artists including The Black Angels, Mdou Moctar, Black Mountain, The Gories, Downtown Boys, Rose City Band, Provoker and numerous Pacific Northwest acts.  
Ryan Granger, guitarist and vocalist for Seattle rock band The Grizzled Mighty, founded the festival and sees the event as an artist-development platform.

“I’ve seen how transformative the right stage, resources and support can be for an artist,” says Granger, who owns and operates venue Baba Yaga on Pioneer Square. “This is about creating those opportunities and building community while celebrating the independent spirit that makes Seattle’s music scene so special.”

Seattle also has a strong performing arts side. McCaw Hall is home to Seattle Opera and Pacific Northwest Ballet, and the Seattle Symphony performs at Benaroya Hall, which reopens Oct. 18 after a $20 million renovation through the symphony’s Amplify campaign. The project reimagines the public spaces and upgrades the hospitality experience, including a new partnership with Sodexo Live! and Seattle chef Ethan Stowell. The renovated hall will reopen with a free, day-long festival featuring Seattle Symphony, the Head and Heart and other Seattle artists.

Also on the horizon is a new 271-seat Center for Chamber Music inside the newly refreshed U.S. Bank Building, which is billed as the city’s first permanent venue designed specifically for chamber music. Construction is expected to begin this winter with the opening planned for early 2028. In addition to the concert hall, the venue will include rehearsal space, recording capabilities, hospitality space for visiting artists and Seattle Chamber Music Society offices.

All of that signals that Seattle’s sports and entertainment ecosystem is entering an expansion phase.

Lumen Field received a 2026 FIFA World Cup makeover with the Super Bowl LX champion Seahawks’ “Voice of the Fan” modernization campaign. The stadium hosted six World Cup matches, including two knockout-round games, making it one of the most visible venues. USA Today ranked Lumen Field the No. 1 NFL stadium in 2023 and, during the FIFA tournament, The Athletic ranked the stadium and Seattle No. 1 in a tie with Mercedes-Benz Stadium and Atlanta as the top World Cup host venue.

“None of these achievements would have been possible without my extraordinary team at Lumen Field,” says Zach Hensley, Vice President of Operations & General Manager of Lumen Field. “Their unwavering commitment to execution, teamwork, and excellence enabled us to deliver some of the most significant events in our venue’s history with a continual focus on delivering the highest guest experience.”

Among the fan-facing changes at the stadium was transforming the Hawks Nest from bench seating to individual seats with seat backs – creating a new hawk-eye pattern in the upper end zone; a new pedestrian walkway along the Lumen Field North Lot to enhance pedestrian safety on event days; and the addition of a Concert Wall on the field level, featuring individual plaques recognizing each of the stadium’s largest concerts to date. The display also includes a jersey showcasing the next artist scheduled to perform at Lumen Field.

“There are a lot of great venues to experience live entertainment throughout the Pacific Northwest, but in my opinion, Lumen Field stands above the rest,” says Hensley. “Few venues can match the iconic backdrop of the Seattle skyline framed behind the stage, creating a truly memorable setting for both artists and fans. The stadium’s unique design also enhances acoustics and crowd energy, helping create an atmosphere that is both intimate and electric despite its scale.”

First & Goal Inc. operates the Lumen Field stadium complex, which includes Lumen Field and the 325,000-square-foot Lumen Field Event Center and WAMU Theater. Lumen Field, which opened in 2002, is home to three professional sports teams – the NFL’s Seattle Seahawks, the MLS’ Seattle Sounders, and the NWSL’s Seattle Reign FC.

WAMU Theater recently launched “Amplified Access,” giving guests the option to upgrade with a private bar, exclusive food and beverage offerings and expedited service lines. The goal is to reduce friction and give fans more time to enjoy the full concert experience.

Lumen Field and the Lumen Field Event Center host more than 300 event days per year and attract more than 2 million attendees annually.

“One of our biggest challenges over the past year was maintaining business continuity with having three tenants in the Seahawks, Sounders FC and Reign FC while also focusing on driving live entertainment events in the WAMU Theater and Lumen Field,” says Hensley. “Additionally, focusing on maximizing event profitability while simultaneously executing millions of dollars in capital improvements tied to the FIFA World Cup and preparing for the upcoming NFL season. The challenge was not simply completing the projects, but doing so without disrupting the guest experience, impacting event operations, or sacrificing financial performance.”

In December, Lumen Field achieved full TRUE Gold Certification under the Total Resource Use and Efficiency (TRUE) rating system, which is administered by Green Business Certification Inc. (GBCI), the world’s leading sustainability and health certification and credentialing body. Lumen Field is the second NFL venue to achieve TRUE Certification for zero-waste sustainability programs.

To earn TRUE Gold Certification, the Seahawks and Lumen Field had to track and demonstrate sustainable practices over a one-year period. Through the facility’s efforts during this window, the venue’s closed-loop recycling program diverted more than 6 million pounds of waste – or 93% – from the landfill.

“Being a global leader in sustainable practices continues to be one of Lumen Field’s top operational priorities,” says Hensley.  
On Sept. 3, 2026, the Seahawks officially completed their sale to the Khosla family for $9.612 billion, a record NFL franchise transaction.

“Demand is healthy, premium experiences are thriving, and marquee content continues to perform at an elite level,” offers Hensley, who was named a 2026 VenuesNow All Star (see page 101). “For venues and operators with strong promoter relationships, diversified revenue streams, and a reputation for execution, I believe the outlook remains very favorable. For Lumen Field specifically, I’d characterize the current environment as an opportunity-rich market where execution and event selection matter more than ever.”

The NHL Kraken ownership group has become the majority owner of Climate Pledge Arena through One Roof Sports and Entertainment, bringing the arena into a broader portfolio that also includes Kraken Community Iceplex, One Roof Foundation, Memorial Stadium redevelopment and interests in the Coachella Valley Firebirds and Acrisure Arena. VenuesNow Conference speaker Samantha Holloway, majority owner and managing partner of One Roof Sports and Entertainment, is driving the organization’s broader strategy by developing a model where team and arena ownership, development and community investment are integrated into a single sports and entertainment company. Holloway represents a new generation of sports owners who are building an ecosystem rather than simply owning a team.

Climate Pledge Arena is central to the strategy. Holloway’s group increased its ownership stake in the arena, making it the majority owner, while Oak View Group (Pollstar’s parent company) retained a significant minority interest and continues to operate and book the building.

Holloway is currently the most visible publicly known potential bidder for an NBA expansion franchise in Seattle, which would potentially revive the SuperSonics franchise. The NBA is considering expansion, with Seattle among the markets viewed as a potential candidate.

Thirty-five miles south of Seattle, the Tacoma Dome is a 43-year-old, city-owned, flexible 21,000-capacity arena that remains a major concert and event venue – which includes hosting 22 high school and college graduation ceremonies in 2026. But its next challenge is keeping a distinctive older building competitive with newer arenas in the region.

A 2012 feasibility and needs evaluation identified approximately $145 million in capital investment needed to address deferred maintenance and ensure the Tacoma Dome’s long-term viability and competitiveness. The city subsequently invested $31 million in 2017–2018 to address some of the most critical needs.

T-Mobile Park, home of Major League Baseball’s Seattle Mariners, continues capital improvements and, with a capacity of 47,943, is expanding its role as a concert venue. The Mariners announced a major package of ballpark improvements for 2026, including a fully upgraded 209-foot center field screen with new LED technology, done in partnership with Amazon, making it the world’s largest Fire TV.

The project is part of the Mariners’ long-term commitment to T-Mobile Park. Under the ballpark’s 25-year lease, the team is responsible for at least $600 million in maintenance, operations, capital upgrades and fan improvements over the life of the agreement, including a minimum of $10 million annually for stadium maintenance and improvements. Noah Kahan played two shows there Aug. 30-31, with premium club and suite inventory being marketed specifically for concerts.

Elevated sports and entertainment facilities are also good for convention business.

After a big expansion, the $2 billion Summit opened in 2023 and continues to have an enormous impact on Seattle’s convention business. Summit nearly doubled the Seattle Convention Center’s overall capacity, adding roughly 575,000 square feet, including 62 meeting rooms, a 58,650-square-foot ballroom, 248,590 square feet of exhibit space and a 14,000-square-foot outdoor terrace.
