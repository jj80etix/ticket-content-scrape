# StubHub Raises Sales Forecast, Announces CFO Change Amid Mixed Q3 Results

## Summary

StubHub posted another quarter of growing sales and profitability, with adjusted EBITDA up 84% year-over-year even as a $410M negative free-cash-flow swing (largely World Cup payment-timing effects) complicated the picture. The company raised its full-year GMS forecast and disclosed that CFO Connie James will step down in January 2027, to be replaced by Tripadvisor CFO Michael Noonan.

### Key Points
- Q3 GMS $2.62B (up from $2.43B); revenue $502M; adjusted EBITDA $124.1M (25% margin, up from 14%)
- Sales/marketing spend fell ~19% YoY while revenue grew 7%, signaling improved acquisition efficiency
- Negative free cash flow of $410.1M in Q3 vs. -$4.6M a year earlier, tied to World Cup-related payment timing
- Net leverage improved to 2.5x trailing EBITDA; term loans extended to 2033 at lower rates
- $19M in litigation reserves this quarter (vs. $7M a year earlier), unspecified matters
- CFO Connie James departs January 2027; Michael Noonan (ex-Tripadvisor, Noom, Booking Holdings) succeeds her

### Highlights
- GMS raised to $10.175B–$10.375B for FY2026, EBITDA guidance held at $400–420M
- Marketing efficiency gain (54%→45% of revenue) suggests resale marketplaces can grow profitably with less customer-acquisition spend
- Rising litigation reserves ($19M) signal continued legal/regulatory cost exposure for resale marketplaces

### Industry Problem
Secondary marketplaces face a structural tension between funding growth through heavy marketing spend and demonstrating durable profitability to public-market investors, compounded by event-driven cash-flow volatility (e.g., World Cup fulfillment costs) and ongoing litigation exposure.

### Proposed Solution
Nothing prescriptive is offered in the article itself; for Etix, the figures are a competitive benchmark on resale-market economics (marketing efficiency, litigation reserve trends) worth monitoring rather than an immediate build/improve trigger.

Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 25%, compared with 14% in the same period last year, reflecting substantial improvement in operating efficiency even as overall marketplace growth moderated from the World Cup-fueled second quarter.

StubHub also increased its full-year 2026 GMS forecast to between $10.175 billion and $10.375 billion, up from its previous projection of $10.1 billion to $10.3 billion. The company maintained its adjusted EBITDA outlook of $400 million to $420 million.

## Marketing Efficiency Drives Earnings Improvement

One of the most significant developments in StubHub’s quarterly results was its reduction in sales and marketing expenses.

The company spent $228.2 million on sales and marketing during the third quarter, down from $281.1 million a year earlier, a decline of nearly 19% despite revenue increasing 7%.

Excluding stock-based compensation, adjusted sales and marketing expenses declined to $225.7 million from $254.7 million. As a percentage of revenue, that represents a reduction from approximately 54% to 45%, an improvement of more than nine percentage points.

The results suggest StubHub is generating additional business with proportionately less marketing expenditure, an important development for a marketplace that has historically committed substantial resources to customer acquisition.

Revenue represented approximately 19% of gross merchandise sales during the quarter, broadly consistent with the prior-year period.

The growth rate also reflects a return to more typical marketplace activity following an unusually strong second quarter.

StubHub generated $3.1 billion in GMS during Q2, a 34% year-over-year increase driven largely by demand surrounding the FIFA World Cup. Revenue rose 33% during that period, while adjusted EBITDA increased 94%.

However, as [TicketNews reported following the company’s second-quarter earnings call](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/08/stubhubs-stock-slides-after-earnings-call-as-media-focus-on-fulfillment-failures-continues/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the same tournament also exposed fulfillment problems that led to increased customer support spending and contributed to pressure on margins.

StubHub’s operations and support expenses fell to $15.1 million in Q3 from $18.8 million in the second quarter, suggesting some easing of the elevated support expenditures associated with the World Cup period. The quarterly results do not establish how much of that reduction was directly attributable to lower fulfillment-related costs.

## Free Cash Flow Falls $410 Million in Third Quarter

Despite its improving adjusted profitability, StubHub recorded negative free cash flow of $410.1 million during the quarter, compared with negative $4.6 million in the same period last year.

The company used $398.8 million in cash for operating activities during Q3, a sharp reversal from the $321.9 million generated in the second quarter.

The figures reflect the importance of payment timing in StubHub’s marketplace business, where customers may purchase tickets months before events take place and sellers receive their proceeds after the relevant transactions are completed.

The company’s cash-flow reconciliation shows a $446.6 million negative change in payments due to buyers and sellers during Q3, compared with a positive $198.8 million change in the second quarter.

StubHub notes in its financial disclosures that the timing of major events, including the 2026 World Cup, and the collection and distribution of payments can create substantial differences in quarterly free cash flow.

The negative quarterly figure therefore does not necessarily indicate deterioration in the company’s underlying marketplace economics. Nevertheless, the size of the cash outflow makes the timing of future collections and seller payments an important issue for investors.

On a longer-term basis, StubHub reported $190.1 million in free cash flow for the first nine months of 2026, compared with $156.2 million during the same period last year. Trailing 12-month free cash flow totaled $192.1 million.

StubHub ended September with approximately $1.17 billion in cash and cash equivalents, down from $1.69 billion at the end of June. Approximately $751 million was owed to ticket sellers, leaving roughly $417 million in cash after accounting for those seller obligations.

The company continued to improve its debt position, reporting net leverage of 2.5 times trailing 12-month adjusted EBITDA, compared with 3.0 times at the end of June and 4.5 times at the end of 2025.

In October, StubHub also [extended the maturities of its senior term loans](https://investors.stubhub.com/news/news-details/2026/StubHub-Announces-Extension-of-Senior-Credit-Facilities/default.aspx?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) to 2033, while reducing borrowing costs by 25 basis points.

## Net Income Improves, but Comparisons Require Context

StubHub reported GAAP net income of $33.6 million for Q3, compared with a net loss of $1.29 billion in the year-earlier period.

Much of that dramatic improvement reflects the absence of an extraordinary accounting expense associated with the company’s September 2025 initial public offering.

StubHub recorded approximately $1.41 billion in stock-based compensation expenses during Q3 2025, compared with $42.5 million during the latest quarter. The unusually large prior-year expense significantly distorted the year-over-year net income comparison.

Net income attributable to common stockholders totaled $18.6 million in Q3 2026, equivalent to diluted earnings of 5 cents per share.

Adjusted EBITDA offers another measure of the company’s operating performance, but it excludes several expense categories, including stock-based compensation, litigation reserves and certain tax-related costs.

The latest reconciliation includes $19 million in litigation reserves during the quarter, compared with $7 million a year earlier, along with $11.5 million in indirect tax contingency costs.

Those figures are relevant given the legal and regulatory scrutiny facing ticket resale marketplaces, although the earnings release does not identify the specific litigation matters responsible for the quarterly reserves.

Wall Street expectations also provide context for the results. Before the announcement, [GuruFocus reported](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9184789/stubhub-holdings-inc-stub-q3-2026-earnings-report-preview-what-to-look-for?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) a consensus revenue estimate of approximately $513.9 million, above the $502 million StubHub ultimately reported.

The company has faced substantial investor skepticism since its September 2025 IPO, when shares were [priced at $23.50](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/09/stubhub-prices-ipo-at-23-50-valuing-ticketing-giant-at-8-6-billion/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). Its stock has since lost much of its value, leaving the company under pressure to demonstrate sustainable earnings growth and improved cash generation.

## CFO Connie James to Depart in January

Alongside its earnings announcement, StubHub disclosed that CFO Connie James will step down in January 2027 to pursue other opportunities.

Michael Noonan, currently CFO of Tripadvisor, will join StubHub as her successor effective Jan. 4, 2027. James will remain with the company in a consulting capacity during the first six months of 2027 to assist with the transition.

Noonan previously served as CFO of digital health company Noom and held senior finance roles at Booking Holdings. His background includes financial leadership positions at companies operating consumer-facing digital marketplaces.

“Mike’s deep consumer background and experience in finance leadership and capital markets roles make him a fantastic fit to serve as StubHub’s next CFO,” Baker said in the separate leadership announcement.

James said she believes the timing is appropriate for a leadership transition following several years overseeing StubHub’s finance organization, including its move into the public markets.

The management change comes as StubHub continues working to improve investor confidence following a volatile first year as a publicly traded company.

Management was scheduled to discuss the third-quarter results during a [conference call at 5 p.m. Eastern on Tuesday](https://investors.stubhub.com/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

Among the questions heading into the call are the sustainability of the company’s improved marketing efficiency, the extent to which World Cup-related payment timing explains the large quarterly cash outflow, and why StubHub raised its full-year marketplace sales forecast without increasing its adjusted EBITDA guidance.
