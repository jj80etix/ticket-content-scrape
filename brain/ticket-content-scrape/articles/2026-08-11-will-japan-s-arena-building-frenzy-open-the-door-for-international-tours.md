# Will Japan’s arena building frenzy open the door for international tours?

## Summary

Japan is in the midst of an arena-building boom, with a new Kubota-led group (Kubota Corporation, Mitsui Fudosan, Kansai Electric Power Real Estate Development) proposing a roughly 12,500-capacity "Kubota LaLa Arena" in Osaka's Namba district, targeted to open around 2032. It joins a wave of new venues — including AEG's own 18,000-cap Osaka Arena (breaking ground 2027, opening 2030), Kyoto's forthcoming 10,000-cap arena, and seven arenas already built in greater Tokyo since 2020 — all aimed at capturing rising domestic demand and drawing more international touring acts to a market historically dominated by domestic stars. Industry voices note the expanding circuit is already boosting international arena bookings, even as a weak yen and rising production costs pressure the economics of touring Japan.

### Key Points

- Kubota Corporation, Mitsui Fudosan, and Kansai Electric Power Real Estate Development have agreed to consider a ~12,500-cap arena ("Kubota LaLa Arena") in Osaka, targeting a 2032 opening.
- It will compete with AEG's separately greenlit 18,000-cap Osaka Arena (with Mitsubishi Corp. Urban Development and Kanden Realty), breaking ground 2027 and opening 2030, expected to host 1.6 million visitors and 145+ events annually.
- Since 2020, seven new arenas have opened in greater Tokyo, led by the 22,000-cap K-Arena Yokohama (2023) and including the 36,000-cap GMO Arena Saitama, the world's second-largest indoor arena.
- AEG's 17,000-cap IG Arena in Nagoya is targeting a 40% event-count boost and 50% visitor increase in its second year; a new 10,000-cap Kyoto arena is due in 2030.
- ACPC's Junichi Tayama says the new venue construction has "led to a significant increase in arena concerts by international artists," while a weak yen and rising labour/production costs are pushing up the cost of touring Japan.
- Japan is the world's second-largest recorded-music market but has historically been dominated by domestic acts; Bruno Mars's six-city 2027 "Romantic Tour" leg is cited as one of the most extensive international interior tours yet.

### Highlights

- Multiple concurrent, large-capacity arena developments (12,500- to 36,000-cap range) across Osaka, Tokyo, Nagoya, and Kyoto signal a structural, multi-year expansion of Japan's touring infrastructure.
- Direct industry testimony (ACPC) links new venue supply to a measurable rise in international artist bookings.
- Countervailing cost pressure (weak yen, rising production/labour costs) is flagged as a headwind that could offset some of the opportunity.

### Industry Problem

Japan's live-entertainment market has been capacity-constrained and domestic-heavy, limiting the ability of international tours — and by extension international ticketing platforms — to scale presence there; new arena supply is now removing that structural bottleneck.

### Proposed Solution

The item describes venue and market infrastructure growth rather than a specific product; for Etix this is a market-opportunity signal to monitor — track whether Japan's expanding arena circuit and rising international-tour volume creates ticketing-partner openings (via promoters, venues, or partnerships like AEG-Creativeman) worth evaluating as the new venues near their 2028-2032 opening window.

## Will Japan’s arena building frenzy open the door for international tours?

Plans for a new multi-use venue in Osaka highlight the rapidly expanding circuit in the domestic-heavy music market

![Osaka, Japan](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Osaka-Japan-©-Creative-CommonsPedro-Szekely.png)

Osaka, Japan © Creative Commons/Pedro Szekely

Japan is currently undergoing a flurry of new arena builds, with plans for another multi-use venue emerging in Osaka, as developers aim to capitalise on a booming entertainment market and maximise viability for international tours.

Last week, a trio of Japanese organisations – equipment manufacturer Kubota Corporation, real estate developer Mitsui Fudosan, and Kansai Electric Power Real Estate Development – announced an agreement to consider redeveloping Kubota’s former headquarters into a mixed-use district, anchored by a multi-purpose arena.

Set in the entertainment-heavy Namba ward of Osaka, Japan’s second-largest metropolitan area, the developers’ plans hinge on an arena to be utilised for music events and sports, given a provisional name of Kubota LaLa Arena and an aim of opening around 2032.

“The Osaka-Namba area, where this project is located, boasts excellent transportation access and has long been a hub for diverse entertainment, attracting many visitors from both within Japan and internationally,” says [Kubota](https://www.kubota.co.jp/news/2026/20260805-001240.html) of the development.

“Amidst a shortage of arenas capable of hosting sporting events and music concerts in the Kansai region, the creation of a multi-purpose arena with a capacity of approximately 12,500 people in the vibrant Osaka-Namba area will create a space where people can gather, become enthusiastic, and interact, generating unforgettable experiences and a new level of vibrancy,” the company says.

It is intended to be set in a wider district, to be known as Kubota Field, which will include a hotel and commercial facilities to create an entertainment hub that capitalises on a profitable domestic market and an influx of international fans.

> “We will expand \[…\] the further revitalisation of the entire sports and entertainment industry”

The Osaka property will accent Mitsui Fudosan’s venue portfolio, which also includes the 55,000-capacity [Tokyo Dome](https://www.iqmagazine.com/featured-list/stadium-stars-2025/tokyo-dome/) and 11,000-cap LaLa Arena Tokyo-Bay, with forthcoming projects including the 10,000-cap Nagoya Arena (expected [2028](https://www.estie.jp/portal/en/article/2Y1hJVP66v05uXtbjimOWA)) and the 20,000-cap New Prince Chichibu Memorial Rugby Stadium (scheduled 2030).

“We will expand the possibilities for attracting and managing events, thereby contributing to the further revitalisation of the entire sports and entertainment industry,” says Kubota.

Currently, Osaka is home to only one other arena at this capacity level, the 16,000-seat Osaka-jō Hall, which has hosted acts including Taylor Swift, TWICE, Red Hot Chilli Peppers, Ayumi Hamasaki, and Oasis in its over 40-year history.

But the Kubota LaLa Arena’s emergence could rival another major development, the 18,000-seat Osaka Arena and surrounding entertainment district, a joint venture from AEG (Anschutz Entertainment Group), Mitsubishi Corporation Urban Development (MCUD) and Kanden Realty and Development that was [greenlit in 2021](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2021/05/aeg-build-18000-cap-arena-osaka/).

The arena, located in the northern part of the metropolitan area, is slated to break ground next year and open in 2030. When it opens, AEG expects to welcome around 1.6 million visitors and host more than 145 events annually. Located in the Expo ’70 Commemorative Park, the arena will be the “ [crown jewel](https://aegworldwide.com/divisions/facilities/osaka-arena) ” of the wider entertainment district, which will include hotels, commercial and retail space, and a residential neighbourhood.

All the developments are evidence of Japan’s recent building frenzy, poised to help meet the demand for both domestic and international tours. Since 2020, seven new arenas have been built in Tokyo and its surrounding areas, according to the [*Global Arena Guide*](https://www.iqmagazine.com/publications/global-arena-guide-2026/japan/).

> “New arena venues has led to a significant increase in arena concerts by international artists”

Leading the pack is the 22,000-capacity [K-Arena Yokohama](https://www.iqmagazine.com/featured-list/arena-stars-2026/k-arena-yokohama/), one of the world’s premier purpose-built spaces that has welcomed everyone from Diana Ross, Post Malone, and Iron Maiden since opening in 2023.

Tokyo is also home to the second-largest indoor arena in the world, the 36,000-cap GMO Arena Saitama, along with a host of additional spaces: Tokyo A-Arena (10,000), Toyota Arena Tokyo (10,000), Ariake Arena (15,000), PIA Arena MM (12,100), Tokyo Garden Theater (8,000), Yokohama BUNTAI (5,000), and LaLa Arena Tokyo-Bay (14,000). Plus, a new venue is also expected in [Kawasaki](https://www.overlandpartners.com/project/kawasaki-arena-2/), and a [Sphere venue could also be on the way for Tokyo](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/04/is-a-sphere-on-the-way-for-tokyo/).

In addition to its Osaka venue, AEG is also behind the 17,000-cap [IG Arena](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/breaking-the-nagoya-skip-ig-arena-on-goals-to-shake-up-japanese-arena-routing/) in Nagoya, which recently celebrated its first year and is targeting a 40% boost in event count and a 50% increase in visitors in its second year.

Over in Kyoto, ITOUCHU Corporation is behind a new multi-purpose 10,000-cap arena that could finally put the city on international tour routings. It is scheduled to open in 2030. Other mainstays of the country’s arena touring circuit include Arena Kobe (10,000) and Anabuki Arena Kagawa (10,000).

> “Rising prices and labour costs continue to drive up production costs for concerts”

Japan is the world’s second-largest recorded music market, and is primarily led by domestic stars, but the new builds could help broaden its horizons. One of the leading international voices is Bruno Mars, who is set to hit six cities in the market on his *[Romantic Tour](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/06/bruno-mars-extends-romantic-tour-with-six-city-japan-leg/)* next year in one of the most extensive interior tours for an international act.

“The continued construction of new arena venues has led to a significant increase in arena concerts by international artists,” Junichi Tayama, executive director of All Japan Concert & Live Entertainment Promoters Conference (ACPC), previously told [*IQ*](https://www.iqmagazine.com/publications/global-arena-guide-2026/japan/).

“Japan is home to festivals such as Fuji Rock Festival and Summer Sonic, which are among the leading events in Asia, and international artists continue to have a strong presence here. On the other hand, the weak yen has led to a rise in performance fees for international artists, whilst rising prices and labour costs continue to drive up production costs for concerts,” adds Tayama.

Taken all together, the expanding circuit could improve flexibility and financial viability for international acts to visit the market and expand their routes in the Asia-Pacific region.
