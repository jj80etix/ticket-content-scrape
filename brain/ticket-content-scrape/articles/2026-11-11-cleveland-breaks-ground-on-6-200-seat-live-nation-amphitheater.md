# Cleveland Breaks Ground on 6,200-Seat Live Nation Amphitheater

## Summary

Bedrock, Rock Entertainment Group and Live Nation broke ground on a new 6,200-seat outdoor amphitheater along Cleveland's Cuyahoga River, targeting a summer 2028 opening. The venue expands Live Nation's local footprint and intensifies competition with AEG Presents, which operates the nearby Jacobs Pavilion.

### Key Points
- 6,200-seat Riverfront Amphitheater anchors a larger 35-acre waterfront redevelopment led by Bedrock
- Projected to generate ~$59M in annual economic impact and support hundreds of jobs
- Sits about a mile from AEG's ~5,000-cap Jacobs Pavilion, a venue Live Nation previously operated before AEG took over in 2022
- No inaugural performances, ticket prices or onsale dates announced yet

## Transcript

![The Riverfront Amphitheater - Photo courtesy of Bedrock](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Rendering-The-Riverfront-Amphitheater.webp)

The Riverfront Amphitheater - Photo courtesy of Bedrock

Bedrock, Rock Entertainment Group and Live Nation broke ground on a new 6,200-seat outdoor amphitheater along the Cuyahoga River in downtown Cleveland. The project, which was first announced in February, is now moving toward construction with an anticipated opening by summer 2028.

The Cleveland Riverfront Amphitheater will be operated by Live Nation and is a centerpiece of Bedrock’s larger effort to redevelop 35 acres of underutilized waterfront property into a mixed-use entertainment district.

Cleveland Mayor Justin Bibb, Cuyahoga County Executive Chris Ronayne and executives from all three companies attended the groundbreaking ceremony. According to the [official announcement](https://rockeg.com/2026/11/10/bedrock-rock-entertainment-group-and-live-nation-break-ground-on-cleveland-riverfront-amphitheater/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the venue will host national headliners, global touring acts and local performers.

The amphitheater will sit adjacent to the Cleveland Cavaliers’ Cleveland Clinic Global Peak Performance Center, which is scheduled to open in February 2027. DLR Group is leading the venue’s design in partnership with Blueprint Studio, Live Nation’s in-house design and development group.

Updated architectural plans feature a partially covered pavilion with tiered seating and a smaller general admission lawn. The layout is intended to accommodate major touring productions while offering views of the Cuyahoga River and downtown skyline.

Bedrock CEO Jared Fleisher described the development as an investment in Cleveland’s cultural identity during the groundbreaking ceremony. “An investment like this, a project like this, is not just a development,” Fleisher said, [according to Cleveland 19 News](https://www.cleveland19.com/2026/11/10/ground-broken-6200-person-cleveland-riverfront-amphitheater/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). “It is truly creating a civic asset, a civic jewel.”

When the project was [initially announced in February](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/02/live-nation-operate-new-amphitheatre-cleveland/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), Live Nation projected that the venue would generate approximately $59 million in annual economic impact. The company also expects the amphitheater to support hundreds of jobs and contribute millions in state and local tax revenue.

## New Competition in Cleveland’s Concert Market

Beyond the waterfront redevelopment, the new amphitheater represents a significant expansion of Live Nation’s Cleveland concert operations and could intensify competition with rival promoter AEG Presents.

Approximately a mile away, AEG operates Jacobs Pavilion, a roughly 5,000-capacity outdoor venue in Cleveland’s Flats entertainment district. Live Nation previously operated Jacobs Pavilion before AEG took over in 2022.

The new amphitheater would give Live Nation a similarly sized downtown venue capable of competing for many of the same touring artists and concert bookings.

As [Axios Cleveland reported](https://www.axios.com/local/cleveland/2026/11/03/mini-blossom-amphitheater-downtown-riverfront?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the development will create another point of competition between the two major concert promoters.

Live Nation already operates Blossom Music Center, a substantially larger outdoor amphitheater in nearby Cuyahoga Falls. The downtown project would provide another option for artists whose audience sizes fall between smaller clubs and the region’s largest concert venues.

For concertgoers, an additional amphitheater could mean more opportunities to see touring artists in downtown Cleveland. However, whether the new venue ultimately increases the total number of concerts in the market or shifts existing bookings between competing facilities remains to be seen.

The project is also part of a larger investment in Cleveland’s entertainment infrastructure, with Bedrock planning additional public spaces, residential development and commercial activity along the riverfront.

Rock Entertainment Group CEO Nic Barlage told Cleveland 19 News that the amphitheater is expected to host its first performance in May 2028, while the project’s official announcement maintains a broader summer 2028 opening target.

No inaugural performances, ticket prices or onsale dates have been announced.
