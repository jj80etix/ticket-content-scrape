# Tickets for Good helps to launch Music + Wellbeing initiative at Tramlines Fringe

## Summary

Tickets for Good, the University of Sheffield, and city partners launched a long-term "Music + Wellbeing" collaboration at the Fringe at Tramlines event during Tramlines Festival 2026 in Sheffield. The gathering brought together music, health, education, and civic leaders to explore live music's role in supporting community wellbeing, and will recur in 2027 as "Music + Wellbeing: In Conversation."

### Key Points
- Held at The Workstation, Sheffield, on Tramlines Festival 2026's opening day; Tramlines' 2026 lineup included Fatboy Slim, Courteeners, Blossoms, Kaiser Chiefs, and Rick Astley.
- Hosted by Tickets for Good (which provides free/discounted tickets to key workers), with Steve Rimmer (CEO) among the contributors.
- Speakers included University of Sheffield's Professor Renee Timmers, former Lord Mayor of Sheffield Safiya Saeed, and international contributors from Belgium and France.
- Session introduced the Harmonic Health Hub and an emerging "Sound Works" partnership linking creative industries, audio manufacturing, and sociological research.
- Follow-up event confirmed for 2027 under the "Music + Wellbeing: In Conversation" banner.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**Tickets for Good, the University of Sheffield and city partners have launched a new long-term collaboration centred on the role of live music in supporting wellbeing.**

Taking place on the opening day of Tramlines Festival 2026, the Fringe at Tramlines event was held at The Workstation in Sheffield. It welcomed leaders from across music, health, education and civic sectors, who helped to launch a wider ‘Music + Wellbeing’ initiative, positioning the English city as a hub for research and innovation in his field.

The Fringe at Tramlines takes place during the annual music festival, continuing the spirit of the original event which began as a free citywide music celebration in 2009. Since the main festival outgrew the city centre over a decade ago, bars, pubs and restaurants have continued to host their own free events.

This year’s Tramlines festival featured a line-up of Fatboy Slim, Courteeners, Blossoms, Kaiser Chiefs, Rick Astley, The Vaccines, Wolf Alice, Wet Leg and more.

**Music + Wellbeing**

Hosted by Ticks for Good, which offers free and discounted tickets for key workers, the Music + Wellbeing event featured a morning session chaired by Henk Schuit, a cultural strategist and long-time advocate for using live events to drive social impact. Contributors also included Professor Renee Timmers, University of Sheffield, Psychology of Music; Safiya Saeed, former Lord Mayor of Sheffield and founder of Reach Up Youth; and Rick Blaskey, music and media partnership specialist.

Wider input was provided by the University of Sheffield’s Dr Emma Risley (filmed contribution) and Stephanie Pitts, and Neil Mc Sweeney, who introduced the Harmonic Health Hub and the emerging Sound Works strategic partnership linking creative industries, audio manufacturing and sociological research.

Music and tech specialist Gareth Deakin was also a key contributor, outlining the importance of embedding wellbeing outcomes within cultural funding frameworks, highlighting the growing recognition of live music as a driver of social value.

International perspectives came from Gérôme Vanherf (ikii, Belgium), Tommy Vaudecrane (Technopol, France), and violinist Iskandar Widjaja, who closed the day with a live performance following discussions on the role of performance, access and cultural policy in shaping wellbeing outcomes.

Additional contributions came from Steve Rimmer, chief executive of Tickets for Good, and Liam O’Shea, community music manager at Rotherham Council and founder of Hope Works and No Bounds Festival, who joined Safiya Saeed in a second panel exploring how cities can translate cultural participation into measurable social impact.

The gathering will return in 2027 as ‘Music + Wellbeing: In Conversation’ as part of the long-term collaboration.
