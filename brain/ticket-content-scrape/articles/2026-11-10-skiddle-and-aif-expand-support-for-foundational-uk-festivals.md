# Skiddle and AIF expand support for ‘foundational’ UK festivals

## Summary

UK ticketing platform Skiddle is continuing its partnership with the Association of Independent Festivals (AIF), funding memberships and marketing packages for 20 small/micro UK festivals (capacity ≤2,000) to support their long-term viability — its second such round, bringing total festivals supported to 42.

### Key Points
- 20 small/micro festivals (≤2,000 capacity) receive AIF membership plus a marketing package via Skiddle
- Second round of the scheme; last year's round supported 22 festivals
- Announcement follows Skiddle's first-ever external investment, aimed at European expansion

## Skiddle and AIF expand support for ‘foundational’ UK festivals

Twenty small or micro indie festivals will receive association memberships and bespoke marketing packages through the partnership

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/07/Ilosaarirock_2025_crowd_water_rock_photocred_ArttuKokkonen-1-scaled.jpg.webp)

UK ticketing platform Skiddle is continuing its partnership with the Association of Independent Festivals (AIF), announcing that it will fund memberships and bespoke marketing packages for 20 small or micro independent festivals to support their “long-term future”.

It is the second round for Skiddle and AIF’s alliance, with last year’s scheme focused on a broader selection of festivals ranging in capacity. This year, the selected indie festivals have capacities of 2,000 people or fewer.

Selected festivals include Torquay’s Burn It Down Festival, Perthshire’s Wildhood Festival, Cardiff Psych and Noise Festival, Birmingham’s Supersonic Festival, Tenby’s Westival, and Portsmouth’s Country on the Coast.

Last year’s efforts, which included the same membership and £5,000 promotional funding, supported 22 festivals, bringing the partnership’s affected events figure to 42 total.

“The UK is home to hundreds of exceptional boutique festival experiences, each offering something unique to a broad range of audiences, and we’re proud to work with so many of these events and their teams,” says Duncan King, Skiddle’s head of partnerships. “Over recent years, the sector has suffered, and we’ve lost many great festivals. Now, the tide is turning, and we’re seeing organisers hosting successful experiences and returning for future instalments.”

He adds that the efforts support the events’ “long-term future” by helping organisers access education, networking, and promotional backing.

As part of Skiddle’s support package, selected festivals will be featured in Skiddle’s Festival Guide, along with editorial support, paid campaigns from the platform’s dedicated team, and account management.

“Micro and very small festivals are the foundation of the festival industry,” says John Rostron, AIF CEO. “It’s where creativity lives because the teams behind them are often doing everything with minimal resources. By Skiddle providing these 20 festivals with AIF membership, it means they won’t have to go it alone. They are joining a supportive community of organisers who share what works and what doesn’t.”

The news lands a week after the independent ticketing firm announced its [first external investment](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/11/skiddle-sets-sights-on-new-european-markets-with-first-external-investment/) in its 25-year history, which it says will fund expansion across Europe and a deepening of its lifestyle events vertical.
