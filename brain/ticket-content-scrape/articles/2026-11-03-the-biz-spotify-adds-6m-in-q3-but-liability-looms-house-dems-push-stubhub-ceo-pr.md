# The Biz: Spotify Adds 6M In Q3 But Liability Looms; House Dems Push StubHub CEO Probe; Is K-pop Outfit JYP For Sale?

## Summary

Pollstar's "The Biz" column covers three stories: Spotify added 6M subscribers in Q3 but flagged up to €538M-€619M in potential liability from a royalty-bundling lawsuit; House Oversight Democrats escalated scrutiny of StubHub CEO Eric Baker over his ties to a bulk ticket-resale firm; and a Japanese investment group floated a bid for 25-50% of K-pop label JYP Entertainment. The StubHub item is the most consequential for ticketing: it raises conflict-of-interest questions about resale-marketplace governance.

### Key Points
- Rep. Robert Garcia sent StubHub CEO Eric Baker a letter addressing him as a director of Andro Capital Management, which allegedly buys tickets in bulk and resells them on StubHub.
- Garcia's letter raises concern that Andro Capital or affiliates could receive "favorable placement or other advantages" on StubHub at the expense of smaller buyers/sellers.
- StubHub has denied any impropriety; the probe follows an earlier Oversight Committee letter addressed to Baker in his StubHub capacity.
- Separately, Spotify's Q3 profit rose 15% to $773M even as it disclosed major exposure from a mechanical-royalty bundling suit; a Japanese group signaled interest in buying a stake in JYP Entertainment.

### Highlights
- Congressional letter specifically targets CEO Eric Baker's dual role at StubHub and at Andro Capital Management, an alleged bulk-ticket reseller active on StubHub's own marketplace.
- Allegation centers on potential self-dealing: favorable placement/advantage for an executive-linked reseller over ordinary buyers and sellers.
- StubHub has publicly refuted the allegations; no formal finding or enforcement action has been announced yet.

### Industry Problem
Secondary-market platforms face recurring credibility and governance questions when there are undisclosed or insufficiently firewalled financial ties between platform leadership and large-scale resellers operating on that same platform, feeding broader concerns about fairness and transparency in ticket resale.

### Proposed Solution
No remedy is proposed in the reporting itself; the appropriate posture is to monitor for escalation (a formal subpoena, FTC/DOJ referral, or new disclosure/conflict-of-interest rules for resale marketplaces) as the trigger that would raise this from background monitoring to something with direct competitive or regulatory implications for primary platforms.

Spotify added 6 million subscribers in the third quarter, 1 million more than expected, pushing profits to $773 million for the quarter, up 15% against last year.

Spotify said growth “was driven by Q/Q gains in Latin America, Europe, North America and Rest of World.”

Meanwhile, the company did issue guidance that if it loses a lawsuit filed by the Mechanical License Collective, it could be on the hook for some $619 million.

At issue is whether Spotify Premium qualifies as a “bundle,” which allows it to pay a lower royalty rate. The court has already ruled that the tier meets the criteria for bundling, but that decision is under appeal.

“If the MLC were to ultimately be entirely successful in its claim alleging that Spotify’s Premium Service is not a bundle, then the liability in relation to the period March 1, 2024 to September 30, 2026 would be approximately €538 million, plus potential penalties and interest, which we cannot reasonably estimate,” Spotify said in a regulatory filing.

**House Dems Ramp Up StubHub CEO Probe**

Rep. Robert Garcia, ranking member of the House Oversight Committee, sent a letter to StubHub CEO Eric Baker this week, seeking answers on his connection to Andro Capital Management.

Garcia addressed the letter to Baker in his capacity as an Andro director in contrast to an earlier letter in which he was addressed as StubHub’s chief.

Andro Capital allegedly buys event tickets in bulk and then sells them on StubHub.

“The financial ties between Andro Capital, its affiliate Colloquy Capital, and StubHub – and your personal connection to all three of these companies – raise concerns that Andro Capital or its affiliates could be receiving favorable placement or other advantages in the resale market at the expense of buyers and sellers of smaller numbers of tickets,” Garcia wrote.

StubHub has refuted any allegations of impropriety.

**Is JYP For Sale**

A Japanese investment group said it intends to launch a bid to buy between 25 and 50 percent of the shares in K-pop outfit JYP Entertainment, the company behind Stray Kids and TWICE.

The NEXROC Group and SVCV, Inc. floated a price at a 30% premium in a press release sent to Korean media.

For its part, JYP said it had not received any tender offers, though the investment group said its tender had not commenced, but that it plans to issue a letter of intent kickstarting a month of negotiations.

JYP shares rose on the news. The company now has a market cap of more than $1 billion.
