# Rock & Roll Hall Of Fame Induction Ceremony Delivers Magic And Unchecked Passion While Expanding The Definition Of Rock

## Summary

A reviewer's recap of the 2026 Rock & Roll Hall of Fame Induction Ceremony highlights standout moments — Phil Collins' emotional tribute from Travis Barker, performances honoring Wu-Tang Clan, Iron Maiden, MC Lyte, Queen Latifah and Rick Rubin — among 18 new inductees. The piece is a critical/editorial recap of the live event itself, with no relevance to ticketing industry structure, regulation or technology. Next year's ceremony will be held in Cleveland.

### Key Points
- 18 members inducted at the 2026 ceremony, including Wu-Tang Clan, Iron Maiden, MC Lyte, Queen Latifah and Rick Rubin.
- Travis Barker's tribute to Phil Collins and Cynthia Erivo's "In the Air Tonight" performance were called out as highlights.
- 2027 ceremony will take place in Cleveland, Ohio.

## Transcript

![GettyImages 2305260537](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/GettyImages-2305260537-scaled.jpg)

Inductee Phil Collins speaks onstage during the 2026 Rock & Roll Hall of Fame Induction Ceremony at Peacock Theater on Nov. 14, 2026, in Los Angeles, California. (Photo by Jeff Kravitz/FilmMagic)

One can always count on the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame to deliver the unexpected, and occasionally have the obvious unravel before your eyes. For the 2026 Induction – with 18 new members being inducted – no one would’ve bet on Phil Collins’ moment to be the emotional high watermark, as well as one of the evening’s most staggering performances.

Yet when Travis Barker, blink-182’s whippet-thin drummer, went to the podium, shaking and reading from his own notes, it was clear how much the moment meant to him. Halfway through his wide-open praise for Collins’ impact on his life, the solo artist and Genesis member climbed the stairs with two canes and stood facing the clearly moved speaker as tears formed in his own eyes.

For every jaundiced musico who’d decried the ubiquitous Adult Contemporary stalwart’s inclusion, the raw emotions struck people dumb, inspiring a standing ovation. For Collins, already an inductee via Genesis, the honor sparked candor as he put one cane, then the other, up on the dais and offered, “It’s really not as bad as it looks.”

[The Coronation Of A Punk Prince: Billy Idol’s Rock Hall Rebel Yell](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/11/13/the-coronation-of-a-punk-prince-billy-idols-rock-hall-rebel-yell-cover/)

Even that couldn’t prepare the room for Cynthia Erivo’s taut, sweltering take on “In The Air Tonight.” Fraught with knowing, desire, and a tension that pulled you in, when the signature riff – what Barker deemed “arguably the most badass drum fill of all time” – came, the tattooed musician delivered the herculean riff with an unbridled glee. To see genres cross, generations quake for their heroes is to understand why the Rock Hall matters.

And there were many moments over the four-and-a-half-hour show that delivered that unchecked passion. Seeing both Remy Ma and Megan Thee Stallion’s reverence and awe as they inducted MC Lyte and Queen Latifah, respectively, shows how strong roots inspire and charge the future of both women in music and women looking for voices that speak their truth. That check was paid in full during a spirited medley later in the show that saw the younger women joining in for a multigenerational “Ladies First.”

Chris Rock brought that same full-tilt energy down to the induction of Wu-Tang Clan, declaring, “Wu-Tang is bigger than hip-hop. They didn’t have a big machine behind them, but they created something the world had never seen: the slang, the attitude, the hoodies… They didn’t leave Staten Island; they brought it with them.”

With the entire group together onstage, they were the Nation of Wu-Tang, and each member’s singular form showed the vastness of rhymes in a genre where nuance is sometimes lost. Each to their own, together, they churned an arena of industry people into a fomenting cloud of throw-it-down believers.

Jack Black, too, brought that over-the-top wit to Iron Maiden’s induction, a role he was born for. With the band not in attendance – which, like with Oasis, felt on brand – an all-star band of Metallica’s Kirk Hammett and Robert Trujillo, Mötley Crüe’s John 5 and Rage Against the Machine’s Brad Wilk with St. Vincent on vocals suggested Maiden’s thrust and pummel was more foundational than most people might realize.

It wasn’t just “the kids.” Neil Young’s extended riff and history about “the Man” set Rick Rubin up for both a retrospective look across eras, genres and legends — and the world’s shortest acceptance speech. Looking quietly moved, the bearded producer and label maven took to the stage to announce succinctly, “You can’t kill rock and roll.”

![GettyImages 2305174096](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/GettyImages-2305174096-scaled.jpg)

Inductee Queen Latifah speaks onstage during the 2026 Rock & Roll Hall of Fame Induction Ceremony at Peacock Theater on Nov. 14, 2026, in Los Angeles, California. (Photo by Kevin Winter/WireImage)

The mic drop of his thought seemed like the moment of the night. Until all four Red Hot Chili Peppers took the stage in full Rick Rubin persona, sporting his clean scalp, grey hair, and flowing beard. Launching into a robust “Give It Away,” they honored their producer and former labelhead with what might have been the night’s punkest and most loving tribute.

When it works, there’s nothing like the Rock Hall induction for those moments that make you scream, cheer or laugh. It’s the magic that makes the length of the event worth it. But when it doesn’t – Gwen Stefani’s cloying, cutesy speech for Billy Idol and Steve Stevens yielded a fairly castrated performance for the pair. Instead of taking “Rebel Yell” and throwing it into overdrive with “White Wedding” or “Dancing with Myself,” something that packed velocity and rock spirit, the performance geared down for Stefani to duet on pretty sodden “Eyes Without a Face.”

Yes, TVQ matters. But never at the expense of the fire and the bravado of the inductees. Collins, which many thought would be the cringe moment, stole the night with the raw excitement and an unexpected, but brilliant performance, while one of MTV’s initial breakout supernovas was reduced to making space for a more current act.

Even U2, clearly there to flog a new release, kicked off the night with an explosive “Silencio” that featured riot police moving in drill-team formation. As much commentary on the state of the world, Bono’s intentions were ratified with a yearning “Pride (In The Name Of Love)” that matched the force of the message with the impact only properly deployed rock & roll manifests.

Personally, Gram Parsons’ impact has never been properly measured. As an artist capable of drawing Emmylou Harris, the Black Crowes, Elvis Costello, Jesse Wells, Grace Potter, Margo Price, Sheryl Crow and many more to the Troubadour on Wednesday, one can only wonder if even Keith Richards might have shown for the induction.

With more diversity – Fela Kuti! Celia Cruz! Sade! Luther Vandross! – it’s a broader spread. Not just the narrow definition of rock, the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame Induction seems a place for groundbreaking and impact beyond one’s bottom line. How it evolves will be worth watching, and we can get a state of the evolution next year in Cleveland, Ohio.
