# Ariana Grande’s London residency drives surge in music tourism

## Summary

IQ Magazine reports that Ariana Grande's residency at London's O2 (the "Eternal Sunshine" dates) is driving a surge in international music tourism, with over a third of ticket buyers coming from outside the UK and expected to spend heavily on travel and hospitality. General demand/tourism trend coverage without a distinct ticketing-platform or market-structure angle beyond what's already been covered.

### Key Points

## Transcript

## Ariana Grande’s London residency drives surge in music tourism

Over a third of ticket buyers for the Eternal Sunshine dates at The O2 will have come from outside the UK and are expected to spend millions

![Ariana Grande at The O2](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Ariana-Grande-at-The-O2-©-Julian-Dakdouk.png)

Ariana Grande at The O2 © Julian Dakdouk

Over a third of ticket buyers for Ariana Grande’s 10-night residency at London’s The O2 – her only dates outside North America on the *Eternal Sunshine* outing – are from overseas, demonstrating the growing power of music tourism.

Running from 15 August to 1 September, the exclusive set of dates will draw roughly 200,000 fans to the UK capital for the final portion of the tour, Grande’s first [widespread outing](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/08/ariana-grande-returns-with-the-eternal-sunshine-tour/) in six years.

Thirty-five percent of them will have come from outside of the UK, adding to a growing trend at one of the world’s busiest arenas, with overseas purchasers accounting for nearly 10% of ticket sales in the AEG venue’s first six months this year, up from 8.4% in 2025.

“The rise in gig tourism is underpinned by the power of fandom and community,” says Steve Sayer, SVP and GM of The O2. “Fans do not want to miss out on a chance to connect with their favourite artists, and they are prepared to travel across the country – or between countries – to enjoy exclusive shows, residencies, and major festival experiences.

“When fans put in the miles, they want to get the most out of their experience, and these hotly anticipated events often form the centre point of a wider trip – whether it lasts two nights, or two weeks,” he says.

As residency-style tours [reshape the live landscape](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/02/global-stadium-boom-accelerates-shift-to-residency-style-tours/), millions of fans are looking to travel to take part in localised tours, with other notable examples including Harry Styles’ *Together, Together* and Beyoncé’s *Cowboy Carter World Tour*.

> “Live music may be the draw, but the entire London economy can benefit”

Looking on a domestic level, more than 47% of UK purchasers for Grande’s shows are travelling in from outside of London, underscoring how residencies reshape the local landscape as well. The figure is on par with other non-London attendance at other O2 residencies, such as Usher’s ten-show *Past, Present, Future* coup (55%) and Lady Gaga’s four-night *Mayhem Ball* stand (51%).

For domestic and international fans alike, live music events spur spending across travel, accommodation, hospitality, and retail, demonstrating the power of live to local economies. Last year, The O2 led to more than £12.5 million (€14.6m) in spending across London.

Financial services firm [Novuna](https://www.novuna.co.uk/news-and-insights/personal-finance/ariana-grande-fans-set-to-pour-up-to-78m-into-london/) estimates that Grande’s residency will generate up to £78m (€91m) in visitor spending for the UK capital, with the average fan expected to spend over £325 (€379) outside of tickets and merchandise.

Sayer adds: “As artists continue to choose London as the exclusive stage for their UK and European tours, more fans will be enticed by everything London has to offer. Live music may be the draw, but the entire London economy can benefit from the excitement and commitment of fans.”
