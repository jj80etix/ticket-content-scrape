# Live Nation Revenue Hits $7.7B as DOJ Antitrust Fight Looms

## Summary

Live Nation reported Q2 2026 revenue of $7.7 billion (up 9%) with Ticketmaster revenue up 15% to $852 million, but the results land against an unresolved federal antitrust fight. The DOJ reached a proposed settlement with Live Nation in June — trading a court-ordered breakup for ticketing restrictions, fee caps at certain amphitheaters, venue divestitures and extended federal oversight — but the deal still needs court approval, and a coalition of state attorneys general is pushing back as inadequate and seeking further discovery, including into a Rapino-Trump meeting that preceded the settlement.

### Key Points
- Q2 revenue $7.7bn (+9% YoY); Ticketmaster revenue $852m (+15%), AOI $331m (+14%), 90 million fee-bearing tickets sold.
- DOJ-Live Nation settlement (reached June 2026) drops the court-ordered breakup in favor of loosened ticketing exclusivity, more third-party distribution, service-fee caps at certain amphitheaters, and relinquished control/booking rights at select venues; still requires court approval.
- A group of state attorneys general is continuing to challenge Live Nation, arguing federal remedies fall short, and is seeking additional discovery into settlement negotiations.
- California AG Rob Bonta publicly called the federal deal a "slap on the wrist sweetheart deal," pointing to the states' courtroom win on the Ticketmaster monopoly claim.
- Live Nation ended Q2 with record $6.4bn in deferred revenue, signaling a heavy second-half schedule even as legal costs weigh on reported operating income.

### Highlights
- DOJ proposed settlement (June 2026): no forced breakup; instead fee caps at certain amphitheaters, loosened ticketing exclusivity, more third-party distribution, and venue divestitures — pending court approval.
- State AGs (led by California's Rob Bonta) are seeking more discovery, including scrutiny of a Rapino-Trump meeting that preceded the settlement, and call the deal insufficient given their own courtroom win on the Ticketmaster monopoly claim.
- Ticketmaster Q2 revenue $852m (+15%), AOI $331m (+14%), 90 million fee-bearing tickets sold (+8%) — the segment central to the antitrust case continues to grow even as litigation continues.

### Industry Problem
The case centers on whether Live Nation/Ticketmaster's integrated control of ticketing, promotion and venues suppresses competition; regulators, artists, venues and fans are questioning how much market power one company should hold, and the outcome (settlement terms vs. stronger state-level remedies) will shape how much room independent platforms have to compete.

### Proposed Solution
The item describes no vendor solution — it's litigation and earnings coverage. For Etix, the right posture is to monitor how the DOJ settlement's specific remedies (fee-cap thresholds, third-party distribution requirements, exclusivity rollbacks) are finalized and whether state AGs win additional concessions, since any forced opening of Live Nation's ticketing exclusivity or venue relationships could create real competitive openings for an independent primary platform.

Live Nation’s concert machine kept roaring in the second quarter, delivering record attendance, accelerating ticket sales and another sizable revenue haul — even as the company’s legal battle over its dominance of the live events business remains far from settled.

The Ticketmaster parent reported second-quarter revenue of $7.7 billion, up 9% year over year, while operating income rose 7% to $522 million. Adjusted operating income increased 2% to $817 million.

Nearly 49 million fans attended Live Nation events during the quarter, a 10% jump that the company attributed largely to international growth. More than 143 million concert tickets had been sold through mid-July, putting Live Nation 14 million tickets ahead of its pace at the same point last year.

“In a world of endless screens and AI-generated everything, the one thing that can’t be copied is being there,” Live Nation President and CEO Michael Rapino said. “More artists are on the road than ever — and fans keep choosing to be in the room with them.”

Ticketmaster once again provided much of the momentum. The ticketing division generated $852 million in revenue, up 15%, while adjusted operating income climbed 14% to $331 million. Ticketmaster sold 90 million fee-bearing tickets during the quarter, an 8% increase, with concerts accounting for nearly all of the volume growth.

International markets were particularly strong, producing 39 million Ticketmaster ticket sales — up 12% — and driving most of the quarter’s growth across the company’s ticketing and sponsorship operations. Live Nation also ended the quarter with a record $6.4 billion in event-related deferred revenue, signaling a packed stadium and amphitheater schedule during the second half of the year. The company now expects full-year attendance to rise 10%.

But the blockbuster numbers arrive under an unusually large legal cloud.

Live Nation and Ticketmaster remain at the center of the federal antitrust case accusing the companies of using their control over ticketing, concert promotion and venues to suppress competition. The Justice Department reached a proposed settlement with Live Nation in June, abandoning the possibility of a court-ordered breakup in favor of ticketing restrictions, fee caps, venue divestitures and extended federal oversight. The deal still requires court approval.

A group of state attorneys general is continuing to challenge Live Nation, arguing that the proposed federal remedies do not go far enough. The states have sought additional discovery into how the agreement was negotiated, including questions surrounding Rapino’s meeting with President Donald Trump before the settlement emerged.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta has been even more direct, [calling](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/california-ag-bonta-calls-dojs-live-nation-settlement-a-slap-on-the-wrist-sweetheart-deal/) the federal agreement a “slap on the wrist sweetheart deal” and pointing to the states’ courtroom victory on their Ticketmaster monopoly claim as evidence that stronger relief remains possible.

The Justice Department’s proposed settlement would require Live Nation to loosen ticketing exclusivity provisions, allow more third-party ticket distribution, cap service fees at certain amphitheaters and relinquish control or booking rights at selected venues. Live Nation would remain intact, however — the central point of contention for states and critics seeking structural changes.

Live Nation’s latest earnings make clear why the stakes remain so high. Ticket sales, attendance and venue investments are expanding rapidly, while Ticketmaster continues to strengthen its position internationally.

For investors, that is a growth story. For regulators, artists, venues and fans questioning how much control one company should hold over the concert business, it is also the heart of the case.
