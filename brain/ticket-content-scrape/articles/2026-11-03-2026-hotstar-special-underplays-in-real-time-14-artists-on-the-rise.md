# 2026 Hotstar Special: Underplays In Real Time—14 Artists On The Rise

## Summary

Pollstar's 2026 "Hotstar Special" profiles 14 rising touring artists, continuing a franchise dating to the mid-1980s that spotlights acts experiencing rapid, socially-driven growth. This year's list is 71% women across genres including pop, punk, Americana and Latin.

### Key Points
- Featured acts include Adéla, Die Spitz, Slayyyter, Tucker Wetmore and The Warning, among others.
- Franchise co-founder Gary Bongiovanni notes artist development now happens far faster than in the 1980s, aided by social media and global digital distribution.
- The piece highlights an "underplay" phenomenon of high-demand artists booking small venues mid-breakout (e.g., SLAYYYTER's Chicago show followed by a Madison Square Garden announcement).

## Transcript

![DieSpitzGettyImages 2289567653](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/DieSpitzGettyImages-2289567653.jpg)

Die Spitz at Outside Lands 2026 in San Francisco. Photo by Dana Jacob / FilmMagic

*“I don’t recall what year we started Hotstars, I’m guessing it was in the mid-’80s,” says Gary “Bonj” Bongiovanni,* Pollstar *’s eminent co-founder, when asked how* Pollstar *’s long-running editorial franchise began. “For us it was all about giving a young act a boost in the early days of their careers when they actually needed it. At first, we didn’t even interview the act but that changed after a few years. It was great because we got to pick who we wanted on the cover, and we were covering acts (and the young agents trying to book them) that appreciated the ink.”  
  
A lot has changed in 40 years. These days artist development happens at warp speed, hastened by social media, instant global digital distribution, and an increased social connectivity never before seen. And it can happen in the middle of a tour that is suddenly transformed into a run of underplays with demand far outstripping capacities.  
  
Skipping steps, once verboten, is becoming de rigueur with more platforms than ever to support that growth. Witness SLAYYYTER, who last summer played Chicago’s Salt Shed and at press time announced a Madison Square Garden headline gig; Karol G, Chappell Roan and Katseye co-signing Adéla; Die Spitz playing Olivia Rodrigo’s wondrous Daisy Chain Festival; and Sienna Spiro getting tipped for a Grammy nom.  
  
Worth noting: The future, and really the present, is women. A sisterhood dominates this year’s Hotstar Special with women over-indexing at 71% of the list. And this is happening in an array of genres including pop, punk, rock, Americana, Latin, club bangers and more. This list could easily have been all women, something a decade ago that would have been harder to accomplish.*
