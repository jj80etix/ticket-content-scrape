# Owning The Global Stage: Mexico, LATAM’s Leader, Builds On Its Success As FIFA World Cup Host

## Summary

A Pollstar feature examines Mexico's standing as Latin America's dominant live-music market, buoyed by a post-World Cup tourism boom, Live Nation's investment in promoter OCESA, and record streaming/concert revenue. Industry voices also flag market saturation and "papering" (discounted/free ticket distribution) as emerging risks in oversaturated cities.

### Key Points
- Latin America was the fastest-growing music market in 2025, up 17.1% in revenue per IFPI; Mexico is the region's #10 largest market
- OCESA (11.3M tickets) and Zignia Live (2.12M tickets) rank among Pollstar's top 2025 promoters; Live Nation has invested heavily in OCESA
- WME's Jacob Fox warns of market saturation and rising use of "papering" in Mexico City, which he says ultimately punishes fans who bought early
- Smaller venues (~8,000-cap, e.g. Pepsi Center WTC) are increasingly valued as alternatives to oversubscribed arenas/stadiums

In the ever-evolving business of live entertainment, the one gift that keeps on giving – and surprising – is Mexico.

Not only a leader in Latin America, but the Mexican market is also among the most lucrative in all facets of the music business worldwide, from live entertainment to streaming to sales. The International Federation of the Phonographic Industry’s most recent report said Latin America was the fastest-growing region in 2025, with a 17.1% revenue surge, marking the 16th consecutive year that the region has seen growth.

Mexico, in particular, is thriving and is the No. 10 largest record music market, with streaming platforms garnering new users by the day. Spotify dominates the country with more than 30 million users, whose music tastes have grown beyond the Latin umbrella and rock.

That consumption of music is just as strong in the live sector, with promoters such as OCESA and Zignia Live leading the way. Both companies cracked *Pollstar* ’s 2025 Year End Top 100 Promoters ticket sales chart, with OCESA moving 11.3 million tickets for fourth place and Zignia Live reporting 2.12 million tickets sold, which was good for the No. 16 spot.

Concerts generated nearly $4 billion in economic impact last year, according to financial publication *Mundo Ejecutivo*, and such influence is a major reason why Live Nation invested heavily in OCESA, a Latin giant that promotes the country’s biggest shows and operates chart-topping venues such as Estadio GNP Seguros, the king of all stadiums, and Palacio de los Deportes. Michael Rapino, president and CEO of Live Nation, said last year that it has “more than tripled the number of fans attending” concerts in Mexico since 2019, making it “the third largest music market in the world.”

**[La Nueva Hoya De Jalisco: Coliseo GNP Seguros Cements Guadalajara As No. 2 Mexican Market](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/11/04/la-nueva-joya-de-jalisco-coliseo-gnp-seguros-cements-guadalajara-as-no-2-market/)**

Wesley Cullen, managing director of Latin America for Oak View Group, *Pollstar* ’s parent company, has witnessed firsthand Mexico’s rise to prominence in the live business and believes that, while business has slowed for some companies, the LATAM leader will continue to develop steadily.

“The massive growth that occurred over the last couple of years isn’t sustainable. No one would expect that in their market, but it is still going to be incredible,” Cullen says. “Mexico City will continue to grow as a market, and I think we’ll see more developments there and throughout the country. Guadalajara is one example.”

Mexico City tops every venue chart, from clubs to stadiums, drawing artists from all across the globe. Two of the top grossing acts are domestic artists: rock bands Zoé and Caifanes. The top artists are non-Mexican Latin standouts like Bad Bunny, Chayanne and Shakira, along with K-pop stars BTS and English-language superstars The Weeknd and Dua Lipa.

Every one of them draws fans from all over the world, contributing to Mexico’s tourism boom. The World Travel & Tourism Council stated that Mexico was North America’s top performer in 2025, leading the region in visitor spending growth and visitor arrivals, and this year’s numbers may continue to grow after the country successfully hosted the FIFA World Cup in its top three markets: Mexico City, Guadalajara and Monterrey.

Soccer’s biggest event, however, didn’t boost the local live music scene. Armando Barona, director of events at Mexico City’s Pepsi Center WTC, said after a strong first quarter, business stalled as a result of the tournament with the various events, including fan fests, throughout the city drawing locals and visitors.

“Everyone took to the streets just to live the moment and focus on soccer,” Barona tells *Pollstar*. “We really struggled in the summer. Many shows had to be rescheduled to the second half of the year. It was a bit of a slump in July and August, but then the numbers shot right back up. From September onwards, things really took off.”

Pepsi Center is a venue that hits the sweet spot of up to 8,000 people, a number below arena capacity but above that of theaters. Cullen says those spaces in particular are the ones needed in various Mexican cities to help local live ecosystems grow.

Various markets, even tertiary ones such as Tijuana, Mérida and Queretaro, could benefit from such spaces as major artists and festivals tend to book soccer stadiums, racetracks and arenas. It’s why Barona’s venue is a mainstay on global charts. With little to no avails at the larger buildings, artists will sometimes book multiple nights at Pepsi Center WTC.

“That’s what’s happening, fortunately. Mexico City has a great influx of artists, but some choose us because they prefer this size, a more intimate show,” Barona says. “We have Two Door Cinema Club coming here for two nights in October, and they could have played an arena show, but they chose us, with a capacity of up to 8,000.”

While the big three of Mexico City, Guadalajara and Monterrey are blessed with good infrastructure, Jacob Fox, an agent at WME who works in the Latin music division, said that the growing interest and demand have led to two major challenges: market saturation and overuse of papering strategies.

“Many artists are overplaying the same cities in Mexico, competing against a laundry list of entertainment options every night. Consequently, soft sales call for promotion, and unfortunately there is a strong habit of papering shows in the market which ultimately punishes the fan who purchased early and rewards those who buy late,” Fox says. “You can guess what happens when that practice becomes rampant and fans catch on. The solution is simple: artists need better touring teams with a better understanding of the country and other markets outside of the majors. There is more than Mexico City, Monterrey, and Guadalajara. In fact, there is more than just Mexico! This is really where the other Central American markets can become key to routing into Latin America.”

Many Anglo acts, especially those from the rock genre, are also drawn to the festival circuit in the region, which saw some regression last year but has since gained some momentum with notable events, including the first-ever Vans Warped Tour at Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez in September.

The beloved rock festival, which gave many alternative, pop-punk and emo bands of the 2000s a platform for years, was such a success in Mexico that the event’s official Instagram page already posted, “See you in 2027!” According to OCESA officials, more than 405,000 people showed interest in purchasing tickets by visiting the event’s Ticketmaster page. Fans from 45 countries were at the festival, and 42% of attendees traveled from outside Mexico City.

Event producer Apodaca Group saw similar success in their festivals. The promoter organizes Tecate Pa’l Norte in Monterrey, one of Mexico’s premier annual music festivals that always features stacked, diverse lineups. The 2026 edition in March was headlined by Tyler, The Creator, Guns N’ Roses, The Killers, Interpol, Deftones, Fabulosos Cadillacs, Kygo, Zoé, The Lumineers and Halsey.

Singer Roco Pachukote of Maldita Vecindad group performs during a concert at Arena CDMX on Oct. 30, 2026, in Mexico City. (Photo by Medios y Media/Getty Images)

Apodaca Group Directors and siblings Oscar and Luis Flores founded the festival in 2012 and continue to innovate the festival experience in the country. They successfully launched Carín León’s La Cura Fest in Hermosillo, welcoming over 47,000 fans across two days and bridging two cultures by featuring country music on Mexican soil.

“This year has been a period of significant growth and a focus on consolidating Apodaca Group’s presence across various cities and countries, enabling us to continue expanding while aiming to position Mexican talent in global markets. Among our recurring annual events is Tecate Pa’l Norte in Monterrey; expectations and challenges rise each year as we integrate a diverse range of Latin and international talent, spanning all genres, generations, and communities,” the Flores brothers and Live Talent Coordinator Macarena González tell *Pollstar*. “…\[La Cura Fest\] demonstrated that audiences are seeking concepts centered on specific genres and experiences, and we are confident these will prove successful in 2027.”

Whatever gap there may have been between Mexico and its neighbors to the north has greatly narrowed over the past five years, with venues and festivals investing in the live experience for fans and artists and giving consumers premium options, which is what can entice tourists from the States and Europe.

Mexico leads the way in Latin America because it continues to expand and develop while showing the world what it can do and what it is missing out on. Fans’ passion shows up in viral videos and even concert films, and it’s so infectious that notable global rock acts such as Twenty One Pilots, Depeche Mode, Rammstein, Ghost, and Monterrey’s own The Warning filmed their concert movies in Mexican venues.

“The business is very strong. While sales per show may be lower than previous years, the sheer volume of tickets sold across the country is certainly up,” Fox says. “There are a growing number of events and more infrastructure coming online soon in several markets – primary, secondary, and tertiary – across the country. Mexico will continue to be a powerful music market for the foreseeable future and there is no sign that it will stop.”

Promoters like OCESA, Apodaca Group and Zignia Live certainly deserve credit for Mexico’s continuous growth by producing shows and operating venues, but the demand and expectations from modern fans, especially those from other countries, are keeping those companies on their toes, always looking for ways to make live music in Mexico an experience that can’t be replicated elsewhere.

“Given our global connectivity, the public now demands that Mexico be prioritized in the experiences and shows offered, often comparing the large-scale formats found elsewhere in the world with those in our country and South America,” the Flores brothers and González say. “Mexico boasts one of the most loyal and devoted audiences, benefiting from its proximity to the U.S. and its role as a link to the rest of the continent. This advantage helps us champion the idea that we do not need to simply replicate what is happening elsewhere; instead, we can work together to build a market with its own unique identity.”
