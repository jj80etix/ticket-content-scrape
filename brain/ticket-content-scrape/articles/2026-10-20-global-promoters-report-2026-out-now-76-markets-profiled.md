# Global Promoters Report 2026 out now: 76 markets profiled

## Summary

IQ released its 2026 Global Promoters Report, a fifth-edition guide profiling 76 touring markets worldwide, with editorial framing a "two-speed" live-music market: stadium and arena shows for top-tier stars remain unstoppable while the "difficult middle" tier struggles. The report's opening interview with AEG Presents' Rich Schaefer highlights oversaturated on-sale patterns and growing affordability pressure across markets.

### Key Points
- 5th edition; expanded to 76 countries profiled.
- Theme: a widening "two-speed" market -- top-tier stadium/arena shows thriving, mid-tier acts struggling.
- Recurring promoter pain points cited: venue availability, currency swings, complex permits, climate-related festival cancellations, tightening sponsorship budgets.
- Genre/market growth areas: Latin and K-pop reaching mass appeal; Caucasus, Central Asia and Gulf governments investing in concerts as tourism drivers.

The industry's indispensable guide to the key promoters and international touring territories is available to read online or in print

![GPR 2026](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/GPR-2026.png)

GPR 2026

The 2026 edition of *IQ* ‘s Global Promoters Report, the latest indispensable guide to the industry’s key promoters and international touring territories, is [out now](https://www.iqmagazine.com/product/global-promoters-report-2026/).

In this expanded fifth edition, *IQ* explores 76 countries, giving readers the most complete overview of conditions in all key markets for international artists, along with dedicated editorial on key trends and developments across the global live music business.

“For the past couple of years, the theme of a ‘two-speed’ market has been bubbling under, but this is the year it feels like it’s really set in,” says *IQ* editor James Drury. “At the top end, stadium and arena shows seem unstoppable as audiences flock to the hottest stars in droves. At the other end, the buzzy acts doing their first club shows are also doing well. But it’s the difficult middle where the struggle is hitting hard.”

The opening interview features AEG Presents’ Rich Schaefer, who describes artists “playing on top of each other” in some markets, on-sale patterns shifting, and ensuring affordability an increasingly complicated challenge.

Around the world, the breadth of genres continues to expand, including Latin and K-pop reaching mass appeal, while governments in the Caucasus, Central Asia, and the Gulf are investing in concerts as tourism drivers.

“What is apparent no matter which market you look at is that everyone offers the same advice: Don’t confuse awareness with demand. Build audiences over time. Partner with people who know the market. And as Schaefer reminds us, a long-term plan beats a quick payday,” Drury says.

“None of this makes the work easy. Venue availability, currency swings, complex permits, climate-related festival cancellations, and tightening sponsorship budgets came up again and again, while global conflicts mean some fans are being denied the chance to see live music as freely as those of us lucky enough not to be in such circumstances.”

“My thanks to every promoter who contributed their time and candour to this edition, and to everyone who supported the Global Promoters Report 2026. It’s been fascinating putting this together again, and I hope you find it valuable,” Drury concludes.
