# Ticketmaster Keeps Top NFL Resale Role as League Expands Access for Competitors

## Summary

The NFL and Ticketmaster struck a new multiyear "Ticketing 3.0" agreement that keeps Ticketmaster as the league's exclusive top-tier ("platinum") secondary partner and operator of the NFL Ticket Exchange, while opening season-ticket-holder resale inventory from all 32 teams to other authorized marketplaces for the first time. SeatGeek has reportedly agreed in principle to a new "gold" partner tier, and a source told TicketNews additional teams are weighing a switch of primary ticketing from Ticketmaster to SeatGeek for 2027, contingent on the new structure finalizing. The deal, approved by owners Oct. 21, is expected to roll out next offseason and is expected to generate more fee revenue for the NFL than the expiring four-year contract, though financial terms were not disclosed.

### Key Points
- "Ticketing 3.0" ends Ticketmaster's exclusive access to the NFL's full season-ticket resale feed; other approved marketplaces will now receive that inventory too.
- Ticketmaster retains primary ticketing for 26 of 32 NFL clubs; SeatGeek holds the other 6 — and may gain more as teams reportedly consider switching ahead of 2027.
- SeatGeek's "gold" partner deal is agreed in principle but not yet finalized; Sports Illustrated Tickets and Tixr are also already in the NFL's authorized network (Tixr added May 2026).
- This resolves a review the NFL had been conducting since at least Oct. 2025 of more sweeping options, including building an NFL-owned ticketing platform.
- NFL exec Bobby Gallo: "We've created more value through less exclusivity"; the new deal runs longer than the prior 4-year term.
- Expanded resale distribution does not itself guarantee lower fees — the NFL hasn't detailed cross-marketplace fee structures.

### Highlights
- Oct. 21, 2026: NFL owners approve "Ticketing 3.0," rolling out next offseason.
- Ticketmaster keeps primary ticketing for 26/32 clubs and the platinum secondary slot; SeatGeek becomes a "gold" secondary partner (pending) and primary partner for 6 clubs, with more team switches reportedly under discussion for 2027.
- Existing authorized network already includes Sports Illustrated Tickets and Tixr (added May 2026).
- League had been evaluating building its own ticketing platform before settling on this expanded-access model.

### Industry Problem
A dominant primary platform having exclusive access to a major league's full resale inventory limits competing marketplaces' ability to offer buyers comparable selection, and concentrates negotiating leverage with one vendor — a structural competitive bottleneck the league itself chose to loosen here.

### Proposed Solution
The NFL's fix is structural, not technical: keep one exclusive top-tier technology partner but broadcast resale inventory across multiple authorized marketplaces so competitors can offer buyers comparable listings. For Etix, this is a #monitor/#build signal on two fronts: (1) watch how "shared inventory, exclusive platinum operator" models play out for major-league ticketing contracts, since it's a template rights-holders may replicate; (2) if pursuing primary deals with mid-size leagues/venues, open inventory-sharing terms (rather than demanding full exclusivity) could be a differentiator against Ticketmaster-style lock-in.

The NFL has reached a new multiyear agreement with Ticketmaster that will significantly expand access to verified resale tickets across competing marketplaces while keeping Ticketmaster in its leading role within the league’s ticketing infrastructure.

The [agreement announced Wednesday](https://media.nfl.com/news-and-releases/corporate-communications/nfl-and-ticketmaster-transform-longstanding-partnership-to-usher?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) introduces what the NFL calls “Ticketing 3.0,” an expanded open-distribution model designed to give season-ticket holders broader exposure for their listings and allow fans to shop for more verified tickets across multiple authorized platforms.

Under the new arrangement, Ticketmaster will remain the NFL’s exclusive top-tier, or “platinum,” secondary ticketing partner and continue powering the NFL Ticket Exchange. However, other authorized secondary marketplaces will gain access to season-ticket resale inventory from all 32 NFL teams, a significant departure from the current arrangement in which Ticketmaster has exclusive access to that comprehensive inventory.

The changes were approved by NFL owners Wednesday and are expected to be implemented during the upcoming offseason, according to [Sports Business Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Articles/2026/10/21/nfl-retains-ticketmaster-as-top-secondary-marketplace-but-changes-model/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), which reported additional details of the agreement.

The agreement represents an important change in how NFL resale inventory reaches consumers. Other companies currently operating within the NFL’s authorized ticketing network include Sports Illustrated Tickets and Tixr. The league [added Tixr to its ticketing network in May](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/nfl-expands-ticketing-lineup-tixr-partnership/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), expanding the number of companies eligible to work directly with NFL clubs on ticketing services.

Although Ticketmaster will retain its preferred position, the expanded distribution represents a meaningful opportunity for competing marketplaces to reach NFL customers with inventory that previously was not available to them through the league’s full season-ticket resale feed.

It could also have significant implications for the competition for primary ticketing services.

Ticketmaster currently handles primary ticketing for 26 NFL clubs, while SeatGeek serves the other six. However, a source familiar with the situation told TicketNews that additional NFL teams have expressed interest in switching their primary ticketing operations from Ticketmaster to SeatGeek for the 2027 season. Those potential deals depend on the league’s new ticketing arrangements being finalized, the source said. No additional team contracts have been publicly announced.

The agreement does not directly change the league’s primary ticketing operations for events such as the Super Bowl and most international games.

The NFL has operated an open ticketing network since 2017, allowing approved marketplaces to participate in the sale of authenticated tickets. The new agreement goes further by extending access to inventory that had remained concentrated on Ticketmaster’s platform.

The decision also resolves a closely watched round of negotiations over the NFL’s future ticketing structure.

As [TicketNews reported in October 2025](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/10/nfl-reportedly-considering-sweeping-ticketing-changes-for-27/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), league executives had been evaluating potentially sweeping changes to their ticketing arrangements ahead of the expiration of existing agreements in 2027. Options reportedly under consideration included establishing an NFL-owned ticketing platform, gaining greater control over ticketing data and restructuring the relationship between teams and technology providers.

Ultimately, the league has chosen to maintain Ticketmaster’s central role while expanding opportunities for competitors to participate in secondary ticket distribution.

The revised structure is also expected to generate substantially more fee revenue for the NFL than its previous agreement, Gallo told SBJ. Financial terms were not disclosed, although the new agreement will run longer than the expiring four-year contract.

“We’ve created more value through less exclusivity,” Gallo said in the NFL’s official announcement.

For Ticketmaster, the agreement preserves its position as the league’s principal ticketing technology partner, even as competing marketplaces receive greater access to inventory flowing through that infrastructure.

Ticketmaster President Saumil Mehta said the company is focused on extending the league’s verified-ticketing experience across additional sales channels.

“Fans should be able to easily find and buy verified tickets with confidence,” Mehta said.

The arrangement could create more competition among consumer-facing ticket marketplaces, particularly as companies compete on pricing, fees, customer experience and marketing to attract buyers looking at the same inventory.

However, expanded distribution does not automatically mean lower ticket prices or reduced fees. The NFL has not detailed how transaction fees will work across participating marketplaces or whether the new structure will change the overall cost of purchasing resale tickets.

The distinction between broader marketplace access and control of underlying ticketing technology remains important. While competitors will gain access to more inventory, Ticketmaster will continue operating the NFL Ticket Exchange and retain its preferred partnership status.

The league has not announced a specific date for the new distribution system to become operational or identified which additional marketplaces will participate under the revised partner structure.
