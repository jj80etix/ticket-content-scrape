# Nadler Calls Live Nation Settlement ‘Grotesquely Politicized’ Following New Trump Disclosure

## Summary

Rep. Jerry Nadler escalated criticism of the DOJ-Live Nation antitrust settlement after an October 21 amended Tunney Act court filing acknowledged, for the first time, that President Trump was present at the March 5 White House meeting where settlement terms were finalized — a detail the company's original June 22 disclosure had omitted. The amendment also confirmed Trump personally called Live Nation CEO Michael Rapino in February to discuss the Kennedy Center and the status of the antitrust suit. Live Nation maintains Trump did not take part in substantive negotiations, but the disclosure adds to an already-documented pattern of company ties to the administration (a $500K inaugural donation, retained Trump-connected advisers, and a board seat for Richard Grenell) that preceded DOJ's decision to drop its push to break up Ticketmaster.

### Key Points
- Oct. 21 amended Tunney Act filing is the first to admit Trump's presence at the settlement meeting; the June 22 original filing omitted it.
- Filing also confirms Trump directly called CEO Michael Rapino in February re: the Kennedy Center and the antitrust case's status.
- Nadler, ranking Democrat on the House Judiciary antitrust subcommittee, calls the deal "grotesquely politicized."
- The settlement lets Live Nation and Ticketmaster remain under common ownership — DOJ abandoned its structural-breakup demand.
- Prior documented ties cited: $500K to Trump's 2025 inaugural committee, advisers Kellyanne Conway/Mike Davis/Brian Ballard, and Richard Grenell's May 2025 board appointment.

### Highlights
- The Oct. 21 amended disclosure is the first Live Nation filing to admit Trump's presence at the settlement meeting; the original June 22 filing did not mention it.
- Nadler's position (ranking member, House Judiciary antitrust subcommittee) gives the criticism institutional weight within ongoing Tunney Act review.
- The settlement's core term — continued common ownership of Live Nation and Ticketmaster — remains the central competitive issue for independent platforms.

### Industry Problem
The settlement resolving DOJ's antitrust suit against Live Nation/Ticketmaster determines whether the two stay vertically integrated, and is now under renewed political scrutiny — raising doubt about whether the underlying competitive harms (bundled promotion/ticketing dominance) were meaningfully addressed rather than negotiated away.

### Proposed Solution
No product response is implied by this item; the posture is to monitor how the disclosure affects judicial review of the settlement (Tunney Act public-interest determination) and watch for a trigger — court intervention, a Congressional inquiry, or amended settlement terms — that would reopen competitive space for independent primary-ticketing platforms like Etix.

Rep. Jerry Nadler says Live Nation’s [newly amended court disclosure acknowledging President Donald Trump’s presence at a critical White House settlement meeting](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/live-nation-trump-settlement-disclosure-amended/) has intensified his concerns about political interference in the Justice Department’s antitrust case against Live Nation and Ticketmaster.

“DOJ’s belated admission that President Trump was in the room when DOJ negotiated its proposed settlement with Live Nation Ticketmaster only further deepens my concerns that the deal was grotesquely politicized,” Nadler, the ranking Democrat on the House Judiciary subcommittee overseeing antitrust matters, told TicketNews in a statement.

The response follows an October 21 court filing in which Live Nation acknowledged for the first time in its formal Tunney Act disclosures that Trump briefly attended a March 5 meeting where company executives and senior federal officials finalized the terms of a settlement that would allow Live Nation and Ticketmaster to remain under common ownership.

The company’s original June 22 disclosure identified extensive settlement discussions involving the Justice Department and White House Counsel’s Office but did not mention Trump’s attendance at the meeting.

Live Nation maintains that Trump did not participate in substantive settlement negotiations during either interaction. The October 21 amendment was submitted by Live Nation and Ticketmaster, rather than DOJ, which Nadler referenced in his statement.

The new acknowledgment adds another documented presidential interaction to an extensive history of political contributions, Trump-connected advisers, meetings with senior administration officials and changes in DOJ leadership that preceded the government’s decision to abandon its pursuit of a Ticketmaster breakup.

Live Nation’s efforts to establish relationships with figures close to the Trump administration began well before the antitrust trial.

In April 2025, [TicketNews first reported Live Nation’s $500,000 contribution to Trump’s second inaugural committee](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/04/live-nation-donated-500000-to-trump-inauguration-committee/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), based on federal inaugural fundraising disclosures.

The contribution came while Live Nation was defending itself against a Justice Department lawsuit filed in May 2024 that sought structural remedies, including the separation of Ticketmaster from its parent company.

Live Nation also retained several individuals with close connections to Trump and his administration, including former Trump adviser Kellyanne Conway, attorney Mike Davis and lobbying firm founder Brian Ballard.

Davis had already attracted attention for his involvement in another controversial antitrust settlement involving Hewlett Packard Enterprise’s acquisition of Juniper Networks, where disagreements over the handling of the case contributed to turmoil within DOJ’s Antitrust Division.

In May 2025, Live Nation added Richard Grenell, a longtime Trump confidant and former ambassador who was then serving as president of the Kennedy Center, to its board of directors.

The appointment [drew immediate criticism from independent venue operators and other industry figures](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/05/23/live-nations-trump-allied-board-pick-draws-sharp-backlash/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), who questioned the decision to add a politically influential figure while the company was facing litigation that threatened its business structure.

Grenell’s simultaneous roles at Live Nation and the Kennedy Center became particularly relevant when subsequent disclosures established that the performing arts institution was the subject of Trump’s February conversation with Rapino.

Live Nation also publicly embraced Trump’s March 2025 executive order addressing ticketing practices. In June, the company announced a $1 billion venue investment initiative and cited the administration’s ticketing policies favorably, although a [TicketNews examination of the announced projects](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/06/live-nations-venue-pr-blitz-raises-eyebrows-amid-antitrust-fight/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) found that nearly all were already in development before Trump returned to office.

The company’s political relationships were not merely peripheral to its legal strategy.

Its [October 21 amended disclosure](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/gov.uscourts.nysd_.621993.1595.0.pdf?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) specifically identifies Conway, Davis and Grenell among the company representatives and advisers who substantively participated in at least one communication with federal officials concerning the proposed settlement.

The filing also confirms that Davis and attorney William Levi sought to initiate discussions with DOJ’s Antitrust Division between February and June 2025, including conversations about potential settlement possibilities.

Those contacts eventually developed into formal negotiations. Live Nation submitted an initial settlement proposal in September 2025 and a written proposal the following month. DOJ delivered a counterproposal in January 2026.

The disclosure does not reveal the contents of those proposals or establish that the company’s political contributions and relationships improperly influenced the government’s decisions.

## DOJ Departures and the Pardon of a Potential Witness

The negotiations unfolded alongside significant personnel changes within the Justice Department’s Antitrust Division.

In July 2025, senior antitrust officials Roger Alford and Bill Rinner were dismissed amid disputes over enforcement decisions and politically connected lobbying surrounding major corporate cases.

Reporting at the time tied the dismissals principally to internal disagreements involving the HPE-Juniper merger. Alford subsequently alleged that he had also resisted inappropriate lobbying connected to the Live Nation litigation.

Speaking at the National Independent Venue Association’s conference in June 2026, Alford said he and other officials had sought to resolve cases on their legal merits rather than through political intervention.

“We wanted to resolve these cases on the merits,” Alford said, according to [TicketNews’ coverage of the former DOJ officials’ remarks](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/06/former-doj-antitrust-attorneys-slam-live-nation-settlement-as-remedies-fight-intensifies/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

He said there had been lobbying surrounding the Live Nation case that he and colleagues considered inappropriate.

Another notable development occurred on December 2, 2025, when Trump granted a full presidential pardon to Tim Leiweke, the former CEO of Oak View Group, who had been charged in a separate federal criminal bid-rigging case involving the University of Texas’ Moody Center.

Oak View Group, co-founded by Leiweke and Irving Azoff, was an important third party in the government’s antitrust allegations against Live Nation.

DOJ had accused Live Nation and OVG of maintaining arrangements that discouraged competition in concert promotion. Separate government proceedings also uncovered financial agreements under which OVG received substantial payments connected to Ticketmaster ticketing contracts.

On December 4, just two days after receiving his pardon, Leiweke invoked his Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination during questioning in the Live Nation antitrust litigation, according to Bloomberg reporting [detailed by TicketNews at the time](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/12/fresh-off-trump-pardon-ovgs-leiweke-refuses-to-testify-in-live-nation-antitrust-suit/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

The pardon eliminated the federal criminal charge against Leiweke but did not result in his cooperation with attorneys pursuing the Live Nation case.

The available evidence does not establish that the pardon was intended to affect the antitrust litigation or that it caused Leiweke to decline answering questions. Nevertheless, the sequence removed a criminal prosecution involving a figure with potentially important knowledge of the relationships at issue in the broader case.

DOJ’s leadership turmoil continued into 2026.

On February 12, Antitrust Division chief Gail Slater announced her departure amid reports of disagreements with senior administration officials concerning enforcement priorities and the handling of major corporate cases.

As [TicketNews reported when Slater departed](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/02/the-fix-is-in-doj-antitrust-turmoil-boosts-live-nation-bid-to-escape-ticketmaster-monopoly-trial/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the announcement came shortly after Semafor reported that Live Nation executives and lobbyists had been pursuing settlement discussions with senior DOJ officials outside the division, at times sidelining Slater.

The department disputed portions of that reporting, and Slater’s departure alone does not establish that the Live Nation case caused her removal.

The personnel changes nevertheless left the Antitrust Division under new leadership just weeks before the trial was scheduled to begin.

## Trump’s Personal Involvement and the March Settlement

The most consequential interactions occurred during the final weeks before DOJ withdrew from the litigation.

In August, [The Wall Street Journal reported that Trump personally directed a senior Justice Department official to settle the Live Nation case](https://www.wsj.com/business/media/trump-live-nation-antitrust-doj-f82b2c55?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) after meeting with Rapino in the Oval Office.

According to the Journal, the February 27 meeting was primarily intended to address programming and other issues at the Kennedy Center.

During that meeting, Trump reportedly asked Rapino why the company had not yet resolved the federal antitrust lawsuit. Rapino explained that negotiations had been ongoing but no agreement had been reached.

The Journal reported that Trump subsequently instructed a senior DOJ official to settle the case shortly before trial began.

The company’s amended October filing confirms a February contact initiated by Trump, including his inquiry about the lawsuit, but does not identify the exact date or establish whether that contact was the same encounter described by the Journal.

It also does not address the Journal’s reporting that Trump subsequently directed DOJ leadership to reach an agreement.

The Journal separately reported that DOJ antitrust attorneys had initially prepared a counterproposal requiring Ticketmaster’s divestiture, but that provision was removed before the government delivered its January proposal.

The January terms reportedly remained considerably more aggressive than the eventual settlement, including potential requirements concerning Live Nation’s amphitheater holdings and Ticketmaster’s technology.

On March 2, the federal antitrust trial began in New York.

Three days later, Live Nation executives and advisers met with senior Justice Department officials and representatives of the White House Counsel’s Office to finalize a settlement.

According to the October 21 amended filing, the discussions included officials from the offices of the attorney general, deputy attorney general and associate attorney general, along with the Antitrust Division and White House Counsel’s Office.

The filing states:

“President Trump briefly appeared at this meeting but did not participate in any substantive settlement discussions.”

A term sheet outlining the material provisions of the agreement was signed that day.

DOJ publicly announced the settlement on March 9, one week after the trial began.

The agreement had been negotiated without the knowledge of some members of the government’s trial team, and U.S. District Judge Arun Subramanian criticized the department’s handling of the disclosure during the ongoing proceedings.

Several senior DOJ antitrust litigators subsequently left the department, including David Dahlquist, who had led the federal trial team. Bloomberg reported that some departures reflected frustration over the settlement and broader concerns about the direction of antitrust enforcement.

The settlement itself represented a substantial change from the government’s original litigation objective.

Rather than requiring Live Nation and Ticketmaster to separate, the [proposed agreement](https://www.justice.gov/atr/case/us-and-plaintiff-states-v-live-nation-entertainment-inc-and-ticketmaster-llc?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) would retain their existing ownership structure while imposing restrictions on certain exclusive ticketing contracts, expanding access for competing primary ticketing marketplaces and limiting service fees at Live Nation-operated amphitheaters.

The agreement also requires changes to approximately 13 amphitheater booking arrangements and establishes a $280 million fund intended to address state damages and civil penalty claims. The fund is not a fine paid to DOJ.

Live Nation has defended the agreement as a meaningful reform package that would improve competition and give artists and venues greater flexibility.

The Justice Department has similarly maintained that negotiated remedies would provide more immediate competitive benefits than continuing federal litigation with an uncertain outcome.

But most participating state attorneys general rejected the agreement and continued pursuing the case independently.

On April 15, a federal jury found Live Nation and Ticketmaster liable on the antitrust claims presented by the remaining states, including findings of unlawful monopoly conduct in ticketing and amphitheater markets.

The verdict also found that Ticketmaster’s conduct resulted in consumer overcharges of $1.72 per affected ticket. The scope of damages and appropriate remedies remain subject to further proceedings and legal challenges.

The states are pursuing structural remedies that could still require Live Nation to separate from Ticketmaster.

## Nadler, Ticketing Coalition Call for Further Scrutiny

The October 21 disclosure has renewed questions about whether the federal settlement received adequate public scrutiny, particularly because Trump’s attendance at the March 5 meeting was not identified in the company’s original Tunney Act filing.

Nadler had already joined Rep. Jamie Raskin in [submitting formal comments to the court in September](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/judiciary-democrats-challenge-doj-s-live-nation-ticketmaster-settlement-urge-court-to-investigate-trump-administration-s-backroom-deal?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), urging an examination of whether political influence affected the negotiations and whether the proposed agreement adequately protects competition.

The lawmakers questioned the roles played by White House officials and politically connected advisers, as well as the decision to abandon the structural separation DOJ originally sought.

Nadler’s fresh criticism comes less than a week after he joined Sen. Elizabeth Warren and four other Democratic lawmakers in releasing a broader [congressional report examining the administration’s antitrust settlements](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/congressional-antitrust-report-live-nation-settlement-trump-doj/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

That report, titled *Settling for Less*, examined Live Nation’s political relationships and characterized the administration’s approach to antitrust enforcement as a pattern of pay-to-play dealmaking.

Former senior DOJ antitrust official Roger Alford raised similar concerns during a May 18 congressional forum convened by Raskin and Sen. Richard Blumenthal, arguing that politically connected lobbyists and presidential allies had gained inappropriate influence over antitrust enforcement decisions.

“The rule of law quickly becomes the rule of lobbyists,” Alford told lawmakers.

During questioning from Rep. Pramila Jayapal, Alford was specifically asked about Live Nation’s $500,000 inaugural contribution, its hiring of Trump-connected advisers Mike Davis and Kellyanne Conway, Grenell’s appointment to the company’s board, and reports that Trump personally intervened in the case. Asked whether he believed undue political influence, rather than the merits of the case, had driven the settlement, Alford responded: “Yes, I think the jury spoke to the merits of the case.”

The Coalition for Ticket Fairness, an advocacy organization supported by businesses involved in ticket resale, technology and related services, has also called for greater transparency following the amended disclosure.

“Live Nation’s latest disclosure raises serious questions about the transparency of the process that produced its antitrust settlement with the Department of Justice,” CTF spokesperson Geoff Vetter said in a statement provided to TicketNews.

Vetter specifically criticized the company’s failure to identify Trump’s presence in its original filing and argued that the proposed agreement fails to address Live Nation’s market power.

“Even after a jury found that Live Nation and Ticketmaster violated antitrust law, the company is fighting to preserve a settlement that protects its market power and leaves the companies together rather than restoring meaningful competition,” Vetter said.

The coalition is urging both the judiciary and Congress to examine the circumstances surrounding the agreement.

“The court and Congress should demand full transparency into how this deal came together and ensure that any final agreement delivers real, enforceable reforms,” Vetter said.

Other industry and consumer organizations raised similar objections during the original public-comment process.

The National Independent Venue Association and the Break Up Ticketmaster Coalition submitted formal objections in September, challenging both the adequacy of the proposed remedies and the transparency of the negotiations.

Those earlier submissions were made before Live Nation acknowledged Trump’s presence at the March 5 meeting.

The timing is relevant because the Tunney Act public-comment period closed September 4, more than six weeks before the company amended its disclosure.

Live Nation maintains that its original filing complied with the law and that it submitted the amendment out of caution following an August ruling in the separate HPE-Juniper antitrust case concerning disclosure obligations.

The amended filing does not independently establish that Trump improperly influenced DOJ’s settlement decision or that Live Nation received favorable treatment in exchange for political contributions or relationships.

It does, however, add previously undisclosed presidential involvement to a record that already includes substantial lobbying activity, White House participation in negotiations, reported disagreements within DOJ and a settlement that preserved the corporate structure the government originally sought to dismantle.

Subramanian has previously declined to authorize broad discovery into the settlement negotiations but indicated that narrower, targeted information requests could be considered.

The judge must still determine whether the proposed federal agreement satisfies the Tunney Act’s public-interest requirements. That review remains separate from the remedies proceedings arising from the states’ jury verdict.

Whether the newly acknowledged presidential involvement prompts additional disclosures or further examination of the negotiations remains an open question as the court considers the proposed agreement.
