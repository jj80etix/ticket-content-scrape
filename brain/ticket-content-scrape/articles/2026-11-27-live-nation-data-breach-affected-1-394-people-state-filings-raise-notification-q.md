# Live Nation Data Breach Affected 1,394 People, State Filings Raise Notification Questions

## Summary

State regulatory filings reviewed by TicketNews establish that a June 23, 2026 Live Nation cybersecurity incident affected 1,394 individuals, with Live Nation completing its internal review of affected data on Sept. 4 and sending notification letters dated Oct. 2 — but Massachusetts' own filing is reportedly dated Nov. 24, raising questions about a roughly seven-to-eight-week gap between the notification letter date and the state filing. The incident, distinct from the 2024 Ticketmaster cloud breach and a separate Brazil breach, appears to involve personnel/employment data (including Social Security and driver's license numbers) rather than ticket-purchaser data, and has already prompted three proposed class actions.

### Key Points
- Timeline per filings: breach June 23, 2026 → review complete Sept. 4 → notification letter dated Oct. 2 → three class actions filed Oct. 6-7 → Massachusetts filing reported Nov. 24, affecting 42 Massachusetts residents (SSNs, driver's license data).
- Indiana AG records show 1,394 affected individuals total; two class-action plaintiffs identify as former Live Nation employees, suggesting the breach centers on personnel records, not Ticketmaster customer/purchase data.
- Massachusetts law (Ch. 93H §3) requires notification "as soon as practicable and without unreasonable delay" and bars delaying notice solely to pin down the total affected count; a criminal-investigation exception can permit delay but isn't confirmed here.
- Live Nation is offering 24 months of identity-protection services; Massachusetts separately mandates at least 18 months of credit monitoring when SSNs are compromised.

### Industry Problem
Multi-state breach-notification regimes create a compliance burden with inconsistent timing requirements, and this incident shows how a gap between an internal review date, a notification-letter date, and a state's recorded filing date can itself become a source of regulatory and litigation exposure for a ticketing/event company — a peer-company risk any primary platform handling PII (including employee data) should note.

### Proposed Solution
The article proposes no fix; TicketNews is seeking clarification from Live Nation and Massachusetts regulators. For Etix: monitor how this timeline resolves and whether regulators treat the Oct.-to-Nov. gap as noncompliant — the trigger for a posture change would be any enforcement action or fine tied to notification delay, which would argue for tightening Etix's own breach-notification runbook/timelines preemptively.

## Massachusetts Notification Requirements

The timing of the Massachusetts disclosure is significant given the state’s requirements for reporting data breaches.

Under [Chapter 93H, Section 3 of Massachusetts General Laws](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter93H/Section3?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), organizations that become aware of a qualifying breach involving Massachusetts residents’ personal information are required to notify affected individuals, the attorney general and the Office of Consumer Affairs and Business Regulation “as soon as practicable and without unreasonable delay.”

The statute also prohibits organizations from postponing notification solely because the total number of affected residents has not been determined, instead providing for supplemental disclosures as additional information becomes available.

Massachusetts law does allow for delayed notification under [certain circumstances involving criminal investigations](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter93H/Section4?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). Live Nation’s acknowledgment that it notified federal law enforcement does not establish whether such an exception applied in this case.

The dates currently available do not establish that Live Nation violated Massachusetts law. It remains unclear whether Nov. 24 represents the company’s initial notification to Massachusetts authorities, a subsequent submission or the processing or publication of an earlier notification. The Oct. 2 date on the letter does not establish when affected Massachusetts residents actually received their notices.

Massachusetts also [requires companies to offer at least 18 months of complimentary credit monitoring](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter93H/Section3A?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) to residents whose Social Security numbers were compromised or are reasonably believed to have been compromised.

TicketNews has asked both Massachusetts agencies to confirm when they first received notice of the incident and has requested copies of the complete regulatory filing and any supplemental submissions, beyond the sample consumer letter.

## Questions Remain About Whose Data Was Compromised

Live Nation previously characterized the incident as limited in scope, saying in October that it had contained the cybersecurity incident, retained outside specialists to investigate and experienced no disruption to its operations.

The total of 1,394 affected individuals recorded by Indiana authorities provides a clearer indication of the scale of the incident, although the records do not identify the relationship between those individuals and Live Nation.

As TicketNews previously reported, two of the plaintiffs in the proposed class actions identified themselves as former Live Nation employees who had provided personal information to the company in connection with their employment.

The types of information identified in the Vermont disclosure, including Social Security numbers, government identification numbers and health records, also raise the possibility that the incident involved personnel records rather than information collected through ordinary ticket purchases.

There has been no public confirmation that Ticketmaster customer accounts or ticket purchasing systems were affected by the June incident.

The breach appears distinct from both the much larger 2024 Ticketmaster cybersecurity incident involving a third-party cloud database and the [Ticketmaster customer data breach disclosed in Brazil this fall](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/ticketmaster-brazil-data-breach-2026/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

The Brazilian incident also raised questions about notification timelines. Brazil’s National Data Protection Authority confirmed to TicketNews in October that Ticketmaster had met the deadline for its initial regulatory disclosure, while questions remained about when affected ticket purchasers were notified.

TicketNews has asked Live Nation to confirm whether the state disclosures concern the same incident, whether the reported total of 1,394 remains accurate, whose personal information was affected and why the Massachusetts disclosure carries a reported filing date nearly eight weeks after the date on the company’s notification letter.
