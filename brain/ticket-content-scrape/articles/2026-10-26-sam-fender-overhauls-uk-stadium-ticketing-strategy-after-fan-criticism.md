# Sam Fender overhauls UK stadium ticketing strategy after fan criticism

## Summary

Sam Fender has overhauled his UK stadium "Proper Massive Tour" ticketing plan after fans criticized leaked pricing details, scrapping a split front/rear standing-admission tier structure and lowering prices. The artist said he had "no idea" about the original tiered pricing plan, pointing to friction between artists and the ticketing/promotion chain over pricing decisions.

### Key Points
- Fender removed a planned multi-tier standing-admission structure (separate front/rear pricing) after fan backlash over leaked prices.
- He said he was not consulted on or aware of the original tiered-pricing plan before it leaked.
- Tiered standing pricing (paying more for closer standing position) is described as "common" industry practice.
- Fender is lowering overall ticket prices for the stadium run in response.

### Highlights
- Fan backlash followed an online leak of tour pricing ahead of the official on-sale.
- Fender publicly disowned the original split-tier pricing plan, saying he lacked visibility into it.
- The artist is now lowering prices and simplifying the standing-admission structure.

### Industry Problem
Patrons are increasingly reacting to opaque, tiered pricing structures (e.g., paying more for a better standing position) that get revealed only through leaks rather than clear upfront disclosure — and artists themselves can be insulated from or unaware of how their own shows are priced and tiered by promoters/ticketing partners.

### Proposed Solution
Fender's fix was manual and reactive: removing the tier and cutting prices himself after public pressure. A primary platform like Etix could get ahead of this by giving artists/promoters clear pre-sale visibility and sign-off on tiered pricing structures, and by surfacing tier definitions and price-by-section transparently to fans before purchase rather than relying on leaks to force scrutiny.
