# Louder Than Life, Bourbon & Beyond Draw 410,000 Fans in Louisville

## Summary

Danny Wimmer Presents' Louder Than Life and Bourbon & Beyond festivals drew over 410,000 fans combined in Louisville this September, down from last year's record attendance, with Bourbon & Beyond also signaling an eventual rebrand away from the "Bourbon" name. An attendance/programming recap with no ticketing-platform, pricing, or policy content.

### Key Points
- Combined attendance of 410,000+ across both festivals, down from last year's record.
- DWP estimates ~$40 million in economic impact for Kentucky from the eight festival days.
- Bourbon & Beyond is quietly de-emphasizing "Bourbon" branding ahead of an expected 2027 name change.
- No ticketing-platform, fee, or policy content reported.

Danny Wimmer Presents says more than 410,000 fans attended Louder Than Life and Bourbon & Beyond across eight festival days in Louisville this September, down from the record attendance reported for the two events last year as one of the festivals also prepares for a significant branding change.

Nearly 300 artists performed across the back-to-back four-day festivals at the Kentucky Exposition Center, with DWP estimating the events generated nearly $40 million in economic impact for Kentucky. Organizers did not disclose individual attendance figures for each festival.

This year’s Louder Than Life ran September 17-20 and featured more than 200 bands across seven stages, led by My Chemical Romance, Iron Maiden, Tool, Limp Bizkit, Gojira and Pantera. Other notable appearances included Pierce The Veil, Papa Roach, A Day To Remember, Sublime, Megadeth and Danny Elfman.

Among the weekend’s notable sets were Iron Maiden’s first Kentucky performance in more than 40 years, a surprise appearance from Louisville natives Knocked Loose and one of Pantera’s few U.S. performances of 2026. Danny Elfman’s appearance marked his first show in the eastern half of the U.S. in nearly a decade, according to DWP.

Bourbon & Beyond followed September 24-27, with more than 100 artists performing across five stages. Foo Fighters, Mumford & Sons, Chris Stapleton and Dave Matthews Band headlined, with Queens of the Stone Age, Kacey Musgraves, The Red Clay Strays, Hootie & the Blowfish, Counting Crows, Foster The People, Goose and Jason Isbell and the 400 Unit among the rest of the lineup.

The eighth edition of Bourbon & Beyond also featured the festival’s familiar culinary and spirits programming, but the name under which the event returns in 2027 appears likely to change.

DWP began deemphasizing the word “Bourbon” ahead of this year’s festival, with signs, merchandise and social media branding increasingly displaying simply “& Beyond.” Some signs at the event covered the Bourbon portion of the existing name with “Louisville, Kentucky.”

DWP General Manager Chamie McCurry confirmed the branding shift in a statement during the festival, saying bourbon “will always be part of our story and an essential part of the festival’s DNA.”

McCurry said the broader “& Beyond” identity reflects how the festival has grown to encompass music, food, drinks and Kentucky culture while giving DWP additional room to attract new audiences, artists and commercial partners.

The branding change may also have a practical digital-marketing benefit. [WDRB+WAVE reported](https://www.wdrbwave.com/2026/09/25/bourbon-beyond-faces-rebrand-reach-larger-audience/) that social platforms’ age-related alcohol restrictions can limit the reach of accounts containing alcohol references in their names or profiles, potentially giving the festival greater access to younger audiences under an “& Beyond” identity.

The change does not appear to signal the removal of bourbon programming itself. DWP said the category will remain part of the festival, and the 2026 event continued to feature bourbon, wine and spirits programming alongside its culinary stages. The festival’s existing website similarly continues to describe bourbon tastings and spirits experiences as part of the event.

DWP has not yet formally announced the 2027 dates or finalized the festival’s future name. The promoter said dates for both Louisville festivals will be announced soon. Whatever name Bourbon & Beyond ultimately carries next year, both events are already committed to returning to the Kentucky Exposition Center under DWP’s agreement with Kentucky Venues through 2035.
