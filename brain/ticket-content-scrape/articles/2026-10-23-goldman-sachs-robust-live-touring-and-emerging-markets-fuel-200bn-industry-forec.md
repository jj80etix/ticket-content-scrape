# Goldman Sachs: ‘Robust’ live touring and emerging markets fuel $200bn industry forecast

## Summary

Goldman Sachs' annual "Music in the Air" report raised its 10-year growth forecast for the global music industry for the first time in three years, projecting nearly $200bn in value by 2035 (5.8% CAGR) driven by emerging-market streaming growth and "robust" global touring demand. The report flags average ticket prices up 37% since 2019 ($91→$124), stadium shows commanding 2-3x per-ticket revenue versus smaller venues, and sees DSP integrations (Spotify Reserved, Apple Music/Ticketmaster) and "agentic commerce" as emerging, largely positive forces for primary ticketing platforms specifically.

### Key Points
- Industry forecast raised to ~$200bn by 2035 (5.8% CAGR), up from $112.5bn in 2025; live music segment alone grows to +$27.7bn.
- Emerging-market streaming revenue growth led by MENA (26%), Sub-Saharan Africa (25%), Latin America (25%).
- Average ticket price rose 37% from $91 (2019) to $124 (2025); industry ticket revenue up ~60% since 2019.
- Gen Z spends 23% more per month on concerts than the average US listener.
- DSP/ticketing integration (Spotify Reserved with Live Nation/Ticketmaster; Apple Music with Ticketmaster/Bandsintown) flagged as an emerging pre-sale monetization channel.
- Goldman expects "agentic commerce" to be a net positive for ticketing, with primary platforms best positioned given contractual inventory control — but warns of price-transparency risk and AI platforms acting as "toll collectors."

### Highlights
- $200bn industry value forecast by 2035 (5.8% CAGR), first upward revision in three years.
- Average ticket price +37% since 2019 ($91 → $124); stadium tickets sell for 2-3x smaller-venue prices.
- DSP pre-sale partnerships (Spotify, Apple Music) and agentic commerce named as the next ticketing monetization/discovery frontier.

### Industry Problem
As AI agents and DSPs increasingly mediate event discovery and pre-sale access, primary ticketing platforms risk being disintermediated or forced to share margin with new "toll collector" layers unless they control the inventory relationship directly.

### Proposed Solution
Goldman frames owning direct pre-sale/inventory relationships with DSPs and AI assistants as the defensible position; for Etix, the signal is to evaluate early-stage integrations (data feeds, pre-sale APIs) with streaming/AI discovery surfaces before third-party aggregators establish the toll points, and to watch agentic-commerce purchasing flows as a long-horizon distribution channel.

## Goldman Sachs: ‘Robust’ live touring and emerging markets fuel $200bn industry forecast

Momentum for live activity is accelerating, despite macro uncertainty, as both supply and demand widen, per the Music in the Air report

![Allegiant Stadium ](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Allegiant-Stadium-.png)

Allegiant Stadium © Allegiant Stadium

Goldman Sachs has raised its growth forecast for the global music industry for the first time in three years, in part due to the “globalisation of the concert ecosystem” opening up a world of opportunities.

With its annual *Music In The Air* report, the US banking institution projects the industry’s value will rise to nearly $200 billion (€178bn) in the next decade, representing a 5.8% compound annual growth rate (CAGR). By comparison, the industry was valued at $112.5bn (€100bn) in 2025.

Recorded is expected to lead the growth, swelling by $45.8bn (+6.3% CAGR) by 2035, followed by live music at $27.7bn (+5.7% CAGR). Publishing ranks third, with an additional $12.1bn (+4.8 % CAGR) in value.

The projected growth is underpinned in part by “emerging market monetisation”. Subscription streaming revenues in emerging markets grew by double digits last year, led by MENA (26%), Sub-Saharan Africa (25%), Latin America (25%), and Asia (15%). Among mature markets, Europe ranked first with a recorded growth of 7%.

The increasingly global consumption of music is also creating opportunities for an expanding touring landscape.

Although economic uncertainties around tariffs, oil prices, and a “volatile political backdrop” remain, the report finds that “demand for live events has structurally increased on a global basis coming out of the pandemic” along with a “robust momentum for concerts”.

The report attributes the industry’s resilience “against the backdrop of weaker economic growth” in part to “increasing global diversification beyond developed economies in Europe and North America to markets in Latin America and Asia”, allowing supply to be matched more dynamically with markets where demand remains robust.

> “These examples are the first step towards DSPs further leveraging listener data”

**Supply & demand**  
On the supply side, the industry is expected to benefit from an “increasing supply of diverse & popular artists touring” in the next decade, supported by “insatiable consumer demand for live music”.

Stronger financial initiatives and the promotional value of touring are helping artists build “long-term global brands that will drive consumption” of their music and wider brand.

The report finds that, even as streaming revenues grow, financial incentives to tour will remain strong, supported by “increased live music venue capacity” worldwide, which will provide the infrastructure needed for further growth.

On the demand side, millennial and Gen Z fanbases are expected to help usher in the industry’s next chapter. According to the report, Gen Z spends 23% more per month on concerts than the average US listener.

The report also identifies an opportunity for live music ticketing to benefit from “increased integration with streaming services”, reflecting the consumption habits of younger generations. Spotify’s Reserved alliance with Live Nation and Ticketmaster, alongside Apple Music’s partnerships with Ticketmaster and Bandsintown, both point to an emerging trend.

“We believe that these examples are the first step towards DSPs \[Digital Service Provider\] further leveraging listener data to facilitate connections between highly motivated fans and their most listened-to artists,” reads the report. “Ultimately, we see the acquisition of pre-sale rights for premium DSP users as a new way for ticketers to monetise pre-sale partnerships.”

> “Stadium tickets can also sell for 2-3x the average price per ticket compared to smaller venues”

**Ticketing  
**Across the industry, gross revenue from tickets has swelled around 60% from 2019 to 2025. Among the top 100 tours, the average number of tickets sold increased roughly 20%, while in the same window, the average price of said tickets rose by nearly 40%.

More specifically, the average ticket price has risen from $91 (€81) in 2019 to $124 (€111) in 2025, a 37% increase from pre-pandemic.

Price growth has extended across touring tiers over the past two years, with average prices rising by 29% for shows at venues with capacities of 30,000 or more, around 15% at the mid-tier level and 20% at venues with capacities below 750.

“Stadium tickets can also sell for 2-3x the average price per ticket compared to smaller venues, magnifying the effects on profitability for artists (and for the ticketing platforms which get a higher fee per ticket),” authors say.

“Conversely, while smaller venues sell fewer tickets at a lower price per fan, these events can still be highly profitable for the venue owners that continue to benefit from high food & beverage per caps, and ancillary revenues like parking and VIP experiences.”

Supply at the amphitheatre and arena level is expected to grow, while stadium supply is anticipated to keep pace, creating a “robust touring slate” across the board.

Another notable development is that total attendance for live shows at North American college football stadiums is up 25% year-on-year, underscoring how the circuit is [opening up](https://www.iqmagazine.com/magazine/the-game-plan-college-stadiums/) a new avenue for touring in the busy market.

> Agentic commerce is expected to be “ultimately a net positive” for the ticketing sector

**AI  
**A timely section for the report is the impact of agentic commerce on the ticketing sector, which Goldman Sachs says will be “ultimately a net positive”, although its benefits will “accrue unevenly across the ecosystem”.

Primary ticketing platforms are best positioned to capitalise, “given their control of inventory through contractual relationships”, while secondary markets will face a “balanced mix” of opportunities and risks.

Potential benefits are easier discovery, smoother purchasing processes, and improved sell-through rates and venue utilisation, “particularly for smaller concerts”, authors say.

However, risks include questions over price transparency, pressure on ancillary/advertising monetisation, and the potential emergence of AI platforms as “toll collectors” that charge for preferred placement.

**Looking ahead  
**Ultimately, while the industry is not immune to economic downturns, the report finds that the effects are less prevalent on live than on other entertainment spending.

Historically, however, the industry has “experienced delayed reactions to periods of economic weakness”, partly because tickets are sold in advance, and fears around demand can trigger supply shocks.

The report projects that the industry will become “more cyclical” over time and “more sensitive to macroeconomic conditions”, indicating that the sustained success of the post-pandemic period may have its limits.
