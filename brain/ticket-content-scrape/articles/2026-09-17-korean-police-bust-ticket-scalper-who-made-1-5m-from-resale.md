# Korean police bust ticket scalper who made €1.5m from resale

## Summary

South Korean police arrested and indicted a man accused of reselling nearly 9,000 concert and sports tickets over almost eight years (Sept. 2018-April 2026), netting roughly ₩2.5 billion (~€1.5M), with some tickets resold at up to 30x face value. Authorities estimate about two-thirds of the proceeds came from resale of tickets to a single K-pop act's shows.

### Key Points
- ~8,967 tickets purchased and resold over nearly 8 years; some marked up as much as 30x face value.
- Total illicit proceeds estimated at ~₩2.5B (~€1.5M).
- Roughly two-thirds of proceeds tied to resale of one K-pop act's concert tickets.
- Case brought by Gyeonggi Bukbu Provincial Police Agency (north of Seoul).

### Highlights
- Long-running, high-volume scalping operation (nearly 9,000 tickets, 8 years) shows sustained enforcement gaps closed only after years of activity.
- Markups up to 30x face value on high-demand (K-pop) inventory.

### Industry Problem
Illustrates the persistent global gap between primary ticket pricing and secondary-market extraction on high-demand events, and that criminal enforcement against bulk resale often lags years behind the activity — relevant context for both secondary-market dynamics and resale-enforcement policy trends.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor international resale-enforcement actions and bot/bulk-purchase mitigation approaches (queue integrity, purchase limits, identity verification) as an input to Etix's own anti-scalping controls on high-demand onsales; no new mechanism described in this item to adopt directly.
