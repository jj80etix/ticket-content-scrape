# What legal risks face festivals in a future of extreme weather?

## Summary

Following a summer of European festival cancellations from extreme heat and storms, legal and insurance experts tell IQ that festivals have "very little manoeuvrability" against government shutdown orders, and insurers are growing more reluctant to cover live events against weather risk.

### Key Points
- A wave of 2026 European festival cancellations (e.g., Defqon.1) was driven by extreme heat and storms.
- Legal experts say organizers have limited recourse to challenge authorities' cancellation orders.
- Insurers are reportedly pulling back appetite for weather-related event cancellation coverage.
- Signals a growing operational/legal risk category for event organizers and, downstream, ticketing partners handling cancellations and refunds.

### Highlights
- Summer 2026 saw multiple major European festival shutdowns tied to extreme weather.
- Insurers are described as increasingly reluctant to underwrite cancellation risk in the sector.
- Legal experts frame organizers' leverage against shutdown orders as minimal.

### Industry Problem
Extreme-weather cancellations are becoming a recurring operational and legal exposure for event organizers, compounded by shrinking insurance coverage — a structural pain point for the live-events/ticketing supply chain, not just a one-off incident.

### Proposed Solution
The article doesn't prescribe a fix; worth monitoring how insurers and organizers adapt (e.g., new weather-contingency clauses or parametric coverage), and whether ticketing platforms are asked to build more flexible, climate-driven refund/rebooking tooling as this risk grows.
