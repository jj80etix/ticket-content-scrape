# StubHub Sales Surge 34% as World Cup Powers Strong Q2

## Summary

StubHub posted a sharp Q2 rebound, with gross merchandise sales up 34% to $3.1B and a swing to $14.6M net profit (from a $53.8M loss a year earlier), driven largely by record World Cup demand. The company raised its full-year 2026 GMS outlook to $10.1-10.3B and has cut debt by $1.1B over the past 12 months, improving leverage to 3.0x adjusted EBITDA.

### Key Points
- Q2 GMS: $3.1B, up 34% YoY from $2.3B; revenue up 33% to $573.1M.
- Swung to $14.6M net profit vs. a $53.8M loss in Q2 2025; adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled to $105.7M (+94% YoY).
- Free cash flow surged to $309.7M from $9.7M a year earlier; ended the quarter with $1.7B cash on hand.
- Raised full-year 2026 GMS guidance to $10.1B-$10.3B; maintained adjusted EBITDA guidance of $400-420M.
- Net leverage improved to 3.0x trailing EBITDA (from 4.5x at end of 2025) after $1.1B in debt reduction over 12 months, including $200M so far in 2026.

### Highlights
- 34% GMS growth and a swing to profitability show the secondary market's largest player converting a marquee live-sports calendar (World Cup) into durable financial strength, not just a one-quarter spike.
- Full-year guidance raise ($10.1-10.3B GMS) signals StubHub management expects the demand and margin trend to persist beyond the World Cup quarter.
- Deleveraging (4.5x → 3.0x EBITDA) improves StubHub's financial flexibility for further market investment or M&A.

### Industry Problem
Secondary-market volume and platform economics are increasingly driven by marquee, calendar-concentrated demand events (World Cup, major tours) rather than steady baseline resale activity, which rewards platforms with scale and marketing reach over smaller competitors.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor StubHub's post-World Cup GMS trajectory into Q3/Q4 to see whether the growth persists at baseline demand levels or reverts closer to prior-year run rates — that trend line is the clearer signal of underlying secondary-market health than the World Cup quarter alone.

StubHub posted a sharp jump in second-quarter sales and returned to profitability as strong demand for live events — led by a record-setting World Cup — helped drive growth across its resale marketplace.

The ticketing company reported $3.1 billion in gross merchandise sales for the quarter ended June 30, up 34% from $2.3 billion a year earlier. Revenue climbed 33% to $573.1 million. StubHub also swung to a $14.6 million net profit, compared with a $53.8 million loss in the year-ago quarter. Adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled to $105.7 million, up 94% year over year, while free cash flow surged to $309.7 million from just $9.7 million in Q2 2025.

“The second quarter demonstrated strong demand for live events, highlighted by a record-setting World Cup,” StubHub founder and CEO Eric Baker said in a press release. “Our results reflect StubHub’s leadership in the resale market and our ability to deliver these experiences to fans at scale.”

The company ended the quarter with $1.7 billion in cash and cash equivalents and said it has cut debt by $1.1 billion over the past 12 months, including $200 million in reductions so far this year.
