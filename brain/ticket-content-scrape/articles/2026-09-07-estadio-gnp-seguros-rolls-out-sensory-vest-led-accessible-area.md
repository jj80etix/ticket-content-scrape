# Estadio GNP Seguros rolls out sensory vest-led accessible area

## Summary

Mexico's Estadio GNP Seguros (promoted by insurer GNP Seguros, operated by OCESA) has launched a dedicated "Zone for People with Hearing Disabilities," giving deaf and hard-of-hearing fans sensory vests plus live Mexican Sign Language (LSM) interpretation at concerts. The area debuted at Yuridia's shows on 3–4 September, expanding on OCESA's existing sensory-vest program to a fuller accessible-seating/service zone.

### Key Points
- New zone combines sensory vests (haptic feedback of music) with in-venue LSM sign-language interpreters.
- Launched at Mexican singer Yuridia's 3–4 September shows.
- Builds on OCESA's pre-existing, venue-wide sensory vest scheme rather than introducing the technology from scratch.
- Article is paywalled beyond the intro — rollout scope (permanent fixture vs. per-event) not confirmed in the free excerpt.

### Highlights
- Concrete accessibility feature: a named, purpose-built zone (not a generic ADA seating block) combining assistive tech (sensory vests) with live human interpretation (LSM).
- Backed/branded by the venue's naming-rights sponsor (GNP Seguros, an insurer), suggesting a sponsorship-funded model for accessibility investment.
- Debuted at a single artist's shows (Yuridia, 3–4 Sept) — scale/permanence beyond that engagement is unconfirmed.

### Industry Problem
Deaf and hard-of-hearing patrons are largely underserved by standard venue accessibility offerings, which tend to focus on physical/mobility accommodations rather than sensory access to the music itself — a gap in both the fan experience and the accessible-ticketing/seating data platforms typically expose.

### Proposed Solution
OCESA's answer is a combination of assistive hardware (sensory vests) and staffed sign-language interpretation tied to specific shows/zones. For a primary ticketing platform, the trigger to watch is whether this kind of service becomes a bookable seating/accommodation category (vs. a manual venue add-on) — an opening for Etix to build structured accessible-service selection (e.g., LSM interpreter requests, sensory-vest zone seating) directly into the checkout and seating-map flow rather than leaving it to day-of-show venue operations.
