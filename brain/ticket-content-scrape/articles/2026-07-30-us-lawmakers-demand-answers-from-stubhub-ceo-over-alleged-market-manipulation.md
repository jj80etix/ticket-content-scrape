# US lawmakers ‘demand answers’ from StubHub CEO over alleged ‘market manipulation’

## Summary

The US House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform sent StubHub CEO Eric Baker a letter demanding answers over alleged "market manipulation," after SEC filings revealed Baker is part-owner and managing partner of Andro Capital, a StubHub reseller that has made "millions of dollars in profit" through the platform, plus a related affiliate, Colloquy Capital. The letter is the latest in a string of 2026 legal and regulatory actions against StubHub, including a $5m class action, a $10m FTC settlement, and a UK CMA fine over hidden fees.

### Key Points
- Ranking Member Robert Garcia (House Oversight Committee) demands CEO Eric Baker respond in writing by Thursday, August 6, 2026.
- SEC filings show Baker is part-owner/managing partner of reseller Andro Capital, and the committee is also probing affiliate Colloquy Capital, both StubHub-linked.
- Additional SEC filings show StubHub owed over $3m (€2.6m) as of Q1 2026.
- Prior 2026 actions against StubHub: a $5m class action over deceptive practices/cancelled World Cup tickets, a $10m FTC settlement over undisclosed fees, and a ~£1.5m UK CMA fine/refund order covering 50,000+ fans over hidden fees.

### Highlights
- Committee deadline for Baker's written response: Thursday, August 6, 2026.
- SEC filings confirm Baker's ownership stake in reseller Andro Capital and ties to affiliate Colloquy Capital.
- StubHub owed >$3m (€2.6m) per Q1 2026 SEC filings.
- 2026 penalty history: $10m FTC settlement (undisclosed fees) and ~£1.5m UK CMA fine/refund (50,000+ fans).

### Industry Problem
Congressional scrutiny alleges StubHub's own CEO holds an undisclosed financial stake in a broker profiting from the scalping activity the platform is supposed to police, compounding a pattern of fee-transparency violations already penalized by the FTC and UK CMA — evidence of a structural conflict-of-interest and trust problem in secondary-market platform governance.

### Proposed Solution
The article describes no remedy beyond the committee's demand for disclosure; for an independent primary platform like Etix, this reinforces the value of keeping any resale/marketplace operations transparent and free of insider broker relationships, and warrants monitoring how this investigation and related fee-disclosure enforcement reshape resale-market trust and regulatory expectations.

## US lawmakers ‘demand answers’ from StubHub CEO over alleged ‘market manipulation’

Federal authorities say they are "investigating" Eric Baker's ties to a brokerage that received money from StubHub, according to SEC filings

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/StubHub-1.png)

Federal lawmakers in the US have written to StubHub CEO Eric Baker expressing concern he “manipulated markets for his own benefit” after his involvement in one of the platform’s resellers was [exposed](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/stubhub-named-in-us-class-action-over-deceptive-practices/) in SEC filings this month.

In a recent [letter](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_mr_eric_hbakerceostubhubholdingsinc.pdf) to Baker, the US House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform probed Baker’s involvement in Andro Capital, a known StubHub reseller. SEC filings confirm that the StubHub CEO is also part-owner and managing partner of Andro, which has made “millions of dollars in profit through StubHub,” according to the letter.

Additionally, the committee is investigating the involvement of a separate Andro affiliate, Colloquy Capital, which is also owned by StubHub. Additional SEC filings show StubHub owes over $3 million (€2.6m) in Q1 2026.

With the letter, Ranking Member Robert Garcia said: “Reports that StubHub’s CEO may have profited from a ticket brokerage while running one of the nation’s largest ticket marketplaces are deeply troubling. If true, this is a blatant conflict of interest that raises serious questions about whether consumers were treated fairly. We are demanding answers because the American people deserve transparency.”

The committee’s Democrats requested information on the nature of the relationships between StubHub, Andro, and Colloquy, including listing, pricing, and fulfilment practices, asking Baker to respond in writing by next Thursday (6 August).

“ [Reports](https://www.cbc.ca/news/investigates/stubhub-marketplace-mass-scalper-sec-filings-9.7265230) suggest that you have financed and profited from the very scalpers that are locking fans out of affordable ticket sales in the first place, and that you are further ripping off consumers and smaller sellers by giving yourself preferential treatment on the StubHub platform,” Garcia wrote to Baker. “Oversight Democrats are investigating whether you have manipulated markets for your own benefit, while Americans are left paying higher costs in an unfair system.”

> Earlier this month, Baker and StubHub were named in a $5m class action lawsuit for alleged ties to large-scale scalpers

The committee’s demand is the latest in a recent series of legal and regulatory strains for StubHub.

Earlier this month, Baker and StubHub were named in a [$5 million class action lawsuit](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/stubhub-named-in-us-class-action-over-deceptive-practices/) for alleged ties to large-scale scalpers and “deceptive practices”. The suit represents an undisclosed number of fans in the wake of multiple reports that StubHub recently cancelled significant numbers of World Cup tickets in the US without notifying their putative holders.

StubHub was also recently forced to [pay $10m](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/04/stubhub-fined-10-million-in-undisclosed-fee-row/) (€8.52m) to settle charges brought by the US Federal Trade Commission, after the regulator found the company violated federal law by advertising ticket prices without clearly disclosing mandatory fees upfront.

Over in the UK, the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) ordered StubHub to [refund over 50,000 fans](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/06/stubhub-fined-900000-by-uk-regulator-over-hidden-fees/) and fined the company for applying hidden transaction fees, amounting to a total of almost £1.5m (€1.74m).

Last month, *IQ* also revealed that three-quarters of tickets listed for UK arena concerts on StubHub UK, operated as a separate entity from StubHub US by StubHub International, came from just [three large-scale sellers](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/06/exclusive-three-quarters-of-stubhub-uk-arena-listings-from-three-sellers-research-finds/).

The platform was founded in 2000 and operates as StubHub in North America and Viagogo in the rest of the world.
