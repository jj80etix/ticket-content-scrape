# Alanis Morissette Accuses Longtime Live Nation Israel Executive of Extortion in Tour Manager Lawsuit

## Summary

Alanis Morissette has sued her former temporary tour manager, Keren Urinov — a longtime Live Nation Israel operations executive — alleging civil extortion, fraud, and negligent misrepresentation after a UK airport drug-related incident in 2025. Morissette claims Urinov threatened to falsely implicate her in drug trafficking unless she agreed to a 15-year, full-time employment deal; the complaint does not allege any Live Nation involvement in the underlying conduct.

### Key Points
- Filed in U.S. District Court, Central District of California; Morissette seeks $75,000+ in compensatory damages plus punitive damages and a jury trial.
- Dispute stems from a July 2025 UK Border Force bag seizure Urinov allegedly claimed as her own, followed by demand letters threatening to report Morissette for drug trafficking.
- Urinov allegedly demanded a 15-year tour-director employment contract and U.S. visa sponsorship to drop the threats.
- Complaint also alleges Urinov concealed a prior Ecuador drug-smuggling arrest during hiring and made a costly hotel-booking error.
- Urinov filed a separate UK High Court claim against Morissette on Aug. 19; neither Urinov nor Live Nation had responded to TicketNews as of publication.

Alanis Morissette has sued her former tour manager Keren Urinov, a longtime Israeli live entertainment executive who spent years running operations for Live Nation in the country, accusing her of attempting to extort a 15-year employment deal by threatening to falsely implicate the singer in drug trafficking.

The lawsuit, filed Tuesday in U.S. District Court for the Central District of California, accuses Urinov of civil extortion, fraud and negligent misrepresentation. Morissette is seeking more than $75,000 in compensatory damages, punitive damages and a jury trial. The complaint is embedded at the end of the article.

The allegations center on Urinov’s brief tenure as temporary tour manager for Morissette in 2025 and an incident involving a bag seized by British Border Force officials while the touring party was traveling through the United Kingdom.

But Urinov also has a significant history elsewhere in the concert business. Billboard, [which first reported the lawsuit](https://ca.billboard.com/business/legal/alanis-morissette-blackmail-lawsuit-tour-manager), identified Urinov as a former head of Israeli operations for Live Nation who spent nine years in the role.

TicketNews emailed Live Nation on Tuesday seeking confirmation of Urinov’s employment dates, whether she remained employed by or otherwise affiliated with Live Nation when she joined Morissette’s tour, and whether Live Nation or any affiliate played a role in recommending or introducing her to Morissette’s organization. ## Airport Incident Leads to Alleged 15-Year Demand

According to Morissette’s complaint, she and members of her touring party were preparing to fly from Farnborough Airport in the United Kingdom to Spain on July 12, 2025 when British Border Force officials flagged a bag for inspection.

Urinov allegedly identified the bag as hers.

The lawsuit says Urinov subsequently told Morissette and other members of the team that officials had arrested her and issued a seizure notice alleging the bag contained marijuana and xylazine. According to Morissette, Urinov told the group that she admitted to Border Force officials that both the bag and its contents belonged to her and showed them an Israeli medical marijuana license.

The complaint says the bag was returned and Urinov was released. When she returned to the United Kingdom the following day, Border Force officials allegedly detained her for additional questioning. Morissette says Urinov later told the touring party that she again acknowledged that the bag and alleged drugs belonged to her.

Morissette alleges the dispute escalated after the tour ended.

According to the complaint, Urinov and her attorney sent a series of letters threatening litigation in the United Kingdom and demanding that Morissette tell British authorities that the alleged drugs actually belonged to the singer.

The lawsuit claims Urinov proposed that Morissette could put “an end to the matter” by entering into a 15-year, full-time employment agreement under which Urinov would work as a tour director and receive corresponding income, annual increases and bonuses.

Urinov allegedly also demanded that Morissette retain an immigration attorney to obtain a visa allowing her to work in the United States, with additional compensation owed if a visa could not be secured.

After Morissette rejected those demands, the singer alleges Urinov’s attorney sent another letter implying that Urinov would report Morissette to British authorities for drug trafficking in addition to suing her unless she agreed to the requested employment and compensation.

The demand letters are not attached as exhibits to the federal complaint, meaning TicketNews has not independently reviewed the correspondence and the filing reflects Morissette’s characterization of what the letters said.

## Complaint Alleges Earlier Drug Arrest Was Not Disclosed

Morissette’s fraud claims rely in part on another allegation that received less attention in initial reports about the case.

According to the complaint, Urinov told members of Morissette’s touring party that she and a friend had been arrested several years earlier for allegedly smuggling drugs inside a surfboard in a Latin American country. Morissette claims Urinov said she was released on bail and subsequently fled the country illegally.

Later in the complaint, Morissette identifies the country as Ecuador.

That history was relevant to Urinov’s hiring, Morissette argues, because Urinov had allegedly told the singer’s team during her job interview that there was nothing in her background that would interfere with obtaining visas or traveling internationally.

Morissette alleges Urinov never disclosed the Ecuador episode before being hired.

The complaint also attacks Urinov’s performance as tour manager, alleging that one hotel-booking mistake unnecessarily cost Morissette nearly $70,000.

Those allegations form the basis for Morissette’s separate fraud and negligent misrepresentation claims. She says she relied on Urinov’s representations about her background when entering into an employment agreement with her in California.

The lawsuit additionally says Morissette has already paid some of Urinov’s legal fees, although it does not explain what those fees related to or why Morissette paid them.

TicketNews emailed Morissette attorneys Howard E. King and Heather L. Pickerell seeking additional information about how Urinov was hired, whether Live Nation or another promoter recommended or introduced her, whether Morissette’s team understood her to remain affiliated with Live Nation at the time, and why Morissette paid some of Urinov’s legal expenses.

TicketNews also asked whether Morissette’s attorneys would provide the demand correspondence described in the complaint and whether they had independent documentation corroborating the alleged Ecuador arrest beyond what Urinov purportedly told the touring party.

No response had been received as of publication.

Urinov has not presented her version of the dispute in the U.S. complaint. Billboard reported that a law firm representing Urinov did not respond to its request for comment Tuesday. The publication also reported that Urinov filed her own claim against Morissette in the U.K. High Court on August 19, before Morissette brought the California action. Court records reportedly categorize that matter as a media and communications claim, but details of Urinov’s allegations were not immediately available.

Morissette is asking the California federal court for compensatory damages exceeding $75,000, punitive damages and any additional relief the court deems appropriate.
