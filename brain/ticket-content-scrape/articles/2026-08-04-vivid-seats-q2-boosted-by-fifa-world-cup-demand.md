# Vivid Seats Q2 boosted by FIFA World Cup demand

## Summary

Vivid Seats' Q2 2026 net loss narrowed sharply to $14.3 million (from $263.3 million a year earlier, which included a $320 million impairment charge), even as revenue continued to soften year-over-year ($129.8M vs. $143.6M). Marketplace orders fell to 1.8 million (from 2.1 million), but management points to FIFA World Cup demand lifting results above expectations.

### Key Points
- Q2 net loss $14.3M vs. $263.3M prior year (prior year included a $320M impairment charge); H1 net loss $15.2M vs. $336M.
- Revenue down year-over-year: $129.8M vs. $143.6M in Q2; H1 $255.6M vs. $307.6M.
- Marketplace GOV $659.4M (vs. $685.5M in Q2 2025); marketplace orders 1.8M (vs. 2.1M); resale orders 84,000 (vs. 97,000).
- CEO Lawrence Fey attributes the quarter beating expectations to FIFA World Cup-driven demand.

### Highlights
- Net loss improvement is driven almost entirely by the absence of last year's $320M impairment charge, not underlying revenue growth — revenue and order volume both declined y/y.
- FIFA World Cup demand is credited as the swing factor that pushed results above the company's own expectations despite the broader revenue decline.

### Industry Problem
Secondary-marketplace financial performance is increasingly tied to mega-events (FIFA World Cup) rather than steady organic growth, illustrating how volatile and event-dependent resale-marketplace revenue can be even as headline losses shrink.

### Proposed Solution
No product action indicated; monitor whether Vivid Seats' underlying (ex-World Cup) demand trend stabilizes in H2 2026 as a read on baseline secondary-market health.

**Vivid Seats has seen its net loss shrink for the second quarter of 2026 compared to the same period last year, though revenue continues to be down.**

The online ticket marketplace posted a net loss of $14.3m (£10.6m/€12.4m) in the second quarter of 2026 compared to $263.3m for Q2 last year.

However, revenues continue to be down on 2025, with the total amounting to $129.8m for the three months ended June 30. This is compared to $143.6m during the same period the year prior. For the first six months of 2026, revenues amounted to $255.6m compared to $307.6m this time last year.

The net loss has shrunk significantly due to the improving loss from operations, which has gone from $331.7m in the first three months of 2025 to $6.4m in 2026. For the first six months, net loss reached $15.2m compared to $336m last year. In Q2 last year, Vivid Seats recorded a $320m impairment charge which [affected the operation’s financials](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/03/vivid-seats-expects-further-declines-after-429m-full-year-net-loss/) and is not recorded in 2026.

Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation) equalled $12.6m for the second quarter, while marketplace GOV (gross order value) totalled $659.4m. This is compared to $14.4m and $685.5m in the second quarter of 2025, respectively. For the first six months of 2026, marketplace GOV totalled $1.27bn, a decrease from $1.5bn for the same period in 2025.

Marketplace orders amounted to 1.8 million in the second quarter of 2026, compared to 2.1 million in 2025. At the end of the first six months, marketplace orders equalled 3.5 million, down from 4.5 million the year prior. Resale orders totalled 84,000 compared to 97,000 for the second quarter, while resale orders amounted to 166,000 for the six months, a drop from 202,000.

Total assets amounted to $688.8m as of June 30 compared to $636.9m on December 31, 2025.

However, bosses are positive as the company’s second quarter results surpassed expectations, in part driven by demand for tickets to the FIFA World Cup, which took place across the US, Mexico and Canada, and concluded last month.

“We are encouraged by the progress we’ve made through the first half of the year. Our second quarter results exceeded expectations as we delivered sequential growth driven by the extraordinary demand created by the FIFA World Cup,” said Lawrence Fey, chief executive of Vivid Seats.

“We continue to successfully execute against our strategic objectives. With leading technology, a compelling value proposition, differentiated data, and a relentless focus on operational excellence, we remain confident in our ability to drive long-term shareholder value.”
