# California festival claims 99% ticketing fee cut with self-made platform

## Summary

San Diego's Same Same But Different Festival (SSBD) cut its lowest ticket tier by $60 for 2027 by moving entirely onto ShopTickets, a self-built Shopify-based ticketing app that it says brought per-ticket fees down from ~$56 to $0.75 — a ~99% reduction the organizers chose to pass to fans rather than keep as margin. ShopTickets (in which SSBD holds an equity stake) is being opened to other organizers at $1.50/ticket or $49/month, supports reserved seating and interactive seat maps, and explicitly targets events wanting to avoid long-term ticketing contracts — a direct, concrete whitespace/fee-disruption example from inside the industry rather than a vendor press release.

### Key Points
- SSBD's 2027 Community Tier GA price drops to $295 (from $355), driven by cutting ticketing fees from ~$56 to $0.75/ticket.
- ShopTickets is a Shopify-native ticketing app (reserved seating, interactive seat maps, QR scanning) built by the festival's own founder, who holds an equity stake.
- Opened to other organizers at $1.50/ticket or $49/month flat, explicitly pitched as an alternative to "long-term ticketing contracts."
- Positioned to scale from GA festivals to arena-scale reserved seating.

### Highlights
- Per-ticket fee cut from ~$56 to $0.75 (~99%) by building on Shopify's infrastructure rather than a traditional ticketing platform.
- Self-serve pricing for other organizers: $1.50/ticket or $49/month flat, no long-term contract required.
- Already supports reserved seating with interactive seat maps, positioned for arena-scale use.

### Industry Problem
Organizers increasingly view traditional per-ticket fees as "disconnected from the actual cost of selling a ticket" (the founder's own words) and are willing to build and even commercialize their own low-cost alternative rather than accept standard platform economics or long-term exclusivity contracts — direct evidence of whitespace that incumbent platforms aren't filling.

### Proposed Solution
This is itself the "build" case study: a near-zero marginal-cost, contract-free ticketing layer with reserved-seating support. For Etix, it's a competitive signal worth tracking closely — both as a low-end disruption threat for price-sensitive independent organizers and as a reminder that fee transparency/flexibility (not just features) is becoming a competitive axis; worth evaluating whether a lower-friction, shorter-commitment tier could preempt this kind of defection.

SSBD organisers say a home-made Shopify ticketing platform can all but cut out ticketing fees while reducing a ticket tier by $60

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/SSBD.png)

Same Same But Different © Same Same But Different

A California festival has said it has lowered the price of one of its ticketing tiers by $60, using a self-made ticketing platform on Shopify, which it has reduced ticketing fees by around 99%.

San Diego-based Same Same But Different Festival (SSBD) has announced its 2027 Community Tier, in which general admission presale tickets are priced at $295, compared to $355 last year. Other ticket tiers remain more expensive than this.

The fan-owned festival said this has been achieved through the use of its own ticketing platform made by festival organisers on Shopify, called ShopTickets, which has allowed it to bring the ticketing fee to $0.75.

“Last year, a GA ticket carried approximately $56 in ticketing fees. With ShopTickets, the ticketing fee included in the new $295 Community Tier is just $0.75. Rather than keeping the difference as an additional festival margin, SSBD is passing those savings back to fans,” organisers said.

Festival founder Brad Sweet, who also worked on building ShopTickets, told *IQ:* “For the 2027 festival, all of our SSBD ticket types and tiers will be sold through ShopTickets. We’re not splitting inventory between ShopTickets and a traditional ticketing platform, so the lower ticketing fee applies across the event.”

SSBD organisers also said the tool can also be used by other events “without long-term ticketing contracts”, due to being built on Shopify’s public infrastructure. They said it can support “festivals, promoters, venues and touring events, from general admission to arena-scale reserved seating”, SSBD said.

The publicly accessible ShopTickets page gives venues looking to use the service the option of paying $1.50 per ticket or $49 per month.

Asked how indoor or seated venues could use the service, Sweet told *IQ:* “ ShopTickets handles reserved seating much like the ticketing platforms people are already familiar with. An arena can have a full interactive seating map where customers can view the entire venue, zoom into individual sections and select specific seats.

> “Ticketing fees have gotten disconnected from the actual cost of selling a ticket”

“Once a customer selects a seat, ShopTickets temporarily holds it while they complete checkout so someone else can’t purchase the same seat. After checkout, the customer receives a ticket tied specifically to that seat.

“At the venue, when the ticket is scanned, the scanner also displays the customer’s section, row and seat, which helps venue staff direct people to the right location. There are additional tools on the organiser side for managing seating maps, inventory, holds and the other operational needs that come with reserved-seat events.”

Images on ShopTickets’ page show physical tickets labelled with seat numbers and QR codes for indoor events. The page also shows examples of seat maps that it says are available for venues, including stadiums, to use in their customisable Shopify ‘shopfront’.

SSBD disclosed it owns an equity stake in ShopTickets. Other Shopify-based event ticketing services exist, notably GM Event Ticketing, made by Guest Manager, which says it has vended around 1.5 million tickets to its events.

Asked to comment on the use of Shopify for the feature, Sweet told *IQ:* “ Other organisers use ShopTickets essentially the same way they would use another ticketing platform, except it operates directly through their Shopify store. We already have a pretty wide range of use cases, from festivals selling thousands of GA and VIP tickets to small jazz venues selling reserved tables and seats for multiple shows in a single night.”

He added: “ They don’t pay SSBD to use it. ShopTickets is its own platform, and the $0.75 per-ticket fee is paid directly to the ShopTickets app through the organiser’s Shopify store.”
