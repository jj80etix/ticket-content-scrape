# Infantino requested to appear before House committee over World Cup ticketing

## Summary

Rep. Jamie Raskin has asked FIFA president Gianni Infantino to testify before the House Judiciary Committee over World Cup ticketing practices, alleging illegal price gouging (finals tickets surging from a promised $1,550 cap to nearly $33,000) and deceptive dynamic pricing, alongside separate active investigations by the New York and New Jersey Attorneys General.

### Key Points
- Raskin demands all FIFA-Trump administration communications on ticketing, pricing mechanisms, and consumer complaints.
- Alleges FIFA can reassign ticketholders to worse seats post-purchase at its own discretion.
- NY and NJ AGs subpoenaed FIFA in May over pricing/transparency; NJ's AG says its probe continues post-tournament.
- Cited complaints include misleading stadium seat maps, dynamic pricing, manufactured scarcity, and a new higher-priced ticket tier added after millions of tickets had already sold.

### Highlights
- Congressional request for FIFA testimony on World Cup ticketing, alongside ongoing NY/NJ AG subpoenas and investigations.
- Alleged price surge from a promised $1,550 cap to nearly $33,000 for finals tickets.

### Industry Problem
Large-scale dynamic pricing and post-purchase seat reassignment on a marquee primary-market event drew bipartisan-adjacent political and multi-state AG scrutiny, showing how quickly aggressive pricing practices can escalate into regulatory and legislative exposure for a primary ticketing operation.

### Proposed Solution
No vendor solution is proposed in the article; the takeaway for an independent primary platform is to monitor how this scrutiny shapes disclosure and pricing-transparency expectations, which could spread into state junk-fee rules and inform Etix's own pricing-communication practices.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**Leading Democratic lawmaker Jamie Raskin has written to FIFA president Gianni Infantino requesting that he appear before the House Judiciary Committee to question his relationship with United States president Donald Trump.**

Raskin is pushing FIFA to provide all of its records on its contacts with Trump, his administration and the Trump Organization in light of the organisation allegedly “employing illegal price gouging and fraudulent sales tactics for the World Cup”.

“The original North American bid promised a maximum finals ticket price of $1,550 – FIFA surged finals tickets to nearly $33,000 per seat, more than 20 times the promised price,” he wrote.

“FIFA also introduced dynamic pricing, leading to sharply higher prices for many games.

“Even after a fan buys a ticket, FIFA may, at its own discretion, move the ticketholder into a worse seat in a worse section.

“This is plainly unlawful, anti-consumer conduct.”

As a result, Raskin has requested that Infantino produce “all communications with the United States government relating to your ticketing practices, including consumer complaints, discussion regarding variable or other pricing mechanisms, third-party sellers, and ticket prices.”

FIFA’s ticketing operation for the recent World Cup held in the United States, Canada, and Mexico from June 11 to July 19 has received significant criticism.

In May, the Attorneys General of New York and New Jersey launched an investigation into the governing body’s ticketing practices and sent [subpoenas](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/05/fifa-subpoenaed-over-world-cup-ticketing-prices/) regarding the organisation’s pricing and transparency.

The Attorney General of New Jersey has said that the state will continue its [investigation](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/07/new-jersey-ag-to-continue-investigation-into-fifas-world-cup-ticketing/) into the ticketing practices following the conclusion of the tournament.

The AGs cited complaints from fans, including some who felt misled by stadium maps over the location of tickets, FIFA’s use of variable pricing, the perception of scarcity in available tickets, as well as FIFA’s invention of a [more expensive category](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/04/fifa-adds-new-more-expensive-world-cup-ticket-tier/) after millions of tickets had already been sold.

Raskin has alleged that FIFA “has taken its newfound favored status in the Trump Administration as a sign that it may rip off its consumers”.
