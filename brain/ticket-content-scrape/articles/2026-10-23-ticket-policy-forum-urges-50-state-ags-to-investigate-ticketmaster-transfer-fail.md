# Ticket Policy Forum Urges 50 State AGs to Investigate Ticketmaster Transfer Failures

## Summary

The Ticket Policy Forum (TPF), a trade group representing major resale marketplaces (StubHub, SeatGeek, Vivid Seats, TickPick, Gametime, Events Ticket Center), sent an Oct. 19 letter urging all 50 state attorneys general to investigate Ticketmaster's Oct. 2 ticket-transfer failures, which left fans at shows for Harry Styles, Bruno Mars and others unable to accept, cancel or retrieve transferred tickets. TPF argues the incident exposes a structural conflict: Ticketmaster both runs its own resale marketplace and controls the transfer infrastructure competing marketplaces depend on to deliver tickets, and is asking regulators to determine whether Ticketmaster-ecosystem transactions were treated more favorably during the outage. The request lands amid an already-active antitrust backdrop — an April jury verdict found Live Nation/Ticketmaster liable for unlawfully maintaining a primary-ticketing monopoly.

### Key Points
- TPF's Oct. 19 letter to all 50 state AGs seeks disclosure of the Oct. 2 transfer failure's cause, scope, and remediation plan; eight specific requests including per-state transaction counts and any customer-data exposure.
- TPF says the incident caused "chaos and lots of refunds, replacements and pointed blame" among member marketplaces but has no hard figures on affected transactions or costs.
- Structural concern: tickets resold on independent marketplaces often still depend on transfer through the original issuer's (Ticketmaster's) system — a dependency not unique to Ticketmaster (AXS and Paciolan run similar systems) but acute here given Ticketmaster's dual role as competitor and infrastructure gatekeeper.
- No finding yet that Ticketmaster favored its own transactions during the outage — TPF says that's exactly what it wants investigators to determine.
- Context: an April jury verdict already found Live Nation/Ticketmaster liable for unlawfully maintaining a primary-ticketing monopoly; TPF members separately sued D.C. (Oct. 14) over new resale price/fee caps.
- Ticketmaster had acknowledged "stuck transfers" and said most were cleared within a day, per earlier reporting; company did not respond to TPF's letter as of publication.

### Highlights
- 50-state AG letter (Oct. 19) targets Ticketmaster's Oct. 2 transfer outage, which affected fans at Harry Styles, Bruno Mars and other shows.
- TPF's central ask: determine whether Ticketmaster's own marketplace transactions were shielded from the failure while competitors' customers were not.
- Comes on top of an April jury verdict finding Live Nation/Ticketmaster liable for unlawfully maintaining a primary-ticketing monopoly.

### Industry Problem
When a primary ticketing provider controls the transfer infrastructure that competing resale marketplaces must also use, any outage in that infrastructure creates both a consumer-trust failure and a competitive-fairness question that the provider alone cannot credibly resolve.

### Proposed Solution
TPF wants state AGs to force public disclosure and equal-treatment verification; for an independent primary platform, the strategic takeaway is to treat ticket-transfer reliability as a trust-critical system (not an afterthought) and to make clear, auditable commitments about equal handling if third parties ever depend on the platform's transfer rails — turning a competitor's structural liability into a credibility advantage.

The Ticket Policy Forum (TPF), a trade group representing several of the country’s largest ticket resale marketplaces, is calling on state attorneys general nationwide to investigate Ticketmaster’s October 2 ticket-transfer failures, seeking a public accounting of the disruption and its impact on consumers and competing ticketing platforms.

In an [October 19 letter to attorneys general in all 50 states](https://ticketpolicyforum.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TPF-Letter-to-AGs-TM-Transfer-Failure-1.pdf?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), TPF Executive Director Brian Berry urged regulators to require Live Nation and Ticketmaster to disclose what caused the problems, how many transactions and customers were affected, and what the company intends to do for fans who could not access tickets they had purchased.

The letter follows [TicketNews reporting on October 3](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/ticketmaster-transfer-problems-october-2/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) documenting transfer failures affecting fans across multiple states, including customers attending concerts by Harry Styles, Bruno Mars and other artists. Fans reported being unable to accept transferred tickets, cancel pending transfers or retrieve tickets that appeared stuck between accounts.

Ticketmaster’s customer support team acknowledged the problems as they unfolded and directed some customers with imminent events to venue box offices for assistance. By the following day, the company said most pending transfers were being processed within an hour. [Ticketmaster Global President Saumil Mehta also acknowledged](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/ticketmaster-president-stuck-transfers/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) that the company was working to clear “stuck transfers.”

## Resale Marketplaces Report Refunds and Replacement Tickets

In follow-up responses to TicketNews, TPF said member marketplaces encountered customer-service and fulfillment problems during the disruption, including refunds and replacement tickets.

A TPF representative described the situation as involving “chaos and lots of refunds, replacements and pointed blame.” The organization said it has no hard figures for affected transactions, customers or missed events, and could not provide an aggregate cost for refunds or replacement tickets.

The impact on independent marketplaces is particularly relevant because transactions completed through those platforms often still depend on the original ticket issuer’s transfer infrastructure. When a ticket first issued by Ticketmaster is resold elsewhere, delivery may require the seller to transfer it through Ticketmaster’s system. If that process fails, a buyer may be unable to enter an event even though the resale purchase was completed through another company.

This dependency is not unique to Ticketmaster: other primary ticketing providers, including [AXS](https://support.axs.com/hc/en-us/articles/360031482794-What-is-Transfer-Share?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) and [Paciolan](https://www.paciolan.com/ticketing-solutions?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), operate digital ticket-transfer systems through which their originally issued tickets can be delivered to new holders. TPF’s current request, however, concerns the October 2 problems involving Ticketmaster-issued tickets, not any alleged outage at those other providers.

Ticketmaster’s [published guidance for recipients of transferred tickets](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/9715611361425-Can-I-get-a-refund-if-I-receive-a-ticket-through-Ticket-Transfer?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) directs people who bought resale tickets elsewhere to contact their original point of purchase about available refunds. That makes the independent marketplace a customer’s first stop for relief, even when the underlying delivery problem involves a different company’s system.

TPF wants regulators to establish how many customers were denied entry or missed part of an event, and what remedies will be available to affected buyers, including those who purchased from Ticketmaster competitors. Its responses did not establish which companies ultimately absorbed the costs of the reported refunds and replacement tickets.

## Questions About Differential Treatment Remain Unresolved

Beyond the consumer impact, TPF is asking attorneys general to investigate whether transfers tied to independent resale marketplaces failed at different rates or were given different priority from transactions within Ticketmaster’s own marketplace.

Asked whether its members had identified evidence of unequal treatment, TPF said that was a question it wants investigators to answer. The organization said it suspects transactions within Ticketmaster’s own ecosystem may have escaped the disruption, but offered no supporting data. There is currently no established finding that Ticketmaster favored its own transactions during the incident.

The distinction matters because Ticketmaster competes in resale while also operating transfer infrastructure that other marketplaces often must use. TPF argues that this creates competitive risks, but the letter does not show that the October 2 failure resulted from intentional conduct rather than a technology problem.

“These fans deserve answers and a clear plan to make them whole,” Berry said in announcing the letter.

## State Attorneys General Respond as Broader Scrutiny Continues

TPF’s eight requests include the incident’s technical cause and timeline, the number of transfers and events affected by state, any customer-data exposure, Ticketmaster’s internal incident classification, and the company’s plans for refunds, remediation and future public outage reporting. The group has asked regulators to make their findings public.

In its follow-up response to TicketNews, TPF said some state attorneys general’s offices had acknowledged the letter. “We are pleased that some state AG offices have been quick to respond and thank TPF for bringing this to their attention,” a representative said. “We will make ourselves available should they need further assistance from us.”

The organization did not identify the offices involved or indicate that any formal investigation had begun. An acknowledgment of a letter does not itself amount to a regulatory inquiry.

The request arrives amid separate antitrust scrutiny of Live Nation and Ticketmaster. In April, [a federal jury found the companies liable for antitrust violations](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-and-coalition-states-win-trial-against-live-nation-and?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) in a case pursued by a coalition of state attorneys general, including a finding that Ticketmaster unlawfully maintained a monopoly in primary ticketing services for major concert venues. That verdict did not address the October 2 transfer disruption or determine whether competing resale marketplaces received unequal treatment.

TPF has also pursued other ticketing-policy disputes, including an [October 14 lawsuit with four member marketplaces](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/dc-resale-act-lawsuit-ticket-marketplaces/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) challenging the District of Columbia’s new resale price and fee caps. That case concerns different legal issues from the request to investigate Ticketmaster’s transfer failures.

TicketNews requested comment from Ticketmaster on the TPF letter and sought additional information about the October 2 disruption. No response had been received as of Friday afternoon.
