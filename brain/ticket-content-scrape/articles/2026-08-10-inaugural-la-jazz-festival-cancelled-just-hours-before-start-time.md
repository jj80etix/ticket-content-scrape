# Inaugural LA Jazz Festival cancelled just hours before start time

## Summary

The LA Jazz Festival cancelled most of its multi-week program hours before its Aug. 7 start, citing public-safety and permitting issues around its flagship "Jazz on the Beach" show; artist representatives say performers were left uninformed and out of pocket.

### Key Points
- Label Leaving Records says artists were promised "big fees, big stages" and suffered real financial pressure from the late cancellation.
- Founder Martin Ludlow, a former LA city councilman, says the event will return in 2027.
- The festival's website now lists only Aug. 22-23 dates, with details "coming soon."

## Transcript

Organisers blamed public safety and permitting issues for the decision, as some artist representatives say performers are left out of pocket

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/John-Legend-and-Janelle-Monae-LA-Jazz-Festival.png)

John Legend and Janelle Monáe were set to headline the event © right: Marc Ducrest

The LA Jazz Festival has cancelled the majority of its events just hours before the multi-week event was set to begin, with organisers blaming “public safety” and “municipal cost” concerns.

The city-wide festival was due to stage headline performances by artists including John Legend and Janelle Monáe, as well as jazz artists including Ezra Collective and Nubya Garcia, between 7 and 23 August, with organisers previously having suggested an expected attendance of 250,000 fans across the period.

A message on the festival’s website now gives the dates of the event as 22-23 August, and promises more event information “coming soon”.

These dates were originally slated to host the festival’s flagship event, titled *Jazz on the Beach,* across what would have been its final weekend at Dockweiler Beach, and led by sets from Legend and Monáe. It is unclear if this is the event organisers are now hinting at for these dates.

However, in a statement provided to the *Los Angeles Times* on 7 August, festival CEO Martin Ludlow announced the event would not go ahead as planned, and referenced permitting issues specifically surrounding *Jazz on the Beach*.

> “we look forward to presenting all of the components of this incredible cultural opportunity for Los Angeles and the world in 2027”

“Due to significant, unanticipated last-minute public safety and municipal costs associated with the final permitting of the *On The Beach Festival*, we have reached the decision to unfortunately postpone this year’s inaugural LA Jazz Festival,” Ludlow told the newspaper.

The event was to be the LA Jazz Festival’s debut outing, after Ludlow announced the event in February at a press conference featuring the city’s mayor Karen Bass. Ludlow himself formerly worked as a City Councillor from 2003 to 2006, when he resigned before pleading guilty to charges of breaching the city’s campaign laws. He subsequently founded live events production company Bridge Street in 2012.

LA-based label Leaving Records said in a statement addressed to the festival: “Our artists were promised big fees, big stages with big audiences, big opportunities”, adding that the cancellation has caused “pressure and stress for a lot of our artists in this moment”. Early reports also suggest many artists had already travelled to Los Angeles for the event before learning of the cancellation.

Meanwhile, trombonist Khristian Foreman said in a social media post: “I can’t tell you how many musicians that I know personally who were going to perform, and it’s sad to see that not only did they miss out on money, because LA is not cheap, but they missed out on opportunity.”

“Although this decision was an extremely difficult one to reach, our commitment to the audiences and artists participating in the festival is to have a first-class experience worthy of the great legacy of Jazz and our great city,” Ludlow said, via *The Los Angeles Times,* adding: “We were encouraged by the enthusiastic support of the artists on the bill, the fans and our sponsors for this inaugural event, and we look forward to presenting all of the components of this incredible cultural opportunity for Los Angeles and the world in 2027 and beyond.”
