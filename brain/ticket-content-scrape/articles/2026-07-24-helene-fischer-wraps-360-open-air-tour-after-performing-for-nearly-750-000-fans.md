# Helene Fischer Wraps 360° Open Air Tour After Performing for Nearly 750,000 Fans

## Summary

Helene Fischer concluded her 2026 360° Open Air Tour with a sold-out, 71,000-capacity finale at Munich's Allianz Arena, capping a 15-show European stadium run that drew nearly 750,000 fans across Germany, Austria, Switzerland, and the Netherlands. The tour, produced by Live Nation GSA, featured a 360-degree stage and marked two decades since Fischer launched her career.

### Key Points
- Tour totaled roughly 750,000 attendees across 15 stadium shows in four countries.
- City-by-city attendance included Berlin (90,000), Hamburg (120,000 across two shows), Frankfurt (90,000 across two shows), Cologne (85,000), Vienna (57,500), and Stuttgart (52,000, sold out).
- Production used a 360-degree in-the-round stage design with a 20-dancer troupe and 12-piece live band.
- Each show ran over two and a half hours with a career-spanning setlist.
- Live Nation GSA CEO Marek Lieberberg praised the production as delivering on the tour's original ambition.

## Transcript

Helene Fischer has wrapped up her 2026 360° Open Air Tour with a sold-out performance at Munich’s Allianz Arena, bringing an end to a stadium run that attracted nearly 750,000 fans across Europe.

The final concert welcomed 71,000 attendees and marked the last stop on a 15-show itinerary that visited Germany, Austria, Switzerland, and the Netherlands. The tour ranks among the largest live undertakings of Fischer’s career, drawing massive audiences throughout its run and celebrating two decades since the singer launched her professional career.

The production centered on a 360-degree stage, allowing audiences to surround the performance from every angle. The design was intended to create a more immersive concert experience by positioning Fischer closer to fans throughout each stadium.

Each performance lasted more than two and a half hours and featured a career-spanning setlist of Fischer’s best-known songs. She was joined onstage by an international cast that included Thomas Seitel, a troupe of 20 dancers, and a 12-piece live band.
