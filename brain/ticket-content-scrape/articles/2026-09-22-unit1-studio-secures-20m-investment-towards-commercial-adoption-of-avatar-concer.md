# Unit1 Studio Secures $20M Investment Towards Commercial Adoption Of Avatar Concerts (And Taking Them On The Road)

## Summary

Unit1 Studio raised a $20 million round led by Balderton Capital to scale commercial adoption of its mixed-reality avatar concerts, technology it says can move productions like its KT Tunstall hologram show between venues far more cheaply than bespoke installations such as ABBA Voyage. The company completed what it calls the first-ever relocation of an avatar concert, moving the show from Hammersmith to a new London studio, and is adding senior hires including former Universal Music UK/IFPI chief John Kennedy.

### Key Points
- Unit1 Studio raises $20M (led by Balderton Capital) for avatar-concert production and touring.
- Claims to remove the need for dedicated venues (as required by ABBA Voyage, KISS Forever) via relocatable staging tech.
- Completed first-ever avatar concert relocation, moving its KT Tunstall show to a new London studio.
- New hires include ex-Universal Music UK/IFPI CEO John Kennedy and veteran avatar/VFX creators.

## Transcript

![image0](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/image0.jpeg)

The hyper-realistic avatar of KT Tunstall. The company that created it, Unit1 Studio, says it can now move avatar shows from venue to venue.

Unit1 Studio announced it secured a $20 million investment that’ll fuel its work, which consists of “creating and operating mixed reality avatar concerts.” The company, an “end-to-end avatar concert producer,” has successfully moved its flagship show from one London venue to another, effectively proving that these shows can be taken on the road.

Unit1 Studio technology removes the barriers currently associated with avatar shows like ABBA Voyage, or [KISS Forever, scheduled to open in Las Vegas in 2028](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/04/14/kiss-forever-at-pollstar-live-2026-how-pophouse-defines-the-next-era-of-kiss-in-vegas/), mainly “the eye-watering costs of development,” as the London-based studio puts it.

The aforementioned shows, for instance, required the construction of dedicated venues.

Thanks to its technology, Unit1 Studio says it has “the ability to rapidly and economically transfer avatar concert productions between venues – effectively breaking a huge barrier to market adoption of avatar concerts.”

Unit1 sees the successful move of its KT Tunstall avatar show from its former home in Hammersmith, London, to a new purpose-built studio at Tileyard London, as proof of concept. By the studio’s own admission, the move completed “the first ever relocation of an avatar concert.”

The $20 million equity investment round – led by Balderton Capital, who are joined by existing investors Mercuri, Gilston Music, and legendary artist manager Paul McGuinness – will support the work Unit1 is doing, including additional production financing “to expand the roster of concerts currently in development.”

The company also announced a few new hires, including John Kennedy, former CEO/Chairman of Universal Music UK and IFPI, joining as a non-executive director; and hyper-real avatar creators Greg Meeres Young (Planet of the Apes, Lord of the Rings) and Sefki Ibrahim (ABBA Voyage). They solidify the producer’s ability “to pioneer new developments in VFX and AI-enhanced CGI,” according to the press announcement.

It concludes, “Unit1’s shows break the ‘fourth wall’ between screen content and live experience, giving fans from the ‘TikTok & Netflix generation’ the concert experience of artists they discover from the 70s, 80s, 90s and beyond, while offering longtime fans a new and exciting way to connect with the artists they’ve always loved.”
