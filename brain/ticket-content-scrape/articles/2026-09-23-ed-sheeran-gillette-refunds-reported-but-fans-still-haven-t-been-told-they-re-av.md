# Ed Sheeran Gillette Refunds Reported, But Fans Still Haven’t Been Told They’re Available

## Summary

Fans holding tickets to Ed Sheeran's two Gillette Stadium shows this week say Ticketmaster is now granting full refunds on request — a reversal from earlier denials — but neither Ticketmaster, Gillette Stadium nor the tour has publicly announced the policy or how broadly it applies. The shift follows at least 25 refund-related complaints to the Massachusetts Attorney General's Office, up from nine previously reported, after Macklemore's removal from the tour and the subsequent exit of several other support acts.

### Key Points
- Multiple Sept. 25/26 Gillette ticketholders report successful refunds after directly contacting Ticketmaster by phone or chat; some had been denied refunds earlier.
- No official refund policy has been announced; Sheeran's tour FAQ still states sales are final with no refunds/exchanges for lineup changes.
- Ticketmaster says event organizers set refund eligibility and approved refunds return within 5–7 business days — but consumers only learn refunds are available by asking.
- Massachusetts AG complaints rose from 9 to at least 25; the office says it is reviewing complaints and applicable terms but has no further comment.
- The shows proceed as scheduled (now 8 p.m. start, changed from 5:30 p.m.) with a changed support lineup after Macklemore's exit and other acts withdrawing in solidarity.

### Highlights
- Ticketmaster is reportedly granting full refunds for Gillette Stadium Sheeran shows on request, but with no public announcement — leaving buyers who don't know to ask without a clear path to a refund.

### Industry Problem
A refund option that exists but isn't proactively communicated creates an information gap: patrons who happen to contact customer service get relief, while others relying on published terms (which still say "no refunds") do not — undermining trust and inviting regulatory complaints.

### Proposed Solution
When an organizer authorizes refunds mid-cycle, a primary ticketing platform should be able to push that change directly to affected ticketholders (email/account notification) rather than relying on inbound requests, and keep the public FAQ/terms in sync with the live refund policy in real time.

Ed Sheeran fans holding tickets for this week’s two Gillette Stadium concerts are now reporting that Ticketmaster is granting full refunds upon request, marking a significant change from earlier unsuccessful attempts by some buyers to get their money back.

What remains unclear is whether the option has been communicated to the broader population of ticketholders.

Multiple purchasers for Sheeran’s Sept. 25 and Sept. 26 concerts in Foxborough, Massachusetts, said this week that they successfully obtained refunds after contacting Ticketmaster by phone or through its customer-service chat. One customer said Gillette Stadium responded to an earlier inquiry by telling them that refunds were “now authorized,” after Ticketmaster had previously denied their request.

[Boston.com separately reported Tuesday](https://www.boston.com/news/local-news/2026/09/22/ed-sheerans-concerts-at-gillette-stadium-will-look-different-than-planned/) that fans had received full Ticketmaster refunds for the Gillette concerts but that no official refund policy had been announced. Ticketmaster told the outlet that event organizers determine refund policies.

That distinction means customers who know to ask may currently have an avenue to exit their purchase that is not apparent to buyers relying solely on the tour’s published terms or the absence of a public refund announcement.

The apparent change comes after the [Massachusetts Attorney General’s Office received at least 25 consumer complaints](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/ed-sheeran-refunds-ticket-market-softens/) involving refund requests for the Gillette concerts, the office told TicketNews last week.

That was up from at least nine complaints that had previously been reported.

The Attorney General’s Office cautioned at the time that ticket terms state that opening acts are subject to change and that changes to the support lineup do not necessarily entitle purchasers to refunds.

“We are reviewing the complaints and the applicable terms and conditions and have no further comment at this time,” the office told TicketNews Friday.

TicketNews contacted the Attorney General’s Office again this week seeking an updated complaint count and asking whether the office was aware that some Gillette customers are now receiving refunds. No additional response had been received as of publication time.

TicketNews also sought additional clarification from parties involved in the concerts about whether refunds have now been authorized generally for Ticketmaster purchasers, when that option became available and whether affected customers would be notified.

The absence of an announced policy makes it difficult to establish precisely how broad the accommodation is. The reports reviewed by TicketNews involve customers who purchased through Ticketmaster and affirmatively contacted the company seeking refunds.

Ticketmaster’s own guidance says consumers who received transferred tickets generally must return them to the original purchaser for that purchaser to exercise available refund options. Buyers who purchased resale tickets elsewhere are directed to their original point of purchase for whatever options that marketplace provides.

## Gillette Shows Move Forward With Changed Lineup

The refund issue is the latest development in a week of upheaval surrounding the remaining U.S. dates of Sheeran’s Loop Tour.

Macklemore was removed from the tour after multiple stadiums objected to his continued participation, according to promoter Louis Messina. Other performers subsequently withdrew from the tour, leaving the lineup sold to consumers substantially changed.

Sheeran resumed the tour Saturday in Philadelphia without the originally advertised support lineup [and addressed the dispute from the stage, questioning the degree of control venues should have over performers appearing inside their buildings](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/ed-sheeran-venue-controls-macklemore-loop-tour/).

Before that Philadelphia concert, TicketNews reported that some customers had begun receiving full refunds despite the event proceeding as scheduled. At the time, comparable refund availability for Gillette remained uncertain, with Massachusetts buyers reporting mixed results.

That uncertainty now appears to have shifted. Customers for both Gillette dates are reporting successful refunds, but there has still been no broad announcement telling ticket holders what option is available or who qualifies.

The concerts themselves remain scheduled. Ticketmaster currently lists both Friday and Saturday’s Gillette shows for 8 p.m., changed from the 5:30 p.m. start time displayed earlier this week.

For fans who still plan to attend, the shows are moving forward. For those who no longer want to go, the emerging refund reports suggest an option now exists — but, at least for the moment, consumers may have to know enough to ask for it.
