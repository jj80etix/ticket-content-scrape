# Ticketek Entertainment Group raises A$100m from its lenders – report

## Summary

Ticketek Entertainment Group (TEG), Australia's largest ticketing provider, has reportedly raised A$100m in fresh lender financing described as its "one final chance" to fund a turnaround, per an Australian Financial Review report. Majority owner Silver Lake did not add further equity, leaving TEG's banks — already owed roughly A$1bn — to extend the additional debt so new CEO Cameron Hoy can bid for upcoming ticketing contracts. The raise follows a string of lost Australian venue contracts to Ticketmaster and AXS, a UK contract at risk, and a June-reported restructuring and cost-cutting effort.

### Key Points

- TEG reportedly secured A$100m (~£52m/€61m/$70.5m) from existing lenders, on top of roughly A$1bn already owed to banks, without new Silver Lake equity.
- The financing is intended to let new CEO Cameron Hoy bid competitively for upcoming ticketing contracts amid recent losses.
- TEG lost the Venues NSW contract (Sydney Cricket Ground, Accor Stadium, CommBank Stadium, Allianz Stadium, Penrith Stadium) to Ticketmaster, the Melbourne Parks contract (Rod Laver Arena, AAMI Park, John Cain Arena, Margaret Court Arena) to AXS, and Sydney's Hordern Pavilion also switched to AXS.
- TEG's UK business risks losing its Motorpoint Arena Nottingham contract as the venue opens the tender to other providers.
- TEG had roughly $950m in borrowings as of June 30 last year, including a $940m primary loan (matures December 2030) and a $110m credit line (matures 2028); a June 2026 report said TEG would also undergo restructuring following CEO Brad Banducci's exit.

### Highlights

- A$100m emergency lender financing, layered on ~A$1bn existing debt, explicitly tied to retaining/winning contracts — a distress signal from a top-tier peer ticketing company.
- Concrete contract losses to name-brand competitors (Ticketmaster, AXS) across multiple major Australian venues within the past year.
- Debt maturity structure ($940m loan to 2030, $110m credit line to 2028) frames a multi-year runway rather than an immediate crisis, but the "final chance" framing signals real distress.

### Industry Problem

A major peer ticketing provider is showing financial strain and losing marquee venue contracts to rivals, illustrating how competitive contract cycles and leveraged balance sheets can erode even a dominant national incumbent's position.

### Proposed Solution

The item describes no product fix, only a financial rescue; for Etix this is a monitor-and-watch situation — track whether TEG's distress triggers further contract churn (potential opening for independent platforms to bid into displaced venues) or a sale/restructuring event, and reassess posture if TEG exits or is acquired out of additional markets.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**Ticketek parent company Ticketek Entertainment Group (TEG) has reportedly raised A$100m (£52m/€61m/$70.5m) from its lenders to target its rivals, following the loss of key ticketing contracts.**

According to a report from the [*Australian Financial Review*](https://www.afr.com/companies/media-and-marketing/ticketek-owner-s-banks-write-100m-cheque-to-fund-turnaround-20260805-p60llq), citing those with knowledge of the talks, month-long discussions with TEG’s lenders led to the boost which has also been described as Ticketek’s “one final chance”.

However its majority owner, private equity giant Silver Lake, did not provide any further funding. TEG’s banks, which were already owed around A$1bn according to the report, chipped in roughly A$100m so that [new chief executive](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/05/people-on-the-move-teg-uk-music-and-more/) Cameron Hoy could bid for upcoming contracts.

The report added that Silver Lake was involved in the capital-raising talks, despite not contributing, and it is expected that the fresh debt will grant lenders additional rights. Silver Lake secured a majority stake in TEG nearly [seven years ago](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2019/10/teg-acquired-us-private-equity-giant-silver-lake/).

A TEG spokesperson told the publication that it had “raised additional financing from existing investors”.

“This will strengthen our balance sheet and allow us to invest further in delivering a massive pipeline of live entertainment, driving growth across the business, and continuing to invest in technology for our partners and fans.”

*TheTicketingBusiness* has reached out to TEG for comment.

As Australia’s largest ticketing provider, Ticketek has partnerships in place with the Melbourne Cricket Ground, Brisbane’s Suncorp Stadium and Sydney’s [newly renamed](https://www.thestadiumbusiness.com/2026/07/27/afterpay-arena-enters-new-era-after-revamp/) Afterpay Arena.

However, last year, Ticketek lost its ticketing contract with Venues NSW to Ticketmaster, which included Sydney Cricket Ground, Accor Stadium, CommBank Stadium, Allianz Stadium and Penrith Stadium. The contract for Melbourne Parks, which included events at Rod Laver Arena, AAMI Park, John Cain Arena and Margaret Court Arena, was then won by AXS in April.

Sydney’s Hordern Pavilion also made the switch to AXS [last month](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/07/axs-named-ticketing-partner-for-sydneys-hordern-pavilion/).

Internationally, its UK business is facing the possibility of losing the Motorpoint Arena Nottingham contract after bosses [invited Expressions of Interest](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/08/motorpoint-arena-nottingham-invites-expressions-of-interest-from-ticketing-providers/) from ticketing providers, ahead of a forthcoming ticketing services tender. Ticketek had first signed a deal with the venue in [February 2023](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2023/02/ticketek-boosts-uk-presence-with-motorpoint-arena-nottingham-deal/).

In June, following the exit of Brad Banducci and the appointment of Hoy as CEO, it was also [reported](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/06/ticketek-entertainment-group-reportedly-set-to-undergo-restructure/) that TEG would undergo a restructuring and cost-cutting project.

According to AFR, TEG had roughly $950m in borrowings as of June 30 last year, including a primary and fully drawn $940m loan and a mostly drawn $110m line of credit. Its bank loans were interest-bearing and would mature in December 2030, according to accounts, while the line of credit would mature in 2028.
