# Dom Dolla in Melbourne: EDM boom shaping a new wave of agents

## Summary

Dom Dolla's sold-out 60,000-capacity Marvel Stadium show in Melbourne illustrates electronic music's rise to stadium scale, with agent Dave Blackgrove (The Team) describing a storytelling-driven, tour-style strategy replacing conventional nightlife promotion tactics.

### Key Points
- Sept. 24 show at Marvel Stadium (60,000 cap) sold out; framed as an "inflection point" for EDM at stadium level.
- Agent Dave Blackgrove credits community-building narrative, scaling from small Melbourne clubs to stadium.
- Describes top-tier DJ talent now being strategized like full concert tours rather than nightlife promotion.
- Signals a growing dedicated agent/touring infrastructure forming around top electronic acts.

## Transcript

SVP at The Team Dave Blackgrove says 'explosive' demand for electronic music is bringing new strategies to the genre as it makes itself at home at stadium level

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Dom-Dolla-Melbourne.webp)

Dom Dolla at Marvel Stadium © Sam Verbisky

Storytelling was a key factor behind Australian DJ Dom Dolla’s sold-out 60,000-cap show at Melbourne’s Marvel Stadium, agent Dave Blackgrove reveals.

“One key story for Dom is his journey as an artist from Melbourne,” explains Blackgrove, SVP at The Team. “He’s been a constant community builder in the city. We’ve gone from small nightclubs to a sold-out stadium, building in capacity with each step, and always giving a nod to the people and places that put him there” he says.

It is not just the capacity of Dom Dolla’s shows that have multiplied many times over, but also the production value, Blackgrove says. “Production is a huge part of how artists like Dom show up and deliver an amazing fan experience.”

The Marvel Stadium show on 24 September marks an inflection point underlining how electronic music has grown in recent years to reach stadium and arena level. And with this, the genre has moved to the point where the upper echelons of DJ talent demand being strategised like a concert tour, rather than with the tactics of conventional nightlife promotion.

Equally, the “explosive” demand for the electronic genre is seeing it develop its own emerging strand of agents and touring strategies, Blackgrove says. “It feels like it’s leading the way — often the fastest‑growing genre, especially in ticket sales.”

Explaining that traditional live sector structures that haven’t often applied to DJs are now coming increasingly into play, the agent adds: “Traditionally, dance music existed in clubs, and clubs have their own communities. But with artist‑led hard ticket shows — like a traditional band — you are selling every ticket yourself. You have to take a knowledgeable and strategic approach to how you announce that show, how the artist delivers that message, and how they interact with their fan base.”

Asked whether he has observed an uptick in audience demand for electronic music, he says: “It’s been in serious growth for a decade and longer, but what we are seeing in the last few years is that some of these artists are selling out the biggest venues in certain countries. It’s definitely shifting to a mainstream position, and awareness is the highest it’s ever been. There is huge buy‑in, and that’s not just for dance music artists with cross over pop hits — it’s for underground genres and the superstars of those lanes too.”

Australia is a market seeing successive milestones for the genre, with Calvin Harris having recently [sold 330,000 tickets](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/09/calvin-harris-sets-new-bar-with-330k-tickets-sold-for-oz-debut/) for his debut ten-show run in the country.

In July, Belgium’s Tomorrowland also hit a livestreaming record with 233 million views.
