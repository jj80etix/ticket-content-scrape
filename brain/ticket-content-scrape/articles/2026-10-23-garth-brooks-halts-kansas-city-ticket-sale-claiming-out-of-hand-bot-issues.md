# Garth Brooks Halts Kansas City Ticket Sale, Claiming “Out of Hand” Bot Issues

## Summary

Garth Brooks halted ticket sales for a newly added Nov. 7 Kansas City matinee after "excessive bot activity" disrupted the AXS onsale Friday morning — the second time in two months a Brooks onsale has been shut down over suspected bots, following a September incident on SeatGeek in Salt Lake City. Unlike the Salt Lake City case, Brooks has not yet confirmed whether tickets already purchased for the Kansas City show will be honored, and the disruption is notable for implicating two different primary ticketing platforms within weeks of each other.

### Key Points
- Kansas City matinee (Nov. 7, T-Mobile Center) onsale shut down Friday after bot activity during the 10 a.m. Central onsale via AXS; all seats were priced at $155 ($140 + $5 facility fee + $10 service charge).
- One week earlier, Brooks had publicly praised AXS for keeping tickets "in the hands of REAL people" for the same tour's initial Kansas City onsale.
- Follows a near-identical Sept. 18 and Sept. 22 bot disruption on SeatGeek for Brooks' Salt Lake City shows, where queues exceeded 50,000 with 90%+ verified-human users.
- Brooks has not confirmed (as of publication) whether already-purchased Kansas City tickets will be honored, unlike the Salt Lake City case where he explicitly guaranteed validity.
- Tour's uniform per-seat pricing strategy doesn't by itself solve inventory allocation when bot-driven demand exceeds capacity.
- Neither AXS nor Brooks' team had responded to TicketNews' request for technical details as of publication.

### Highlights
- Two different primary ticketing platforms (AXS, SeatGeek) have each had bot-driven onsale shutdowns on the same tour within about five weeks.
- AXS had been publicly credited for anti-bot effectiveness just one week before this failure.
- No confirmed resolution yet on whether purchased Kansas City tickets remain valid — a direct consumer-trust risk.

### Industry Problem
High-demand onsales for major touring artists remain vulnerable to bot-driven inventory exhaustion even on platforms that specifically tout anti-bot measures, and the resulting ambiguity over already-completed purchases erodes fan trust in the primary platform.

### Proposed Solution
The piece itself proposes no fix beyond Brooks' team "looking into" remediation; the build/improve signal for a primary platform is twofold — invest in bot mitigation that holds up under real artist-driven demand spikes (not just marketing claims), and establish a clear, pre-committed policy for honoring completed purchases if an onsale is halted mid-stream, so fan trust doesn't depend on ad hoc statements after the fact.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Garth-Brooks-Kansas-City-1.webp)

Garth Brooks has shut down ticket sales for his newly added Kansas City concert, blaming excessive bot activity for another disrupted onsale and raising questions about the future of the November 7 performance and the validity of tickets already purchased.

The country star’s representatives announced Friday, October 23, that sales for the third Kansas City concert on his “Blame It All On My Roots” Arena Tour had been suspended after problems during the morning onsale through AXS.

“Due to excessive bot activity, ticket sales for Garth’s third show in Kansas City have been shut down,” [Brooks’ team announced on X](https://x.com/garthbrooks/status/2113655436373373167?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). “Garth and the promoters are evaluating next steps and the future of the show.”

Brooks subsequently issued a personal apology, expressing particular concern for fans who successfully purchased tickets before the disruption.

“The bot situation is out of hand. It’s been progressively getting worse on this tour,” [Brooks wrote](https://x.com/garthbrooks/status/2113658998167585188?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

“My concern is for the people who managed to buy tickets today for the third show in Kansas City before the bots crashed the on sale. I will personally look into what can be done.”

He promised that everything possible would be done to address the situation, while offering his apologies and appreciation to fans who attempted to purchase tickets.

The interrupted sale involved a newly announced 2 p.m. matinee on Saturday, November 7, at T-Mobile Center. Brooks had [added the performance earlier this week](https://garthbrooks.com/news/garth-brooks-turns-kansas-city-into-a-three-show-weekend?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) following strong demand for his previously announced November 6 and November 7 evening concerts.

Tickets for the additional performance went on sale at 10 a.m. Central on Friday through AXS. All seats were offered at $155 plus applicable taxes, consisting of a $140 ticket price, $5 facility fee and $10 service charge.

The announcement did not clarify whether tickets purchased before the shutdown would remain valid, whether sales might resume, or whether the matinee itself could be canceled.

The two previously announced Kansas City concerts have not been identified as affected by Friday’s shutdown.

The Kansas City incident follows a similar disruption involving Brooks’ Salt Lake City concerts in September, although the two onsales were handled by different ticketing companies.

As [TicketNews previously reported](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/garth-brooks-adds-third-salt-lake-city-show-as-weekend-first-tour-strategy-repeats/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), SeatGeek encountered suspected bot activity during the September 18 onsale for Brooks’ original two concerts at Delta Center.

The venue reported queues exceeding 50,000 people for each performance, with more than 90% of users verified as human. After the ticketing system indicated that available inventory had been claimed, SeatGeek identified discrepancies in the numbers and began investigating what the venue characterized as bot activity.

That initial sale remained operational.

Four days later, on September 22, Brooks stopped the onsale for a newly added third Salt Lake City concert approximately 30 minutes after it began, again citing bot interference.

In that instance, Brooks quickly [assured customers that tickets purchased before the shutdown would be honored](https://garthbrooks.com/news/salt-lake-city-gets-a-fourth-show-and-a-new-opening-night?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

“It’s important you know the tickets you have bought are valid, and the Sunday show will be played,” Brooks said at the time.

He subsequently announced a fourth Salt Lake City performance, adding an October 8 opening night to the engagement. Tickets for that additional concert went on sale through SeatGeek the following morning.

Brooks’ latest statement does not provide the same explicit assurance regarding tickets purchased for the Kansas City matinee.

## Questions Remain About What Caused the Disruption

The latest shutdown is particularly noteworthy because it involved AXS rather than SeatGeek, indicating that Brooks has now reported significant problems with automated ticket purchasing activity across two different primary ticketing platforms.

Just one week earlier, following the initial Kansas City onsale on October 16, Brooks had publicly praised AXS and T-Mobile Center for their efforts to ensure tickets reached legitimate fans.

“AXS and the family at T-Mobile Center are making sure these tickets get into the hands of REAL people,” Brooks wrote at the time, thanking customers for their patience.

Friday’s shutdown raises additional questions about the effectiveness of those measures and what circumstances prompted organizers to suspend the newer sale.

Neither Brooks’ announcement nor the available public information establishes the number of automated accounts involved, how much ticket inventory may have been affected, or whether suspicious activity caused a technical system failure or led organizers to halt sales as a precaution.

The statements also do not identify who was responsible for the suspected activity. The recurring problems nevertheless present a challenge for Brooks’ approach to ticketing. His arena tour has emphasized uniform prices across seating locations, with additional performances frequently regularly announced after strong demand for the initial shows – often adding Thursday or Sunday early shows to previously announced Friday and Saturday spots.

While that pricing structure eliminates some of the differences between premium and standard ticket prices at the point of initial sale, it does not by itself resolve how available inventory is allocated when demand exceeds capacity.

For Kansas City fans, the immediate issue is more straightforward: whether the November 7 afternoon concert will proceed, whether completed purchases will be honored and whether another opportunity to buy tickets will be announced.

TicketNews requested additional details from Brooks’ publicity representatives and AXS media relations, including clarification of the ticketing disruption, the status of completed transactions and plans for the November 7 matinee. Neither had responded as of publication time.
