# Reeperbahn 2026 records growth in international reach

## Summary

Reeperbahn Festival's 2026 Hamburg edition drew record international participation, with international speakers up 20% and attendee countries nearly doubling from roughly 30 to 52, alongside a 10% rise in overall industry participants.

### Key Points
- 5,500 industry figures attended, up from 5,000 in 2025; 420 international speakers vs. 350 the prior year.
- Attendees came from 52 countries, up from ~30 in 2025.
- 45,000 total visitors across the four-day event, including free public programming at Festival Village and Spielbudenplatz.
- Festival leadership framed the growth around supporting emerging artists and cross-border industry connection.

## Transcript

The number of countries represented at the conference almost doubled from 2025 as international speakers also rose 20%

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/reeperbahn-2026-1.jpg)

Reeperbahn Festival has hailed growth in its status as an “international platform”, as the conference event reported a 20% jump in international speakers as part of a 10% increase in its industry participants at its 21st edition in Hamburg.

The event, which combines industry conference programming with emerging artist concerts and showcases, closed its 2026 edition this past weekend (19 September), having hosted 5,500 industry figures and featured 420 international speakers. This compares to 2025’s event, which brought in 5,000 conference attendees and 350 international speakers.

Attendees for 2026 came from 52 countries in 2026, an increase from the 3o countries represented last year.

“I’m glad that the Reeperbahn Festival has steadfastly bucked the trend and delivered strong numbers for both festival-goers and industry professionals, proving its appeal and relevance to everyone. It’s clearer than ever that we need to support emerging artists and showcase music on small stages—where the roots of everything our industry is about lie,” Detlef Schwarte, Reeperbahn’s director, told *IQ.*

“We need this festival in Central Europe, where the entire industry can come together across market segments and national borders to find ways to somehow keep our music ecosystem in balance—so that it remains legitimate in the eyes of younger generations as well,” he added.

The 2026 festival saw 45,000 total visitors across its fours days of concerts, showcases, and conference events, including “spontaneous guests” at the freely accessible Festival Village at Heiligengeistfeld.

“Thousands” of visitors were also drawn to the free programme at Spielbudenplatz, organisers said, including to performances from Germany’s Udo West, South Korea’s Okashii, and Japan’s She Her Her Hers.

“A key thematic focus of this year’s festival and conference was the stronger involvement of younger generations, whose nightlife, listening, and purchasing habits are already shaping the future of the music and creative industries,” organisers said.

> “This year’s edition highlighted the importance of Reeperbahn as an international platform”

They added: “This was closely connected to the guiding theme ‘Forever Legit?’, which was explored across numerous programme sessions. Discussions examined how the legitimacy of music industry structures must continually be renegotiated in response to societal, demographic, and technological change in order to enable future-proof and sustainable business models.”

On the showcase side, Reeperbahn held around 540 concerts by 425 acts. These artists came from 44 countries, with live events taking place across more than 40 venues. Alongside the music programme was also the Arts and Words programme, which presented around 30 events, including readings, live podcasts, art exhibitions, and guided tours.

Ukrainian artist Ganna was awarded the festival’s Anchor Award for international music, as Ukraine was chosen as Reeperbahn’s first ever ‘guest country of honour’ for 2026.

“As every year, the heart of the festival remained the discovery of promising emerging artists and the unifying power of shared live experiences. This was particularly evident in the international music award Anchor,” the festival said.

“This year’s edition once again highlighted the importance of Reeperbahn Festival as an international platform connecting German and European artists, companies, and institutions with partners from around the world, initiating relevant discussions, and actively contributing to shaping the future of the music industry,” festival organisers said.
