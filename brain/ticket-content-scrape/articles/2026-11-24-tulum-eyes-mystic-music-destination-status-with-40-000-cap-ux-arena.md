# Tulum eyes ‘mystic’ music destination status with 40,000-cap UX Arena

## Summary

Tulum, Mexico is positioning itself as a "mystic" music destination with the new 40,000-capacity UX Arena, an open-air multi-stage venue built on a disused limestone quarry. It will open New Year's Eve with electronic brand Zamna's 10th-anniversary series as its founder pursues a broader "entertainment ecosystem" for the town.

### Key Points
- UX Arena: 40,000-capacity, three-stage open-air venue on a 1.2M sq.m. site in Tulum.
- Opens New Year's Eve with Zamna's 10th-anniversary event series.
- Part of a stated ambition to build Tulum into a music-destination "entertainment ecosystem."

## Transcript

## Tulum eyes ‘mystic’ music destination status with 40,000-cap UX Arena

The open-air jungle venue will open on New Year's Eve with electronic brand Zamna's 10th anniversary series

![UX Arena rendering](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/UX-Arena-rendering-©-Zamna.png)

UX Arena rendering © Zamna

The jungles of Tulum will soon be home to the UX Arena, a 40,000-capacity, multi-stage development, a move its founder tells *IQ* is the first step toward an “entertainment ecosystem” for the coastal Mexican town.

Set across a 1.2 million-square-metre site on a disused limestone quarry, the open-air space will feature three stages, offering organisers a modular setting that can host upwards of 500 people. The venue is designed for large-scale productions and will launch with electronic brand Zamna’s 10th anniversary series.

Developed and operated by Universal Xperience (UX), the setting will also feature on-site parking and a transport hub, food & beverage infrastructure, and premium hospitality offerings, all while being easy to access from the coastal Mexican town on the Yucatán Peninsula’s Caribbean coast.

“We want to go against \[enclosed spaces\] because the world and the market are changing,” UX founder Fabricio Ledesma Heinrich tells *IQ*. “People want freedom; they want the jungle. We don’t want big stadiums. We want to keep nature and mix our guests with this kind of experience, who want to come and see the jungle, freedom, and open spaces.”

He describes the space as a “great mix” between “huge productions” and the “mystic setting” of Tulum and Maya civilisation, a notable difference from the country’s newest large-scale venues of Guadalajara’s [Coliseo GNP Seguros](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/04/stadium-revamp-offers-guadalajara-an-international-standard-space-for-global-tours/) or the refreshed  [Estadio Banorte](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/03/mexico-citys-estadio-banorte-reopens-after-e175m-refresh/) in Mexico City.

“We’ll drive you through the jungle, and when you’re out there, these massive productions suddenly appear,” he says, adding the setting is easily accessible from Tulum or Playa del Carmen via highway.

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/UX-Arena-rendering-©-Zamna-2.png)

UX Arena rendering © Zamna

Created in collaboration with the Quintana Roo government, Mexican Caribbean tourism authorities, and the Municipality of Tulum, UX Arena arrives as Tulum looks to strengthen its draw as a global music destination.

Annually, the site is expected to draw 160,000 international visitors to Tulum, generating approximately $320 million (€292m) for the local economy, with the stakeholders looking to make it a leading venue in the southern region.

“Tulum is one of the strongest brands of Mexico; everybody knows Tulum worldwide,” Heinrich says. “We want to develop an entertainment ecosystem around this.”

After it opens with a New Year’s Eve celebration, electronic mainstays like Amnesia, Anyma, Black Coffee, and Adriatqiue will bring their productions through as part of Zamna’s six-show series that closes on 10 January. UX acquired the Zamna brand earlier this year, with the milestone series to serve as a launchpad of sorts for a global tour in 2027.

“Part of my vision as the new owner is to keep this or bring back this freedom and essence, the soul of Zamna. We’re going to recover this magic, with more space for production,” he says.

> “I want to attack those low seasons and get the economy moving again”

Following Zamna, the site will be available to host artists of any genre, with Heinrich saying it is not meant to be pigeonholed as an electronic venue. Its position is enhanced by the available accommodations in the state of Quintana Roo, which is on par with major transit hubs like Orlando and Las Vegas.

Adding to this, next month will also see the opening of UMusic Hotel & Beach Club, which will also serve as an official Zamna partner, offering hospitality and VIP packages for ticket holders.

“We have the infrastructure to have a big arena and bring in high-quality festivals or different shows,” Heinrich says.

UX Arena is not designed to offer camping in its first season, but operators are planning to add on-site accommodation and glamping options in the future.

Beyond transient audiences, UX Arena is also intended to serve its local audiences and offer a year-round programme that disrupts the “low seasons” between tourism peaks.

“We need to develop this market. It’s already here, but we need to bring them content, because if you don’t have good content, they are not coming,” Heinrich says. “You have to bring them what they are asking for, no? Our vision is to develop. I want to attack those low seasons and get the economy moving again.”
