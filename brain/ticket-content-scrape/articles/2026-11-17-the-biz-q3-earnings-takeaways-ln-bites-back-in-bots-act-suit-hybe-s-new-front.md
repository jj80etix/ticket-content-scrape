# The Biz: Q3 Earnings Takeaways; LN Bites Back In BOTS Act Suit; HYBE’s New Front

## Summary

Live Nation posted a record Q3 ($10.1B revenue, +19% YoY, EPS beat), with Sphere and MSG Entertainment also topping estimates and international growth (+29% for Live Nation, up 15%+ in every region) outpacing a cautious domestic consumer. Separately, Live Nation/Ticketmaster fired back in their BOTS Act prosecution, arguing the FTC is enforcing the decade-old law selectively — this is its first-ever use against a ticketing platform — and that tracing bot-bought resale tickets back to the original purchase is effectively impossible; the underlying suit (filed Sept. 2025) alleges the companies profit from bot-driven resale "multiple bites" on the same ticket. HYBE also topped $1B in quarterly revenue for the first time, with record $512M in concert revenue from BTS's ARIRANG tour, and is now "incubating" a Southeast Asia expansion.

### Key Points
- Live Nation Q3: $10.1B revenue (+19% YoY), EPS $1.74 vs. $1.50 consensus; Sphere and MSG Entertainment also beat.
- Live Nation/Ticketmaster's BOTS Act defense: claims selective/arbitrary FTC enforcement (first-ever ticketing-platform case) and that bot-to-resale traceability is practically impossible.
- The underlying Sept. 2025 suit alleges LN/Ticketmaster "tacitly worked" with bulk secondary sellers who resell — often on Ticketmaster's own marketplace — giving the company revenue from the original sale, the resale listing, and the resale purchase.
- HYBE Q3 revenue topped $1B for the first time; concert revenue hit a record $512M; company eyeing Southeast Asia expansion.

### Highlights
- First-ever BOTS Act prosecution of a ticketing platform; Live Nation/Ticketmaster argue bot-to-resale traceability is practically impossible, a defense that could limit future enforcement if it succeeds.
- Underlying suit alleges a structural conflict: the same platform profits from the original sale, the bot-driven resale listing, and the resale purchase.
- Live Nation Q3 revenue $10.1B (+19% YoY); international growth (+29%) now outpacing domestic.

### Industry Problem
Enforcement of bot/resale consumer-protection law is still being tested in court, and the core dispute — whether a platform that also profits from secondary-market activity has an incentive to under-police bot-driven resale on its own exchange — points to a structural conflict of interest facing any primary platform that also operates resale.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor how the BOTS Act case resolves (a successful traceability defense could set precedent limiting enforcement) and watch for any court-ordered transparency requirements around primary-to-resale ticket tracing; the improvement opportunity for a primary platform is better bot-purchase traceability built in at the point of primary sale, rather than retrofitted after resale.

## Transcript

The third quarter was another strong one for major players across the live industry.

Live Nation reported a record Q3 with revenue of $10.1 billion, a 19% increase over the same quarter last year, as 61 million fans attended concerts.

That was a beat over analyst expectations, as Zacks pegged the consensus at $9.57 billion.

On the bottom line, EPS of $1.74 was a strong beat, with Zacks setting it at $1.50.

Sphere Entertainment similarly exceeded expectations — as the operator of the eponymous Las Vegas venue has done repeatedly, particularly since the debut of the glitteringly successful The Wizard of Oz — with revenues of $274.3 million in Q3, ahead of the Zacks consensus of $269.2 million. Sphere was also a beat on the bottom line, posting a per-share loss, adjusted for asset impairment, of 98 cents; Zacks’ consensus was a loss of $1.79.

Sister company MSG Entertainment had a record quarter — it’s the first quarter for MSGE, which operates on a June-start fiscal calendar — with revenues up 37% to a record $217 million. Part of the growth was due to the New York Knicks championship run, which drove sports revenues far later into the year than usual, but Harry Styles’ lengthy residency at The World’s Most Famous Arena gets plenty of credit, too.

In the broader economy, consumer sentiment is making modest gains, though fuel price pressure and inflation still makes for nervy purchasing behavior. And yet, none of that is seemingly slowing the public’s need for live entertainment.

“All year, we’ve been reading in the press about all the concerns about consumer spending, are they going to keep going to shows? Are they going to keep spending at shows? Will shows close? Will they go on sale well? And we are absolutely seeing continued strong consumer demand globally in all these venues.” Live Nation president and CFO Joe Berchtold said on the company’s Q3 earnings call. “And you look at some of the amphitheaters we opened here in the U.S., had just about a full slate of shows, performed very well. Premium did well, continuing to see strength there.”

For Live Nation, rabid growth internationally continues to pace growth, a trend that’s been apparent since the (slight) cooling of the domestic market after the Great Return.

“International, in particular, was up 29% in every one of the regions,” Berchtold said. “So Latin America, Europe, Asia, all up at least 15%. So we’re seeing consistently strong performance globally.”

Sphere, too, is looking overseas with work in the preliminary stages on the Abu Dhabi location of the supervenue.

“In Abu Dhabi, construction is moving forward. The venue’s underground infrastructure is currently being installed. Procurement is also underway, with the general contractor starting to award key contracts. Construction remains on track to be completed by the end of 2029,” executive chairman and CEO Jim Dolan said.

Financing votes are also underway for the second US Sphere in National Harbor, Maryland. Dolan said early talks are underway for what would be the fourth Sphere, which he expects to be stateside.

Sphere is also focused on building off Oz’s success. Concerts are a good revenue driver, but the Sphere Experience vertical offers far more show opportunities. The company is completing work on Rocky Horror Picture Show and Dolan says they are in negotiations with studios and other rightsholders for other IP.

**LN Bites Back In BOTS Prosecution**

Live Nation and Ticketmaster continue to fight back against a prosecution for violating the BOTS Act. In a recent filing, the companies said the FTC is allegedly “belatedly, selectively, arbitrarily, and unequally enforcing the BOTS Act and the FTC Act against” Live Nation and Ticketmaster, noting that the BOTS Act has never been used for the prosecution of a ticketing platform.

The companies also claim restitution and correction would be difficult because it’s virtually impossible to trace secondary sales back to a primary ticketing purchase made using bots.

The suit filed in September 2025 claims that Ticketmaster and Live Nation “tacitly worked” with secondary sellers, allowing them to buy tickets in the thousands and then list them for resale at marked-up prices, often on Ticketmaster’s own platform, thus giving Ticketmaster multiple bites at the revenue apple: from the initial purchase, the listing by the secondary seller and the consumer who ultimately buys the resold ticket.

**HYBE Eyes Market Expansion**

K-pop giant HYBE surpassed $1 billion in revenue in the third quarter, the first time it’s hit that mark.

The company also reported record concert revenues — $512 million — driven largely by the European and American legs of BTS’s “ARIRANG” tour.

The company looks to be eyeing a new front: Southeast Asia. The company’s CFO said it is “incubating” markets in the region, though the project is early days.
