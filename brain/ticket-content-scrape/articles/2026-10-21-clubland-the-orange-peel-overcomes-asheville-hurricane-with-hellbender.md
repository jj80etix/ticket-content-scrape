# Clubland: The Orange Peel Overcomes Asheville Hurricane With Hellbender

## Summary

The Orange Peel opened Hellbender, a new outdoor concert venue in Asheville built on land along the Swannanoa River that was devastated by Hurricane Helene flooding in Sept. 2024, as part of the community's post-storm rebuilding.

### Key Points
- Venue name references the giant salamander native to Western NC rivers, symbolizing regional resilience.
- Helene caused 43 storm-related deaths in Buncombe County, damaged 11,000+ homes, and destroyed roughly 250 artist studios in the River Arts District.
- Developed by Big Picture Concert Group (also behind The Orange Peel); opening act included Warren Haynes and Govt. Mule on Sept. 25.

## Transcript

![Hellbender3](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Hellbender3.jpg)

HELLRAISERS: Warren Haynes and Govt. Mule played Hellbender on Sept. 25. Photo by Tom Farr Photography

Hellbender by The Orange Peel, Asheville’s latest outdoor concert venue, derives its name from the giant salamander native to Western North Carolina’s rivers that evokes the resilience and adaptability of Appalachia.

The choice is deliberate and a fitting symbol for a region that is still rebuilding after catastrophic flooding; and aided by a lauded music venue reclaiming a once verdant swath of land on the Swannanoa River that was washed away by Hurricane Helene on Sept. 27, 2024.

The storm took a devastating toll on lives, homes, businesses and the region’s cultural landscape. Buncombe County reported 43 storm-related deaths, while more than 11,000 homes required repairs. Asheville’s municipal water system took 53 days to be fully restored, and the storm destroyed approximately 250 artist studios and creative spaces in the River Arts District.  
“It had a huge impact on our community as a whole, and we did not take it lightly to be thinking about how to rebuild in this part of town that was ground zero for Helene damage in the city limits,” says Liz Tallent, co-owner of The Orange Peel and Hellbender. “We really wanted to do that thoughtfully and respectfully, and in a way that was forward thinking.”

The storm reshaped the landscape of a city whose identity is deeply identified with live music and the creative communities that make it a cultural destination. In the aftermath, rebuilding became about more than restoring what was lost and Hellbender by The Orange Peel represents healing grounds.

Developed by Big Picture Concert Group, LLC, the team behind The Orange Peel, Hellbender sits on three acres along a bend on the Swannanoa River that was once home to Brouwerïj Cursus Kĕmē brewery. Post Helene, the only thing left of the 100-year-old warehouse venue was a cement foundation.

During the hurricane, the river rose more than 20 feet. Rebuilding from scratch in a flood zone would have meant 10-foot piers and other costly flood mitigating measures. The brewery owners called The Orange Peel with an idea.

“They said, ‘We want you to come look at this property because we think it could really be an interesting location for an outdoor venue, and we know you guys are kind of subject matter experts in that world.’ And so, we went and looked,” recalls Asheville native Tallent.

A thriving music venue wasn’t easy to visualize for the business collective formed by seven of The Orange Peel’s longtime operational managers.

“It certainly took a lot of creativity to imagine what it could be, especially at the time that we went to see it, which was late winter, 2025,” recalls Tallent. “Everything was gray. They had taken down most of the scraps of buildings by then and there were all these concrete foundations, and everything covered in gray dust. It looked like the surface of the moon in some places – just so empty and desolate.”

Local business owners and the city believed that an outdoor music venue would be the right anchor for the redevelopment of the Swannanoa River Road industrial corridor with a planned greenway that extends to Biltmore Village, which was also heavily damaged.

“Hellbender has become a keystone project for a transformation of that area that leans toward drawing folks in and celebrating the arts and culture,” says Clay Mitchell, Planner/Development Liaison for the City of Asheville. “It means a lot in terms of showing that we can rebuild and live with our rivers and the increased flooding that we’re experiencing, while at the same time doing it in a way that really sets the stage for what the city of Asheville supports in terms of its identity and the culture and the diversity of residents that we have.”

With a capacity of up to 6,000, Hellbender is rustic in all the ways that make Asheville, Birkenstock friendly. The footprint has adequate infrastructure and thoughtful design, but it doesn’t interfere with the natural vibe, which leans toward tie-dye and craft beer.

“We have so much empathy for what they went through and a desire to do right by them,” says Tallent, who joined The Orange Peel in 2006. “It isn’t ever going to go back to exactly what it was, but we think there’s a lot of cool, interesting stuff now coming over to the area, and probably Hellbender being the tip of the spear has helped with some of that.”

The outdoor venue is built around a concert lawn with ample stage-front, general-admission standing room and a mix of standard lawn chairs and premium seating that is quickly making it a destination for touring acts and a gathering place for a music-loving community.

![Hellbender4](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Hellbender4.jpg)

Asheville Rising: Warren Haynes, an Asheville native, played Hellbender two days before the anniversary of Hurricane Helene. Photo by Tom Farr Photography

The 1,000-cap Orange Peel and much larger Hellbender are functioning as a single talent ecosystem, moving artists between venues according to the right capacity and production requirements.

The debut Hellbender show was July 17, 2026, featuring Dinosaur Jr. and Band of Horses. It was an ambitious beginning, although severe weather affected the opening weekend.

“It was definitely a very intense process to get it to the opening date,” offers Tallent. “I mean it helped that we were not building some giant building. Intentionally, we were not and we never will. This is an outdoor use of the space – as it should be.”

The stage is mobile, but spacious and the venue delivered professional-quality sound and lighting. Hellbender is more than a jam-band or Americana amphitheater. Its 2026 season included LCD Soundsystem, Gov’t Mule/Ziggy Marley, Houndmouth, Geese, Dylan Gossett/Charles Wesley Godwin, Knocked Loose/Denzel Curry and concludes the season on Oct. 30 with Bitch Cabal featuring Wednesday and Mannequin Pussy.

Future programming is consistent with The Orange Peel’s established taste, just scaled up for an outdoor environment. The first full outdoor concert season begins in spring 2027 and in the meantime, Tallent says they plan to build a permanent stage and make other site improvements.

“We are trying to walk and chew gum right now with running the venue, running all the shows, doing all the actual operating and also very much knee deep in the plans for the next phase of our development,” says Tallent. “We’ll be working hard on logistics to make sure we button things up and make things work as smoothly as possible for next year.”

A tour staple in the market, The Orange Peel began producing outdoor concerts in 2015 as a way to increase revenue over the summer and eventually opened the 4,500-cap Rabbit Rabbit (now Asheville Yards) with Asheville Pizza & Brewing Co. in 2020. That relationship later ended and the downtown outdoor concert venue continues to be operated by Asheville Pizza & Brewing Co.

The Orange Peel is still the intimate downtown anchor, with Hellbender providing an outdoor room and a substantially different concert experience just two miles away.

“It’s nice to have that option again for Asheville music fans to get to be outside and have the river going by and have these great old trees that survived the flood and provide this great shade and have the mountains in the distance,” says Tallent. “I find that when times are hard, when things are uncertain, I still find that there’s such an appetite to go out to a show and have that experience. There’s just nothing that can substitute for it.”
