# Run-DMC Reunite For Aerosmith & Friends Hollywood Bowl Show, Is There More To Come?

## Summary

Run-DMC made a surprise reunion appearance during Aerosmith's second Aerosmith & Friends show at the Hollywood Bowl on November 1, joining for "Walk This Way" after being billed but absent from the first night. The appearance has fueled speculation about whether more Run-DMC activity could follow.

### Key Points
- Run-DMC (Rev Run, DMC) joined Aerosmith for "Walk This Way" at the Hollywood Bowl, Nov. 1, 2026.
- The group had been advertised but did not appear at the first of the two Aerosmith & Friends shows.
- The surprise appearance has raised speculation about a fuller Run-DMC comeback.

## Transcript

As the first of two Aerosmith & Friends concerts at The Hollywood Bowl was in full swing, there was an air of confusion when Run-DMC didn’t join Steven Tyler, Joe Perry, Tom Hamilton, Brad Whitford and Joey Kramer for “Walk This Way.” After all, they were named on the flyer and there were hundreds of people in the crowd who were expecting Rev Run and DMC to materialize. When they didn’t, the possibility of a Run-DMC reunion seemed to die. That all changed on Sunday night (Nov.1), when Aerosmith started to play the first few notes of their classic hit.

“Something’s wrong—we’re missing something,” Tyler quipped. With that, the crowd lost its collective mind as Rev Run, dressed in his signature Adidas jacket and fedora, and DMC, wearing a black Beastie Boys t-shirt, converged from opposite sides of the stage. It was a brief but potent moment that fans of the group will likely never forget.

[California, Dream On: Aerosmith Returns To Headline First Show In 3 Years At The Hollywood Bowl](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/10/07/california-dream-on-aerosmith-returns-to-headline-first-show-in-3-years-at-the-hollywood-bowl/)

The fact Rev Run and DMC were sharing vocal duties was colossal in itself. In 2023, as Hip-Hop turned 50, they performed what they dubbed the “last inning” during the stacked Hip-Hop 50 concert held Aug. 11 at Yankee Stadium. It was supposed to be Run-DMC’s last performance ever. The murder of their beloved DJ, Jam Master Jay, stopped them in their tracks and effectively ended Run-DMC in 2002. Sans a few reunion shows here and there, Run and DMC primarily branched out on their own.

As transcendent as Sunday’s appearance was—and even miraculous considering DMC was involved in a car accident with a garbage truck on the 105 Freeway in Los Angeles just two days prior—the reunion sadly does not signal a full-fledged reunion between Rev Run and DMC.

“We had to do it for Steven, Joe and the rest of the group,” DMC tells *Pollstar.* “The only way Run-DMC gets back together is if The Beatles get back together. It’s like Led Zeppelin. They can’t do it without John Bonham. It just wouldn’t be the same.”

![[4B_UYYPb-Gk]]

Even so, performing “Walk This Way” with his old bandmate and fellow Hollis, Queens, native was electric. It catapulted DMC back to a time when Run-DMC was dominating the Billboard charts. “Walk This Way,” which was included on their third studio album, *Raising Hell*, had landed at No. 4 on the Hot 100 and the album itself had become hip-hop’s first platinum and multi-platinum effort.

“It felt like ’86 again,” he says. “It was a really weird feeling. It was great to see the guys together again.”

Run-DMC was among the many special guests on a bill that included The Black Crowes, Heart, Slash and Tom Morello—obviously, lots of rock. But DMC, being a rock aficionado, loved it, saying, “I’m a rock guy. It felt like a rock revival. I went back in time to the ‘60s and ‘70s, all those great groups. What we did in ‘86 was because of hip-hop, but it wouldn’t have existed without the rock music that existed before us.”

Run-DMC’s version of the track, a remix of Aerosmith’s 1975 *Toys in the Attic* single “Walk This Way,” helped revitalized Aerosmith’s career and famously helped demolish perceived barriers between rap and rock.

Produced by Def Jam Recordings co-founders Rick Rubin and Russell Simmons, “Walk This Way” isolated the breakbeat of the original Aerosmith song. It came as no surprise that Rubin, who later sampled Led Zeppelin, The Clash, Black Sabbath and other rock acts for Beastie Boys’ 1986 Def Jam debut, *Licensed to Ill*, would incorporate his love of rock into hip-hop.

“We were just rapping over Aerosmith’s ‘Walk This Way,’’ DMC says. “It was just like rapping over James Brown or \[Clyde Stubblefield’s\] ’Funky Drummer.’ It was a breakbeat. What we did at The Hollywood Bowl is helping a new generation understand the importance of what Run-DMC and Aerosmith did so they can understand the importance of rock, hip-hop and all the other genres as a whole. It’s all just music. There would be no hip-hop without the music.”  
  
DMC then recalls the time Lou Reed of The Velvet Underground defended the hip-hop Run-DMC was making in the 1980s. The duo had opened up for Reed early in their career and were essentially booed for not playing their own instruments. Reed told the hostile crowd, “Before I do my set, I just want to address those people that was cursing at Run-DMC. If you’re doing that to them, you’re doing that to me. Maybe they ain’t got no instruments, but I was just like them, trying to figure out who and what I am.”

“Rock always had a foundational pillar in hip-hop,” he says. “Every MC before Run-DMC rapped over that ‘Walk This Way’ beat. We were just the first to do it on record. Before we did it, Melle Mel did it, Funky 4 +1 did it, Treacherous Three did it, Zulu Nation did it, Crash Crew did it—everybody who was doing hip-hop rapped over that ‘Walk This Way’ beat off *Toys in the Attic*. Aerosmith was always with us.”

The domino effect of “Walk This Way” was significant. Everyone from Limp Bizkit and Korn to blink-182 and Linkin Park followed in its wake. “It inspired all of those groups,” he says.

And 40 years later, DMC is still making waves. He flew from L.A. to Las Vegas after the show, where he spoke about mental health at Jewel’s Not Alone Summit & Award Show. His latest vinyl EP, *America*, will be released on Nov. 27 for Record Store Day. It’s another rap-rock hybrid with contributions from blink-182’s Travis Barker, Guns N’ Roses’ Duff McKagan, Motley Crüe’s Mick Morris and Skid Row’s Sebastian Bach as well as producers Bumpy Knuckles and Sonix. And with Christmas right around the corner, he’s slinging “Christmas in Hollis” sugar cookies, Darryl Makes Cookies, and promoting a new children’s book, the aptly titled *Darryl’s Christmas in Hollis*.  
  
DMC’s secret to success is simple: “I do everything that I was doing when I was 12 years old—eating cookies, making comic books, doing music—that’s the secret to life. The only thing that’s adult about us is our responsibilities. All the great scriptures say the kingdom of heaven is childlike. You share, you be nice and even if you have a fight, you shake hands, you make up and you share.”
