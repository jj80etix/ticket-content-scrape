# Feds Sue San Diego Broker Who Allegedly Bought 18.5M Tickets For BOTS Act Violations

## Summary

The Department of Justice, on an FTC referral, sued San Diego-based ticket broker REPS & Co. and three of its executives for violating the BOTS Act, alleging the firm used thousands of accounts since 2022 to bypass Ticketmaster and AXS purchase limits and acquire more than 18.5 million tickets across 83,500+ events. The complaint says REPS spent over $1.3 billion to amass that inventory and generated more than $120.8 million in resale profit, including $833,000 from a single Beyoncé Arrowhead Stadium show using 1,300 accounts to buy 7,624 tickets.

### Key Points
- Defendants: REPS & Co., CEO Ryan Pancheri, President Brian Archibald, COO Nicholas Totagrande.
- Alleged conduct since 2022: purchased 8+ tickets for 83,500+ events, spending $1.3B+ to acquire 18.5M+ tickets.
- Alleged profit from resale on the secondary market: $120.8M+.
- Example cited: 1,300 accounts used to buy 7,624 tickets to a Beyoncé "Renaissance" tour stop (Arrowhead Stadium, KC), netting $833,000.
- Complaint filed by DOJ following FTC referral/notification; FTC's Bureau of Consumer Protection director issued a public warning to brokers gaming purchase limits.
- Circumvention targeted ticket-limit controls on both Ticketmaster and AXS.

### Highlights
- 18.5M+ tickets allegedly acquired via bulk-account evasion of purchase limits since 2022.
- $120.8M in alleged resale profit, including a documented $833,000 single-show case study (Beyoncé/Arrowhead Stadium).
- Enforcement action brought jointly by DOJ and FTC under the BOTS Act, following the referral/notification process.

### Industry Problem
Large-scale brokers can still systematically defeat per-account ticket limits through mass account creation, letting a handful of operators capture outsized inventory on high-demand events and drive up resale prices for ordinary fans — the exact harm the BOTS Act and FTC enforcement are meant to prevent.

### Proposed Solution
The case itself is enforcement, not a product fix, but it reinforces that platforms bear scrutiny for how well their own limit-enforcement and bot/account-abuse detection actually works. For Etix, it strengthens the case for proactively investing in and publicizing robust account-level purchase-limit and bot-detection tooling, both as fraud defense (Q7) and as a differentiator against primary platforms facing scrutiny over lax enforcement.

San Diego-based ticket broker REPS & Co. has been charged with violating the BOTS Act after allegedly purchasing more than 18.5 million tickets to high-demand events using thousands of accounts created to circumvent purchasing limits on Ticketmaster and AXS.

The scheme, the feds allege, resulted in profits of more than $120 million.

The complaint, filed by the Department of Justice upon notification and referral from the FTC, charges REPS, along with its CEO Ryan Pancheri, President Brian Archibald and Chief Operating Officer Nicholas Totagrande.

The complaint alleges that, since 2022, REPS “used a variety of unlawful tactics to bypass security measures from ticket issuers in order to exceed ticket limits set by ticket issuers.” REPS purchased more than eight tickets for at least 83,500 events, spending more than $1.3 billion to amass more than 18.5 million tickets for these events, generating at least $120.8 million in profit by reselling these tickets on the secondary marketplace, the complaint says. For Beyoncé’s tour stop at Arrowhead Stadium in Kansas City during the “Renaissance” tour, the feds say REPS used 1,300 accounts to purchase 7,624 tickets and generated $833,000 in profit.

“REPS’ entire business model is focused on trying to game the ticket-purchasing system in order to obtain as many tickets as possible to resell at inflated prices to consumers,” said Christopher Mufarrige, Director of the FTC’s Bureau of Consumer Protection. “Companies that seek to profit by driving up prices or depriving ordinary fans of access to reasonably priced tickets to their favorite shows or events should be on notice that we are watching and will take action to stop this unlawful conduct.”
