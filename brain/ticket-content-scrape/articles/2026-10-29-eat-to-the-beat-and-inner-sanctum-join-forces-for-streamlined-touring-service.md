# Eat to the Beat and Inner Sanctum join forces for ‘streamlined’ touring service

## Summary

Backstage suppliers Eat To The Beat and Inner Sanctum formed a partnership to bundle catering, furniture hire, laundry and other back-of-house services into a single package for touring productions. Both companies will continue operating independently while offering a combined, single-point-of-contact service.

### Key Points
- Combines ETTB's production/crew catering with Inner Sanctum's concierge and furniture-hire services
- Aims to simplify logistics and unlock cost efficiencies for tour and production managers
- Builds on prior shared work on tours for Nicki Minaj, Kiss and festivals including Isle of Wight and BST Hyde Park
- Partnership also opens the door to greater international collaboration between the two firms

## Eat to the Beat and Inner Sanctum join forces for ‘streamlined’ touring service

The catering and backstage suppliers will leverage decades of experience to simplify logistics for organisers

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/10/TRAILER-WEMBLEY-scaled.jpg.webp)

Catering specialist Eat To The Beat (ETTB) and backstage service provider Inner Sanctum have formalised a partnership to “streamline” touring services into a package deal.

The alliance will combine catering, furniture hire, laundry, and other back-of-house essentials into one tailored package. By doing so, the collaboration is aiming to simplify logistics and unlock cost efficiencies for touring personnel.

“The planning process should be much more straightforward,” ETTB’s project director Jayne Ashman tells *IQ*. “Ultimately, our focus is on making life easier for tour and production managers.”

ETTB is highly regarded as a leader in production and crew catering worldwide, and UK-based team Inner Sanctum has spent three decades building its reputation as a live event concierge, bespoke furniture hire outfit, and backstage services.

Both businesses will retain their identities and continue operating independently.

The collaboration builds on years of experience working in the same backstage environments, from headline tours by Nicki Minaj and Kiss to festival outposts at the UK’s Isle of Wight and BST Hyde Park.

It was in London earlier this year that Ashman and Embla Gisladottir, Inner Sanctum’s business development manager, explored how the teams’ expertise could deliver greater value for their clients.

“Productions are becoming increasingly complex, while budgets and schedules remain under pressure,” Gisladottir tells *IQ*. “Rather than creating something entirely new, we’re building on what we already do well and making the process more efficient for everyone involved. Combining our knowledge, experience and skill sets can only benefit everyone.”

With over 70 years of combined experience, the teams see the partnership as a “natural progression for both businesses”.

> “Having an experienced team that can adapt quickly and find solutions makes all the difference”

“By offering tailored packages to suit productions of all sizes, we can provide a single point of contact, streamline their workload while also creating opportunities for greater cost efficiencies,” Ashman says.

The partnership also opens the door to greater international collaboration, drawing on ETTB’s decades of experience supporting global productions.

“One of the exciting things about working with Eat to the Beat is the international experience they bring to the table,” Gisladottir says. “They’ve been travelling the world with productions for decades, and there’s a lot we can learn from one another. We see real opportunities to combine our expertise and support clients beyond the UK, particularly where we’re already working with the same touring teams.”

[ETTB](https://www.iqmagazine.com/featured-list/indie-champions-2025/eat-to-the-beat/) adds that sharing knowledge could help Inner Sanctum “adapt its offering to different markets”, Ashman says, as ultimately, the pair’s goal remains the same: “creating a home from home, wherever the production takes us.”

The announcement arrives as Inner Sanctum enters its fourth decade this year.

Founded in 1996 by Jamie Wood, son of Rolling Stones guitarist Ronnie Wood, the company has supplied backstage comfort for some of the UK’s biggest concerts, tours, and events. Since 2022, George Hone and Dilli Sahi have led the business.

“Despite working on some of the largest productions in the country, Inner Sanctum remains a small, independently run business, and that personal approach is something we’re keen to preserve,” Hone says.

From artist dressing rooms to wider back-of-house facilities, the company says production expectations are only rising, making a one-size-fits-all approach increasingly impractical.

“Every production has its own priorities, and budgets can vary enormously, even between artists playing similar-sized venues,” Ashman says.

Hone adds: “What hasn’t changed is the need for flexibility. Whether we’re supplying a small production or a major international tour, there will always be last-minute changes and unexpected requests. Having an experienced team that can adapt quickly and find solutions makes all the difference.”
