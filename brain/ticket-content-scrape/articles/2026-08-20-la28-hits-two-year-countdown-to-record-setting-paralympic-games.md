# LA28 Hits Two-Year Countdown to Record-Setting Paralympic Games

## Summary

LA28 marked the two-year countdown to the 2028 Paralympic Games, which will bring a record 4,480 athletes across 23 sports to Southern California, including the Paralympic debut of Para Climbing and record gender parity across events. The Games will also break tradition by starting three sports (Boccia, Goalball, Wheelchair Rugby) before the Opening Ceremony, spread across eight competition zones within a 35-mile radius.

### Key Points
- Record 4,480 athletes, 23 sports, 15 days (14 days of competition).
- Para Climbing debuts as a new Paralympic sport for the first time via an organizing committee addition.
- 17 sports achieve full gender-parity medal events; another 16 offer equal quota places.
- Tickets go on sale in 2027; Visa is the Official Way to Pay for the Games.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/la28.jpg)

Los Angeles is officially two years away from hosting its first Paralympic Games, with LA28 promising one of the largest editions of the event ever staged.

The 2028 Paralympic Games will bring a record 4,480 athletes to Southern California to compete across 23 sports over 15 days, including 14 days of competition throughout the Los Angeles region.

LA28 is also set to deliver several Paralympic firsts. Para Climbing will make its Games debut in Los Angeles, marking the first time an organizing committee has added a sport to the Paralympic program.

Gender representation will reach new levels as well, with a record 17 sports achieving gender parity across their medal event programs. Another 16 sports will offer an equal number of quota places for male and female athletes.

The competition schedule will also break with tradition. Boccia, Goalball and Wheelchair Rugby are set to begin before the Opening Ceremony — the first time Paralympic competition in multiple sports will get underway ahead of the Games’ official opening.

Events will be spread across eight competition zones within a 35-mile radius, part of LA28’s push to deliver a compact and accessible Games while matching sports with venues suited to athletes and competition requirements.

The two-years-out milestone was also used to spotlight Los Angeles’ place in Paralympic history.

LA28 joined Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass and Council Member Imelda Padilla in Van Nuys for the dedication of Wheelchair Basketball Square at Victory Boulevard and Balboa Boulevard.
