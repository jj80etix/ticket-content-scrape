# United Festival Force forges ahead with sponsorship arm

## Summary

United Festival Force, a coalition of seven European metal festivals that already pool buying power on artist booking, is expanding into a shared brand-sponsorship vertical to pitch its combined audience to sponsors.

### Key Points
- Coalition members: Bloodstock (UK), Alcatraz (Belgium), Brutal Assault (Czech Republic), Dynamo Metalfest (Netherlands), Leyendas del Rock (Spain), Motocultor (France), and Summer Breeze (Germany).
- Existing model lets the group book touring acts collectively, since member festivals operate in different countries and don't compete with each other.
- Bloodstock director Adam Gregory says the collective can now pitch brands a cost-effective way to reach audiences across Europe.

## Transcript

The coalition of seven European metal festivals is expanding its collaborative model of artist booking into a brand partnership vertical

United Festival Force (UFF), a coalition of seven European metal festivals, is expanding its collaborative model into brand partnerships, aiming to pitch its combined audience and digital footprint to increase its attractiveness for sponsors.

The initiative builds on the group’s existing work to combine their buying power to offer agents and artists an alternative touring cycle, addressing a longstanding challenge for European festivals taking place in the second half of the summer.

The group comprises Bloodstock Open Air in the UK, Belgium’s Alcatraz, Brutal Assault in the Czech Republic, Dynamo Metalfest in the Netherlands, Spain’s Leyendas del Rock, France’s Motocultor, and Germany’s Summer Breeze.

Organisers now believe the same collective approach can create a compelling commercial proposition for brands seeking to reach live music audiences across Europe.

Speaking to *IQ*, Adam Gregory, director of Bloodstock, says the move follows discussions between the participating festivals about the potential for brands to “engage with a significant audience across Europe each year at a relatively modest cost compared with some of the bigger events.”

Speaking of the origins of UFF and its ambitions of creating a unified buying force for festival acts, he says: “Over the last few years, it has worked really well. It’s enabled us to bring bands over that ordinarily weren’t looking at that sort of touring cycle.”

“Because we’re in different countries, there was no real competition with each other at all,” he adds.

> “We can now demonstrate what that potential could be”

Now with its new sponsorship vertical, Gregory says UFF’s independence allows the group to take a more flexible approach to commercial negotiations than some corporate festivals.

UFF instead intends to develop opportunities that reflect the needs of both sponsors and festivals, with an emphasis on long-term relationships rather than maximising the value of individual deals, he explains.

“We’d probably look to forge deals that make sense as a long-term endeavour, rather than just go out there with a high price and try to capitalise on a complete budget,” he says.

The group has already worked with brands individually across recent festival editions, and the new initiative will seek to build on those relationships by offering access to multiple events through a single commercial proposition.

“It’s one thing to say we can offer you seven festivals, but what does that actually look like as a digital footprint and an on-site footprint?” he asks. “We can now demonstrate what that potential could be.”

Although UFF’s membership is rooted in heavy music, the group is not restricting its ambitions to brands already associated with rock and metal. Gregory says genre-relevant partnerships remain a priority where appropriate, but argues that the audience’s wider consumer interests should not be overlooked.

“We always look for rock- and metal-associated brands where we can,” he says, but isn’t restrictive in what partnerships are considered; “If that brand offering is correct and it’s presented in the right way, then we’re happy to look at all brands, really.”

> “I’m surprised no one has really done it before”

He explains the strategy is based around combining the strengths of these individual events to offer brands a wider European presence without requiring them to negotiate separate arrangements with every festival.

“All the festivals currently have sponsor deals in place on local levels,” he says. “It just made sense that where some of these companies that we deal with are global companies, there is a potential to offer more.”

He adds that approaching brands collectively could deliver better value than individual festivals negotiating independently, while enabling UFF to provide a more personal service. He notes that the group hopes the initiative will ultimately increase sponsorship revenue across its members.

As well as the new commercial vertical, the seven festivals will continue to use their combined strength to jointly book acts, with Gregory saying late summer festivals typically face greater difficulty in booking acts.

“Traditionally, there’s been a few touring windows where bands from, whether it be the US or overseas territories, look to see what’s happening across Europe, and it’s always been the earlier part of the year, which meant any festival that happens, sort of August onwards has kind of really struggled to get bands,” he explains.

“I’m surprised no one has really done it before,” he says. “When you’ve got a genre-specific set of events in different territories that don’t impact each other commercially in a negative way, it makes sense that they should work together.”
