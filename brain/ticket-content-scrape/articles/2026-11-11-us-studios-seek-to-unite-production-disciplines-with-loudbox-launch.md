# US studios seek to unite production disciplines with Loudbox launch

## Summary

US production studios FragmentNine and Jaw.Studio.NY have combined to form Loudbox, a full-service show-direction and production group, continuing a broader trend of consolidation among live-production vendors (following the EPS/NUSSLI merger and Unlimited Productions' US expansion). The new 22-person operation debuts on XG's ongoing "The Core" tour.

### Key Points
- FragmentNine previously worked on BTS, Keith Urban, alt-J, Karol G and Green Day productions
- Jaw.Studio.NY (founded 2017) has worked with Lana Del Rey, Dua Lipa and Florence + The Machine
- Follows the EPS/NUSSLI merger (August) and Unlimited Productions' new NYC office as part of a full-service consolidation trend among production vendors
- First production under the combined Loudbox name: XG's "The Core" US arena run, which began Nov 3 at Oakland Arena

## Transcript

FragmentNine and Jaw.Studio.NY are looking to remove 'the divide between disciplines' and offer production services under one roof

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/11/Lady-Gaga-mayhem-ball-scaled.png.webp)

Lady Gaga's Mayhem Ball © Loudbox

US-based production studios FragmentNine and Jaw.Studio.NY have combined to form show direction and production group Loudbox, as the sector sees more moves towards full-service production services.

The new operation now boasts a 22-strong team consisting of designers, animators, programmers, and producers, across US-based and international teams.

A spokesperson for Loudbox said following the announcement: “Large tours are usually split between several companies, each handling one part of the show from production design, lighting, video and direction. Loudbox brings that work together under one roof. One team that takes an idea from the first conception through final delivery, so nothing is lost or compromised along the way.”

FragmentNine previously worked on BTS’ *Love Yourself* tour, as well as productions from Keith Urban, alt-J, Karol G, and Green Day, where it has specialised in show design, lighting, and video.

Jaw.Studio.NY works in scenic and production design and is the production house of Jason Ardizzone-West. Founded in 2017, it has worked across arena tours for Lana Del Rey, Dua Lipa, and Florence + The Machine, as well as theatre productions and live concert broadcasts.

Their combination follows a trend of the production sector moving towards full-service providers in recent months, led by [EPS and NUSSLI’s merger](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/eps-and-nussli-merge-to-form-globally-active-event-infrastructure-group/) in August, which aimed to combine the two firm’s complementary market presences to make a “globally active” group. Earlier this week, [Unlimited Production also expanded into the US](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/11/unlimited-productions-to-open-flagship-new-york-office/), saying its selling point was its ability to offer audio, video, lighting, and more under one roof.

> “Loudbox removes the traditional divide between disciplines”

Following this, Loudbox said its foundation would allow the two studios to “leverage their complementary strengths as a single creative practice, removing the traditional divide between disciplines and allowing one team to shape every aspect of the live experience”.

The first tour to carry the name of the new combined company is XG’s ongoing *The Core,* which began its US leg on 3 November at Oakland Arena, the first of five arena shows in the country.

The two companies have previously worked together on live productions including including Lady Gaga’s *The Mayhem Ball* tour, Jon Batiste’s *Big Money* tour, and Utada’s *Science Fiction* tour, before officially combining.

“Over several years, Jaw.Studio.NY and FragmentNine built a close creative friendship, searching for the right opportunity to formally collaborate on. That project became Hikaru Utada’s *Science Fiction* tour, a fittingly named debut for a first-time melding of two distinct creative approaches,” the companies said.

The two companies will also continue to operate independently on some projects outside the Loudbox umbrella.
