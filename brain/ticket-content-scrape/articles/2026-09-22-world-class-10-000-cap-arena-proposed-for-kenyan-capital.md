# ‘World-class’ 10,000-cap arena proposed for Kenyan capital

## Summary

Pan-African developer The Zaria Group has proposed a $294.5 million, 10,000-capacity multipurpose arena in Nairobi, Kenya, backed by Dallas Mavericks president Masai Ujiri and Helios Sports & Entertainment. The venue would anchor a broader entertainment district within the government-backed Nairobi Railway City regeneration project, hosting concerts, sports, and conferences.

### Key Points
- Zaria Group proposes a $294.5M, 10,000-seat arena in Nairobi via new subsidiary Metroarena Development Company.
- Backed by Masai Ujiri and investment vehicle Helios Sports & Entertainment.
- Sited within the government-backed Nairobi Railway City regeneration district.
- Planned district also includes an amphitheatre, plaza, hotels, and retail/office space.

## ‘World-class’ 10,000-cap arena proposed for Kenyan capital

Pan-African developer The Zaria Group has announced plans for a multi-purpose $295m venue in Nairobi to help bridge the infrastructure gap

![Rendering of Kenya's proposed arena](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Rendering-of-Kenyas-proposed-arena.png)

Rendering of Kenya's proposed arena

In a bid to bridge the infrastructure gap in Kenya, Pan-African developer The Zaria Group has revealed plans for a 10,000-seat, $294.5 million (€256.8m) multipurpose arena in the capital city, Nairobi.

The Zaria Group is leading the project, with support from Dallas Mavericks president Masai Ujiri and investment vehicle Helios Sports & Entertainment. The Group is establishing a local subsidiary, Metroarena Development Company Ltd., to oversee its progress.

The arena is slated to be utilised for sports, entertainment, concerts, and conferences and exhibitions, and is being built as an anchor to a wider entertainment district, to include an amphitheatre, a public plaza, a green pedestrian walkway, hotels, apartments, and retail and office spaces.

It is proposed to be located within the Nairobi Railway City urban development, in the centre of the Kenyan capital, which is backed by the government as part of a commercial regeneration initiative. The [proposal](https://nema.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/NEMAENVISSR00724-METROARENA-DEVELOPMENT-COMPANT-LTD.pdf) outlines that the government entities are “committed to collaborating” with the private sector to “undertake the development” of the City, though the arena will be privately financed.

“By integrating commercial, residential, and mixed-use facilities with a world-class arena facility, the redevelopment will foster a balance between social benefits, including improved urban living and access to cultural and recreational amenities, and economic returns, through enhanced business activity, employment generation, and investment attraction,” reads the proposal.

> The arena is expected to be operated by QA Venue Solutions, which also manages Rwanda’s BK Arena

If completed, it could be an integral addition to the market’s touring ecosystem. According to *IQ* ‘s [Global Arena Guide](https://www.iqmagazine.com/publications/global-arena-guide-2026/kenya/), the country lacks a music-ready arena, with major shows often staged at “a mix of stadiums, open grounds, and adapted multipurpose venues,” said stakeholder and former government official Thomas Kwaka.

In Nairobi, there is the 5,000-capacity Safaricom Indoor Arena, with larger shows often held at outdoor venues such as Uhuru Gardens and Ngong Racecourse, all of which require full technical builds from scratch. The 48,000-capacity Kasarani Stadium in Nairobi can also host large-scale events.

Outside the market, the biggest indoor arena in East Africa is Rwanda’s 10,000-capacity BK Arena, which opened in 2019 and has since hosted acts like Kendrick Lamar, Doja Cat, and Davido.

Following completion, the arena is expected to be operated by QA Venue Solutions, The Zaria Group’s management affiliate, which also manages BK Arena and Rwanda’s Amaharo Stadium.

Just south of Kenya, Tanzania’s government announced plans to build a 15,000-seat indoor arena in the country’s economic capital, Dar es Salaam, last year, designed to rival Rwanda’s arena.
