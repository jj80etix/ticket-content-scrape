# Live Nation To Turn San Diego’s Wonder Bread Building Into New Music Venue

## Summary

Live Nation is converting San Diego's historic Wonder Bread building into a new 4,000-capacity mid-size music venue near Petco Park, expected to open in 2028. The company says the project fills a gap in San Diego's live-entertainment market between small clubs and large arenas, and will restore rather than demolish the historic property.

### Key Points
- Live Nation will convert San Diego's Wonder Bread building into a 4,000-capacity venue, opening 2028.
- Project targets a gap between San Diego's small clubs and large-scale arenas.
- Plan restores and adapts the historic building rather than demolishing it.
- Positioned to attract a broader range of touring artists to the market.

Live Nation is adding a new mid-size music venue to San Diego, with plans to transform the city’s historic Wonder Bread building into a 4,000-capacity concert space.

Expected to open in 2028, the venue will sit near Petco Park and is designed to fill a longstanding gap in San Diego’s live entertainment market. The city currently lacks a purpose-built music venue between its smaller clubs and large-scale arenas, according to Live Nation.

“This project represents an investment in San Diego’s future as a destination for live music and entertainment,” said John Schalk, Vice President, San Diego Market at Live Nation. “We’ve been missing a venue of this size, and this project will help attract a broader range of touring artists, bring more shows to the region, and contribute to the continued growth and energy of downtown.”

Rather than demolishing the Wonder Bread building, Live Nation plans to restore and adapt the historic property while adding the production infrastructure required for a modern concert venue.

Live Nation’s in-house Blueprint Studio is working with architecture and design firm Gensler on the project, with the new space being designed around acoustics, sightlines, backstage amenities and the overall artist and fan experience.

The restoration will also preserve key elements of the building’s history, including its façade and silo.

Megan Heine and Torrey Lee, whose family has owned the Wonder Bread building for many years, said preserving that history was an important part of backing the project.

“We have always believed in the importance of honoring the history of this building,” Heine and Lee said. “We agreed to support this vision because it respects what makes this place special while giving it a new purpose that will serve the community for generations to come.”

The preservation plans have also drawn support from Save Our Heritage Organisation, which highlighted several elements of the proposed restoration.

“We appreciate the project’s thoughtful approach to the adaptive reuse of the historic Wonder Bread building,” said SOHO Executive Director Bruce Coons. “Restoring the building’s character-defining historic façade, retaining and renovating the historic silo, and recovering the building’s earliest brick façade from its concrete entombment is a major step in the right direction.”

Coons added that incorporating a new entertainment venue into the property “demonstrates how historic resources can be preserved while continuing to serve San Diego’s future.”

Once open, the venue is expected to host touring concerts alongside private, corporate and community events.

Live Nation is also projecting a sizable economic boost from the development. An analysis by Oxford Economics estimates the venue could generate approximately $97 million in annual economic impact and welcome 316,000 fans per year.

Of those attendees, an estimated 126,000 are expected to travel from outside the San Diego metro area. The project is projected to drive $53 million in spending at businesses including nearby restaurants, hotels and retailers, while supporting 660 jobs and contributing $12.6 million in state and local tax revenue.
