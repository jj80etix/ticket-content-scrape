# Paciolan Hires Former Ticketmaster VP Alanna Kitaeff to Lead Product Strategy

## Summary

Paciolan has named former Ticketmaster product VP Alanna Kitaeff as SVP, Head of Product, putting her in charge of the PAC io operator platform, PAC Live consumer experience, and PAC io Fundraising. The hire follows a string of integration-minded moves for the Learfield-owned primary-ticketing vendor, including a renewed Georgia Tech ticketing/fundraising deal and an April facial-recognition venue-entry pilot with Wicket, as Paciolan leans into a "ticketing, fundraising, and marketing as one relationship" pitch to its 500+ client organizations.

### Key Points
- Kitaeff spent 8 years at Ticketmaster, most recently as VP of Product over access control, digital ticketing/pass delivery, and event-offer configuration tooling.
- She reports to Paciolan President Brendan Lynch and is based in Irvine, CA.
- Paciolan's stated differentiation: unify ticketing, fundraising, and marketing under one client relationship and one data layer (Paciolan iQ).
- Cited recent product moves: a renewed 1984-era Georgia Tech ticketing/fundraising/analytics partnership ahead of a stadium reseating project, and a facial-recognition venue-entry pilot with Wicket inside PAC io Entry.
- Company claims 500+ client organizations and 120M+ tickets sold annually; references an "AI-accelerated" product approach without naming specific AI features.

### Highlights
- Kitaeff's mandate spans the full client-facing product stack of a direct primary-ticketing competitor: operator platform, consumer experience, and fundraising.
- Paciolan is explicitly positioning product strategy around unifying ticketing + fundraising + marketing data per client.
- Concrete recent capability moves: Wicket facial-recognition entry (April 2026) and the Paciolan iQ unified data layer for segmentation/renewals.
- Scale claim (500+ orgs, 120M+ tickets/year) positions Paciolan as a credible primary-platform peer to benchmark against.

### Industry Problem
Primary-ticketing clients (college athletics, performing arts, venues) increasingly want ticketing, fundraising, and marketing handled through one vendor relationship instead of stitched-together systems — the gap Paciolan is organizing its product leadership and roadmap around.

### Proposed Solution
Paciolan's response is organizational and architectural: centralize product ownership across ticketing/consumer/fundraising lines under one SVP, lean on a shared client-data layer (Paciolan iQ), and keep adding adjacent capabilities (e.g., facial-recognition entry). For Etix, the signal is to benchmark how unified its own ticketing + fundraising/marketing story is for clients, and to watch whether Kitaeff's tenure produces shipped integration features worth matching rather than just a reorg.

Paciolan has named former Ticketmaster executive Alanna Kitaeff senior vice president, head of product, putting her in charge of product strategy and development across the company’s ticketing, fundraising and marketing technology platforms.

The appointment brings an executive with more than 15 years of experience in live entertainment and consumer technology to one of Ticketmaster’s established competitors in the primary ticketing market.

Kitaeff will oversee product management and design across Paciolan’s technology portfolio, including the company’s PAC io operator platform, PAC Live consumer experience and PAC io Fundraising products. She will report directly to Paciolan President Brendan Lynch and work from the company’s Irvine, California, headquarters.

The hiring comes as Paciolan continues expanding its ticketing technology offerings for college athletics, performing arts organizations, arenas and professional sports teams, with an emphasis on connecting ticket sales, fundraising, marketing and customer information through integrated platforms.

## Product Expansion Across Ticketing and Fundraising

Kitaeff’s appointment places her in charge of a product portfolio that Paciolan has been expanding through new integrations and broader use of customer data.

Earlier this month, [Georgia Tech extended its longstanding ticketing partnership with Paciolan](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/georgia-tech-paciolan-ticketing-renewal/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), a relationship dating back to 1984. The renewed agreement includes Paciolan’s ticketing, fundraising and analytics technology as Georgia Tech prepares for a major stadium reseating initiative.

That relationship also includes Paciolan iQ, which combines ticketing, fundraising and consumer information into a centralized database. The system allows organizations to segment audiences and develop marketing campaigns using purchasing histories and other customer information.

Paciolan has also been developing its venue-entry capabilities. In April, the company [announced a partnership with Wicket](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/04/paciolan-partners-with-wicket-in-latest-facial-recognition-venue-entry-play/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) to integrate optional facial authentication into its PAC io Entry platform, allowing participating venues to offer an alternative to traditional ticket scanning.

Paciolan is now a subsidiary of Learfield and says its technology serves more than 500 live entertainment organizations, supporting sales of more than 120 million tickets annually.

The company also referenced an AI-accelerated approach to product development in announcing Kitaeff’s appointment, although it did not identify specific new AI features or product launches tied to her hiring.

For Kitaeff, the immediate emphasis will be on understanding how Paciolan’s customers use its products and incorporating that feedback into future development priorities.

“What struck me in my early conversations here is how well Paciolan’s clients know the people they serve – by name, by seat, by how many years they’ve renewed,” Kitaeff said. “That knowledge should be visible in the products.”

“My first priority is spending time with the teams who use these tools every day, so the roadmap reflects what they are actually trying to accomplish,” she added. “I want clients to see where we’re going and recognize their own priorities in it.”
