# Acima Resolves 43-State Multistate Investigation for $183.5 Million

## Summary

Forty-three state Attorneys General settled a multistate investigation into rent-to-own lenders Acima Digital and Acima Holdings for $183.5 million, covering allegedly deceptive rental-purchase-agreement practices unrelated to event ticketing — Acima partners with retailers on consumer electronics and furniture leasing, not live-event ticket sales. The settlement imposes extensive disclosure, advertising, servicing and credit-reporting requirements, plus $130 million in lease forgiveness, without admission of wrongdoing. This is a consumer-finance/rent-to-own enforcement action and does not bear on ticketing pricing, fees or platforms.

### Key Points
- $183.5M settlement: $43M consumer restitution, $10.5M to AGs, $130M lease forgiveness, led by an 11-state executive committee.
- Allegations included cash-price markups, misleading disclosures, and obstructed contract termination rights.
- New conduct requirements include total-cost disclosure rules, a credit-reporting pause on new originations, and multi-year compliance reporting.
- Notably does not include an outright prohibition on rent-to-own leasing services, unlike some other state settlements.

## Transcript

On November 12, forty-three State Attorneys General (AGs) announced the settlement of a multistate investigation into the rent-to-own companies Acima Digital, LLC and Acima Holdings, LLC (collectively, Acima). The settlement resolves an investigation into Acima’s rental-purchase agreements (RPAs), with no admission of liability or wrongdoing by Acima. In addition to substantial monetary payments to states, restitution, and consumer lease forgiveness, the settlement imposes detailed injunctive requirements on how Acima advertises, discloses, services, and reports on RPAs, and on how it oversees its retail partners.

The investigation was led by an 11-state Executive Committee consisting of Colorado, Connecticut, Illinois, Iowa, Nebraska, New Hampshire, Maryland, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Tennessee, and Washington.

The investigation was resolved through consent judgments, which were filed in each state along with a complaint. An example from one state is available here (*see* [complaint](https://www.njoag.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/2026.11.12-Complaint.pdf) and [stipulated final judgment and consent decree](https://www.njoag.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/2026.11.12-Final-Consent-Judgment.pdf)).

### The Allegations

The AGs allege that Acima partners with retailers to offer RPAs to consumers and that, among other things, it:

- Marked up the cash price disclosed to consumers above the amounts advertised by the retailer, in amounts of up to $100, which the AGs characterized as a disguised finance charge;
- Presented electronic disclosures that emphasized the 90-day purchase amount over the higher 12-month total of payments into which consumers were automatically defaulted, and, for a period, prompted consumers to sign RPAs without first viewing them;
- Obstructed consumers’ ability to terminate RPAs, despite contract language permitting termination without penalty;
- Furnished inaccurate information to consumer reporting agencies without reasonable written policies and procedures; and
- Engaged in deceptive servicing and collection practices, including charging for merchandise that was damaged or never delivered, misrepresenting payment processing delays, and implying that nonpayment would lead to seizure of merchandise or court action when Acima rarely, if ever, intended to pursue either.

### Monetary Relief and Debt Forgiveness

Nationwide, the settlement agreement includes $183.5 million in monetary relief. That amount includes a total of $43 million in consumer restitution, distributed through a settlement administrator; $10.5 million in payments to the participating attorneys general; and $130 million in lease forgiveness. Acima will also separately fund up to $700,000 in settlement administrator costs.

For the lease forgiveness, Acima must reduce to zero the balances on certain delinquent, charged-off lease accounts of consumers in the participating states. Consumers on those accounts will keep the merchandise, Acima must treat the accounts as disputed debts, and it must ask the consumer reporting agencies to remove the associated tradelines. Acima also represents that it has not demanded payment on those accounts since May 29, 2026, and it must make reasonable efforts to refund any payments made after that date.

### Key Injunctive Terms

The judgment’s conduct requirements are extensive. Acima generally has 60 days to implement them, with longer transition periods for certain retailer-facing provisions. The highlights include:

- **Advertising.** Acima may not advertise that consumers will pay only the cash price, including through phrases such as “same as cash,” “cash option,” or “no additional fees required” (with a limited exception for early purchase options priced at or below the cash price). It also may not describe RPAs as loans or credit, or imply that they do not create debt.
- **Total Cost disclosure.** Before signing, Acima must disclose the Total Cost of an RPA (the total of all rental payments through the maximum time to ownership) more prominently than any lower early purchase option cost. After signing, it must display the remaining Total Cost at least as prominently as any early purchase alternative.
- **Execution process.** Acima may not allow consumers to e-sign an RPA without the agreement first being presented to them, and it must promptly provide an electronic copy of the executed RPA by email or text message link.
- **No markup of the cash price.** The cash price that Acima discloses to consumers in its RPAs must be less than or equal to the retailer’s cash price.
- **Termination rights.** Acima may not mislead consumers about, or prevent or limit, their right to terminate without penalty. It may make only one promotional offer in response to a termination request, and it may not require payment of overdue amounts as a condition of terminating.
- **Servicing and collections.** The judgment sets timelines for late deliveries and for repairing or replacing damaged merchandise, and it limits the use of terms such as “repossession” and “court action” in collection communications to situations where the action is lawful and Acima actually intends to take it. The servicing provisions run for ten years.
- **Credit reporting.** For RPAs originated after the effective date, Acima may not report to any of the national consumer reporting agencies for five years, although it may continue to provide data to specialty consumer reporting firms.
- **Retailer oversight.** Acima must implement onboarding, annual, and new-hire training for retailer employees who explain its RPAs, verify completion, and take corrective action against noncompliant retailers. These obligations run five years, extended to seven if Acima is adjudicated to have violated them.
- **Compliance reporting.** Acima must conduct annual compliance reviews and deliver three annual compliance reports to the multistate Executive Committee.

### No Explicit Prohibition on Leasing Services

From our perspective, one of the most notable features of this settlement is what it omits. Some other recent state-specific settlements in the lease-to-own or rent-to-own space, including settlements with states participating in the Acima settlement, have included explicit prohibitions on leasing or renting services. The Acima settlement does not. This omission may signal that, despite some states’ prohibition on leasing services, no nationwide consensus around such a prohibition has emerged among the AGs.
