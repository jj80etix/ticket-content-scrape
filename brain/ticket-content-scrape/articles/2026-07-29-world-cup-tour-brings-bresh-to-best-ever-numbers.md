# World Cup tour brings Bresh to ‘best ever numbers’

## Summary

Argentine live-event brand Bresh recorded its best-ever numbers from a 17-event "Futbol Celebration Tour" timed to the FIFA World Cup, drawing 40,000 fans across North America by tailoring events to the national fanbases following specific teams.

### Key Points
- Strategy split between established markets (larger venues, up to 5,000-cap) and new markets (smaller, up to 2,000-cap venues).
- World Cup Final event was held at Manhattan's 4,000-cap Pier 17.
- Bresh views the tour as proof of concept for further US market expansion.

## World Cup tour brings Bresh to ‘best ever numbers’

How following the tournament around North America gave the travelling dance brand a golden opportunity to tailor events to different demographics

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Bresh-New-York-Pier-17.png)

Bresh at Pier 17, New York City © Bresh

Argentinian live event brand Bresh has recorded its “best ever numbers” with its FIFA World Cup tour, as organisers tell *IQ* how targeting pre-curated audiences assembled around football matches helped them host 40,000 fans in 17 events across North America during the tournament.

Known as the *Futbol Celebration Tour,* the strategy involved staging events in cities hosting major World Cup matches, seeking to attract national fanbases gathering in cities around Latin American countries’ matches to the brand’s reggaeton and Latin-centric live events.

After beginning in Buenos Aires ten years ago, the event series, which stages a mixture of DJ, electronic, and acoustic events, had expanded out of Argentina into other Latin American markets when the pandemic hit. Fernandez explains the team started to show its events on YouTube and Instagram live streams, maintaining a following that allowed it to launch in Spain in 2021, before further growth across Europe which has now brought the brand to 30 countries, making 500 events per year.

Of the landmark *Futbol Celebration Tour*, Bresh chief strategy officer Nicolás Fernandez tells *IQ:* “We did 17 events and we are really happy with the World Cup tour experience, because we started to think about the strategy many months before, and because the challenge was very big, with a lot of matches, a lot of cities, very long distances, including games in Mexico and Canada.”

He says the months of planning for the tour revolved around a strategy with two parts. “The first part was our strong markets,” he says, listing US cities where Bresh is “already growing and where we already had a lot of fans, like New York, Los Angeles, Dallas, DC, Boston”.

> “ The success of Bresh is to connect with the culture of each market”

He adds: “In these cities, we wanted to grow even more, so we went for big venues, more tickets, more people, and we made the show and the production even bigger and better, and in these cities, we had our best numbers ever.”

The second prong of the strategy was new city markets, where, Fernandez explains, the team “ used the World Cup as a great moment to arrive in new cities where Bresh was not known before”.

With half the tour routed through unproven markets, choosing the right venue sizes was key to avoiding over-exposure. While Bresh typically uses up to 5,000-cap indoor venues in tried and tested cities, such as Silo in Dallas, organisers targeted no bigger than 2,000-cap spaces in new locations.

The exception to the brand’s usual preference of indoor venues was its World Cup Final event on 19 July in the picturesque setting of Lower Manhattan’s 4,000-cap Pier 17 venue, which Fernandez says was too good to pass up due to its “ incredible view of all New York City and the Brooklyn Bridge”.

Central to the tour’s conception was an idea of demographic tailoring. For this, Bresh seized on the pre-curated audience that World Cup matches brought to a given city. The team especially focused on routing the tour through cities hosting Spanish-speaking countries, banking on this providing a ready-made audience for its reggaeton and Latin-themed events.

“We tried to do these parties close to the games of Latin American and European teams like Spain, Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, because in these countries the fandom is very strong.,” Fernandez says. “So the idea was to mix the local people that we want to keep forever for the future with the fans that were travelling for the World Cup. So that is how we arrived in new cities like Kansas City, Atlanta, Philadelphia, Arlington (Texas), and also Toronto and Vancouver in Canada, where we had never been before.”

This strategy of targeting ready-made audiences, however, required set lists and genre balances at events to be tweaked based on the fan demographic likely to attend, based on which teams were in the city that day.

> “ the challenge was very big, with a lot of matches, a lot of cities, and very long distances”

But Fernandez says this was nothing new, explaining: “ The success of Bresh is to connect with the culture of each market, to mix the DNA of the brand with the local culture. So we adapt the music, we adapt the show, we adapt the guest list, and the way we communicate the party.”

He adds the ultimate result was a “very cosmopolitan, international parties” to match the melting pot audiences attracted by the World Cup, even if “ the Latin community of the national teams” formed the central pillar of programming and promotion strategies.

But the relentless schedule of the World Cup meant the team had to adapt to faster turnarounds, with Fernandez saying: “ The most difficult thing was the logistics of our team, because with the World Cup we had some events scheduled on Wednesdays or Thursdays or Mondays. It’s not normal for us because we are well placed on the weekends.”

Logistics for the tour primarily involved transporting large set pieces which were installed in venues across the country, with sound and lighting equipment largely used on a plug-and-play basis in venues.

“ We have a lot of sets of our decorations and experience. We prepared four or five for this tour, and then, depending on the city and the place and the venue and the capacity, sometimes we travelled by plane but with a truck to transport all the decoration sets. And most of the tour was that type of logistics,” Fernandez says.

Having executed the tour and being “very happy with the result”, Fernandez says Bresh is now taking the *Futbol Celebration Tour* as proof of concept for further US growth, which he calls “one of the main goals of the company for the next year”.

“ This showed us that we can do big events in many markets and always keep the same premium quality in the brand, and that is the most important thing,” he reflects.
