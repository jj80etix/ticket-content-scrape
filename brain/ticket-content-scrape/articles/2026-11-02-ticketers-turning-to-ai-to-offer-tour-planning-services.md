# Ticketers turning to AI to offer tour planning services

## Summary

SeatGeek, Skiddle, and Vivenu have each launched AI "predictive intelligence" tools within weeks of each other that mine historical ticketing transaction data to help promoters and agents plan tour routing and pricing. SeatGeek's TourIQ and Skiddle's Brightlights analyze past sales/booking patterns to recommend venue size, market fit, and price-level scaling; Vivenu's tool is a server accessible through AI assistants like Claude and ChatGPT for querying live ticketing data directly.

### Key Points
- SeatGeek's TourIQ draws on "everything that has ever happened on SeatGeek" plus artist-popularity signals (not just raw pricing) to recommend venue size, market selection, and price-level structuring (e.g., scaling to hit a revenue target or cap top ticket prices).
- Skiddle's Brightlights blends historical booking data with external signals like streaming behavior to help talent buyers and venues reduce financial risk on bookings.
- Vivenu differentiates by exposing an AI-agent-queryable interface (Claude/ChatGPT) rather than a standalone dashboard.
- All three launched within the same recent window, signaling a fast-moving competitive push into tour-planning/pricing-intelligence as a service layer beyond core ticketing.

### Industry Problem
Promoters and agents currently rely on manual, time-intensive analysis (per SeatGeek's Casey: "highlighters" and hand-drawn price maps) to route tours and set price levels, with no easy way to benchmark against cross-platform demand or artist-comparison data.

### Proposed Solution
The item itself describes ticketing platforms monetizing their transaction data as a promoter-facing AI advisory layer (tour routing, venue sizing, dynamic price-level scaling). For Etix, this is a direct #build/#improve signal: an analogous predictive-planning or pricing-assist tool built on Etix's own primary-sale transaction history could extend the platform's value to promoter/venue clients and benchmark against SeatGeek/Skiddle/Vivenu's early offerings; also relevant to the Etix Assistant AI roadmap as a model for exposing proprietary ticketing data through an AI interface.

Several recent product launches have seen ticketing platforms move to open up troves of transaction data to help promoters and agents plan tours and price shows

Ticketers says “the sky is the limit” to how a spate of AI ‘predictive intelligence’ tools recently launched across the sector can advance the ways promoters and agents route and price live tours.

In recent weeks, SeatGeek, Skiddle, and Vivenu have each launched services which they say can help tour planners by analysing patterns from historical ticket buying data. With this, the firms are introducing another source of data that agents and promoters can draw from when developing touring and pricing strategies, on top of in-house data analysis that is already widely used.

As part of this trend, SeatGeek has launched a “small team” fielding inbound requests from tour planners enquiring about using its new TourIQ feature, says ArinMichelle Casey, director of partner services at SeatGeek. Years of selling tickets to concerts have left SeatGeek with “millions of data points” that it now wants to put to a new use by opening them up to tour planners, she says.

“ Between agents and promoters, they may leverage TourIQ to help them do the routing and the initial planning for a tour,” explains Casey of SeatGeek’s service. “They might have an artist in mind and say, ‘we’re debating between doing a couple nights in arenas or doing a stadium, so let’s take a look and see the best venue fit by size and let’s take a look at a couple different markets, and let’s plot the tour’. Then it lets them see what the tour is worth.”

The launch of TourIQ comes as UK ticketer Skiddle also brings out Brightlights, which founders also describe as a “predictive intelligence” tool to “help venues, promoters, and talent buyers make smarter booking decisions”.

“Brightlights will give promoters access to the predictive insights needed to build better lineups and reduce financial risk. When promoters can match the right artists to the right audiences, it elevates the entire nightlife experience for fans, venues, and artists alike,” says Duncan King, Skiddle’s head of partnerships.

Skiddle says Brightlights is designed to consolidate fragmented data from past bookings, echoing another tool launched by Vivenu in the same month, which the firm describes as letting tour organisers use AI tools to “ask questions about their live ticketing data and act on the answers”.

The three tools diverge in their offerings, however. Vivenu’s tool differs from Brightlights and TourIQ by being a server that is accessible through AI tools including Claude and ChatGPT. Brightlights, meanwhile, aims to unite ticket data with other indicators including streaming behaviour, in its pitch to agents and promoters.

Outside of tour routing, though, Casey says SeatGeek’s TourIQ service can also help planning for individual shows, saying it can aid the process of scaling inventory into different price levels across a venue.

> “ The sky is the limit as for how we can customise the application”

With its history as a ticket aggregator before moving into primary sale, Casey says SeatGeek is moving towards promoter-side services because “we have such robust data about market clearing prices and what fans pay, when and where, and for whom”. Indeed, she says, TourIQ is based on a dataset comprised of “everything that has ever happened on SeatGeek”.

Asked how a historical price dataset accounts for inflation and macroeconomic factors, Casey explains TourIQ uses inputs that measure the relative popularity of an artist compared to peers, and not just pricing numbers. When asked what the tool can add beyond data analysis already done by agents and promoters, she says these inputs allow TourIQ to be “cultural” as well as numerical.

As a result she says, “we can work with the model any which way, so if a promoter came to us and said, ‘I’ve got this artist, I want to do this stadium, and I need to make $6 million; can you scale it for me? We could”.

She adds TourIQ can also be used in the reverse direction, for example “if a promoter came to us and said, ‘it’s important to us that we keep the highest ticket price below a certain amount, but we want to maximise revenue; can you show us how to do it?

Casey says SeatGeek is agnostic in terms of the different pricing strategies it can be used towards: “We will work with folks whose priorities run the gamut from, wanting to keep the prices as low as they can, and others that are more open to doing what would otherwise be done in the market.”

Proponents of the recent launches say AI tools of this nature can benefit tour planning with advanced pattern recognition that can feed strategy decisions.

“Brightlights gives talent buyers the resources needed to book acts intelligently and reinforce their decision-making without taking the human element out of the process,” says Brightlights co-founder Dan Leizell.

SeatGeek’s Casey echoes this, saying: “Two artists that might seem very different might have surprisingly similar fan behaviour and sales trends. That’s why it’s so compelling to have this robust dataset because it’s reaching so far into pockets that we wouldn’t think to look at if we were running these comparisons manually,” she says.

Speaking to the potential uses of ticket platforms’ data for tour planners as the sector increasingly looks to open up this vertical, Casey says the idea boils down to saving time on “what might have taken us a few hours to do the maths, colour the map, get our highlighters and draw in the numbers, where you’re getting maybe 35 price levels”.

She adds: “Some promoters prefer fewer price levels; some have no preference. The sky is the limit as for how we can customise the application.”
