# New Megaforce staging systems pave way for ‘significant’ expansion

## Summary

German staging company Megaforce is investing in two new stage systems — the crane-free Vega and the high-capacity Jupiter — to expand its footprint in Spain, Portugal and southern France starting in 2027, with a seasonal rotation plan moving equipment between Europe and Latin America. The company frames the investment as part of a broader logistics strategy to cut transport costs via regional staging hubs.

### Key Points
- Vega: crane-free stage system using electric linear actuators, targeting stadium/festival-scale builds.
- Jupiter: up to 200 tonnes of roof rigging capacity for "highest production demand" shows.
- Seasonal deployment plan rotates equipment between the European summer and Latin American summer touring seasons.
- Part of a wider push to build regional staging hubs and reduce cross-continental transport costs.

## New Megaforce staging systems pave way for ‘significant’ expansion

The staging company says two new systems will lead a push for 'significantly expanded' western European market presence in 2027

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Megafroce.jpg)

A Megaforce stage at Rock for the People © Megaforce

German staging company Megaforce has said it is pushing for a “significantly expanded” footprint in western Europe to be led by an investment in two new staging systems which will be rolled out in 2027.

The company said it plans to introduce the models for Europe’s 2027 festival season, before moving them to Latin America in the European winter months.

The two new systems form part of what Megaforce called a “long-term investment in the western European live entertainment market”. With this, the firm is aiming to grow its stage capacity and market presence in Spain, Portugal, and southern France next year.

The first of the new stage systems is a crane-free model known as Vega. Targeting site specifications up to stadiums and festivals, Megaforce said it combines “the capability of a major festival stage with a modern electromechanical drive system using electric linear actuators; its defining feature is that no crane is needed at any point of the build.”

> “We are investing now because international productions need more volume, more flexibility and infrastructure that can move with the market”

The second, the Jupiter stage system, has up to 200 tonnes of rigging capacity in its roof, and is designed for the “highest production demands”. Its width, depth, and configuration are adaptable to “different venue dimensions, layouts, and touring specifications while providing the scale required for international headline shows,” it added.

“We are investing now because international productions need more volume, more flexibility and infrastructure that can move with the market,” Megaforce CEO Michael Brombacher said.

“With Vega, the crane-free build reduces dependencies at venues and opens up locations where conventional crane access is limited. At the same time, our seasonal deployment model allows this new capacity to follow the live event cycle – from the European summer to the Latin American summer.”

Describing the strategy behind the Latin American rollout, the company said: “This seasonal rotation is part of Megaforce‘s new global logistics strategy. Since transport represents one of the most significant cost and complexity factors in international staging, the company is developing a network of strategically positioned hubs and predefined corridors across its key markets.”

“The objective is to reduce transport distances, avoid unnecessary material movements, and create predictable, repeatable routes between Europe and Latin America,” Megaforce added.
