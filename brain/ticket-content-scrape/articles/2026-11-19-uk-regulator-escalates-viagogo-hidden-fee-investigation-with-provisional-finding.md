# UK Regulator Escalates viagogo Hidden-Fee Investigation With Provisional Findings

## Summary

The UK's Competition and Markets Authority has provisionally found that resale marketplace viagogo engaged in "drip pricing" — not disclosing mandatory booking fees and taxes in the price initially shown to consumers — across its viagogo.co.uk, viagogo.com and viagogo.com/ww sites. This is the first case to reach the provisional-infringement-notice stage under the CMA's new direct consumer-enforcement powers (Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024) without the company settling, unlike StubHub UK, which settled a similar case in June 2026 with a £889,200 fine plus £590,000+ in refunds to 51,350 customers. A final decision isn't expected before spring 2027; potential penalties run up to 10% of worldwide turnover or £300,000, whichever is greater.

### Key Points
- CMA provisional finding: mandatory fees (booking fees, taxes) were not included in viagogo's initial displayed prices, a practice known as drip pricing.
- Covers viagogo.co.uk (Apr 6–Dec 29, 2025), viagogo.com (Apr 6, 2025–at least Jul 29, 2026), viagogo.com/ww (Apr 6, 2025–present).
- First case to reach this stage under the CMA's new direct-enforcement powers (in force since April 2025) without settlement — viagogo is contesting rather than settling, unlike StubHub UK's June 2026 settlement (£889,200 fine, £590K+ refunds to 51,350 customers).
- Potential final penalty: up to 10% of worldwide turnover or £300,000, whichever is greater, plus possible corrective measures/compensation.
- Next case update expected spring 2027; viagogo can still contest evidence and appeal any final infringement notice in court.
- CMA frames the issue as price-comparability (seeing the full mandatory price upfront), not resale markup levels — relevant to both primary and secondary ticket sellers.

### Highlights
- Alleged drip pricing spans April 2025 through at least mid/late 2026 across three viagogo domains.
- Maximum potential penalty: 10% of global turnover or £300,000 (whichever is greater).
- StubHub UK's comparable 2026 settlement: £889,200 fine + £590K+ refunds to 51,350 customers — a concrete benchmark for what unresolved all-in-pricing exposure can cost.

### Industry Problem
Upfront, all-in price disclosure at the point a consumer first sees a price — not just at checkout — is becoming a hard regulatory requirement across resale and (by extension) primary ticketing in multiple jurisdictions, mirroring the US FTC junk-fee rule; platforms that still reveal mandatory fees late in the funnel face direct, uncapped-percentage-of-revenue financial exposure.

### Proposed Solution
No new product is described — this is enforcement, not innovation. For Etix, the clear trigger is to confirm all displayed/advertised prices (listing pages, search results, marketing) already show the full mandatory total before a buyer reaches checkout, in any market where DMCC/FTC-style all-in pricing rules apply or are likely to extend, rather than waiting for a comparable investigation.

The UK’s Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) has provisionally found that ticket resale marketplace viagogo failed to disclose mandatory fees upfront, escalating a yearlong investigation that could ultimately result in financial penalties and customer compensation.

The regulator [announced Thursday](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-provisionally-finds-that-viagogo-may-have-broken-the-law-on-hidden-fees?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) that it had issued provisional infringement notices to two companies operating three viagogo websites, concluding that consumers may have been presented with ticket prices that excluded unavoidable charges until later in the purchasing process.

The practice, commonly known as “drip pricing,” can make tickets appear less expensive than their actual purchase price and complicate comparisons between competing marketplaces.

The CMA emphasized that its findings are provisional and do not establish that viagogo has broken the law. The companies will have an opportunity to review the regulator’s evidence and respond before a final decision is made.

## StubHub UK Investigation Took a Different Path

The provisional findings mark a significant development in an enforcement campaign that began in November 2025, when the CMA [opened investigations into both viagogo and StubHub UK](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/11/stubhub-viagogo-investigated-by-uk-regulator-over-price-transparency-rules/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) over concerns about mandatory fees appearing late in the ticket-buying process.

Although announced together, the investigations have followed different paths.

In June 2026, the regulator [ordered StubHub UK to refund more than 50,000 ticket buyers](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/06/uk-cma-orders-stubhub-uk-refund-50-k-fans/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) after concluding that the marketplace had violated consumer protection law by failing to disclose mandatory service and delivery fees upfront.

The CMA imposed a £889,200 fine and ordered more than £590,000 in refunds to 51,350 customers. StubHub UK admitted the violation and agreed to settle the case, receiving a 40% reduction in its financial penalty for resolving the matter early.

Importantly, the StubHub UK marketplace involved in that case is operated by Ticketbis S.L., a company separate from StubHub Holdings Inc., which owns viagogo. The UK StubHub business was separated following regulatory intervention in viagogo’s acquisition of StubHub from eBay.

The comparison illustrates how the CMA’s expanded enforcement powers can produce different outcomes depending on whether a business settles or proceeds through the regulator’s formal decision-making process.

The CMA said the viagogo investigation is the first case under its new direct consumer enforcement powers to reach the provisional infringement notice stage without the business having sought to enter settlement discussions.

That distinction does not establish wrongdoing by viagogo, but it means the regulator is moving toward a contested determination rather than announcing an agreed resolution.

## Potential Penalties Under Expanded Enforcement Powers

The case is proceeding under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, which gave the CMA substantially stronger authority to enforce consumer protection law directly when the relevant provisions took effect in April 2025.

Previously, many consumer protection enforcement actions required the regulator to pursue court proceedings. Under the new framework, the CMA can determine whether a company has violated consumer law and impose penalties without first obtaining a court judgment.

If a final infringement notice is issued, the regulator can impose financial penalties of up to 10% of a company’s worldwide turnover or £300,000, whichever is greater. It can also require corrective measures and compensation for affected consumers.

No financial penalty or customer refund requirement has been announced against viagogo in the current investigation.

The company can challenge the provisional findings, inspect supporting evidence and present its position to a decision-making group before the CMA determines whether a final infringement notice is warranted. Any final decision can also be appealed in court.

The case adds another chapter to viagogo’s history with British regulators. In 2018, the CMA secured a court order requiring changes to how the marketplace disclosed ticket information, including seating details, resale restrictions and seller identities. The regulator subsequently [suspended preparations for further court action in 2019](https://www.ticketnews.com/2019/09/cma-viagogo-court-action-halted/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) after the company addressed outstanding concerns.

The current investigation concerns different conduct and is being pursued under a more powerful enforcement framework.

It also highlights an important distinction in ticketing regulation. The CMA’s concerns are not about whether resale tickets are priced above their original face value, but whether consumers can see the full mandatory purchase price before deciding which tickets to buy.

That transparency requirement is relevant across the ticketing industry, including primary sellers and resale marketplaces. Upfront disclosure allows consumers to compare competing ticket offers on a consistent basis without discovering additional unavoidable charges during checkout.

The CMA has not publicly specified how many viagogo customers may have been affected or the total value of the fees under examination.

According to the regulator’s [published case timetable](https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/viagogo-consumer-protection-enforcement-case?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the next update is expected in spring 2027, following its consideration of the companies’ responses to the provisional findings.
