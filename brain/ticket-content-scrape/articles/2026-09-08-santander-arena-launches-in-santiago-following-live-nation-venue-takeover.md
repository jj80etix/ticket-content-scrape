# Santander Arena Launches in Santiago Following Live Nation Venue Takeover

## Summary

Santiago's former Movistar Arena has been rebranded Santander Arena under a naming-rights deal, less than a year after Live Nation became majority stakeholder in the 15,000-capacity venue via its BeLive Entertainment Group partnership. The relaunch is part of a broader Live Nation push into major Latin American venues, following similar moves in Buenos Aires and São Paulo. Ticketing at the venue remains with Puntoticket rather than shifting to Ticketmaster, despite Live Nation's ownership stake.

### Key Points

- Live Nation became majority stakeholder in the Santiago arena (formerly Movistar Arena) in a December 2025 deal with BeLive Entertainment Group; the venue hosts 170+ shows and 1M+ fans annually.
- Banco Santander is now the venue's naming-rights sponsor under a deal announced in July 2026, taking effect in September.
- Puntoticket remains the exclusive ticket seller — the venue has not moved to Ticketmaster despite Live Nation's ownership.
- The Santiago deal follows Live Nation's majority investment in Movistar Arena Buenos Aires and its new 21,000-capacity São Paulo arena, signaling a regional venue-consolidation pattern in Latin America.

### Industry Problem

Live Nation is steadily acquiring majority stakes in top-tier Latin American venues, extending the same venue-control playbook that U.S. antitrust litigation has scrutinized domestically — concentrating ownership of major concert venues in one company's hands even where local ticketing incumbents (like Puntoticket) are left in place for now.

### Proposed Solution

The item describes no product response, only continued M&A activity. For an independent primary platform, the right posture is to monitor whether Live Nation's Latin American venue acquisitions eventually pull ticketing exclusivity toward Ticketmaster (as they have in some other markets) — that shift, if it happens, would be the trigger to reassess competitive positioning in those regions.

Santiago’s former Movistar Arena has officially begun operating as Santander Arena, completing a naming-rights change announced earlier this summer at one of Latin America’s busiest live entertainment venues.

The new branding was formally unveiled Sunday night at an event attended by public officials, artists and other guests, according to Live Nation. Banco Santander becomes the venue’s lead sponsor under a naming-rights agreement first announced in July.

The change also comes less than a year after Live Nation became the majority stakeholder in the Santiago arena through a partnership with BeLive Entertainment Group, adding one of the region’s leading venues to the concert giant’s growing Latin American portfolio.

Live Nation announced that deal in December 2025, describing the then-Movistar Arena as a 15,000-capacity venue hosting more than 170 shows annually and attracting more than 1 million fans each year.

The building drew more than 1.8 million visitors in 2025, according to Live Nation and Santander. Its own website lists a maximum capacity of approximately 15,500, with 12,000 numbered seats, 3,200 removable floor seats, nine suites and 28 boxes.

The venue is located in Santiago’s Parque O’Higgins and traces its modern history to 2006, when it opened as Arena Santiago. Movistar took over the naming rights in 2008.

Santander and Arena Santiago announced their agreement July 29, saying the bank would become the arena’s principal sponsor beginning in September. The companies said at the time that the change would extend beyond simply replacing the venue’s name and branding.
