# Securities Investigations and Enforcement Newsletter – August 2026

## Summary

Troutman Pepper Locke's August 2026 Securities Investigations and Enforcement Newsletter covers general SEC/securities-law developments — a proposal on novel ETF regulation, a potential rollback of the SEC's "no-deny" settlement policy, an Examinations risk alert on adviser conflicts of interest, and AI-related privilege issues in securities litigation. None of the items concern the ticketing industry.

### Key Points
- SEC issued a public-comment request on regulating "Novel ETFs" pursuing innovative asset classes or strategies; comments due August 31, 2026.
- SEC sent a rule to OMB signaling a possible rollback of its "no-deny" settlement policy, with a related challenge pending at the Supreme Court.
- SEC's Division of Examinations issued a Risk Alert on economic conflicts of interest and fee practices for investment advisers.
- Newsletter also covers "AI washing" securities-litigation risk and attorney-client privilege questions around generative AI use by law firms.

### SEC Reform + Market Innovation

#### SEC Seeks Public Comment on Novel Exchange-Traded Funds

By [Terrance James Reilly](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/terrance-james-reilly/) and [Taylor M. Williams](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/taylor-m-williams/)

On June 30, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) issued a request for public comment (the Request) on exchange-traded funds (ETFs) that seek to invest in innovative asset classes or employ novel investment strategies (Novel ETFs). The Request signals the SEC’s intent to examine and potentially reshape the regulatory framework governing a rapidly evolving segment of the ETF market.

The Request was published in the *Federal Register* on July 2, 2026. Comments must be received on or before August 31, 2026.

[Read more](https://www.troutman.com/insights/sec-seeks-public-comment-on-novel-exchange-traded-funds/)

#### SEC’s Registered Offering Reform Proposal: A Potential Game-Changer for Public Non-Traded REITs

By [Mary Katherine Rawls](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/mary-katherine-rawls/) and [Heath D. Linsky](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/heath-d-linsky/)

Public non-traded real estate investment trusts (non-traded REITs) that register their offerings with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) under the Securities Act of 1933, as amended (Securities Act), have long been subject to a fragmented patchwork of state securities regulation. Under current law, Section 18(a) of the Securities Act restricts states from imposing registration or qualification requirements only on covered securities. Exchange-listed securities enjoy this protection, while unlisted securities, including shares of non-traded REITs, do not.

[Read more](https://www.troutman.com/insights/secs-registered-offering-reform-proposal-a-potential-game-changer-for-public-non-traded-reits/)

### Enforcement + Settlement Developments

#### SEC’s ‘No‑Deny’ Settlement Policy Heads for a Crossroads at OMB and the Supreme Court

By [Jay A. Dubow](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/jay-a-dubow/) and [Ghillaine A. Reid](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/ghillaine-a-reid/)

On May 8, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) quietly sent a final rule titled “ [Rescission of Policy Regarding Denials in Settlements of Enforcement Actions](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eoDetails?rrid=1372212) ” to the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) for review under Executive Order 12866. Although the text of the rule has not yet been released, the title strongly suggests that the SEC is preparing to roll back or significantly revise its decades‑old “no‑deny” settlement policy. That development arrives just as a major challenge to the policy is pending before the U.S. Supreme Court.

### Advisor Compliance + Emerging Litigation Risk

#### FUNDamental QuickStudy: SEC Division of Examinations Issues Risk Alert on Economic Conflicts of Interest for Investment Advisers

By [Michael K. Renetzky](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/michael-k-renetzky/), [Genna Garver](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/genna-garver/), [Tom Bohac](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/tom-bohac/), and [Brooke Labonski](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/brooke-labonski/)

On June 9, 2026, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission’s (SEC) Division of Examinations published a Risk Alert highlighting exam observations related to investment advisers’ obligations concerning economic conflicts of interest and associated fee practices. The Risk Alert signals that examiners will be taking a closer look at the economic incentives that advisers and their financial professionals may have when recommending products, services, or account types to clients, including the source and structure of compensation and other economic benefits.

[Read more](https://www.troutman.com/insights/fundamental-quickstudy-sec-division-of-examinations-issues-risk-alert-on-economic-conflicts-of-interest-for-investment-advisers/)

#### AI-Related Issues in Securities Cases: Privilege Pitfalls and ‘AI Washing’ Claims

By [Jay A. Dubow](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/jay-a-dubow/), [Erica Hall Dressler](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/erica-hall-dressler/), and [Milica Krnjaja](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/milica-krnjaja/)

Artificial intelligence (AI) tools have quickly become integral to users across the spectrum, from large public companies to individual consumers. Public companies now routinely reference “AI-driven” strategies on earnings calls, and boards are being briefed on AI risks alongside cybersecurity and ESG. The legal industry is no exception. Law firms and their clients increasingly rely on AI to draft disclosures, summarize documents, and prepare for litigation. Given this widespread use and popularity, AI-related issues have unsurprisingly begun to feature prominently in recent securities cases. These issues range from allegations of “AI washing” and overstated AI capabilities to disputes over whether clients’ use of generative AI tools can be shielded by the attorney-client privilege.
