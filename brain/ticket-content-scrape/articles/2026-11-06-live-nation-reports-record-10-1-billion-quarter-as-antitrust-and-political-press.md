# Live Nation Reports Record $10.1 Billion Quarter as Antitrust and Political Pressures Mount

## Summary

Live Nation reported record third-quarter 2026 revenue of $10.1 billion, up 19% year-over-year, driven by 61 million concert attendees (also a record, +19%) and 98 million fee-bearing tickets processed through Ticketmaster (+10%). The results land alongside an April antitrust jury verdict against the company, an unresolved settlement with the Trump administration, and the prospect of renewed congressional scrutiny following this week's midterm elections.

### Key Points
- Q3 revenue: $10.1bn (+19% YoY); operating income $892m (+13%); adjusted operating income $1.18bn (+14%).
- Ticketmaster processed 98 million fee-bearing tickets (+10%), generating $870m in ticketing revenue.
- Concert division revenue $8.75bn (+20%); international attendance +29% vs. +13% domestic growth.
- Results arrive amid an April antitrust jury verdict, an unresolved Trump-administration settlement, and possible new congressional scrutiny post-midterms.

### Highlights
- Ticketmaster: 98 million fee-bearing tickets processed in Q3, +10% YoY, $870m ticketing revenue.
- Company explicitly flags "potentially far-reaching antitrust remedies" and a "shifting political environment" as live risk factors alongside record growth.

### Industry Problem

Live Nation's continued double-digit growth and expanding international footprint sustain the scale argument at the center of DOJ's antitrust case, even as an April jury verdict and an unresolved settlement with the current administration leave the ultimate structural remedy (e.g., forced divestiture of Ticketmaster) unresolved.

### Proposed Solution

No build/improve action is implied by the item itself; this is a #monitor signal for an independent primary platform like Etix — watch whether the antitrust remedies (divestiture, ticketing-fee restrictions, venue-contract limits) materialize in a form that would reshape competitive access to venues or artists, which would be the trigger to reassess posture.

## Transcript

Live Nation Entertainment reported record third-quarter revenue of $10.1 billion on Thursday, November 5, as surging global concert attendance and continued growth at Ticketmaster reinforced the company's dominant position in live entertainment, even as it faces potentially far-reaching antitrust remedies and a shifting political environment in Washington.

The company's [third-quarter earnings report](https://news.livenationentertainment.com/news/live-nation-entertainment-reports-third-quarter-2026-results?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) showed revenue increasing 19% from a year earlier, while operating income rose 13% to $892 million. Adjusted operating income, Live Nation's preferred profitability measure, climbed 14% to $1.18 billion.

Concert attendance reached 61 million fans during the quarter, up 19%, with international markets accounting for much of the growth. Ticketmaster processed 98 million fee-bearing tickets, a 10% increase, while the company's ticketing business generated $870 million in revenue.

The results arrive at an unusual juncture for the entertainment giant. Live Nation continues expanding its concert promotion, venue operations and ticketing businesses while confronting the consequences of an April antitrust jury verdict, unresolved challenges to its settlement with the Trump administration and the prospect of renewed congressional scrutiny following Tuesday's midterm elections.

## Ticketmaster Growth and Record Concert Demand

Live Nation's concert division generated $8.75 billion in third-quarter revenue, a 20% increase, and $603 million in adjusted operating income, up 17%.

International concert attendance increased 29%, compared with 13% growth in North America. Attendance at third-party stadiums climbed more than 40%, accounting for over half of the company's worldwide fan growth.

President and CEO Michael Rapino said the results reflected sustained demand for live entertainment across markets.

"Through October, we've sold 170 million tickets, up 13% and already ahead of all of last year," Rapino said in the earnings announcement.

Live Nation now expects nearly 180 million fans to attend its concerts during 2026, with ticket sales for shows scheduled next year already running ahead of comparable 2025 levels.

Ticketmaster's adjusted operating income increased 10% to $316 million, supported by growing ticket volume and new commercial agreements.

The company reported adding ticketing contracts representing 23 million net new tickets, with more than 70% of that expansion coming from international markets. Concerts accounted for approximately 95% of the increase in fee-bearing ticket volume.

Ticketmaster also reported $11.2 billion in fee-bearing gross transaction value, an increase of 13%.

The secondary ticketing business presented a different picture. Resale gross transaction value was flat compared with the previous year, as growth in sports resale offset declining concert resale activity.

Live Nation characterized the decline in concert resale as consistent with its efforts to constrain ticket scalping. The company did not provide sufficient information to independently establish how much of the decline resulted from enforcement measures rather than changes in consumer demand, inventory availability or other market conditions.

For the full year, Live Nation now expects Ticketmaster's adjusted operating income to grow at a high-single-digit rate.

## Venue Expansion Becomes an Increasingly Important Profit Driver

Beyond ticketing, Live Nation's expanding portfolio of operated venues is becoming an increasingly significant component of its growth strategy.

Its Venue Nation business hosted 26 million fans during the quarter, up 17%, and is expected to exceed 75 million attendees for the full year.

The company remains on track to open or acquire 10 large venues during 2026, adding facilities with a combined annual attendance run rate approaching 8 million fans.

Live Nation expects to spend approximately $1.1 billion on capital expenditures this year, including $800 million directed toward venue expansion and improvements.

During Thursday's [earnings conference call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/06/live-nation-lyv-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), President and CFO Joe Berchtold emphasized the financial advantages of operating venues directly, explaining that Live Nation generally earns higher margins per fan at its own facilities than at third-party locations.

That distinction has implications beyond the company's financial performance.

Greater control over venues allows Live Nation to capture revenue from additional aspects of the concert experience, including premium seating, concessions, sponsorships and other onsite spending.

The company's U.S. amphitheaters reported double-digit increases in upgrade purchases and VIP revenue per ticket during the quarter. At the same time, unit sales from lower-priced value menus more than doubled.

The strategy also places Live Nation's expanding venue footprint squarely within the broader debate over competition in live entertainment.

State attorneys general pursuing antitrust remedies against the company have challenged the relationship between Live Nation's concert promotion operations, its venue interests and Ticketmaster's ticketing business.

The company's financial results demonstrate why those integrated operations are attractive to investors, while the ongoing litigation raises questions about whether the same business structure improperly restricts opportunities for competing promoters, venues and ticketing companies.

## Antitrust Verdict Leaves Major Questions Unresolved

Live Nation's strong quarterly performance comes nearly seven months after a federal jury found that Live Nation and Ticketmaster violated antitrust laws in a case brought by state attorneys general.

The April 15 verdict followed the Justice Department's decision to reach a settlement with Live Nation during the trial, an agreement that would preserve the combined company while imposing restrictions on certain ticketing and venue practices.

A bipartisan coalition of state attorneys general rejected that resolution and continued litigating, ultimately securing a jury verdict in their favor.

The case has since moved toward a remedies phase in which the court will consider what measures are necessary to address the violations established at trial. Potential remedies include structural changes to Live Nation's businesses, although no final remedy has been imposed.

Live Nation is challenging the verdict through post-trial motions and maintains that the jury's findings and damages award are legally flawed.

Meanwhile, the Justice Department's proposed settlement remains subject to judicial review under the Tunney Act, which requires a federal judge to determine whether the agreement serves the public interest.

The financial implications are already visible in Live Nation's regulatory filings.

The company's [third-quarter Form 10-Q](https://investors.livenationentertainment.com/sec-filings/quarterly-reports/content/0001335258-26-000053/lyv-20260930.htm?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) includes a $450 million charge recognized during the first nine months of 2026 related to the antitrust litigation and settlements. That amount represents the company's estimate of losses associated with settling states and the jury's damages award, rather than a new expense incurred during the third quarter alone.

The filing also warns that additional monetary penalties, compliance obligations or court-imposed remedies could materially affect the company's operations and financial results.

Live Nation separately faces a Federal Trade Commission lawsuit concerning allegations about ticket-price disclosures, purchase limits and ticket broker activity. The company has contested those claims, and the case remains pending.

## Trump Settlement Faces a Changed Political Landscape

The political circumstances surrounding Live Nation's antitrust proceedings have also changed considerably since the Justice Department reached its settlement in March.

The agreement attracted scrutiny partly because of the involvement of senior Trump administration officials in the negotiations.

As [TicketNews reported in October](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/live-nation-trump-settlement-disclosure-amended/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), Live Nation amended its court disclosures to acknowledge that President Donald Trump briefly appeared at a March 5 meeting where company representatives and federal officials finalized the settlement terms.

The revised filing also described a February conversation between Trump and Rapino during which the president asked about the status of the antitrust lawsuit.

Live Nation maintains that Trump did not participate in substantive settlement discussions during the March meeting and that no substantive settlement terms were discussed during the February conversation. The disclosures do not establish improper presidential influence over the agreement.

Nevertheless, the contacts have become part of the broader debate over the administration's decision to resolve the federal case without requiring Ticketmaster's separation from Live Nation.

That debate could take on additional significance following Tuesday's midterm elections, in which Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress.

Although the election results do not alter the jury verdict or automatically affect the Justice Department's settlement, Democratic control of Congress could create new opportunities for hearings, investigations and oversight of the administration's antitrust enforcement decisions.

The political transition also includes the departure from the Senate of Amy Klobuchar, one of Ticketmaster's most prominent congressional critics, following her election as Minnesota's next governor.

Klobuchar has spent years scrutinizing competition in the ticketing industry, including the consequences of Live Nation's 2010 merger with Ticketmaster. Her move to Minnesota's governorship removes an established voice from the Senate's antitrust debates, even as her party prepares to assume greater congressional oversight authority.

Whether that changing political balance produces additional pressure on Live Nation remains uncertain. The court proceedings will continue independently of the election results, and the company retains the ability to challenge the verdict and proposed remedies.

## Management Focuses on Growth as Legal Proceedings Continue

Despite those uncertainties, Live Nation executives largely concentrated on operating performance and future expansion during Thursday's earnings call.

Berchtold attributed the company's improved attendance outlook partly to stronger concert sell-through and lower cancellation rates. Management also highlighted early demand for major 2027 tours and continued opportunities to expand internationally.

The published earnings call transcript contains no substantive discussion of the antitrust verdict, the proposed Justice Department settlement or the potential implications of the midterm elections.

Investors instead heard a company projecting continued growth across concerts, ticketing, sponsorships and venue operations.

Year-to-date operating cash flow reached $2.1 billion, up 45%, while adjusted free cash flow increased 25% to $1.6 billion, providing additional financial resources for the company's expansion plans.

Live Nation has scheduled an investor presentation for November 10, when management is expected to provide further details about its venue development strategy, capital investments and longer-term growth opportunities.

For now, the company's financial trajectory and its legal outlook are moving along markedly different paths. Live Nation is attracting more fans, processing more tickets and expanding its venue portfolio, while courts continue weighing remedies that could ultimately change the structure of the business responsible for that growth.

The next phase of the antitrust proceedings will help determine how much of Live Nation's increasingly integrated operation can remain intact.
