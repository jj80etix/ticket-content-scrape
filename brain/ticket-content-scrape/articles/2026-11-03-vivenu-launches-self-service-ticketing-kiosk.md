# vivenu Launches Self-Service Ticketing Kiosk

## Summary

Ticketing platform vivenu has launched Kiosk, a self-service on-site terminal that lets attendees buy tickets without staff assistance, aiming to shorten box-office lines while still capturing buyer data for organizers. At racing venue Hockenheimring, an early adopter, Kiosk handled 20% of day-of-event ticket sales at its first event. The launch follows vivenu's recent MCP Server integration, which lets organizers query and operate their ticketing/inventory data through conversational AI tools including ChatGPT, Claude and Codex.

### Key Points
- Kiosk operates inside vivenu's existing ticketing stack, drawing on live inventory and processing payments via vivenu Managed Payments; tickets print with QR codes or go out by email/SMS.
- At Hockenheimring (a vivenu customer since 2023), Kiosk captured 20% of day-of sales at its first event while still collecting buyer names and emails for follow-up marketing.
- The launch comes shortly after vivenu's MCP Server integration, which lets organizers interact with live sales/inventory data via ChatGPT, Claude and Codex instead of traditional admin UIs.
- vivenu frames both launches as extending its single existing platform rather than requiring organizers to adopt separate systems.

### Highlights
- Kiosk processed 20% of all day-of-event ticket sales in its first deployment at Hockenheimring.
- Kiosk tickets and transactions stay inside vivenu's existing inventory, payments (vivenu Managed Payments) and access-control systems — no bolt-on third-party stack.
- vivenu's separate MCP Server integration lets ticketing operators query/operate sales and inventory data via ChatGPT, Claude and Codex conversational interfaces.

### Industry Problem
Box-office lines and staff workload at on-site, day-of-event sales are a recurring operational pain point for venues/organizers, and walk-up buyers who transact at a staffed window often don't leave the contact/email data that online buyers do, limiting post-event remarketing.

### Proposed Solution
vivenu's answer is a native self-service kiosk tied to its existing inventory, payments and access-control systems, paired separately with an MCP-based conversational-AI layer for ticketing operations. For Etix, this is a direct build/improve signal: a self-service on-site kiosk integrated with Etix's own platform could cut box-office queues and labor while capturing walk-up buyer data for remarketing, and the MCP/AI admin interface is a concrete competitor benchmark worth evaluating against Etix Assistant's own roadmap.

Ticketing technology provider vivenu has launched Kiosk, a self-service terminal designed to streamline on-site ticket purchases and reduce lines at venue box offices. The technology allows attendees to purchase tickets without staff assistance, freeing employees to focus on customers who require additional help.

Germany’s Hockenheimring racing circuit was among the first venues to adopt the technology. At its first event using Kiosk, the terminal processed 20% of all day-of-event ticket sales, reportedly shortening box-office queues while capturing customer names and email addresses for subsequent engagement.

The system operates within vivenu’s existing ticketing infrastructure, drawing from an organizer’s available inventory and processing card transactions through vivenu Managed Payments. Customers can receive printed tickets containing QR codes or email confirmations, with SMS delivery also available in the United States. Tickets are compatible with vivenu’s access-control technology.

“Someone buying at the entrance should get the same experience as someone buying on their phone, and the organiser should get the same data. Kiosk puts the purchase in the buyer’s hands and keeps every sale inside the platform organisers already run,” said Jens Teichert, vivenu’s chief technology officer.
