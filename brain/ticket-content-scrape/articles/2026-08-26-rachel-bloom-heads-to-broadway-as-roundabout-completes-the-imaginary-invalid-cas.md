# Rachel Bloom Heads to Broadway as Roundabout Completes ‘The Imaginary Invalid’ Cast

## Summary

Rachel Bloom will make her Broadway debut in Roundabout Theatre Company's revival of Molière's "The Imaginary Invalid," which begins previews September 25 at the Todd Haimes Theatre ahead of an October 22 opening. The limited engagement runs through November 22, with a cast led by Tony winner Bill Irwin.

### Key Points
- Previews begin September 25; opens October 22; runs through November 22.
- Rachel Bloom's Broadway debut, alongside Bill Irwin (also adapter) and a full ensemble cast.
- Uses a new Doug Skinner translation with Bill Irwin's adaptation.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/The-Imaginary-Invalid.webp)

Rachel Bloom will make her Broadway debut this fall as part of Roundabout Theatre Company’s upcoming revival of Molière’s “The Imaginary Invalid.”

Roundabout has announced the complete cast for the production, which will begin previews September 25 at the Todd Haimes Theatre before officially opening October 22. The limited Broadway engagement is scheduled to continue through November 22.

The revival uses a translation by Doug Skinner and an adaptation by Tony Award winner Bill Irwin, who will also appear on stage in the central role of Argan.

Irwin will be joined by Brooks Ashmanskas as Dr. Diafoirus and Purgon, Dylan Baker as Beralde and Hiram Delgado as Bonnefoy, Thomas and Fleurant. Crystal A. Dickinson will play Beline, while Daniel Croix Henderson will take on the role of Cleante. Ava Lalezarzadeh has been cast as Angelique, completing the principal company alongside Bloom.

Brandon J. Dirden will direct the production, marking his first Broadway credit as a director. The Tony Award nominee previously appeared on Broadway in productions including “Waiting for Godot.”

“The Imaginary Invalid,” first performed in the 17th century, centers on Argan, a wealthy hypochondriac whose fixation on his health and medical treatments creates turmoil for those around him. Irwin’s new adaptation will bring the Molière comedy back to Broadway with a cast drawn from theater, television and film.

The creative team includes Beowulf Boritt on scenic design, with costumes by Karen Perry and lighting by Allen Lee Hughes. John Gromada will serve as sound designer and composer of original music, while Nikiya Mathis will oversee hair and wig design. Lorenzo Pisoni has been tapped as movement director.
