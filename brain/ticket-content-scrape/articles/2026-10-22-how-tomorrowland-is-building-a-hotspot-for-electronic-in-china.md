# How Tomorrowland is building a ‘hotspot’ for electronic in China

## Summary

IQ Magazine profiles how Tomorrowland's organizers are localizing the festival brand to grow electronic music in China, following two sold-out editions in Shanghai with INS Land/Hero Esports.

### Key Points
- The Magic of Tomorrowland's second Shanghai edition (Oct. 17–18) drew 20,000+ visitors at Hero Dome, with Tiësto, Axwell, Lost Frequencies, Miss Monique and Alison Wonderland performing.

## Transcript

## How Tomorrowland is building a ‘hotspot’ for electronic in China

Organisers tell IQ that localising the global festival brand has opened the door for the genre's expansion

![Tomorrowland China](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Tomorrowland-China-©-Hero-Esports.png)

Tomorrowland China

The team behind Tomorrowland in China is helping pave the way for the mainstream adoption of electronic music in the market, purpose-building an immersive venue and localising marketing to bring the global brand to an insulated audience.

The Magic of Tomorrowland returned to Shanghai’s Hero Dome for its second edition at the weekend, 17-18 October, attracting over 20,000 total visitors to see acts like Tiësto, Axwell, Lost Frequencies, Miss Monique, and Alison Wonderland perform. The event is put together in partnership with Chinese nightlife complex INS Land and its sibling brand Hero Esports.

Two sell-out editions have not come without a learning curve for both organisers and fans alike, says Clinton Lau, head of music at INS Land and Hero Esports.

“Although Tomorrowland is a very established brand overseas, it is a very new brand in China, and the majority of Chinese don’t know it,” he tells *IQ*. “Whether we started our own brand or imported Tomorrowland, it would have been a similar process of starting at a sort of net-zero.”

Part of the success of creating a “one-of-a-kind” destination for what Lau considers a sort of “niche” genre in the market has come down to two core factors: localising content and government support.

“We want to introduce Tomorrowland brand DNA properly to China, so what we do is we take Tomorrowland brand guidelines, we take content, and we localise it. Not translate, localise,” he explains.

For digital campaigns, this centred around engaging raver communities with dedicated WeChat forums. For more tangible approaches, Lau says they looked to “peripheral markets” in fashion, food, and geography to meet people in the middle.

> “The scale of this stage, and the sort of immersive aspect of it, is one-of-a-kind globally”

“We did a fashion campaign collaboration with pop-up stores in Hong Kong and Shanghai with a famous streetwear store called Juice. They were successful because it brought in people from outside the music scene, from fashion to influencers to lifestyle, and we were able to spread a lot of awareness in both markets,” he shares.

Extending the reach of the brand resulted in over 80% of ticket buyers coming in from outside Shanghai, with the figure of customers entering with foreign passports sitting at 13.4% for this year’s edition.

Ultimately, the aim is to create a movement beyond a singular festival, in line with the wider offerings of the INS Land club brand or Hero Esports one.

“We’re very ambitious about the customer experience. We’re a culture company first,” Lau says. “We want to see how we can bring things to China, or even to the world, that don’t exist. The scale of this stage, and the sort of immersive aspect of it, is one of a kind globally, not just for China.”

Located along the Huangpu Riverside, Hero Dome underwent an intensive renovation after its debut with the previous edition, with operators installing a 99-meter, 25K resolution curved LED screen along with over 80 LED ceiling panels and around 120 new speakers to create a cohesive, immersive environment.

In addition to an interior revamp, there is a new three-story “Bali-style” beach club with a pool outside the venue, which Lau calls a potential market first.

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/10/Tomorrowland-China-©-Hero-Esports-2-430x269.png.webp)

The brand’s physical and conceptual foundation is due largely in part to “critical” government approval and support, Lau says, with support for this project going so far as to having the venue built on government land.

“We need to get the green light for them for any of our things to move forward – ticketing, marketing, festival dates – everything,” he says. “We have to get all the planning done very early, and then submit all the details down to the last screw for them to review, give feedback, then greenlight.”

An added benefit of government support is the approval of alcohol sales at the festival, something Lau describes as incredibly rare.

“For most events for the past 15 years, there was no alcohol allowed to be sold,” he says, adding that a show with Anyma this year did not have any alcohol sales. “But we’re gaining a lot of confidence with the government.”

Fans at the GA level were capped at three drinks per person this year, up from two in 2025, while VIP holders had the option of buying champagne or Tomorrowland’s own wine brand, Solo Vida. Budweiser is also the festival’s main sponsor and is the brand associated with the festival’s second stage.

> “We want the venue to be a hotspot for great talent”

“We’re working with government slowly to see how we could safely increase the limits, all for increasing customer experience, so we’re hoping that we could sell a little bit more in the future. But again, it’s safely increasing the amount,” he says.

What will be increasing soon is the Hero Dome’s touring activity, with the venue to host its first headline show next month, with Greek DJ Argy’s *NEWORLD* tour coming through on 15 November.

It’ll launch the next chapter for the space, which Lau says is set to host a variety of genres and touring entertainment offerings.

“It’ll be a wide range, and we want the venue to be a hotspot for great talent,” he says.

Shanghai will continue to be home to the Tomorrowland brand in China for the foreseeable future, though Lau says the team is exploring other formats and locations for future editions. Next year’s dates are to be announced, though it is confirmed.

“It was a good event last year, but it was not so cohesive. This year we took it to the next level,” Lau concludes.
