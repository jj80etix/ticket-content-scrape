# Choosin’ Texas (and Ella), Burnin’ Barn, Returnin’ Taylor: Stars Shine At CMAs

## Summary

Recap of the 60th Annual CMA Awards at Bridgestone Arena in Nashville (Nov. 18), hosted by Lainey Wilson. Ella Langley dominated the night, sweeping Single, Song, Album, Female Vocalist and Entertainer of the Year.

### Key Points
- Langley won five awards including Entertainer of the Year for "Choosin' Texas" and album *Dandelion*.
- Taylor Swift made a surprise performance and later presented Brooks & Dunn the Willie Nelson Lifetime Achievement Award.
- Tributes honored Dolly Parton and the late John Anderson; Stephen Wilson Jr. won New Artist of the Year.
- Chris Stapleton won Male Vocalist of the Year for the ninth time.

## Transcript

![GettyImages 2305816250](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/GettyImages-2305816250.jpg)

Ella Langley and Miranda Lambert perform onstage during the 60th Annual Country Music Association Awards at Bridgestone Arena on November 18, 2026 in Nashville, Tennessee. (Photo by Kevin Mazur/Getty Images for CMA)

The CMA Awards returned to Bridgestone Arena in Nashville for their 60th Annual ceremony Nov. 18. Host Lainey Wilson returned for her third stint as emcee, opening the show in a tribute duet with Vince Gill on Dolly Parton’s “I Will Always Love You,” which has become the go-to song to honor the legendary artist since her death in September.

As host, Wilson is capably entertaining, this year leaning heavily on her hosting predecessors to help present awards and performances. She teamed up with Brad Paisley for the opening monologue which he helped to write, spoofing classic country tunes with irritatingly modern topics. It’s nothing new, but the crowd was into it, especially when the duo channeled Lynn Anderson to take a playful jab at country’s prodigal daughter Taylor Swift’s recent arena ~~tour~~ nuptials, singing “I beg your pardon, I never promised you Madison Square Garden.” They also made a nod to the controversy surrounding Sammy Kershaw’s request to walk the red carpet ahead of this year’s ceremony, coming on strong but softening quickly.

*George and Tammy* actress Jessica Chastain presented the first award of the night, Single of the Year, to relative newcomer Ella Langley for the inescapable “Choosin’ Texas,” beginning her home run evening. From here, it didn’t take a crystal ball to see that Ella was going to sweep.

The first non-tribute performance came from Kacey Musgraves and Miranda Lambert, making the live debut of their collaboration “Horses and Divorces.” It’s a solid country tune and a great show of solidarity from the former sort-of rivals, proving the grass really can grow back greener after everything burns down.

Speaking of fire, Zach Top performed a new single “Moonshine” in front of a literal burning barn, then dueted with Dierks Bentley on “Swingin” in tribute to Country Music Hall of Fame member John Anderson, who died in October.

Three awards were announced outside of the television broadcast: Musician of the Year to Charlie Worsham, Musical Event of the Year to “McArthur,” and Music Video of the Year to “Choosin’ Texas.”

British folk-rock band Mumford and Sons came from across the pond to perform the live debut of their song “Here” with Chris Stapleton. Marcus Mumford and Stapleton are both excellent vocalists, and with a seasoned backing band such as the Sons, they put on a great performance, no bells and whistles needed.

Next up, *911: Nashville* star Kimberly Williams Paisley and Darius Rucker presented New Artist of the Year to a very emotional Stephen Wilson Jr. By now, it’s looking like his career pivot from bioengineer to full-time musician has paid off.

Ella Langley then took the stage to perform “Loving Life Again.” While she may lack the vocal strength of some of her contemporaries, her songs are undeniably popular and the audience ate up every minute of the sentimental performance, backed with strings and the backdrop of a beautiful forest.

Every cowgirl’s boyfriend Glen Powell and Shaboozey presented the CMA award for Song of the Year to Ella Langley for “Choosin’ Texas.” Langley thanked everyone once again, saying, “This song is the gift that keeps on giving, but I will take every gift it gives us.”

Florida Georgia Line – who were supposedly split or at least on hiatus – presented Vocal Duo of the Year to the outlaw stalwarts Brooks and Dunn, their 17th win in the category.

Kimberly Williams Paisley returned to the stage to introduce a behind the scenes segment, oh wait! It’s Taylor Swift performing her nominated song “I Knew It, I Knew You” from *Toy Story 5* mashed up with her 2006 hit “Our Song.” The performance featured cameos from many special guests, including Keith Urban on the banjo, and Easter eggs hinting towards the re-release of her debut album. She may have her detractors, but to her credit, she dances to the music even when the cameras aren’t watching.

Lady A offers up a brief tribute to the Stanley Brothers and introduces Tim McGraw’s performance of “Don’t Take the Girl,” his first time performing the throwback hit at the CMAs.

Leanne Morgan hit on Glen Powell in a charming way that only she could pull off and introduced her co-star Lainey Wilson’s performance of a brand new song, “Just a Woman.” Wilson is an incredibly consistent vocalist, and made it seem like nothing to debut a new song in between jokes and outfit changes.

Vocal Group of the Year was awarded to the Red Clay Strays before up-and-coming starlets Avery Anna, Carter Faith, and Emily Anne Roberts introduced Megan Moroney’s soaring performance of her heartstring puller “Beautiful Things.”

Lainey Wilson returned to her hosting duties to introduce a performance by Tucker Wetmore, a medley of his song “Who Told You That” and Hank Williams Jr.’s “All My Rowdy Friends are Coming Over Tonight.” Wetmore is shaping up to be Country’s Benson Boone.

Carly Pearce and inexplicably Michael Buble then presented Album of the Year to Ella Langley’s *Dandelion*. She told the crowd, “We’re just getting started,” and evidently she meant that in the context of winning CMA awards.

Why is Halestorm playing “Boot Scootin’ Boogie”? Even Brooks and Dunn don’t know. They are excellent performers, but the choice to include them was an unusual choice. The tribute performance lineup was rounded out by Marcus King, the Brothers Osborne, HARDY, Luke Bryan and Brad Paisley. Afterwards, Taylor Swift made a surprise return to the stage to present a still baffled Brooks and Dunn with the Willie Nelson Lifetime Achievement Award.

After Zack Top orchestrated a military family reunion (sponsored by Chevrolet), Luke Combs performed “Miss You Here” with no introduction – the lights came up, he ripped and roared, and then disappeared into the night.

Miranda Lambert and Ella Langley returned to the stage for the TV debut of their collaboration “Snakeskin Boots.” Then, Cece Winans, Leanne Womack, and Martina McBride present Female Vocalist of the Year to, you guessed it, Ella Langley.

Stephen Wilson Jr. and Chris Stapleton joined forces to perform “I’m a Song.” For as strong as Stapleton is on his own, he may be even better as a supporting voice. It’s a skill that’s hard to master, supporting in a way that makes everyone else sound better, and he’s mastered it.

Immediately afterwards, he took home Male Vocalist of the Year, his ninth win of the title despite not having any recent releases to his name.

Matt Rife introduced a performance from “his favorite band,” Red Clay Strays, who play their song “Revival” in front of what can only be described as the barricade from a production of *Les Miz*, then Lainey Wilson finally showed us that she doesn’t have a bald spot by arriving hatless with Vince Gill to announce that the final award of the evening, Entertainer of the Year. Who won? Obviously Ella Langley, who Wilson sweeps off the stage with a bedazzled broom just before the lights go down.
