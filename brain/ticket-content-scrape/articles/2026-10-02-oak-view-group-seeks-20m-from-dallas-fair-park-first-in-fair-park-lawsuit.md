# Oak View Group Seeks $20M From Dallas, Fair Park First in Fair Park Lawsuit

## Summary

Oak View Group sued the city of Dallas and nonprofit Fair Park First seeking up to $20 million over the collapse of its Fair Park management arrangement, a sharp escalation from the roughly $5 million pre-suit demand reported in March. OVG alleges Dallas withheld operating funds, improperly ended the arrangement, and concealed the extent of Fair Park's deteriorated infrastructure; the city has previously blamed OVG for deferred maintenance and accountability failures under the two-contract (Fair Park First/Spectra-OVG) management structure established in 2018.

### Key Points
- New suit seeks ~$20 million, versus the ~$5 million initially demanded in a December 2025 notice.
- OVG alleges Dallas withheld funds and hid Fair Park's infrastructure condition; Dallas has cited deferred maintenance and a 2024 finding that $5.7M in restricted donor funds were misused.
- Dispute stems from a 2018 two-contract structure where Dallas contracted with nonprofit Fair Park First, which then subcontracted management to OVG's predecessor, Spectra/Global Spectrum.

Oak View Group has sued the city of Dallas and nonprofit Fair Park First seeking as much as $20 million over the collapse of its former Fair Park management arrangement, escalating a dispute that previously surfaced as a roughly $5 million pre-suit demand.

Subsequent reporting from [The Dallas Morning News](https://www.dallasnews.com/news/dallas-local-government/article/fair-park-dallas-lawsuit-22457909.php) and [WFAA](https://www.wfaa.com/article/news/local/fair-park-dallas-oak-view-group-sues/287-20112455-6150-40a3-b7b4-45ef2b7e87b9) provides a substantially broader picture of the case than initial coverage Thursday, which described approximately $5.7 million at issue. The later reports say OVG is seeking $20 million from Dallas and Fair Park First and alleges that the two withheld operating funds, improperly ended the management arrangement and failed to disclose the extent of Fair Park’s deteriorated infrastructure.

## New Lawsuit Goes Beyond Earlier $5 Million Demand

The October case is distinct from the legal demand that became public in March. That month, news surfaced that OVG was demanding roughly $5 million from Dallas after the city terminated the Fair Park arrangement. The company alleged at the time that Dallas had refused to pay invoices and reimburse expenses for work performed before the relationship ended.

The lawsuit also puts the condition of Fair Park itself back at the center of the dispute.

Dallas officials have [repeatedly criticized the condition in which Fair Park was left](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/08/neglect-and-scandal-fair-park-struggles-with-maintenance-ovg-controversy/) when the city resumed direct control in September 2025. City officials cited deferred maintenance, outstanding obligations and a management structure they said made accountability difficult.

OVG is now advancing a competing account. According to the subsequent reporting, the company alleges Dallas failed to disclose the poor condition of Fair Park’s infrastructure, reduced or withheld money needed to operate the property and then blamed OVG for problems that preceded or resulted from those funding decisions.

TicketNews previously reported in March 2025 on [Fair Park First’s position that OVG should shoulder costs](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/03/fair-park-says-ovg-should-fund-facilities-repairs/) associated with some facilities repairs as the city and its private managers argued over deteriorating infrastructure.

## Two-Contract Structure Complicated Accountability

The dispute grew out of an unusual management arrangement established in 2018.

Dallas contracted with Fair Park First, a private nonprofit, to take over management of the city-owned Fair Park. Fair Park First was designated an independent contractor with broad responsibility for operating, maintaining and improving the property.

The agreement in turn authorized Fair Park First to retain Global Spectrum LP, then doing business as Spectra Venue Management and now part of Oak View Group, as its subcontractor to carry out Fair Park management services. Dallas therefore did not have the same direct contractual relationship with OVG that it has with operators under some other city agreements. A Texas appeals court laid out that structure in an unrelated Fair Park case decided in August.

That division of responsibility became a central problem after [questions emerged in 2024 about Fair Park finances](https://www.ticketnews.com/2024/10/report-non-profit-ovg360-misused-5-7m-in-donations-for-dallas-fair-park/).

An accounting review determined that approximately $5.7 million of more than $17 million in restricted donor receipts had been used for operating expenses or projects outside the purposes specified by donors. The money was spent at Fair Park, but not for the projects to which those donations had been restricted.

Responsibility was vigorously disputed. Fair Park First initially placed responsibility on OVG, while OVG said it handled accounts and payments according to the nonprofit’s instructions and written approvals. Language assigning fault to OVG was removed from the accountant’s report following the company’s objection, leaving the underlying financial shortfall established but responsibility contested.

Dallas issued Fair Park First a formal notice of default in November 2024 and notified the nonprofit on June 18, 2025 that it was terminating the management agreement. The termination also ended Fair Park First’s sub-management relationship with OVG. Following the contractual 90-day wind-down, Dallas Park and Recreation took over daily operations on Sept. 16, 2025.

The new lawsuit now challenges the financial consequences of that termination and, according to the subsequent reporting, alleges the management arrangement was wrongfully ended before its expected term.

## Dallas Is Also Winding Down OVG’s Convention Center Role

The lawsuit arrives as Dallas is separately preparing to end another OVG management relationship.

Global Spectrum has continued managing the Kay Bailey Hutchison Convention Center under a direct contract with Dallas, an arrangement structurally separate from Fair Park. On Sept. 23, the City Council [approved a five-month extension worth up to $2.68 million](https://cityofdallas.legistar.com/MeetingDetail.aspx?GUID=5DC0113C-2FE2-4E49-89EA-4ED2EB6B64FE&ID=1237124&Options=info%7C), carrying that agreement through Feb. 26, 2027 unless terminated sooner.

The extension was not designed as a longer-term renewal.

[During the Sept. 23 council debate](https://dallastx.new.swagit.com/videos/402001), Convention and Event Services Director Rosa Fleming said the five-month period was intended “to transition away” from OVG as Dallas reduces the usable portion of the existing convention center during construction. Another city official said Dallas plans to move OVG’s current subcontractor agreements either directly to the city or to Visit Dallas, after which OVG “will no longer be the management entity at the convention center.”

That decision predates the newly reported Fair Park lawsuit. The same council discussion also provides a useful marker for when the litigation emerged. After initially saying she believed an OVG lawsuit existed, a city attorney corrected herself and said Dallas had not been served and did not believe OVG had filed one at that point.

City staff also drew a distinction between OVG’s work at the convention center and the disputed Fair Park arrangement. Fleming told council members that the convention-center operation had passed its audits and was subject to substantially closer city oversight, including regular reporting and contract-compliance reviews. She said the contractual structure was different from Fair Park’s indirect nonprofit/subcontractor model.

At the same meeting, a Dallas Park and Recreation official told council members that the city had initiated an audit related to OVG’s Fair Park work and had received what he described as “phase one findings.” The city was considering whether to proceed with a more detailed second phase, but no findings from the review were disclosed during the discussion.

## Fair Park Fight Moves From Negotiation to Litigation

Dallas has directly operated Fair Park for more than a year while attempting to stabilize finances, address deferred maintenance and revise how the property is managed. The city has also continued making separate payments tied to obligations incurred during the former arrangement, including approving more than $206,000 to Global Spectrum on behalf of Fair Park First in June 2025 for inclement-weather shelter services.

The $20 million lawsuit moves a dispute that spent much of the past two years in audits, mediation, legal notices and public disputes into court.

Its significance extends beyond the amount OVG is seeking. At the center of the case is a management structure in which Dallas owned Fair Park, Fair Park First held the city’s management contract and OVG performed most day-to-day operations under a separate subcontract. City officials later cited that structure as one reason accountability had become difficult, while OVG now alleges that the city and Fair Park First failed to meet obligations necessary for the operator to perform its role.

The litigation could determine not only what, if anything, Dallas and Fair Park First owe OVG from the final years of the arrangement, but also how responsibility for operating shortfalls, maintenance costs and the termination itself was allocated under contracts the parties have disputed for years.
