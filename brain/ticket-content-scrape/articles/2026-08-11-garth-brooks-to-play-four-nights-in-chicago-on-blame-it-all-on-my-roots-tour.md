# Garth Brooks to Play Four Nights in Chicago on ‘Blame It All On My Roots’ Tour

## Summary

Garth Brooks has added four Chicago-area dates to his "Blame It All On My Roots" arena tour, playing Rosemont, Illinois' Allstate Arena September 10–13. The run includes an opening show, two evening performances, and a Sunday matinee, and joins previously announced Indianapolis and Denver stops; tickets for the new dates are on sale now.

### Key Points

- New dates: Allstate Arena, Rosemont IL — Sept 10 (opener), 11 and 12 (evenings), 13 (matinee).
- Joins previously announced dates: Indianapolis Gainbridge Fieldhouse (Aug 20–23) and Denver Ball Arena (Sept 4–5).
- Tour revives Brooks' in-the-round "Drum Pod" stage design from his 1990s arena shows, featured on the best-selling live album in music history, Double Live.
- Performances from the tour will be recorded for an upcoming live release, "Killer Live."
- Tickets for the Chicago-area shows are now available.

Country superstar Garth Brooks has slowly been releasing more dates on his “ [Blame It All On My Roots](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/garth-brooks-announces-blame-it-all-on-my-roots-arena-tour/) ” tour. The next stop? Chicago.

Brooks will take over Rosemont, Illinois’ Allstate Arena on Thursday, September 10 for an opening show, followed by evening performances on September 11 and 12. The brief run will wrap-up with a matinee show on Sunday, September 13.

Tickets for the newly-announced dates are now available.

Earlier this year, Brooks revealed the “Blame It All On My Roots” tour, which aims to bring his legendary arena shows to fans in a more intimate setting. The first pair of shows on the tour were announced for Indianapolis’ Gainbridge Fieldhouse on August 21 and 22. [Additional shows were added](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/garth-brooks-announces-additional-indianapolis-concerts/) in Indianapolis, slated for August 20 and 23, [followed by two gigs](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/garth-brooks-expands-blame-it-all-on-my-roots-tour-with-denver-stop/) at Denver’s Ball Arena on September 4 and 5.

For the upcoming shows, Brooks will bring back his Drum Pod stage, an in-the-round setup that was a major part of his arena performances during the 1990s. The stage design placed Brooks in the center of the venue, allowing fans on all sides of the arena to have a close view of the concert.

The Drum Pod stage was featured on Double Live, which remains the best-selling live album in music history. Brooks will also record performances from the new tour for an upcoming live release titled “Killer Live.”

“Going back into the arenas is about putting the stadium show in a box,” Brooks said in a statement. “Every seat is a great seat. This is personal.”

For more information and additional details, fans of the “Friends in Low Places” singer can visit [garthbrooks.com](http://garthbrooks.com/).
