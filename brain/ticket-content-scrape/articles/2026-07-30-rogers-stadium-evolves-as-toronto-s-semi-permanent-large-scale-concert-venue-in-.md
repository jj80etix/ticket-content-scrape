# Rogers Stadium Evolves As Toronto’s Semi-Permanent Large-Scale Concert Venue In Second Season

## Summary

Live Nation Canada gave a midseason operational update on Toronto's Rogers Stadium, its 50,000-capacity temporary outdoor venue, highlighting improved exit times, higher transit usage, sustainability progress, and expanded fan amenities in its second season. The stadium has drawn 482,000 fans across 10 shows so far in 2026, with high-profile acts including Bruno Mars, Karol G, BTS, and AC/DC on the schedule.

### Key Points
- 700,000+ fans across 14 concerts this season have generated over CAD$500M in economic activity for Toronto/Ontario, including CAD$388M in GDP and CAD$150M in tax revenue, supporting 3,000+ full-time-equivalent jobs.
- Post-show exit times improved from over an hour last year to no more than 45 minutes this year, even at sold-out capacity.
- More than half of concertgoers now use public transit; the TTC and Metrolinx have added service on concert nights.
- Noise monitoring is funded by the venue, with real-time response to 311 complaints and continuous property-line sound measurement to stay within bylaw limits.
- Sustainability initiatives target a 70% waste-diversion rate in 2026 and roughly 10,000 recovered meals through food-recovery partnerships.
- The venue app, used for transit, maps, food, and event updates, has been downloaded more than 76,000 times this season.
- Rogers Stadium operates under a five-year lease from developer Northcrest on the former Downsview Airport lands, with about three summers remaining before longer-term redevelopment of the site proceeds.

Halfway through its sophomore summer concert season, Live Nation Canada says “pre-season adjustments” at Toronto’s 50,000-capacity Rogers Stadium — a temporary outdoor structure in the city’s north end operating under a five-year lease with land developer Northcrest — have addressed concerns raised last year, including faster and safer exiting and noise reduction for local residents.

Speaking to media via Zoom, an operations spokesperson said, “We started the season with a strong operational plan and every concert has given us the opportunity to make thoughtful improvements that have strengthened both our operations and the fan experience.”

According to Live Nation Canada, Rogers Stadium welcomed more than 700,000 fans across 14 concerts, generating more than CAD$500 million (D $370M) in economic activity for Toronto and Ontario. That included CAD$388 million ($287.12M) in GDP and CAD$150 million \[$106.78M\] in tax revenue and support for the equivalent of over 3,000 full-time jobs.

Since opening in May, 482,000 fans have attended 10 shows, including five sold-out performances by Bruno Mars to kick off the season.

After just one show in July by Karol G on July 29 — the stadium’s first Latin show and first by a female solo headliner — the next two months will see shows by Foo Fighters; Guns N’ Roses; two by Chris Stapleton a few weeks apart; the Usher and Chris Brown co-headliner; two sold-out performances by BTS, and the season closer, on Sept. 16, AC/DC.

“We have had great success in filling the stadium and most of the shows this summer are reaching close to capacity,” according to the spokesperson. “I do believe that the demand still exists and that there are enough artists that can sell tickets to fill it. We have various configurations, so a sellout isn’t always necessary to have an incredible event and see great success for the artist as well.”

The spokesperson said the stadium has attracted a diverse audience. “Already to date, we’ve welcomed fans of pop, hip-hop, indie folk and country with a few more demographics to come, demonstrating the versatility of Rogers Stadium and its ability to host world-class events across a wide range of genres.”

Ensuring tens of thousands of fans can exit the venue safely and efficiently has been one of Live Nation Canada’s top priorities this season, along with making it easy for concertgoers to get to a venue located outside Toronto’s downtown core via the three nearby subway stations.

More than half of concertgoers are now choosing public transit, helping reduce vehicle congestion around the venue. Live Nation Canada has worked closely with the Toronto Transit Commission (TTC), which has added subway and bus service on concert nights, as well as Metrolinx, which provides additional options for fans travelling to Union Station and outside the city.

For those who drive or use rideshare services, parking and shuttle programs have continued, while new pedestrian routes have helped disperse crowds more effectively at the end of the night.

The spokesperson said the average exit time after concerts has improved compared with last year. “Last year it was an hour to just a bit over an hour to get people out of here, and this year, even at our peak periods where we’ve seen our sellout crowds, it’s taken no longer than 45 minutes.”

Beyond transportation, Rogers Stadium has expanded the on-site experience to create more of a festival atmosphere. Fan spaces include the Annex and Birkenstock lounges, which offer food, beverages and air-conditioned areas, as well as Terminal 81, featuring covered seating, charging stations, food, drinks and pre-show entertainment.

The Rose Garden, which replaced last year’s Ferris wheel, has become another popular gathering spot where fans can chill and take photos. “We’ve designed Rogers Stadium to have the best of both a stadium and a festival feel.”

Noise management is another major focus for the venue and nearby residents.

“We cover the cost of noise monitoring, which includes several teams of municipal bylaw officers measuring sound levels from our property lines and responding to calls around the area,” the spokesperson said, adding, “They’re responding to 311 calls in real time if people are calling in during the show, as well as monitoring from our property lines. We continue to measure sound inside the stadium so we know what our levels are and can stay within, or below, what the bylaw requires.”

The promoter is also focusing on sustainability, with a goal of achieving a 70% waste diversion rate in 2026 through its waste sorting program. Live Nation expects to recover approximately 10,000 meals this season through food recovery partnerships, while also increasing compostable serviceware, local sourcing and plant-based food options.

“We’re on track to achieve a 70% waste diversion rate in 2026 through our 24-7 waste-sorting program, helping to keep more material out of landfill,” said the spokesperson. “We’ve also expanded our food recovery efforts and expect to recover approximately 10,000 meals this season through our community partnerships, ensuring surplus food is put to good use rather than going to waste.

“Beyond that, we’re continuing to work with Liberty Oak View Entertainment to make our food and operations more sustainable by using compostable serviceware, sourcing local ingredients where possible, and offering more plant-based venue options.”

The two sold-out BTS concerts will feature a unique setup, a stage positioned in the centre of the floor in a 360-degree configuration. Additional seating will be added where the stage is normally located, keeping capacity the same. “We are making operational updates to help ensure a safe and enjoyable experience for all of the BTS fans, including the BTS Army. Early and overnight queuing will not be permitted.”

The venue will also host a dedicated pre-show merchandise shopping day on Aug. 21, as well as “Instagrammable moments on site and unique activations specific to BTS.”

The spokesperson encouraged fans attending upcoming concerts to download the Rogers Stadium app, which provides transportation details, maps, food options and event updates.

“It also allows us to communicate directly with fans if there are any important updates before or during the event. The app has been downloaded more than 76,000 times this season, so we’re very encouraged that fans are using it and making the most of it.”

As Rogers Stadium moves into the second half of its concert calendar, Live Nation Canada says the goal remains balancing large-scale entertainment with a positive experience for fans, artists and the surrounding community. “We’ve learned something with every concert and continue to make adjustments along the way to continuously improve the experience for fans, artists, and most importantly our neighbours.”

Live Nation Canada has another three summers remaining on the grounds. Northcrest, which is transforming the 370-acre former Downsview Airport lands into YZD, a sustainable, mixed-use smart city district housing over 55,000 residents, said Rogers Stadium is expected to operate for approximately five years, and “was never contemplated as part of YZD’s early development phases.”

The developer said in a statement that the temporary venue is helping “bring energy, jobs, investment, activity and interest to the site, while the longer-term redevelopment plans advance. Northcrest is currently focused on YZD’s first neighbourhoods…The stadium lands will ultimately be required to support major infrastructure needed to unlock future phases of development across the site, including roads and servicing. We will share additional details as those phases come into focus.”
