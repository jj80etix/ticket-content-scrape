# FTC Live to build outdoor venue in Quito’s Parque Bicentenario

## Summary

Ecuadorian promoter FTC Live will build a new 8,500-seat semi-outdoor venue, Arena Bicentenario, in Quito's Parque Bicentenario, opening December 2026. The privately operated structure, expandable to 15,000-18,000 with mixed indoor-outdoor configurations, is pitched as a modern, weatherproof alternative to the city's existing arenas to attract more international touring productions.

### Key Points
- Arena Bicentenario opens December 2026 with 8,500-seat standard capacity, expandable to 15,000-18,000 in mixed indoor-outdoor configurations.
- FTC Live acquired and will privately operate the structure on city-owned parkland (the former Quito airport site).
- Framed as increasing operational flexibility for international touring acts versus existing Ecuadorian arena infrastructure.
- Won't be ready in time for FTC Live al Parque, the promoter's new Maroon 5-headlined festival launching this year.

## FTC Live to build outdoor venue in Quito’s Parque Bicentenario

The promoter framed the project as a modern upgrade to Ecuador's existing arenas and as meeting the operational needs of international touring acts

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/unnamed-2.png)

Arena Bicentenario (impression) © FTC Live

Ecuadorian promoter Feel the Club (FTC Live) is to build a new semi-outdoor venue structure in the capital Quito, which is set to upgrade the concert capabilities of the city’s Parque Bicentenario.

A new roof and stage complex, known as Arena Bicentenario, will open in December 2026 on the site, designed to offer weatherproof production capacity and a “modern alternative” to existing arena infrastructure in Ecuador, the promoter said.

It will come with a capacity of 8,500 fans in a standard configuration, though the open sides of the structure will allow mixed indoor-outdoor crowds of up to 15,000, or 18,000, depending on format.

FTC Live said the new space will be “comparable to the city’s established indoor arenas while creating additional opportunities for international tours that require greater operational flexibility and year-round reliability”.

The promoter said it had “acquired” the structure and that it would be privately operated, though the wider Parque Bicentenario is owned by the City of Quito. The green space sits on the site of Quito’s former airport, and is currently undergoing a range of regeneration and vegetation conservation efforts.

> “This investment allows us to bring more of the world’s biggest productions to Quito”

The space is commonly used by FTC for staging some of Ecuador’s biggest live events, but the new structure will not be in place in time for the first edition of [FTC Live al Parque](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/04/maroon-5-to-lead-new-ecuadorian-festival/), a new one-day festival the promoter announced earlier this year.

After securing Maroon 5 as headliner for the new festival, FTC Live CEO Dominguez told *IQ* in April that a “priority was having an Anglo act”, explaining that “Ecuador is used to having so many Latin acts that doing something different, more international, was key”.

FTC live said Arena Bicentenario represents a “significant investment in Ecuador’s live entertainment ecosystem” which will “allow FTC Live to expand its ability to develop, produce and host a broader range of concerts and live experiences while continuing to strengthen Quito’s position within the Latin American touring circuit”.

Speaking upon the announcement of Arena Bicentenario, Dominguez said the new venue “represents much more than a new venue; it reflects our long-term commitment to Ecuador and our belief that artists, fans, and the live entertainment industry deserve world-class infrastructure.

“This investment allows us to bring more of the world’s biggest productions to Quito while strengthening Ecuador’s position as one of the region’s leading destinations for live entertainment,” the CEO added.
