# Macklemore Says Nine U.S. Arenas Rejected Palestine Benefit Tour as MSG Questions His Draw

## Summary

Macklemore says nine major U.S. arenas — including Madison Square Garden, Barclays Center and Crypto.com Arena — withdrew previously available dates for his "Free Palestine" benefit tour once they learned the shows would benefit Palestinian organizations; MSG Entertainment publicly questioned whether he could draw an arena-sized audience. The dispute follows his September removal from Ed Sheeran's stadium tour over pro-Palestinian remarks.

### Key Points
- Nine venues named: MSG, Prudential Center, Barclays Center, UBS Arena, Intuit Dome, Kia Forum, Crypto.com Arena, Chase Center, Climate Pledge Arena.
- Macklemore alleges the venues' decisions reflect industry-figure influence; no evidence of coordination among venues has been established.
- Follows his September removal from Ed Sheeran's U.S. stadium tour after pro-Palestinian comments during his opening set.

Macklemore says nine major U.S. arenas refused to host his Free Palestine benefit tour after initially indicating that dates were available, prompting a sharp response from Madison Square Garden Entertainment, which questioned whether the rapper could attract an arena-sized audience.

In a [November 6 statement posted to Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfaHmBTgRv0/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), Macklemore alleged that his team had received available dates at the venues through the end of 2026, only to see those opportunities disappear once the facilities learned the concerts would benefit Palestinian organizations.

The nine venues he identified were Madison Square Garden in New York; Prudential Center in Newark, New Jersey; Barclays Center in Brooklyn; UBS Arena in Elmont, New York; Intuit Dome and Kia Forum in Inglewood, California; Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles; Chase Center in San Francisco; and Climate Pledge Arena in Seattle.

“Once they found out these were my benefit shows for Palestine, those dates were suddenly off the table,” Macklemore wrote.

Macklemore characterized the rejections as an effort to suppress his political advocacy, claiming that the venues’ decisions reflected the influence of powerful entertainment industry figures. However, the circumstances of the individual booking discussions have not been independently established, and no evidence has emerged demonstrating that the nine arenas coordinated their decisions.

The allegations represent the latest development in a controversy that began when Macklemore was removed from Ed Sheeran’s U.S. stadium tour in September following pro-Palestinian remarks made during his opening performances.

Madison Square Garden Entertainment was the first venue operator to publicly challenge Macklemore’s account, offering a commercial explanation rather than addressing his allegations about political pressure directly.

“We have some of the busiest venues in the world with extremely limited availability and he’s not an arena level act,” an MSG Entertainment spokesperson [told Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/msg-macklemore-arena-level-act-1236376589/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

The statement raises a question about the economics of the proposed concerts and how venue operators evaluate bookings that combine fundraising, political advocacy and live entertainment.

Macklemore’s recent European performances provide some context. His three Free Palestine benefit concerts, held October 26 at Dublin’s 3Arena, October 29 at Paris’ Accor Arena and October 30 at London’s OVO Arena Wembley, were reported as sellouts.

Those concerts also featured numerous guest artists, including Hozier, Kneecap and AURORA, making them substantially different from conventional solo performances. The European results demonstrate that the benefit concept attracted significant audiences, although they do not establish the demand that would exist for comparable shows in individual U.S. markets.

Macklemore also has a history of headlining major American venues. In November 2013, Macklemore & Ryan Lewis performed at Madison Square Garden during the height of their commercial success. That performance establishes previous arena-level demand but offers limited evidence of the rapper’s current drawing power.

For arena operators, booking decisions can involve projected ticket sales, event production requirements, financial guarantees, scheduling conflicts and the availability of suitable dates. An initial indication that a calendar date is available is not necessarily a confirmed booking or commitment to host an event.

The central issue in Macklemore’s account is therefore not simply whether the venues declined his tour. It is his allegation that their willingness to consider the shows changed after learning their charitable and political purpose.

Neither the specific status of the proposed dates nor the commercial terms discussed with the venues have been publicly established. Representatives of the other eight arenas have not provided detailed public explanations for the alleged rejections in the reporting reviewed.

## Venue Access Dispute Follows Ed Sheeran Tour Fallout

The latest controversy follows a separate venue-access dispute that [TicketNews previously covered](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/macklemore-ed-sheeran-stadiums-cancel-shows/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) during Sheeran’s Loop Tour.

Macklemore had been scheduled to support Sheeran on the remaining U.S. stadium dates after performing at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey on September 4 and 5. During those appearances, he called for a Free Palestine and performed his protest song “Hind’s Hall.”

Messina Touring Group, which promoted Sheeran’s U.S. tour, subsequently said multiple stadium operators had notified the promoter that concerts could not proceed with Macklemore on the lineup. Rather than risk canceling the remaining shows, the promoter removed him from the tour.

New England Patriots owner Robert Kraft acknowledged contacting Sheeran to object to Macklemore performing at Kraft-owned Gillette Stadium. Kraft also confirmed communicating concerns to another stadium’s leadership, but denied organizing a coordinated campaign among venue operators.

Sheeran maintained that removing Macklemore was the promoter’s decision and later [publicly questioned whether venues should control the content of artists’ performances](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/ed-sheeran-venue-controls-macklemore-loop-tour/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

The earlier episode demonstrated how control over major venues can influence an artist’s ability to participate in an otherwise confirmed tour. The current dispute raises a related but distinct question: whether an artist attempting to organize an independent concert series can encounter comparable obstacles during the booking process.

As [TicketNews examined in September](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/ed-sheeran-messina-venue-power-live-nation/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), access to essential venues can become a source of leverage over touring decisions. That does not mean a venue’s refusal to host an individual concert necessarily constitutes improper conduct. Venue operators regularly make decisions about which events to book, and their reasons can range from commercial considerations to concerns about security or event operations.

What remains unresolved in Macklemore’s case is whether those ordinary business considerations explain the rejections or whether the concerts’ political message was a determining factor.

## U2 Members Defend Artists’ Right to Speak

The venue dispute has also drawn commentary from U2, whose members addressed Macklemore’s earlier removal from Sheeran’s tour in a BBC interview.

As [reported by Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/u2-macklemore-ed-sheeran-tour-censorship-comments-1236377026/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), guitarist the Edge defended Macklemore’s right to express political views while cautioning against holding Sheeran personally responsible for the controversy.

“I don’t think Ed should be vilified,” the Edge said, acknowledging the difficult position Sheeran faced when venues objected to Macklemore’s participation.

U2 drummer Larry Mullen Jr. took a stronger position against restrictions on artistic expression, arguing that performers should be free to express their political views, provided they are not inciting violence. He said that “no billionaire, no promoter, no owner has the right to censor anybody.”

The U2 comments concerned the earlier Sheeran controversy rather than the specific negotiations involving Macklemore’s proposed U.S. benefit concerts. Nevertheless, they underscore how the dispute has expanded into a broader discussion of the influence exercised by venue owners and promoters over performers.

Following his removal from the Loop Tour, Macklemore [announced the European Free Palestine concerts](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/macklemore-free-palestine-tour-ed-sheeran/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), pledging to direct proceeds toward organizations supporting Palestinians and indicating that U.S. dates would follow.

Those American performances have not been announced.

Macklemore says he intends to continue organizing concerts with other artists and supporters, potentially moving beyond conventional arena facilities.

“We will build our own stages,” he wrote in his November 6 statement.

For now, the disagreement leaves unanswered whether other U.S. venues might accommodate the benefit tour and whether the nine arenas Macklemore identified will provide further explanations of their booking decisions.
