# ‘The bot situation is out of hand’: Attacks disrupt sales for Sam Fender and Garth Brooks

## Summary

"Sophisticated bot attacks" disrupted on-sales for both Sam Fender and Garth Brooks within the same week, forcing Gigantic Tickets to delay Fender's Newcastle stadium presale and resulting in the cancellation of a Brooks show. The incident is the latest in a run of high-profile bot-driven onsale failures across the industry.

### Key Points
- Sam Fender's Newcastle stadium presale paused Oct. 28 after "unusually high volume of suspicious activity" caused site errors
- Gigantic Tickets CEO Simon Carpenter confirmed the sale was pushed back roughly 24 hours
- A Garth Brooks show was cancelled following a separate bot onslaught
- Both incidents follow a string of recent bot-driven presale disruptions reported across the industry

### Highlights
- Two high-profile artists (Fender, Brooks) hit by bot attacks in the same week, with one date outright cancelled
- Gigantic Tickets, a mid-size UK platform, had to publicly delay a stadium on-sale rather than let it proceed under attack
- Attacks are described as "sophisticated," suggesting mitigation vendors are still being outpaced at peak-demand on-sales

### Industry Problem
High-demand on-sales remain acutely vulnerable to automated bot traffic even at platforms with existing bot-mitigation tooling, with attacks now severe enough to force outright cancellations rather than just queue delays — a direct instance of the queue-meltdown / bot-defense-at-scale problem.

### Proposed Solution
The article describes only reactive measures (delaying sales, pushing back queues); no new mitigation technology is detailed. For a primary platform like Etix, the signal is to prioritize proactive bot/identity-verification investment (rotating barcodes, behavioral detection, queue hardening) ahead of major on-sales rather than relying on delay-and-restart as a fallback, and to monitor whether this pattern of cancellations becomes frequent enough to justify new build investment.
