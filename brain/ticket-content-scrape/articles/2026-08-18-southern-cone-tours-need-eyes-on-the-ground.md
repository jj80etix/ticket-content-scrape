# Southern Cone tours ‘need eyes on the ground’

## Summary

Argentina's LEB Entertainment, a boutique full-service tour producer, discusses its expansion across Latin America's Southern Cone (Chile, Uruguay, Paraguay), positioning itself as an independent, locally-rooted alternative to international promoters setting tour terms in the region. Founder Lucas Biren describes a vertically integrated model spanning talent management, booking, and production, with flexibility as its key differentiator.

### Key Points
- LEB Entertainment (Argentina) sees "enormous growth potential" expanding its tour-production model into Chile, Uruguay, and Paraguay.
- Positions itself as an independent local partner handling logistics and market-specific challenges for visiting international tours, distinct from promoter-set terms.
- Model spans talent management, record label operations, booking, and production (venues incl. Movistar Arena, Teatro Ópera).
- Emphasizes tailored marketing/routing strategy, fan data analytics, and currency-hedging tactics for touring productions.
- Tour-production/promoter business-model story rather than a ticketing-platform or client-pain-point item — no clear substantive match to tracked research questions.

## Transcript

Argentina's LEB Entertainment on the expansion plans for its end-to-end local market tour production services in Latin America

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/08/LucasBiren-7-scaled.jpg.webp)

Lucas Biren © LEB Entertainment

Argentina’s LEB Entertainment has said it sees “enormous growth potential” for its model of boutique full-service production for international tours in Latin America’s Southern Cone, as it tells *IQ* of the drawbacks of international promoters typically setting the terms for tours in the region.

Against this backdrop, LEB Entertainment founder Lucas Biren tells says the firm has developed an independent, vertically integrated ecosystem that spans from talent management and record label operations to booking and production in arenas and theatres.

He explains that LEB functions as the local partner responsible for absorbing logistical management and macroeconomic variables in markets such as Argentina, Chile, Uruguay, and Paraguay, allowing artistic teams to focus on performing. Now having worked on shows at regional venues such as Movistar Arena and Teatro Ópera, the company is now eyeing further expansion beyond Argentina based on the pitch that local market knowledge can’t be rivalled.

> “Chile, Paraguay, and Uruguay are markets where we are increasing our presence”

“Argentina remains our primary market and the birthplace of LEB,” Biren says, while adding that “at the same time, Chile, Paraguay, and Uruguay are markets where we are increasing our presence and see enormous growth potential.”

“Our vision has always been to build a regional company, not just an Argentine one. Music knows no borders, and we believe LEB’s way of working can add value to more and more Latin American markets. Our goal is to continue expanding that work culture and become a strategic partner for artists and teams across the entire region.”

Describing what local market full service producers can offer international tours, Biren says every visiting tour “has different characteristics, and it is essential to identify where the true challenge lies in focussing especially on those points requiring more attention, while strengths can move forward without becoming a bottleneck”.

He adds that LEB, by virtue of this model, can offer touring productions a marketing and routing strategy that is tailored to local audiences, manifested in event marketing, fan data analytics for tours, and currency hedging tactics.

He says a result of this is LEB’s boast that it can “develop emerging artists and work with established figures simultaneously, with the same dedication and level of commitment.”

He admits, though, that it can take some persuading to build trust with visiting artists: “The first time always involves a process of building trust between the artist’s team and the local team. Our goal is to become those eyes on the ground, understanding the market and making decisions that support the artist’s vision.”

He proclaims: “We don’t believe in standardised solutions,” believing that “every project has different needs and therefore requires a different operational structure”.

This flexibility in approach is enabled by being an independent company without obligations to any particular promoters, bookers, or venues.

Biren says this affords local end-to-end producers a level of flexibility and adaptability that is rare in the industry, but concedes: “I don’t know if reacting faster means reacting better. What is a fact is that at LEB we can change the rules as many times as necessary, provided it benefits the project we are working on. That flexibility gives teams peace of mind because they know there is nothing stopping us or preventing us from doing things in the way that most benefits the project.”

One such area of flexibility is the freedom to break away, when necessary, from prevailing methods of fan data analytics increasingly at the core of routing, merchandising, and marketing decision for tours.

“Streaming data is one indicator when planning a show or tour, but never the only one. There is no direct conversion between streams and tickets, nor a mathematical formula that can be applied to all artists,” the founder explains.

He adds: “Our strategy is to analyse multiple variables, understand where the artist’s audience is, and develop actions to expand that audience into new segments and markets. Data helps us make better decisions, but it never replaces experience, intuition, or market knowledge. In the end, a concert doesn’t sell plays; it sells emotions and the need to live a unique experience.”

> “Our goal is to become those eyes on the ground, understanding the market and making decisions that support the artist’s vision”

Thirdly, operating across in Southern Cone markets requires financial hedging mechanisms, with Biren saying setting prices in local currency are essential to maintaining public accessibility and protecting project margins against inflation or exchange rate fluctuations.

“In recent years, volatility in Argentina was much lower, and today it can be predicted with fair accuracy. However, you have to be very smart and understand that things can change, and that there will always be some level of fluctuation,” he explains. “It is important not to inflate ticket prices assuming the dollar will skyrocket, because that can directly impact sales. At the same time, there are financial tools that allow us to hedge against inflation and other economic factors in the country.”

In today’s competitive environment, securing international tours is managed by fulfilling the logistical and contractual goals of each market, Biren says., leading the founder to comment: “Today, LEB speaks through its work. I believe trust is not built with sales pitches, but with how we execute every project. Every tour that finishes successfully becomes our best business card.”

“There is a very true phrase in the music industry: reputation travels faster than contracts. When an artist, manager, or agent receives great references from those who have already worked with us, the conversation starts from a different place. There is no better advertising than a happy team at the end of a tour,” he concludes.
