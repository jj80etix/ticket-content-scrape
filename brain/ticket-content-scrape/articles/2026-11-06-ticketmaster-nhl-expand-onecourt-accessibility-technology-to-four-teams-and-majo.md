# Ticketmaster, NHL Expand OneCourt Accessibility Technology to Four Teams and Major Events

## Summary

Ticketmaster and the NHL are expanding OneCourt's tactile accessibility technology, giving blind and low-vision fans complimentary tablets that convert live game data into haptic feedback. The rollout covers every home game for four NHL teams (Capitals, Avalanche, Blackhawks, Red Wings) plus marquee events like the Winter Classic, Heritage Classic, All-Star Weekend, and Stadium Series for the 2026-27 season.

### Key Points
- Four NHL teams offer OneCourt tablets at every home game: Washington Capitals, Colorado Avalanche, Chicago Blackhawks, Detroit Red Wings.
- Also available at NHL Heritage Classic, Winter Classic, All-Star Weekend, and Stadium Series.
- Tablets translate NHL EDGE puck/player-tracking data into real-time vibrations fans can follow by touch.
- OneCourt's tech previously deployed in basketball and football; this is its first hockey rollout, done in partnership with Ticketmaster.

### Highlights
- First hockey deployment of OneCourt's haptic tech, covering 4 full home schedules plus all NHL marquee events in 2026-27.
- Devices are complimentary to blind/low-vision fans, provided directly through the Ticketmaster/NHL partnership.

### Industry Problem

Blind and low-vision fans have historically had no way to follow live, fast-moving action (like a hockey puck) in-venue without relying on narration or secondhand description, leaving accessibility as an underserved dimension of the live-event experience.

### Proposed Solution

Ticketmaster is distributing third-party haptic hardware (OneCourt) tied to league tracking data rather than building the capability itself — a vendor-partnership model for accessibility. For Etix, this signals accessibility-tech partnerships (not necessarily haptics specifically) as a differentiator competitors are actively using; worth tracking whether this expands to other sports/leagues and whether comparable low-cost accessibility integrations (screen-reader-friendly seat maps, audio description feeds, etc.) are feasible to build or partner for.

Ticketmaster and the National Hockey League are expanding access to OneCourt’s tactile broadcast technology, providing complimentary tablets to blind and low-vision fans at major NHL events throughout the 2026-27 season and at every home game for four NHL teams.

The Washington Capitals, Colorado Avalanche, Chicago Blackhawks and Detroit Red Wings will be the first NHL clubs to offer the devices throughout their home schedules. The technology will also be available at the NHL Heritage Classic, Winter Classic, All-Star Weekend and Stadium Series.

OneCourt’s tablets convert real-time game data into vibrations that fans can follow with their fingertips, allowing them to experience the movement of players and the puck as action unfolds on the ice.

“This partnership with the National Hockey League is a milestone achievement for accessibility in live sports,” said Kurt Schwartzkopf, head of sports at Ticketmaster. “Ticketmaster is proud to support our NHL partners in bringing this experience to life and advance our mission to make live events inclusive and accessible to every fan.”

The hockey experience integrates OneCourt’s haptic technology with NHL EDGE puck and player tracking data, translating information from the league’s tracking system into tactile feedback.

“Technology has the power to make hockey more accessible, immersive, and inclusive,” said Dave Lehanski, the NHL’s executive vice president of business development and innovation. “By combining OneCourt’s haptics technology with NHL EDGE Puck & Player Tracking data, we’re creating new ways for fans to experience the speed and excitement of our game and helping bring hockey to more audiences at tentpole events and in arenas across the League.”

The partnership represents another expansion for Seattle-based OneCourt, which has already deployed its technology across professional basketball and football.

“Our community has been asking us for hockey since day one, and launching it with Ticketmaster and the NHL is a milestone for OneCourt,” said Jerred Mace, CEO and founder of OneCourt. “Blind and low-vision fans can now follow the action with their fingertips, feeling the pulse of every move, pass, shot, and goal alongside everyone else in the arena.”

Since 2025, Ticketmaster has supported OneCourt deployments at every home game for nine NBA teams, along with an NFL pilot involving four clubs and an activation at Super Bowl LX.

TicketNews previously [covered OneCourt’s growing presence in professional sports](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/02/haptics-asl-inside-the-push-more-inclusive-live-experiences/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), including efforts to introduce tactile technology and other accessibility services at major live events.

The NHL has separately expanded its accessibility offerings through *NHL in ASL*, which provides live American Sign Language broadcasts of games.

OneCourt tablets will be available free of charge through guest services at North American arenas hosting the NHL’s major events this season. Fans attending participating teams’ home games will also be able to access the devices at no additional charge.
