# Meek Mill Wants Out of Live Nation Contract After Five Tour Dates Canceled

## Summary

Rapper Meek Mill publicly called for an exit from his Live Nation touring contract after five remaining Dream Chasers Nights dates were canceled, saying he felt "trapped and shelved" and wants to work with independent promoters instead. Live Nation has not addressed the allegations or explained the cancellations; the dispute surfaces as competitors and independent venues are already pressing the DOJ over Live Nation's tour-promotion dominance. Ticketmaster is processing automatic refunds (14-21 days) for the five canceled shows in LA, Boston, Baltimore, Chicago, and Atlanta.

### Key Points
- Five of six Dream Chasers Nights dates canceled after the Nov. 24 Philadelphia opener; Meek Mill says he feels contractually "trapped" and wants to self-promote future dates via his Dream Chasers brand.
- Live Nation has not disclosed why the shows were canceled or responded to the allegations; terms of the artist's contract are not public.
- Refunds process automatically to original payment method within 14-21 days; tickets obtained via transfer refund to the original purchaser.
- Dispute lands amid NIVA's September push (as part of DOJ settlement objections) to cap Live Nation-controlled promotion at 50% of a headliner's U.S. tour dates, and promoter Louis Messina's similar concerns about artist choice of promoter.

### Highlights
- An artist is publicly alleging his Live Nation promotion contract restricts which markets he can play, adding a first-person data point to the DOJ antitrust settlement debate over Live Nation's tour-promotion share.
- NIVA's proposed 50%-of-tour-dates cap on Live Nation promotion (raised in its DOJ settlement objections) is the concrete regulatory lever this episode could strengthen.

### Industry Problem
Live Nation's combined promotion-plus-ticketing position can lock artists into single-promoter deals across an entire tour, limiting their ability to route individual markets to independent promoters — a structural concentration concern now visible from the artist side, not just the venue/competitor side.

### Proposed Solution
No remedy is proposed in the article beyond Meek Mill's stated intent to self-promote replacement dates. For Etix: monitor the DOJ settlement proceedings and any tour-cap remedy — a cap that opens individual-date bidding to independent promoters would expand the pool of primary-platform ticketing opportunities outside Live Nation's exclusive deals.

## Meek Mill Looks to Independent Concert Promoters

Meek Mill described his arrangement with Live Nation as exclusive and indicated that he intends to invest his own money in a concert promotion business operating under his Dream Chasers brand, [according to AllHipHop](https://allhiphop.com/news/meek-mill-wants-out-of-live-nation-deal-as-five-tour-dates-are-canceled/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

The rapper also expressed an intention to arrange new performances in the markets affected by the cancellations, though no replacement dates have been announced.

His complaints come at a time when the extent of Live Nation’s control over concert promotion, and not simply its ownership of Ticketmaster, is receiving considerable scrutiny from competitors and independent live entertainment businesses.

In September, the National Independent Venue Association (NIVA) [called for restrictions on Live Nation’s ability to promote entire concert tours](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/niva-live-nation-ticketmaster-breakup-tour-cap/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) as part of its formal objections to the proposed settlement between the U.S. Department of Justice and Live Nation.

Among the association’s proposed remedies was a restriction that would prevent Live Nation-controlled businesses from promoting more than 50% of a headlining artist’s U.S. tour dates in a calendar year, opening opportunities for competing promoters to bid for individual performances.

Veteran concert promoter Louis Messina, whose company promoted Taylor Swift’s Eras Tour, has also [raised concerns about the ability of artists to work with their preferred promoters](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/aeg-louis-messina-live-nation-settlement/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) in submissions related to the antitrust proceedings.

Meek Mill’s dispute offers an artist’s perspective on some of those concerns, although his allegations do not establish that Live Nation violated antitrust law or breached its contractual obligations. Without access to the agreement, it remains unclear what restrictions the rapper accepted when signing the deal and what options are available to him should he seek to terminate it.

For independent promoters, the ability to compete for individual dates featuring established touring artists can be an important source of business. For artists, working with different promoters in different markets can offer an alternative to entrusting an entire tour to a single company, though it may also mean assuming additional financial and operational risks.

The company advises that refunds should appear in customers’ accounts within 14 to 21 days once processed. Fans who received tickets through Ticketmaster’s transfer system should note that refunds will be directed to the original purchaser.

Customers who purchased tickets through third-party marketplaces should consult their respective ticket sellers for information regarding refunds.

Despite Meek Mill’s expressed desire to bring replacement concerts to the affected cities, the original performances are currently classified as cancellations rather than postponements. Any replacement shows would be subject to separate announcements and ticketing arrangements.
