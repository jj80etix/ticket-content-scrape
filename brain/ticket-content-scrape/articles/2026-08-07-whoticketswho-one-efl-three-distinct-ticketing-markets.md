# #WhoTicketsWho? One EFL, three distinct ticketing markets

## Summary

TheTicketingBusiness publishes independent consultant Jonathan Buck's #WhoTicketsWho? analysis of ticketing-system supplier share across England's Championship, League One and League Two ahead of the 2026-2027 EFL season: 72 clubs use 13 different ticketing systems. Ticketmaster Sport remains dominant overall (33 clubs, 59.7% combined with SeatGeek's 10), but nine clubs switched platforms since last season and the supplier count has grown from 12 to 13, with some vendors entering the market (Fever, Hotlizard Sports Technology, Ticketpass) and others dropping out (Glitnir Ticketing, Medoc Computers, Seatlab). The piece closes with qualitative commentary on what EFL clubs are increasingly demanding from ticketing technology: better data-driven selling, connected/simple supporter journeys, and reliability.

### Key Points

- 72 EFL clubs (Championship, League One, League Two) use 13 ticketing systems in 2026-2027, up from 12 last season.
- Ticketmaster Sport leads with 33 clubs; combined with SeatGeek (10 clubs) they hold 59.7% of the EFL, down from 66.7% last season.
- Nine clubs changed ticketing platform since last season: one in the Championship (Bristol City, SeatGeek to Fever), four in League One, four in League Two.
- New entrants to the EFL ticketing market: Fever, Hotlizard Sports Technology, Ticketpass; exits: Glitnir Ticketing, Medoc Computers, Seatlab.
- Consultant Jonathan Buck reports clubs increasingly want ticketing platforms to help "sell more effectively" via supporter-behavior data and demand signals, not just process transactions.
- Clubs want a connected, simple supporter journey (no duplicate logins/confusing steps) regardless of whether it comes from one platform or integrated suppliers.
- Reliability, responsive service, and operational understanding remain key retention factors, especially given the perceived risk/switching cost of changing platforms.

### Highlights

- Market structure: Ticketmaster Sport (33 clubs) and SeatGeek (10 clubs) together supply 59.7% of 72 EFL clubs, down from 66.7% a year earlier as the field fragments (12 to 13 systems).
- Switching activity: 9 of 72 clubs changed ticketing vendor in the past year, including Reading (SeatGeek to NewC Sport) and Stockport County (Future Ticketing to Fourth Wall Creative).
- New/exiting suppliers: Fever, Hotlizard Sports Technology, and Ticketpass entered the EFL market; Glitnir Ticketing, Medoc Computers, and Seatlab exited.
- Client demand shift (per Buck's sourcing with senior ticketing-supplier figures): clubs want actionable supporter-behavior data, connected/simple purchase journeys, and reliable operational support.

### Industry Problem

EFL clubs (Etix's client-side analog: mid-market/organizer clients) are outgrowing basic transaction-processing ticketing systems — they want platforms that convert supporter data into sales action, deliver a single connected buyer journey without duplicated logins, and prove reliable enough to justify the switching risk, yet the market remains fragmented across 13 systems with real switching churn.

### Proposed Solution

The article itself proposes no single vendor fix, but the sourced commentary points to a clear roadmap: primary platforms should invest in supporter-behavior analytics/demand signals to help clients "sell more effectively," unify the buyer journey to avoid duplicate logins/friction, and compete on reliability and hands-on operational support to lower a client's perceived risk of switching. Etix could build stronger analytics/CRM-style tooling and connected-journey features, and lean on reliability/service quality as a retention lever against both Tier 1 peers and Tier 2 consumer majors (Ticketmaster, SeatGeek) actively gaining and losing share.

The Championship has five different systems, while League One and League Two each have nine. The sections below look at the clubs in each division, the suppliers they use and the changes shaping the 2026-2027 ticketing landscape.

### Championship: where supplier concentration is highest

![](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Championship-Supplier-Comparison-300x150.png)

Ticketmaster Sport supplies 13 clubs, SeatGeek supplies seven and Secutix two. Fever and NewC Sport each supply one, giving the division a wider supplier mix than last season, when only three systems were represented.

This change is mainly explained by the clubs occupying the division. Burnley, West Ham United and Wolverhampton Wanderers arrive from the Premier League, while Bolton Wanderers, Cardiff City and Lincoln City come up from League One. Coventry City, Hull City and Ipswich Town move out and into the Premier League, while Leicester City, Oxford United and Sheffield Wednesday drop into League One. The six incoming clubs and six departing clubs all retain their existing suppliers, meaning that most of the movement in the table reflects changing divisional membership rather than widespread platform movement. Bristol City is the only Championship club to have changed platform since last season moving from SeatGeek to Fever.

The Championship therefore remains the most concentrated supplier market of the three EFL divisions.

### League One: where the market begins to broaden

![](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/League-One-Supplier-Comparison-300x225.png)

Ticketmaster Sport supplies 13 clubs, SeatGeek three and NewC Sport two. Fanbase, Fourth Wall Creative, Kaizen Ticketing, Secutix, Ticketpass and Venue Management Systems each supply one club. That gives League One nine systems in 2026-2027, compared with eight in 2025-2026.

Bolton Wanderers, Cardiff City and Lincoln City move up to the Championship, while Leicester City, Oxford United and Sheffield Wednesday come down. Bromley, Cambridge United, Milton Keynes Dons and Notts County arrive from League Two, replacing Exeter City, Northampton Town, Port Vale and Rotherham United. The divisional movement accounts for some of the change in the supplier totals, but four clubs that remain in League One have also changed platform. Burton Albion moves from Glitnir Ticketing to Ticketpass, Reading from SeatGeek to NewC Sport, Stevenage from Venue Management Systems to Fanbase, and Stockport County from Future Ticketing to Fourth Wall Creative.

League One remains clearly led by Ticketmaster Sport, while the suppliers behind it form a fragmented group rather than a single challenger.

### League Two: where supplier share is most evenly spread

![](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/League-Two-Supplier-Comparison-300x225.png)

League Two has the same number of systems as League One, but a more evenly distributed supplier base. Ticketmaster Sport supplies seven clubs, Kaizen Ticketing and Venue Management Systems support four each, while Fanbase, Fourth Wall Creative and TicketCo supply two each. Future Ticketing, Hotlizard Sports Technology and Secutix each supply one.

Four clubs moved away from League Two: Bromley, Cambridge United, Milton Keynes Dons and Notts County. Exeter City, Northampton Town, Port Vale and Rotherham United come down from League One, while Barrow and Harrogate Town move into the National League. York City, the National League champions, and Rochdale, which won promotion through the play-offs, take their places. York City retains Future Ticketing and Rochdale retains Fanbase.

Four clubs in League Two are recorded as changing platform. Fleetwood Town and Swindon Town moved from Ticketmaster Sport to TicketCo. Oldham Athletic moves from Future Ticketing to Kaizen Ticketing, while Colchester United moves from Medoc Computers to Hotlizard Sports Technology.

Although I have had it confirmed that Ticketmaster Sport no longer provides Swindon Town’s ticketing, at the time of writing Swindon had not yet begun selling tickets for its EFL fixtures through TicketCo. The change is therefore reflected in the supplier data, but the new arrangement was not yet fully visible in the ticket buying journey.

The supplier count for League Two remains at nine, but the distribution is considerably more balanced than last season. No supplier accounts for more than 29.2% of the division, while six systems supply at least two clubs.

### The EFL viewed as one market

Across the Championship, League One and League Two, 72 clubs are using 13 different ticketing systems for 2026-2027.

![](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/All-EFL-Supplier-Comparison-300x300.png)

Ticketmaster Sport remains the largest supplier across the EFL, with 33 clubs, followed by SeatGeek with 10. Together they account for 43 clubs, or 59.7% of the 2026-2027 EFL, down from 48 clubs and 66.7% last season. The number of systems represented has increased from 12 to 13.

Promotion and relegation naturally change the composition of each division, which in turn can alter its supplier mix. However, that is not the whole story and since last season, nine EFL clubs have changed their ticketing platform: one in the Championship, four in League One and four in League Two. Fanbase, TicketCo, Kaizen Ticketing, Fourth Wall Creative and

NewC Sport all have more EFL clubs than last season. Fever, Hotlizard Sports Technology and Ticketpass appear for the first time in the 2026-2027 data, while Glitnir Ticketing, Medoc Computers and Seatlab no longer appear.

I also want to reiterate that these figures show market presence, not platform quality, supplier performance or the value of individual contracts. They provide a snapshot of who is supplying the EFL’s 72 clubs as the 2026-2027 season begins.

### What I am hearing across the EFL ticketing market

Although these figures show which systems supply EFL clubs, recent conversations across the sector suggest that clubs are increasingly looking for more from their ticketing technology.

**Selling more effectively**

For a long time, ticketing platforms have been expected to do more than simply process transactions. That expectation is becoming more pressing, with clubs looking for greater help to turn the information they hold into action. They want to understand supporter behaviour, identify demand earlier and make communications and offers more relevant. The focus is increasingly on helping clubs sell more effectively, rather than simply facilitating the sale.

**A connected and simple supporter journey**

A connected supporter view and straightforward customer journey have long been ambitions, and while this isn’t changing, expectations are rising. Generally clubs are less concerned whether this comes from an all in one platform or connected suppliers, it is more that systems work as required and give supporters a simple, consistent experience. The technology may be complex, but supporters should not face unnecessary logins, duplicated information or confusing journeys.

**Reliability, service and operational support**

Reliability, responsive service and a supplier’s understanding of day-to-day club operations remain important, particularly when the perceived risk of changing platform is high. Clubs need systems that can adapt as their requirements change, supported by people who understand the practical realities of selling tickets, managing events and dealing with supporters.

The supplier numbers provide a clear view of how EFL ticketing is structured. These conversations add context to that, highlighting how the role of ticketing software is being discussed across the sector, from supporting sales and connected supporter journeys to the importance of reliability and operational support.
