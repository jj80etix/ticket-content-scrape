# Goldenvoice’s Ocean Way Festival In Santa Monica Canceled Due To ‘Beach Erosion’

## Summary

Goldenvoice's Ocean Way Festival in Santa Monica was canceled weeks before its Sept. 26-27 debut due to beach erosion and flooding from Hurricane Marie. Refunds are being processed automatically, and Goldenvoice says it plans to bring the festival to Santa Monica in 2027.

### Key Points
- Two-day festival was to headline The Killers and Olivia Dean, with Jack White, Sublime and others on the bill.
- City cited storm surf and runoff-driven beach erosion as making the site unsafe.
- Refunds automatic; Goldenvoice targeting a 2027 return.
- Southern California saw broader coastal flooding/erosion from Tropical Storm Marie, including Malibu and Long Beach.

Just weeks before it was set to make its debut, Goldenvoice’s Ocean Way Festival on the Santa Monica beachfront has been canceled.

The City of Santa Monica nixed the event “due to the impacts of Hurricane Marie.” The Killers and Olivia Dean were billed as headliners for the two-day festival, which was supposed to take place Sept. 26-27. Other artists on the bill included Jack White, Khruangbin, Sublime, Durand Jones & The Indications, Alvvays, Hiatus Kaiyote and more.

“Storm surf and runoff has caused significant beach erosion and the festival can no longer be safely held as planned,” according to a statement from the organizers, which assured that refunds are being processed automatically.

Southern California has been hit with extreme heat and high humidity lately as a result of Tropical Storm Marie, which caused coastal flooding and beach erosion throughout local beaches, including Malibu and Long Beach.

Ocean Way was billed as a landmark event for both the promoter and the City of Santa Monica. For Goldenvoice, it was a return to its roots, having produced shows at the Civic Auditorium in the past, and the festival was meant to boost the local economy, which has been affected by the country’s tourism dip, and revitalize the city.

Despite the cancellation, Goldenvoice, the promoter behind the festival and others such as Coachella and Just Like Heaven in Southern California, isn’t giving up on the event and stated that “plans are already underway to bring the festival to Santa Monica in 2027.”
