# Christian Metal Band Demon Hunter Files Trademark Lawsuit Against Netflix Over ‘Kpop Demon Hunters’

## Summary

Christian metal band Demon Hunter has filed a federal trademark lawsuit against Netflix over the film "Kpop Demon Hunters," alleging trademark infringement. Entertainment-IP litigation unrelated to ticketing.

### Key Points
- Demon Hunter (Christian metal band) sues Netflix over trademark rights to "Kpop Demon Hunters."
- Filed in federal court; band alleges the film's title infringes their trademark.
- No ticketing-industry relevance.

The Christian metal band Demon Hunter has filed a trademark lawsuit in federal court against Netflix over their highly-successful film “Kpop Demon Hunters.”

The lawsuit was filed in the Central District of California on August 18 under their company, Hyde Lane, citing Netflix, Netflix Studios, and AEG Presents as defendants. The suit alleges trademark infringement, false designation of origin, and unfair competition, pointing to Netflix’s “Kpop Demon Hunters,” released last year with a soundtrack and lots of merchandise.

Since the film’s release, the [suit](https://www.pacermonitor.com/public/case/66314187/HYDE_LANE,_INC_dba_DEMON_HUNTER,_a_Washington_corporation_v_Netflix,_Inc,_a_Delaware_corporation_et_al) alleges that Netflix moved into “almost complete overlap” with the merch and offerings under Hyde Lane’s Demon Hunter name, noting that Netflix’s brand is so similar to Demon Hunter’s established trademark that it has led to confusion among consumers.

The suit pointed to one email from someone who spent $500 on tickets to a Demon Hunter concert in New York, mistakenly believing they had purchased tickets for their daughters to see the “Kpop Demon Hunters” live show. Additionally, the complaint referenced another mix-up where a television producer reportedly approached the band’s management under the impression that they were connected to “Kpop Demon Hunters.”

“Demon Hunter \[is\] a well-known and celebrated metal band,” the filing read. “Demon Hunter was formed at the turn of the century and, in the quarter-century since, it has consistently released albums, toured, and sold merchandise to a large and dedicated audience.”

Hyde Lane said the band has been pushed aside by Netflix due to its large footprint and funds, disregarding the Demon Hunter trademark the band has built over the past 20 years. They are demanding that Netflix no longer use the “Kpop Demon Hunters” name and are asking for restitution and treble damages.

Demon Hunter, formed in 2000 by brothers Don and Ryan Clark, has released 12 studio albums to-date, garnering attention for singles “On My Side,” “I Will Fail You,” and “Artificial Light.”
