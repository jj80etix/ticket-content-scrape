# Kennedy Center Board Orders Immediate Closure as Trump Ties Renovation to Naming Fight

## Summary

The Kennedy Center's Trump-aligned board voted to immediately close the venue's main building, citing a partial ceiling collapse and a worsening budget shortfall, but President Trump said the renovation itself won't begin until an appeals court rules on his bid to put his name on the building — and possibly not at all if he loses. The vote came hours after a federal judge again blocked the board's attempt to rename the center after Trump, in the latest turn of a seven-month legal fight over both the closure and the naming dispute.

### Key Points
- Board voted to close the main building "forthwith," citing a Sept. 4 ceiling collapse and water damage; reopening targeted for 2028 pending the outcome of Trump's naming litigation.
- Trump says renovation work "will not take place" unless the D.C. Circuit (and potentially the Supreme Court) rules in his favor on the naming dispute.
- Judge Christopher Cooper again ruled trustees can't install memorials to Trump without Congress's approval, blocking the board's latest renaming attempt.
- Center says it faces a roughly $23M projected deficit ($124M revenue vs. $220M budget) and could soon miss payroll.
- Rep. Joyce Beatty's legal team disputes the board's safety rationale, saying it misstated an outside consultant's findings.
- National Symphony Orchestra has already relocated its 2026-27 season to six other D.C.-area venues.

The Kennedy Center’s Trump-aligned board [voted Tuesday to immediately close the performing arts venue’s main building](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/kennedy-center-board-votes-close-182437007.html?fr=sycsrp_catchall), accelerating a two-year renovation shutdown that has already been announced, blocked by a federal judge and approved by the board multiple times over the past seven months.

But the latest attempt to shutter the Washington, D.C., institution arrived with another twist: President Donald Trump said shortly after the vote that the renovation itself will not begin until an appeals court rules on his [continuing attempt to put his name on the Kennedy Center](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/06/trumps-name-wiped-from-kennedy-center-exterior/) — and suggested the work may not happen at all if the courts ultimately reject him.

The result is an increasingly unusual standoff in which Kennedy Center leaders say the building is so physically unsafe that it must be closed immediately, while Trump says the work intended to fix those conditions is contingent on the outcome of his personal naming dispute.

Tuesday’s closure vote came just hours after U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper again rejected an attempt by the Kennedy Center board to place Trump’s name on the congressionally established memorial to President John F. Kennedy.

## Closure Now Immediate, But Renovation Is Not

Trump [originally announced in February](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/02/trump-announces-kennedy-center-closure-renovations/) that the Kennedy Center would close around July 4 for approximately two years of construction. The board formally approved that plan in March, but Cooper blocked it in May after finding that trustees had not adequately investigated the consequences or independently considered alternatives before voting.

The judge did not permanently bar a closure. His ruling left the board free to revisit the issue after conducting a more complete review. Trustees did exactly that in August, voting again to shut down the main building for roughly two years while simultaneously approving another attempt to place Trump’s name on its exterior.

Tuesday’s vote goes further by ordering the main building closed immediately.

The resolution circulated to trustees ahead of the vote cited a recent partial ceiling collapse in the Grand Foyer, additional water intrusion and deteriorating infrastructure. A roughly four-by-five-foot portion of ceiling plaster reportedly fell approximately 60 feet during a Sept. 4 storm, although nobody was injured. Center officials subsequently identified additional areas showing water-related deterioration.

The proposed resolution said the main building should be closed “forthwith” and called for repair and restoration work to begin immediately. It envisioned the building reopening in 2028 while allowing programming to continue at the Kennedy Center’s newer REACH complex and at off-site locations.

Trump, however, complicated that timeline almost immediately.

“The Renovation and Reconstruction … cannot begin until such time as the D.C. Circuit rules on the Board’s approved name,” Trump wrote following the vote, according to multiple reports. He said that if the appeals court rules against the Kennedy Center and that ruling is not overturned by the Supreme Court, the renovation “will not take place.”

That leaves the actual duration of the shutdown less certain than the previously advertised two-year construction schedule.

## Safety and Financial Claims Draw New Scrutiny

The Kennedy Center’s leadership is now pointing to two separate crises to justify the shutdown: deteriorating building conditions and a financial emergency.

Documents circulated before Tuesday’s meeting said the institution had “exhausted its fiscal resources” and could soon be unable to meet payroll or routine maintenance obligations. The Washington Post reported that internal projections showed the center expecting about $124 million in revenue against a $220 million budget for the fiscal year, with an anticipated deficit of approximately $23 million even after cuts.

Another proposed board resolution explicitly tied Trump’s continued fundraising and oversight to giving him public recognition at the Kennedy Center. It presented 10 possible inscriptions bearing Trump’s name and said trustees believed that without “appropriate recognition” it was unlikely he would lead the institution’s financial rescue.

That resolution was ultimately dropped from Tuesday’s meeting agenda, but Cooper nevertheless ruled against the board’s already-approved August plans to add “Restored and Renovated by President Donald J. Trump” beneath the Kennedy Center name and designate its grounds the “President Donald J. Trump Plaza.”

Rep. Joyce Beatty, the Ohio Democrat and ex-officio Kennedy Center trustee whose lawsuit has driven the legal challenge, accused the president of making the institution’s survival contingent on memorializing him.

“President Trump is holding the Kennedy Center hostage unless he can put his name on this sacred memorial,” Beatty said Tuesday.

Beatty’s lawyers have also challenged the board’s characterization of the building’s safety. They said the closure resolution incorrectly attributed a finding to Delta Consulting Group that the main building was “unsafe for continued occupancy,” arguing that the consultant had expressly disclaimed making such a determination. Center management has separately maintained that construction and the building’s increasingly serious physical condition make continued occupancy unsafe.

The Kennedy Center’s main performance spaces were already effectively headed toward a dormant season. The National Symphony Orchestra announced last month that it would play roughly 60 performances across six Washington-area venues during its 2026-27 season rather than at its longtime Kennedy Center home. The current closure plan also calls for the Kennedy Center Honors and Mark Twain Prize to continue at off-site venues while the REACH remains open for some programming.

What happens after that is now entangled with the same naming fight that has followed the Kennedy Center throughout Trump’s takeover.

The board says the building needs to be emptied so urgently needed repairs can begin. Trump, its chairman, says those repairs will wait until the courts decide whether his name can be put back on it.
