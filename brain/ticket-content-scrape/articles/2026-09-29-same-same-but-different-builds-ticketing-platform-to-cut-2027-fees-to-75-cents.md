# Same Same But Different Builds Ticketing Platform to Cut 2027 Fees to 75 Cents

## Summary

Independent festival Same Same But Different is moving its 2027 ticketing to ShopTickets, a Shopify-based platform built by its own co-founder Brad Sweet, cutting the consumer-facing ticketing fee on its cheapest pass from roughly $56 to 75 cents — about a 98% reduction. Sweet built the platform after growing frustrated handing ticketing economics and the customer relationship to third-party providers; ShopTickets uses Shopify as its commerce engine and layers on ticketing features (inventory, onsales, QR scanning, seat maps) at a leaner cost structure than traditional vendors. The platform has already processed thousands of tickets (~$250K in sales) at smaller test events and is being pitched to other independent promoters, festivals, and venues with no long-term contract required.

### Key Points
- SSBD's 2027 Community Tier GA ticket will cost $295 all-in, about $60 less than the prior year's comparable price.
- ShopTickets pricing: free plan at $1.50/ticket, or $49/month Pro plan at 75 cents/ticket, plus Shopify's own ~2.5-2.9% payment processing.
- Publicly launched via the Shopify App Store in December 2025; already tested across 8 SSBD events plus other promoters' events this year.
- Features include ticket inventory, scheduled onsales, payment plans, QR-code tickets, gate scanning, and reserved-seat maps ("arena grade" seat-map system advertised).
- SSBD holds an equity stake in ShopTickets, giving it a financial interest if the platform gains adoption beyond its own festival.
- No multiyear contracts — standardized monthly + per-ticket pricing, aimed at independent promoters/venues/tours rather than big arena-scale accounts yet.

### Industry Problem
An independent event organizer's core complaint — losing both fee economics and the direct customer relationship to a third-party ticketing vendor — pushed it to build and now sell its own low-fee, Shopify-native alternative, illustrating unmet demand among independent promoters for cheaper, contract-free ticketing with retained customer data.

### Proposed Solution
ShopTickets' answer is stripping out "middleman economics" by building ticketing as a thin layer on Shopify's commerce engine rather than a standalone platform, at flat low per-ticket pricing with no lock-in contract; for an independent primary platform like Etix, this is a build/improve signal to watch — a credible low-fee, contract-free challenger targeting the independent-promoter segment, worth monitoring for whether its low-fee structure holds as it scales to larger venues.

The California festival plans to [use ShopTickets](https://shoptickets.net/), a Shopify-based ticketing platform developed by Same Same But Different co-founder Brad Sweet, for its 2027 edition. The festival says its Community Tier general admission ticket will cost $295 all-in, about $60 less than the opening price offered for the previous festival.

SSBD calculates the change from roughly $56 in ticketing fees on a comparable ticket to 75 cents as a reduction of about 98%.

That comparison refers to the **c** onsumer-facing ticketing fee, however, rather than suggesting that the entire cost of processing a ticket transaction is 75 cents. ShopTickets’ public pricing says its Pro plan costs $49 per month plus 75 cents for each ticket sold, while organizers also need a Shopify plan and pay Shopify’s payment-processing costs, which ShopTickets lists at approximately 2.5% to 2.9%.

Rather than build the payment and commerce infrastructure of a conventional standalone ticketing company, ShopTickets uses Shopify as its underlying engine and adds event-specific functionality on top.

The platform handles features including ticket inventory, scheduled onsales, payment plans, QR-code tickets, gate scanning and reserved-seat maps. Its Shopify App Store listing shows that ShopTickets launched publicly in December 2025, with a free plan charging $1.50 per ticket and the $49 monthly Pro plan reducing that charge to 75 cents.

Sweet said relying on Shopify allows ShopTickets to operate with a different cost structure than a traditional ticketing provider.

“The biggest difference is that we have taken a lot of the traditional ticketing middleman economics out of the equation,” he said. “ShopTickets uses Shopify as the underlying commerce engine and adds the ticketing functionality on top of it, so the cost structure can be dramatically leaner.”

That approach also keeps ticket buyers inside an organizer’s own Shopify storefront and gives promoters direct access to the associated customer data and Shopify’s wider ecosystem of marketing, merchandise and commerce tools.

## Platform Tested Before SSBD Rollout

The 2027 festival will be a larger test for a platform that has already been processing tickets at smaller events.

Sweet told TicketNews that ShopTickets has handled thousands of tickets and roughly $250,000 in sales during early testing with multiple promoters and venues.

SSBD had previously said the system was tested across eight of its own smaller events this year as well as several events operated by others.

At least some of those deployments are already visible publicly. A Same Same-produced RENEW Wellness event in San Diego sold tickets through ShopTickets with pricing displayed as, for example, $54.25 plus a 75-cent ticketing fee for a $55 GA ticket.

Sweet said the smaller events allowed the developers to work through practical issues involving scanning, refunds and transfers, reserved seating, payment plans, promoter reporting and customer support before putting larger events onto the system.

“Every time a promoter, box office person, or customer ran into friction, we could look at why and improve the product before putting much larger events through it,” Sweet said.

ShopTickets is already advertising reserved seating and what it describes as an “arena grade” seat-map system, although the transaction figures disclosed by Sweet so far reflect its early testing rather than an arena-scale deployment.

## ShopTickets Looks Beyond Its Own Festival

SSBD’s switch is also intended to serve as a showcase for a ticketing business that Sweet says was never designed solely for the festival.

“We already have other promoters using it, and the broader goal is to build something that works for independent promoters, festivals, venues, tours, and eventually much larger-scale events,” Sweet told TicketNews.

The company is currently talking with additional promoters while adding functionality for more complicated events, Sweet said.

Unlike many ticketing relationships built around negotiated multiyear agreements, ShopTickets publicly advertises standardized monthly and per-ticket pricing without a contract. Sweet said the company wants promoters to be able to adopt the platform based on its technology and economics rather than being locked into long-term agreements.

The announcement also extends SSBD’s broader effort to keep more of its business infrastructure under the control of the independent festival and its community. Last year, SSBD launched an [equity crowdfunding campaign](https://wefunder.com/SSBD) allowing fans to invest in the festival’s parent company. Its Wefunder campaign says the company has grown to more than $2.5 million in annual revenue while remaining without major outside corporate backing.

According to the ShopTickets announcement, SSBD also holds an equity stake in the ticketing platform, giving the festival a financial interest if the software gains adoption beyond its own events.

For consumers, the most immediate result will be visible at checkout when 2027 SSBD tickets go on sale. For the ticketing business, the larger question is whether the Shopify-based model can maintain its low flat-fee structure as it moves from several thousand transactions at smaller events into festivals and, eventually, the larger venues Sweet is targeting.
