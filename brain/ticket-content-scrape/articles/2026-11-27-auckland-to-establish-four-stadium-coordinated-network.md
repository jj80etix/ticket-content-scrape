# Auckland to establish four-stadium ‘coordinated’ network

## Summary

Auckland Council has unanimously approved a Stadium Network Investment Plan combining the city's four stadia (Eden Park, Western Springs Bowl, Go Media Stadium, North Harbour Stadium) into a coordinated network with defined roles, aiming to direct events to whichever venue delivers the greatest overall value amid financial pressure on the council. Eden Park, the only venue over 50,000 capacity, retains independent governance and its role as an international concert host (approved for 32 concerts annually), while the newly renovated Western Springs Bowl takes a "music-first" focus.

### Key Points
- Four Auckland stadia (Eden Park, Western Springs Bowl, Go Media Stadium, North Harbour Stadium) are being coordinated under one investment plan with a "venue of best fit" model.
- Eden Park keeps independent governance but joins the coordinated network; it is approved to host 32 concerts annually.
- Western Springs Bowl (30,000 cap) reopened for music in October after a roughly NZ$2.5m renovation and will have a music-first focus.
- The plan is driven partly by financial pressure on Auckland Council to improve the city's event-hosting capability and value capture.

## Auckland to establish four-stadium ‘coordinated’ network

By laying out "clear roles" for the venue quartet, the New Zealand council is looking to unlock opportunities for hosting major events

![Coldplay at Eden Park](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Coldplay-at-Eden-Park-©-Eden-Park.png)

Coldplay at Eden Park © Eden Park

Auckland Council has unanimously approved a plan to combine the city’s four stadia into a “coordinated” network, as lawmakers look to strengthen their event-hosting abilities amid financial pressures.

By grouping the outdoor spaces into one coalition, the Stadium Network Investment Plan lays out the priority focus for each space, designating the ‘venue of best fit’ to direct events to the venues that will “deliver the greatest overall value”, the report reads.

The plan will see independently operated Eden Park, New Zealand’s national stadium and the only venue with a capacity over 50,000, folded into the mix, though it is currently set to retain its governance.

It will retain its role as an international concert host, having gained approval this year to host 32 total concerts [annually](https://www.iqmagazine.com/featured-list/stadium-stars-2026/eden-park/).

Eden Park will join the newly renovated Western Springs Bowl, which will have a ‘music-first’ focus, along with the Go Media Stadium and North Harbour Stadium sporting hubs.

The Western Springs Bowl (cap. 30,000) reopened for music in October following a roughly NZ$2.5 million (€1.25m) upgrade, which features a semi-permanent concert stage and VIP hospitality areas.

> “This venue strategy will help to attract more exciting content into the region”

“A coordinated stadium network with clear roles for each venue lets every stadium play to its strengths,” says Vicki Salmon, chair of the council-controlled operator Tātaki Auckland Unlimited. “That means more opportunities across the region and more great events for Aucklanders and visitors.

“Major events play a significant role in the cultural and economic vibrancy of Tāmaki Makaurau Auckland, and the development of this venue strategy will help to attract more exciting content into the region,” Salmon adds.

While the plan does not go so far as to approve new funding or governance changes, it marks the first step of the council’s long-term plan to safeguard the venues.

Financial challenges around all four venues are substantial, with an estimated $300m (€150m) in maintenance needed in the next decade to maintain current standards.

The 125-year-old Eden Park is estimated to need over $120m (€60m) in investment to upgrade ageing infrastructure. The venue also holds a $54m (€27m) council loan due in September 2029.

> “We can make the most of what each one does best and invest wisely”

Any funding decisions will be made separately through business cases and the council’s 2027–2037 long-term Plan. Nevertheless, the plan will help establish priority on the maintenance and development of the four venues.

“Auckland has talked about its stadiums for years, but we’ve never had a plan for the whole network, until now,” says Desley Simpson, chair of the Value for Money Committee.

“I wanted two things from this work: a plan that looks at all our stadiums together, and one that delivers value for money for Aucklanders. This plan does both. Each venue now has a clear role, so we can make the most of what each one does best and invest wisely for future generations,” he says.

Outside of the country’s biggest city, national leadership is setting up a [major events funding scheme](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/09/new-zealands-major-events-fund-to-unlock-full-potential/) to attract international cultural events, as part of an effort to enhance its position as a concert host.
