# K-pop’s ‘Big Four’ unite with Korean gov’t to launch Fanomenon festival

## Summary

K-pop's "Big Four" agencies (HYBE, JYP, SM, YG) and the South Korean government are launching a joint "Coachella-like" festival, Fanomenon, debuting in Seoul in December 2027 before expanding to Los Angeles in 2028.

### Key Points
- Projected to generate over KRW 1 trillion in economic impact and draw 520,000 attendees, about 40% from abroad.
- Combines concerts, a fandom awards show, and a K-culture expo; overseen by a presidential committee pending Fair Trade Commission approval of the joint venture.
- Revenue will come from ticket sales and sponsorship.

## Transcript

## K-pop’s ‘Big Four’ unite with Korean gov’t to launch Fanomenon festival

The "Coachella-like" event from HYBE, JYP, SM, and YG will launch in Seoul next December before landing in Los Angeles in 2028

![Stray Kids ](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Stray-Kids-©-JYP-Entertainment.png)

Stray Kids © JYP Entertainment

K-pop’s ‘Big Four’ agencies – HYBE, JYP Entertainment, SM Entertainment, and YG Entertainment – have revealed the first details of their music festival joint venture, dubbed Fanomenon, which is set to launch in Seoul next December and expand to Los Angeles in 2028.

The debut edition is set to run for nearly two weeks, from 2-12 December 2027, spanning across the forthcoming Seoul Arena and Goyang’s KINTEX Exhibition Centre. It is projected to generate over KRW 1 trillion (€604m) in economic impact and attract a total of 520,000 people, with nearly 40% coming in from abroad.

Fanomenon is expected to combine programming from domestic and international acts, fandom awards ceremonies, and K-culture exhibition and experience programs. Following its Seoul debut, organisers are planning the expansion of the “ [Coachella-like](https://www.korean-culture.org/koreanet/view.do?seq=1055109&page=1&pageSize=10&photoPageSize=6&totalCount=0&searchType=null&searchText=&searchCondition=1&cateCode=) ” festival to California in May 2028. The LA venue has not been announced.

The music portion will take place across Seoul Arena, scheduled for completion in May 2027, combining performances from all four agencies alongside “star names from abroad”, according to [*The Korea Herald*](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10821975). The awards segment of Fanomenon, also at Seoul Arena, will honour the year’s 10 most active fan communities, with each fandom’s artist accepting the award on their behalf. The recognition will take place annually in the capital.

Alongside additional live performances, KINTEX will be utilised as an interactive cultural expo, featuring hands-on K-culture experiences and showcases spanning beauty, cuisine, fashion, gaming, and more.

The large-scale festival will bridge South Korea’s public and private sectors, to be run by the [four-agency joint venture](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/04/k-pops-big-four-unite-for-global-music-festival-joint-venture/) after the country’s Fair Trade Commission approves its formation. It is being overseen by the Presidential Committee on Popular Culture Exchange, which is co-chaired by JYP founder J.Y. Park and Chae Hwi-young, Culture, Sports and Tourism Minister. The Ministry will also support its promotion, tourism, and transport, while the venture will generate revenue through ticket sales and sponsorship.

> “We will ensure Korea solidifies its place as the global hub of the fandom industry”

“The true power of K-culture stems not just from the artists and content, but from the fandoms that have nurtured and spread it worldwide,” said Park, per the [*Korea Times*](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/entertainment/k-pop/20260727/new-festival-fanomenon-aims-to-position-korea-as-global-fandom-capital). “By creating a successful model that seamlessly blends private-sector creativity with government support, we will ensure Korea solidifies its place as the global hub of the fandom industry.”

In addition to the festival and awards strands, Park also outlined early-stage plans to build 20,000-capacity venues aligned with K-culture centres in key overseas markets to further South Korea’s cultural reach.

The efforts around Fanomenon, a major alliance for the competing agencies, arrive as live music becomes an increasingly important revenue stream for the companies’ operations.

Earlier this week, HYBE reported record revenues for Q2 2026, with turnover more than doubling year-on-year to KRW 1.45 trillion (€874m) in what the company called an “ [all-time high](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/hybe-revenue-doubles-year-on-year-in-all-time-high-quarter/) ” quarter. At the heart of the record results is BTS’s mammoth *Arirang World Tour*, alongside revenue generated from other “artist activities” like concerts, recorded music, and advertising from across its roster of entertainment acts.

Concerts and merchandise also boosted SM Entertainment to a near [21% revenue bump](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/05/concerts-merch-help-bump-sm-entertainments-q1-revenue-21/?utm_campaign=232706262-IQ%20Index&utm_medium=email&_hsenc=p2ANqtz-_3ztDP3rt27crNq4hXr0f02-cmX2ENTHi4HLjWhGnk6I8Cw6eaeSESzztMkAplrj_sGUgtCgU6UvTpTDtg6yvlYpkE9w&_hsmi=2&utm_content=2&utm_source=hs_email) in Q1 2026, with JYP also seeing a [32% YOY revenue boost](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/08/stray-kids-boost-jyps-concert-revenue-by-342/) in the same period to KRW 186 billion (€112m). [BIGBANG’s 20th anniversary tour](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/06/k-pop-titans-bigbang-plot-20th-anniversary-stadium-tour/) is expected to be a major driver for [YG](https://m.newspim.com/news/view/20260115000072?fbclid=IwY2xjawRO-BxleHRuA2FlbQIxMABicmlkETFnM2RyQVh0clZETldYTmE0c3J0YwZhcHBfaWQQMjIyMDM5MTc4ODIwMDg5MgABHk2-_VaXXcwDDs5B5iQxhUof7bi25zFtJfB1Q-Yj6ShbcD83X7TX6ANyhPtC_aem_ShOlmk8a0WujvoXGCoMqJg) this year, which posted KRW 150.7bn (€86.3m) in revenue in 2025, up 45% YOY.

Taken all together, the alliance between the private and public sectors demonstrates the power of live performance and cultural currency for South Korea, with the planned Fanomenon venture positioning festivals as the union’s next evolution.
