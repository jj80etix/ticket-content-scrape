# ‘This is only the beginning’: Chile’s Bamba Festival draws 50k with debut edition

## Summary

Chile's debut "Bamba Festival," an all-Latin-artist event headlined by Marc Anthony, Fito Páez, Carlos Vives, and Juanes, drew 50,000 fans across two days in Santiago and generated an estimated $8 million in local economic activity. Organizers, including co-creator Juanes and promoter Lotus Producciones, have pledged a second edition in 2027.

### Key Points
- Held at the same Parque O'Higgins site as Chilean Lollapalooza, which draws 80,000/day, suggesting room to scale.
- Positioned explicitly against "Anglo-headliner" international festival franchises in the region.
- Concept originated in 2019; took seven years to reach a debut edition.
- 2027 dates and lineup not yet announced.

## ‘This is only the beginning’: Chile’s Bamba Festival draws 50k with debut edition

Organisers say the response signals 'real demand' for the 'all-Latin' festival brand and pledge its return in 2027

![Marc Anthony at Bamba](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Marc-Anthony-at-Bamba-©-Victor-S.-Galvez.png)

Marc Anthony at Bamba © Victor S. Galvez

Organisers behind Chile’s Bamba Festival have pledged the return of the “all-Latin festival” after drawing 50,000 total fans, saying the debut is “only the beginning” for the brand.

Bamba debuted at Santiago’s Parque O’Higgins from 24-25 October, headlined by Marc Anthony, Fito Paez, Carlos Vives, and Juanes, the latter of which is a co-organiser alongside Chilean promoter Lotus Producciones.

Designed to “take a different position” from international festival franchises, organisers say the aspiration is to build a brand that highlights [Latin culture](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/05/chiles-lotus-rolls-out-details-of-new-two-day-latin-festival/) and talent in its home territory.

“Latin music has never had more global reach, yet the region’s major festivals are still largely built around Anglo headliners,” says Lotus director Sebastián de la Barra. “Bamba shows there is room, and real demand, for a large-scale festival rooted in our own artists and delivered with an international standard of hospitality.”

With 20 artists performing across two stages, including Bomba Estéreo, Orishas, Jovanotti, Elena Rose, Los Vásquez, Conociendo Rusia, Ráfaga, and Amigo de Artistas, organisers curated a programme spanning pop, rock, alternative, and dance music.

The festival, which drew 25,000 people each day, generated an estimated $8 million (€7.13m) in economic activity for the Chilean capital, organisers say.

It was held on the same greenfield site as the Chilean edition of Lollapalooza, also promoted by Lotus, which welcomed 80,000 people per day for its 14th edition earlier this year, demonstrating the site’s scalability for future iterations.

> “A festival that feels like ours, built around the music and culture of Latin America”

The concept has been in development since 2019, when Juanes played that festival, with Lotus and Juanes working with the artist’s manager Rafael Restrepo to curate this year’s event.

“Bamba started seven years ago as an idea in a dressing room in Chile: a festival that feels like ours, built around the music and culture of Latin America,” Juanes says. “Seeing 50,000 people celebrate it this weekend tells us the region was ready for it.”

Hospitality was also tailored to the brand’s overarching ethos, with culinary offerings from Peruvian and Chilean chefs and a Mexican bar in the mix. The festival also featured two premium areas, Club Bamba, presented by LATAM Airlines, and La Terraza, presented by Cenco Malls.

Banco de Chile served as the presenter of the festival and the partner of the main stage.

Now, organisers are looking ahead to the future of the brand, with de la Barra saying: “Our focus now is building on this first edition for 2027.”

Dates and a lineup are to be revealed for a second edition.

“This is only the beginning,” Juanes says.
