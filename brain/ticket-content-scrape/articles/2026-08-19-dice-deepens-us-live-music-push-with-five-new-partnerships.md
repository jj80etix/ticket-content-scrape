# DICE Deepens US Live Music Push With Five New Partnerships

## Summary

DICE signed five new independent venues and promoters across New York, Seattle and Miami — including Brooklyn's reopening Saint Vitus Bar — extending a "record-breaking 12 months" since its Fever merger, and framed the wins around its no-hidden-fees upfront pricing, fan-to-fan ticket returns/waitlists, mobile anti-touting tech, and audience-data tools for operators.

### Key Points
- New partners: Saint Vitus Bar (Brooklyn, reopening in Bushwick), ViViD Presents (Seattle), The Originals, Phase 3 Presents, Studio 60 (all Miami).
- DICE pitches independents on upfront pricing without hidden fees, fan-to-fan returns/waitlists, mobile-first anti-touting technology, and operator audience data.
- Growth follows DICE's combination with Fever, described as a record 12-month stretch for U.S. signings.
- DICE's consumer discovery app is positioned as a demand-generation channel for these venues/promoters.

### Highlights
- DICE explicitly markets "upfront pricing without hidden fees" as the wedge to win independent venues away from incumbent ticketing platforms — a direct fee-transparency pitch.
- Five new signings in one push across three major U.S. markets (NYC, Seattle, Miami) signal accelerating independent-venue market share gains post-Fever merger.

### Industry Problem
Independent venues and promoters are actively choosing ticketing partners based on fee transparency, anti-touting protection, and fan-relationship tools (returns/waitlists) rather than reach alone — meaning any primary platform seen as fee-opaque or return-inflexible is losing consideration in venue RFPs.

### Proposed Solution
Etix should benchmark its own fee transparency, ticket-return/waitlist flexibility, and anti-bot tooling against DICE's stated pitch, since this is the exact feature set independent venues are now weighing competitors on; a build/improve push on fan-to-fan returns and clearer upfront pricing messaging would directly counter DICE's positioning in these venue conversations.

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/IMG_0757.jpeg)

DICE is adding a new slate of independent venues and promoters to its U.S. ticketing network, striking partnerships in New York, Seattle and Miami as the company looks to deepen its position in the American live music market.

The ticketing platform has signed Brooklyn’s Saint Vitus Bar, Seattle-based ViViD Presents and Miami operators The Originals, Phase 3 Presents and Studio 60. The additions come after what DICE described as a record-breaking 12 months following its combination with Fever.

The new partners cover a wide stretch of the live entertainment business, from heavy metal and independent club shows to electronic music, nightlife and large-scale experiential events.

Among the most notable additions is Saint Vitus Bar, the celebrated Brooklyn metal venue preparing to reopen in a new Bushwick location this fall.

The venue has selected DICE as its ticketing partner as it begins its next chapter in Bushwick.

On the West Coast, ViViD Presents joins the platform after establishing itself as a major player in Seattle’s electronic music and nightlife scene.

The promoter is behind events including its EXP summer series and On the Hiyu, an event space housed aboard a converted Washington State ferry. ViViD has also transformed warehouses, museums, parks, boats and piers into temporary dancefloors while working with independent producers throughout the Pacific Northwest.

DICE is also expanding its reach in Miami through three new partnerships.

The Originals, the team formerly behind the HYDE Beach events at SLS Miami, produces nightlife and hospitality experiences ranging from Miami Music Week events to international productions.

Phase 3 Presents, founded by Yair Hilu and Josh Horwitz, has built its presence around major Miami event weeks including Miami Music Week and Art Basel. Its events have featured electronic music heavyweights including DJ Snake, Deadmau5 and Chris Lake.

Meanwhile, Studio 60 brings another Miami venue into DICE’s network. Located in the Allapattah Entertainment District, the newer nightlife destination combines high-end production with a 23-hour liquor license and an emphasis on creative programming.

DICE said the latest signings reflect a wider move among independent venues and promoters toward ticketing platforms designed around closer relationships with fans.

Its platform includes fan-to-fan ticket returns and waiting lists, upfront pricing without hidden fees, mobile-focused anti-touting technology and audience data for event operators. DICE also uses its consumer app as a discovery platform, helping fans find upcoming shows while giving venues and promoters another avenue to reach potential ticket buyers.

The latest partnerships add to DICE’s growing roster of independent operators as it pushes further into major U.S. live entertainment markets.
