# 963 Clean Energy Concerts & Counting: How AY Young Grew His Battery Tour Into ‘A Platform For Impact’

## Summary

Q&A profile of AY Young, founder of "The Battery Tour" (billed as the longest-running clean-energy concert series) and UN Young Leader for the Sustainable Development Goals, on his path from Kansas City songwriter to sustainability-focused touring artist, including a recent National Scout Jamboree stop and upcoming Climate Week NYC appearances.

### Key Points
- AY Young founded The Battery Tour, billed as the longest-running clean-energy concert series; named UN Young Leader for the SDGs (2020) and Kansas City's first Sustainability Ambassador.
- Recently performed for 15,000+ attendees at the National Scout Jamboree.
- Upcoming stops: Houston Energy & Climate Week and Climate Week NYC, partnering with Climate First Bank to promote its Impact Checking account.
- Currently seeking a booking agent and distribution partner.

## Transcript

SUSTAINA-HYPHENATE: AY Young is an artist, producer, United Nations Young Leader for the Sustainable Development Goals, Sustainability Ambassador for Kansas City and founder of Battery Tour. Photo courtesy AY Young

*AY Young’s journey as a musician has taken him from a homeschooled youth in Kansas City who got into songwriting after initially penning poems, to competing on “The X Factor” and then launching “The Battery Tour,” notable as the longest-running clean energy concert series in the world. AY – who names Michael Jackson, Prince, Kid Cudi, Peter Gabriel, The Lumineers and The Head and the Heart amongst his influences – was named a United Nations Young Leader for the Sustainable Development Goals in 2020 and was recently appointed as the first-ever Sustainability Ambassador for Kansas City. Despite all that the 35-year-old has accomplished as a rising independent artist, he’s just getting started and is looking for a booking agent and the right distribution partner as he continues following his passion for music and the planet.* Pollstar *caught up with AY shortly after he performed at the National Scout Jamboree in July.**His upcoming tour schedule includes appearances at [Houston Energy & Climate Week](https://climateweekhouston.com/events/ay-young) and Climate Week NYC. AY Young teamed up with Climate First Bank to promote its new Impact Checking account, which the eco-friendly bank will be spotlighting at Climate Week NYC.*

**Pollstar: How did the National Jamboree go?**  
**AY Young:** The energy was unmatched. As an independent artist, opportunities to perform for an audience that size don’t come along every day. Being able to share my music with 15,000 Scouts and thousands of support staff was thrilling. Everyone was engaged, singing, dancing and making the performance their own. Moments like that remind me why live performance and people are at the center of everything I do.  
  
**How did growing up in Kansas City influence you?**  
Kansas City has deep musical roots. It helped shape modern jazz and has produced icons across genres, from Charlie Parker and Melissa Etheridge to Janelle Monáe and Tech N9ne. Growing up around that Missouri music legacy inspired me, but it also taught me that breaking into the industry would require resilience and creating my own path.  
Mentors like Tech N9ne had a major impact on me. Watching him build Strange Music into one of the world’s leading independent labels showed me what was possible through ownership, consistency and staying true to your vision. That independent spirit helped inspire the path that eventually became Battery Tour.  
  
I also grew up seeing the Troost divide, neighborhood inequality and limited access to opportunity. Coming from those communities shaped what I write about, how I connect with people and my commitment to using music to make an impact.  
  
**Anything else you wanted to add about what inspired your passion for sustainability?**  
It really started with wanting people to hear my music. After “The X Factor,” I didn’t have a booking agent, major streaming numbers or the traditional industry infrastructure behind me, and I kept hearing “no.”  
  
Even today, I’ve built this journey independently without formal booking representation. That pushed me to find another way to perform. The obvious solution was to power my own concerts wherever I could, from street corners to traditional venues, but a loud diesel generator wasn’t a practical option. I went back to the values I learned growing up homeschooled and through Scouting: stewardship, resourcefulness and building solutions with what you have.  
  
Battery Tour began as my Eagle Scout project and developed into concerts powered by batteries and solar energy, creating a model that could work almost anywhere. I have to be honest, initially it wasn’t about starting a sustainability movement. It was about finding a way to make and perform music without waiting for permission.  
But when I learned that more than a billion people lacked access to energy, while millions more lacked clean water and food, I realized Battery Tour could become much more than a touring solution. It could become a business model and platform that used concerts to raise resources and support communities.  
  
Since 2012, we’ve used music to help bring energy, food, clean water and other forms of support to communities in more than 19 countries. Battery Tour started as a solution for my career, but it grew into a platform for real impact.  
  
I’ve now performed more than 1,400 live shows across concerts, festivals, conferences, private events and community activations. 963 of those performances have been powered by clean energy.  
  
\[Battery Tour\] has become something that we can deploy for other artists. It’s evolved into a live entertainment company that can support artists, venues, festivals, conferences, brands and existing tours. We’ve spent years proving this model works in the real world.

**How does Battery Tour work?**  
Battery Tour isn’t a one-size-fits-all production company. It was built to be flexible. We can produce a standalone event, support an existing festival, integrate into another artist’s tour or provide clean-energy production for conferences, cities, venues and private events. It’s also designed to scale. Not every production can become completely clean overnight, but that shouldn’t stop a partner from taking a meaningful step in the right direction. We can support frontlines, backlines, lighting, individual stages or full productions, depending on what makes sense for the artist, event and budget.  
  
The platform is also built on radical collaboration. We work with companies that already have the right technologies, infrastructure and expertise, then help consult, source and procure what is needed for each production. Battery Tour is not about pretending one company has every solution. It is about bringing the right partners together and building the best solution for the show.  
  
One challenge in building Battery Tour is that some people began to assume it was a requirement for booking \[me\]. That isn’t the case. I’m a touring artist who can perform on any professional stage. I can be booked on my own at any venue, festival or event, or Battery Tour can be added to create even more production, storytelling and partnership value.  
  
That flexibility is really the larger vision: make clean-energy production more accessible, prove that collaboration can move the industry forward and give artists, promoters and venues multiple ways to work with us. … That same philosophy of using music for impact became the foundation for Project 17.  
  
**What can you tell readers about Project 17?**  
Project 17 is my legacy project. Really, it came from my conversation with the Secretary-General of the UN, António Guterres, who was discussing the UN’s 17 Sustainable Development Goals and asked me “What should we do, AY?” … At the center of Project 17 is a 17-song album inspired by the United Nations’ 17 Sustainable Development Goals. The music is still in development because I want to build the entire ecosystem around it, not just release an album.  
  
For each goal, we’re creating opportunities to pair a song with major artists with a brand sponsor, an NGO or implementation partner already doing the work, live experiences, and a documentary episode that brings it all together. … We’re actively looking for artists, brands, agencies and strategic partners who want to help shape \[Project 17\], and not for notoriety, but because they want to change the world and believe music and art are the best pathway.  
  
**Any milestone performances that stand out?**  
I’ve been fortunate to perform on some incredible stages, including the Roxy Theatre in Los Angeles with Lime Cordiale, Cervantes’ Masterpiece Ballroom with Living Legends, the first clean-energy concert in Times Square, Rock in Rio in Lisbon and recently halftime for the Phoenix Suns.  
  
But honestly, the performances that stay with me the most aren’t always the biggest ones. It’s the people. Playing at Dzaleka Refugee Camp in Malawi or performing in Honduras for children who may never otherwise experience a concert gives me a different kind of joy.  
  
In those places, I get to use music to share a message of hope, dreams, passion and opportunity. Those moments take me back to my roots and remind me why I started making music in the first place. The people give me more than I could ever give them.  
  
What also fills my cup is seeing what the platform can make possible after the show, whether that’s helping bring energy, clean water, food or other resources into a community. The performance creates the connection, but the impact that follows is what makes it a true milestone.  
  
Whether I’m playing a major festival or for a small community halfway around the world, the goal is always the same: create a moment where people feel connected and walk away with hope.
