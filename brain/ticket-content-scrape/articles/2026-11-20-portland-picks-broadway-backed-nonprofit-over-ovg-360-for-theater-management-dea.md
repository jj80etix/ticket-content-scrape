# Portland Picks Broadway-Backed Nonprofit Over OVG 360 for Theater Management Deal

## Summary

Portland, Oregon selected a new nonprofit backed by Broadway Across America — Portland Cultural Trust — over Oak View Group subsidiary OVG 360 to take over management of five city-owned performing arts venues starting July 2027, ending Metro's ~38-year oversight role.

### Key Points
- Evaluators unanimously favored Portland Cultural Trust, 1,068 points to 849 for OVG 360, across two scoring rounds.
- The nonprofit submitted 37 letters of support from labor and arts groups; OVG 360 submitted none.
- Deal covers Keller Auditorium, Arlene Schnitzer Concert Hall and three Antoinette Hatfield Hall theaters (~800,000 annual visitors).
- Five-year management agreement still pending completion; Metro's role ends June 30, 2027.

The city of Portland has selected a newly formed nonprofit backed by Broadway Across America to negotiate management of five major performing arts venues, choosing the organization over Oak View Group subsidiary OVG 360 as the city prepares to end nearly four decades of regional government oversight.

Portland Cultural Arts Inc., doing business as Portland Cultural Trust, received the city’s notice of intent to negotiate on Nov. 9. The organization is expected to take over operations of Portland’5 Centers for the Arts on July 1, 2027, pending completion of a five-year management agreement.

The selection followed a competitive bidding process in which Portland Cultural Trust received unanimous support from the city’s evaluators, according to [new reporting from Oregon ArtsWatch](https://www.orartswatch.org/portland-cultural-trust-chosen-to-manage-the-portland5-centers-for-the-arts/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

The publication reported that all seven voting evaluators favored Portland Cultural Trust over OVG 360 across every category in two rounds of scoring. The nonprofit received 1,068 points compared with 849 for OVG 360. The evaluation committee also included 12 technical advisers.

The nonprofit submitted 37 letters of support from labor organizations, arts groups and community organizations, while OVG 360 submitted none, according to the report. Supporters included Portland Opera, Oregon Ballet Theatre, White Bird and several International Alliance of Theatrical Stage Employees (IATSE) locals.

The proposed agreement covers Keller Auditorium, Arlene Schnitzer Concert Hall and three theaters within Antoinette Hatfield Hall: the Newmark, Winningstad and Brunish theaters.

Together, the five city-owned venues attract approximately 800,000 visitors annually, hosting Broadway productions, concerts, orchestral performances, opera, dance, comedy and other events.

The management change will end Metro’s oversight of Portland’5, which began in 1989. Metro, the regional government agency currently responsible for operations, is scheduled to conclude its role June 30, 2027.

## Broadway Across America’s Role in the New Organization

Portland Cultural Trust was incorporated in August by leadership associated with Broadway Across America, the national theatrical presenter responsible for touring Broadway productions at Keller Auditorium.

The proposed nonprofit structure brings together Broadway Across America, the Oregon Symphony and the Portland State University Foundation, combining local arts organizations with the operational resources of an established commercial theatrical presenter.

Broadway Across America, part of the John Gore theatrical organization, has presented touring Broadway productions at Keller Auditorium for more than three decades. According to proposal information reviewed by Oregon ArtsWatch, the company has owned, operated or managed 25 professional theaters across 14 states.

OVG 360, meanwhile, cited its much larger international venue-management portfolio in its competing proposal. The Oak View Group subsidiary reported managing 235 venues worldwide, including 57 multi-asset campuses.

Despite that difference in scale, Portland Cultural Trust attracted substantially more local support.

Tracy Wenckus, interim general director of Portland Opera, wrote in a letter supporting the nonprofit’s proposal that no organization was better positioned to manage the transition than Broadway Across America working with local partners.

Portland Opera has a longstanding relationship with Broadway Across America through Broadway in Portland, while the Oregon Symphony is a major resident organization at Arlene Schnitzer Concert Hall.

The nonprofit’s proposal also calls for an Arts Advisory Council that would provide resident performing arts organizations and other venue users with a formal channel to its management and governing board.

The proposed structure represents an alternative to the conventional third-party venue-management model offered by OVG 360. Rather than contracting with an established commercial venue operator, the city would work with a locally governed nonprofit supported by major performing arts organizations and a national theatrical company.

That arrangement also creates questions about how the operator will manage relationships among its participating organizations, commercial promoters and competing presenters.

Broadway Across America already holds important programming rights at Keller Auditorium, where touring Broadway productions are among the most commercially significant events.

A [review of Portland’5 procurement documents by Portland Civic Lab](https://www.portlandciviclab.org/deep-dives/venue-portfolio?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) found that the future operator must honor the existing Broadway agreement, which extends through the 2032-33 season.

The relationship between a major presenter and the prospective venue manager makes booking priorities, rental arrangements and oversight of transactions involving affiliated organizations particularly important provisions of the eventual contract.

There is no indication that existing booking arrangements will necessarily change, but the management agreement will determine how those interests are handled.

## Financial Details Remain Unresolved

Although Portland Cultural Trust received strong support during the evaluation process, important financial elements of its proposal remain undisclosed.

Oregon ArtsWatch reported that projected annual financial benefits to the city, venues and community were redacted from the publicly available proposal.

Portland Cultural Trust CEO Rich Jaffe declined to elaborate on those figures before the agreement is completed, telling the publication that the information would be released when the contract is finalized.

The lack of publicly disclosed financial terms leaves unanswered questions about management compensation, revenue sharing, operating subsidies and how financial risks will be distributed between the nonprofit and the city.

Portland’s [official procurement announcement](https://www.portland.gov/arts/keller/news/2026/9/10/portland-launched-request-proposals-venue-operator-portland5?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) identifies financial sustainability as a central goal. The city wants the new operator to establish a stable, financially positive operating model while diversifying revenue and maintaining affordable access for audiences and local arts organizations.

The city also faces significant capital obligations at the venues, particularly Keller Auditorium, which requires substantial seismic and infrastructure improvements.

The procurement framework calls for the operator to manage day-to-day maintenance while the city retains responsibility for major capital projects.

Ticketing operations and booking policies are additional considerations.

Portland’5 currently accommodates several ticketing arrangements, with resident organizations and other presenters using their own sales systems alongside the venue-operated ticketing platform.

Portland Civic Lab’s review found that approximately 40% of tickets sold during fiscal 2025-26 were processed through Portland’5’s own system. The remaining tickets were handled through other arrangements, including those used by Broadway and resident arts organizations.

The draft procurement framework provides for the transfer of existing ticketing contracts and requires existing booking policies and rental rates to remain in place during the first year. Subsequent changes would require city approval and advance notice.

How the new management structure will handle customer data, ticketing relationships, venue fees and commercial booking opportunities will be important details as negotiations proceed.

## City Council Oversight and the 2027 Transition

The selection has also generated discussion about the role of Portland’s elected officials in approving the management change.

Under the city’s revised government structure, the administration can negotiate and approve the operating agreement without a separate City Council vote.

Oregon ArtsWatch reported that several council members, including Olivia Clark, Mitch Green and Dan Ryan, have raised concerns about the limited opportunity for council review of a decision involving major public cultural facilities.

Council President Jamie Dunphy, however, expressed support for the nonprofit’s selection, emphasizing the importance of maintaining affordable performances, protecting labor relationships and strengthening access for local performing arts organizations.

The transition also takes place alongside Portland’s longer-term consideration of a new Broadway-capable performing arts facility at Portland State University.

In September, the City Council authorized additional planning for a potential new facility and the future of Keller Auditorium. The projects remain subject to further study and funding decisions.

The proposed Portland State University venue is not included in the current Portland’5 management agreement, although its development could eventually affect the distribution of Broadway productions and other programming across the city’s theaters.

The city’s management requirements also emphasize workforce continuity and honoring existing labor agreements as operations move away from Metro.

Portland officials expect performances, bookings and other venue operations to continue during the transition without disruption.

Contract negotiations with Portland Cultural Trust are scheduled to begin in late November. Until an agreement is finalized, the financial terms, governance arrangements and specific management responsibilities remain subject to negotiation ahead of the planned July 1, 2027, takeover.
