# JYP Entertainment reports 15% revenue dip between Stray Kids’ tours

## Summary

K-pop agency JYP Entertainment reported a 15.1% year-on-year revenue drop in Q2, attributed to touring-schedule timing after Stray Kids' record concert revenue surge the prior year, alongside a 41.4% fall in operating profit. TWICE member Chaeyoung also announced her departure from the label during the same period.

### Key Points
- Q2 revenue ₩183.1bn, down 15.1% YoY; operating profit ₩31bn, down 41.4% YoY.
- Overall concert revenue fell 35.7% across JYP's roster; appearances revenue fell 28%.
- Stray Kids remain JYP's primary live-revenue driver, with new tour dates, branded festivals, and a Rock in Rio headline slot planned.
- TWICE member Chaeyoung becomes the second member to depart the group this year.

## JYP Entertainment reports 15% revenue dip between Stray Kids’ tours

The K-pop company's revenue contracted due to the timing of ticket sales from its key artist's touring schedule

![Stray Kids in Amsterdam](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Stray-Kids-in-Amsterdam-©-JYP-EntertainmentVincent-van-den-Boogaard.png)

Stray Kids in Amsterdam

K-Pop production company JYP Entertainment has reported a 15.1% dip in year-on-year revenue, which it blamed on the timing of key tours, as a second member of flagship band TWICE also announced her departure from the company.

In its second quarter results, the company revealed revenue for the period of ₩183.1 billion, a year-on-year drop of 15.1%, and an operating profit of ₩31 billion, a 41.4% fall compared to the same period last year.

It attributed the drop to the “high base” of Stray Kids’ “large scale concerts” during the band’s *DominATE* world tour, when concert revenue [surged 342%](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/08/stray-kids-boost-jyps-concert-revenue-by-342/) to a record high for the company.

With the company’s live revenues dependant on a handful of bands, Stray Kids have recently hit the road once again, having last played a concert on 2 August at the KSPO Dome in Seoul. This wrapped up the opening leg of their *Run It* tour, and will commence the later this month in Tokyo, with the firm’s revenue dip covering part of the period between the two tours.

JYP also noted that appearances revenue, which includes fan concerts, showcases and festival slots, fell 28% year-on-year, despite Stray Kids’ headliner bookings at major festivals and multiple fan meeting events.

Overall concert revenue declined 35.7% across the company’s roster of artists with the biggest contributions to the revenue stream being global tours from DAY6 and TWICE, as well an Asian tour by ITZY.

Stray Kids are positioned as the company’s primary live‑music growth engine for the remainder of 2026 and into 2027, with the company telling investors it expects this to come from a “ [Stray Kids’ branded festivals](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/05/stray-kids-straycity-to-bring-festival-production-to-special-concert-run/) in three cities based on fandom strategy tailored to local festival culture” as well as a headline appearance at Rock in Rio next month.

The company also said its earnings outlook includes an expansion of tour-linked city pop-ups, including those themed around Stray Kids’ cartoon character SKZOO, which will be grown in the second half of the year in North America, Europe, Latin America, and Asia.

This comes as TWICE member Chaeyoung became the second member of the band to depart from the crucial member of JYP’s roster. Writing in a statement, Chaeyoung said: “I want to deeply thank everyone at JYP and Team TWICE for believing in and supporting me over the past 14 years.”

TWICE recently completed one of the largest K‑pop tours of the past year, playing 81 shows across 44 cities spanning Asia, Oceania, North America and Europe, forming a major portion of JYP’s 2025 live revenue.
