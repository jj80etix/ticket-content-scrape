# Funcode.hu and Nuweb Group launch FunPass, Hungary’s new face-value resale platform, with A$AP Rocky and Pitbull events

## Summary

Hungarian primary ticketer funcode.hu has launched FunPass, an in-house face-value resale marketplace built on technology from ticketing infrastructure provider Nuweb Group. The platform caps resale prices at the original face value and keeps transactions inside funcode.hu's own system rather than ceding them to unofficial secondary sites, launching initially across six events including A$AP Rocky and Pitbull concerts at Budapest venues. Nuweb built the underlying transaction technology while funcode.hu retained full control of the branding and customer experience, a partnership model the companies frame as a template other primary ticketers could replicate.

### Key Points
- FunPass restricts resale to face value; a percentage fee applies, split between buyer and seller as they choose.
- Research cited by Hungary's largest independent ticketer estimates 30-35% of tickets for some major concerts get resold after initial onsale.
- Nuweb Group made its resale technology available to ticketing partners in February 2026; funcode.hu built a custom front-end on top rather than adopting a generic product.
- Launch covers six events, including Pitbull and Deep Purple at MVM Dome and A$AP Rocky at Budapest Arena.
- Funcode.hu frames the goal as reclaiming visibility over ticket ownership, transfer, and validity that is lost once tickets move to unofficial resale channels.

### Highlights
- 30-35% of tickets for some major concerts reportedly get resold post-onsale, per Hungary's largest independent ticketing provider — a concrete estimate of leakage to secondary markets.
- FunPass enforces a hard face-value price cap rather than allowing markup, directly countering scalping/inflated-price dynamics.
- The build used an API-led, white-label partnership (Nuweb supplies backend transaction tech; funcode.hu owns the UI/brand) rather than an in-house build or handing off to a third-party marketplace.
- Nuweb's resale product has been available to ticketing partners since February 2026, suggesting this integration pattern is replicable for other primary platforms.

### Industry Problem
Primary ticketers lose visibility, revenue, and fraud control once tickets migrate to unofficial secondary markets — they can't verify legitimate ownership, transaction price, or entry validity, and captured resale volume (30-35% of some shows) represents fees and customer relationships ceded to outside platforms.

### Proposed Solution
Funcode.hu addressed this by partnering with a specialist vendor (Nuweb Group) for the resale transaction backend while keeping the discovery, listing, and purchase experience under its own brand — an approach Etix could evaluate for a face-value-capped, in-house resale offering that keeps fee revenue and ticket-validity control in-house rather than losing that volume to StubHub/Vivid Seats-style secondary markets.
