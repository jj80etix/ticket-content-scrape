# Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders Owner Sues My Chemical Romance, Live Nation Over Stage Costumes

## Summary

Pro Silver Star Ltd., owner of the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders brand, sued My Chemical Romance and promoter Live Nation over costumes resembling the cheerleaders' trademark uniforms worn during the band's Hollywood Bowl "Black Parade" anniversary shows, seeking a restraining order and damages. Trademark attorneys cited by Billboard question whether the claim can overcome First Amendment protection for artistic expression, since the costumes appeared in a theatrical, not commercial, context.

### Key Points
- Lawsuit filed over costumes worn during an Oct. 21 Hollywood Bowl performance of "Teenagers"; band continued using them after an Oct. 22 cease-and-desist.
- Claims include trade dress infringement, false association/endorsement and trademark dilution by tarnishment.
- Live Nation named as a defendant in its capacity as promoter; no public indication it was involved in costume design.
- Legal experts flag a likely First Amendment defense given the theatrical/artistic (not commercial) use.

The corporate owner of the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders has filed a lawsuit against rock band My Chemical Romance and concert promoter Live Nation, alleging that dancers wearing costumes resembling the cheerleading squad’s trademark uniforms during a recent Hollywood Bowl performance infringed its intellectual property rights and damaged the brand’s reputation.

Pro Silver Star Ltd., which owns the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders brand, is seeking a temporary restraining order and injunction to prevent the costumes from being used during the band’s remaining Hollywood Bowl performances on October 30 and 31. The company is also seeking unspecified monetary damages.

The dispute stems from My Chemical Romance’s October 21 performance at the Los Angeles venue, the first of five concerts marking the 20th anniversary of the band’s 2006 album, *The Black Parade*.

During a performance of the song “Teenagers,” dancers appeared onstage wearing blue-and-white costumes that closely resembled the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders’ distinctive uniforms. The outfits included white boots, white shorts, blue blouses tied above the midriff and white vests decorated with blue stars.

The company contends that the resulting association between its brand and a performance involving simulated gun violence threatens the reputation and goodwill it has developed over more than five decades.

The legal claims include trade dress infringement, false association, false endorsement and federal trademark dilution by tarnishment. Trade dress protection can extend to the distinctive visual appearance of products or services when those elements identify their commercial source.

Attorneys for Pro Silver Star, including prominent litigator Alex Spiro, argue that the distinctive appearance of the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders uniform is legally protected and cannot be appropriated without authorization.

The company says it sent a cease-and-desist letter to My Chemical Romance on October 22, requesting that the band discontinue use of the costumes. According to the filing, the band did not comply, and dancers appeared wearing similar uniforms during subsequent Hollywood Bowl performances on October 23 and 24.

Pro Silver Star maintains that its objection is limited to the use of the uniforms rather than the artistic content of the concert itself.

“Silver Star asks only that those performers not wear the DCC Uniform while doing it,” the company’s attorneys wrote, emphasizing that they are not seeking changes to the band’s music, choreography, political messaging or theatrical depiction of violence.

Live Nation is named as a defendant in its capacity as a concert promoter. However, the publicly reported allegations do not establish whether the company participated directly in selecting, designing or approving the disputed costumes.

Neither Live Nation nor My Chemical Romance had publicly responded to the allegations as of early Tuesday.

## Trademark Claims Face Questions Over Artistic Expression

Although Pro Silver Star maintains that the costumes infringe its trademark rights, legal experts have questioned whether the company’s claims can overcome First Amendment protections afforded to artistic expression.

In an [October 26 analysis published by Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/can-the-dallas-cowboys-cheerleaders-sue-my-chemical-romance?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), trademark attorneys David Bernstein of Debevoise & Plimpton and Eric Ball of Fenwick argued that My Chemical Romance’s use of the recognizable cheerleading uniforms could constitute protected artistic commentary rather than an unlawful attempt to associate itself commercially with the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders.

The distinction is significant because the disputed costumes appeared within a theatrical production depicting a fictional authoritarian society, rather than in advertising, merchandise or another conventional commercial use of a trademark.

Trademark law generally protects against consumer confusion regarding the source, sponsorship or affiliation of goods and services. Courts must also consider constitutional protections when recognizable brands or symbols are incorporated into expressive works.

The legal dispute also has an unusual historical precedent involving the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders themselves.

In 1979, a federal appeals court upheld trademark protections for the cheerleaders’ uniforms in litigation involving the adult film *Debbie Does Dallas*. The decision supported the team’s ability to challenge unauthorized use of its recognizable imagery.

Pro Silver Star’s attorneys have cited that precedent in the current dispute. However, attorneys interviewed by Billboard questioned how much weight the older decision should carry given subsequent developments in First Amendment and trademark law.

The competing arguments could determine whether the Cowboys cheerleaders’ owner can restrict the use of its distinctive uniforms in a concert production that incorporates recognizable American cultural imagery as part of its theatrical narrative.

## Emergency Court Hearing Ahead of Final Hollywood Bowl Shows

The [Los Angeles Times reported Monday](https://www.latimes.com/sports/story/2026-10-26/dallas-cowboys-cheerleaders-my-chemical-romance-dancers-uniforms?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) that a hearing on Pro Silver Star’s request for a temporary restraining order was scheduled for Tuesday morning, October 27.

An order granting the requested relief could require My Chemical Romance and its production team to modify or replace the disputed costumes before the band’s remaining performances.

The lawsuit does not seek to cancel the concerts, and there has been no indication that the dispute threatens the performances themselves.

As [previously reported by TicketNews](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/11/my-chemical-romance-halloween-shows-hollywood-bowl/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), My Chemical Romance added the October 30 and 31 performances to its Hollywood Bowl engagement last November, becoming the first band scheduled to play five nights during a single run at the venue.

The Used is scheduled to support the October 30 performance, while Thrice will open the Halloween show.

No ruling on the requested restraining order had been confirmed as of early Tuesday, leaving the production’s use of the disputed costumes unresolved ahead of the final two concerts.
