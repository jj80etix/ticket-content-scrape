# TEG cites ‘refocused growth strategy’ after recent contract losses

## Summary

Ticketek Entertainment Group (TEG) says a "refocused growth strategy" — not distress — explains a recent AUS$100m raise from existing investors, after reports the firm was restructuring following the loss of several key Australian venue-ticketing contracts. TEG lost Sydney's Hordern Pavilion to AXS in July and the eight-venue Venues NSW contract (including Allianz Stadium) to Ticketmaster in October. The company points to new CEO Cameron Hoy and a "refocused" strategy as the story instead.

### Key Points
- TEG raised AUS$100m from existing investors (Silver Lake-backed) after losing the Hordern Pavilion contract to AXS and the 8-venue Venues NSW contract to Ticketmaster.
- Cameron Hoy became CEO in May 2026; Amy Mackie was appointed managing director of ticketing at Ticketek in June.
- TEG declined to directly address restructuring reports, characterizing recent moves as strategic refocusing rather than contraction.
- Company points to strong live-music and sport bookings (Olivia Dean ANZ tour, TEG Sport's "biggest month in company history") as evidence of momentum.

### Highlights
- TEG lost two significant Australian venue contracts within months (Hordern Pavilion → AXS; 8-venue Venues NSW deal, incl. Allianz Stadium → Ticketmaster) before raising AUS$100m.

### Industry Problem
Venue-ticketing contracts are contestable and can flip to a competitor on a single tender cycle — a peer ticketing company losing marquee venues to AXS and Ticketmaster within the same year signals real competitive pressure in the incumbent-vendor space, even amid public reassurance.

### Proposed Solution
No solution proposed by TEG beyond "refocused growth strategy" messaging. For Etix, this is a monitor-only signal: track whether TEG's contract losses reflect a broader pattern of consumer-major ticketers (Ticketmaster, AXS) displacing mid-tier regional incumbents on venue tenders, which would sharpen the competitive contrast Etix can offer as an independent primary platform.

## TEG cites ‘refocused growth strategy’ after recent contract losses

The ticketer said it is "very pleased with the recent momentum" after raising funds from investors amid reports of a leadership restrucutre

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/05/TEG-New-Order-Sydney-Opera-House-Credit-Warren-Jackon-scaled.jpeg.webp)

A TEG-partnered New Order show at Sydney Opera House © Warren Jackson

Ticketek Entertainment Group (TEG) has said a “refocused growth strategy” is behind recent fundraising from investors, after reports suggested the firm was restructuring after losing several key contracts.

The group, which is partnered with leading Australian festival Laneways as well as Sydney’s Afterpay Arena, reportedly raised AUS$100m following the loss of contracts including Sydney concert venue Hordern Pavillion, which struck a ticketing deal with AXS in July.

The company also lost the contract for Venues NSW to Ticketmaster in October, which represented eight venues across the state, including Sydney’s Allianz Stadium.

However, In the wake of the funding round, TEG said in a statement it was “very pleased with the recent momentum across the businesses”.

A company spokesperson confirmed to *IQ* it had “recently raised additional financing from existing investors” to “strengthen our balance sheet and allow us to invest further in delivering a massive pipeline of live entertainment, driving growth across the business, and continuing to invest in technology for our partners and fans”.

Asked to comment on reports of a restructuring of its leadership team, the firm reference only the appointment of Cameron Hoy as CEO in May this year, saying he is “leading the group’s refocused growth strategy”.

> “TEG’s core purpose is to create exceptional experiences for our venue partners and customers”

TEG is backed by venture capital firm Silver Lake, and also appointed Amy Mackie as managing director of ticketing at Ticektek in June.

Explaining its outlook in its live music operations, the company told *IQ*: “Olivia Dean’s Australian and New Zealand tour, presented by Handsome Tours and Laneway Presents, has sold out across Melbourne, Sydney and Brisbane, with her Auckland show upgraded to the newly redeveloped Western Springs Bowl, marking the venue’s grand opening and shaping up as one of her biggest headline shows to date.”

The firm also told IQ its sport division, TEG Sport, is “delivering the biggest month of sport in the company’s history, with 19 matches in 16 days across seven markets”, as well as praising the success of several current theatrical productions it is partnered with.

The spokesperson continued: “TEG’s renewed growth strategy focuses on delivering unforgettable live experiences and technology for our venues, fans and partners. We have refocused on our core markets, our unique assets and our integrated model to drive growth across the business, and this supports our vision to make the world better through human connection”.

“TEG’s core purpose is to create exceptional experiences for our venue partners and customers,” they concluded.
