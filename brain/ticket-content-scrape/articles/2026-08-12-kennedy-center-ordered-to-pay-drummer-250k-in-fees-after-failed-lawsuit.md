# Kennedy Center Ordered to Pay Drummer $250K in Fees After Failed Lawsuit

## Summary

A D.C. judge ordered the Trump-controlled Kennedy Center to pay jazz drummer Chuck Redd over $252,000 in legal fees after the venue's breach-of-contract lawsuit against him — filed after he pulled out of his annual Christmas concert in protest of the venue's short-lived Trump name change — was thrown out for lack of a contract. It's the latest setback for the venue, which has faced roughly 20 artist cancellations and a court ruling that its Trump branding violated a 1964 law.

### Key Points
- Kennedy Center must pay Chuck Redd $252,479.70 in legal fees within 45 days.
- Judge Bosier dismissed the Center's underlying lawsuit in June, finding no contract existed between Redd and the venue.
- Redd withdrew from his annual Christmas concert (held since 2006) in protest of the venue's Trump renaming.
- A separate federal ruling found the Kennedy Center board violated a 1964 law by adding Trump's name; all such signage has since been removed.
- Roughly 20 acts have canceled Kennedy Center performances since the Trump takeover, and the venue also announced a two-year renovation closure.

A court has ordered the Trump-backed Kennedy Center to pay over $250,000 in fees to jazz drummer Chuck Redd after a lawsuit failed to materialize.

On Tuesday, a Washington, D.C. judge ordered the John F. Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts to pay Redd $252,479.70 in legal costs. Judge Tayna Jones Bosier said that the venue owed Redd for legal fees — which must be paid within 45 days.

Lisa J. Banks, an attorney for Redd, noted in a statement that “the Trump-controlled Kennedy Center tried to silence opposition to its illegal name change by filing a meritless lawsuit against jazz musician Chuck Redd.”

“Now the court has ordered the Kennedy Center to pay Mr. Redd’s legal fees, which is appropriate as no citizen should have to spend time and money fending off baseless and politically motivated lawsuits filed by the Trump Administration,” Banks said.

When Trump and his allies tried to change the venue’s name to the Donald J. Trump and John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, Redd backed-out of his annual Christmas concert, which he had held annually at the Kennedy Center since 2006. Then, in March, the venue sued Redd, claiming that the musician broke a contract when he cancelled the Christmas concert in protest of the name change. However, Judge Bosier threw-out the lawsuit in June after Redd showed evidence that there was never a contract between himself and the venue.

This is the latest blow for the Kennedy Center; earlier this year, U.S. District Court Judge Christopher Cooper ruled that the Kennedy Center board violated the law when they added Trump’s name to the venue’s exterior, as well as its website and marketing materials. Cooper pointed to a 1964 law that named the national cultural center after the recently assassinated President John F. Kennedy.

All signage [has since been removed](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/06/trumps-name-wiped-from-kennedy-center-exterior/) from the exterior of the Kennedy Center, as well as “all physical signage on the Kennedy Center building and grounds, including the front portico, that purports to rename the Kennedy Center after President Trump.”
