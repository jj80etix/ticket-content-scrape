# Youth Music Awards 2026 Winners

## Summary

The UK's Youth Music Awards 2026 moved to Liverpool's St. George's Hall for the first time after six years in London, recognizing grassroots music talent. The relocation reflects the charity's funding priorities toward organizations outside the capital.

### Key Points
- Ceremony held Oct. 28 in Liverpool after six years based in London
- Youth Music is funded via the National Lottery through Arts Council England and Postcode Lottery players
- 86% of raised funds support organizations outside London; northern England waitlists run 20% higher than the south
- Jury included Myles Smith, BICEP, Sister Bliss of Faithless and ALT BLK ERA

## Transcript

![TPW20042](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TPW20042-scaled.jpg)

Liverpudlian rapper KOJ live on stage at the 2026 Youth Music Awards. Courtesy Youth Music

The UK’s grassroots music talent was recognized at the Youth Music Awards 2026 in Liverpool, Oct. 28. After six years in London, it marked the ceremony’s debut year at Liverpool’s St. George’s Hall.

Youth Music is a national charity funded by the National Lottery via Arts Council England, players of the Postcode Lottery and support from partners, fundraisers and donors.

The move to Liverpool made sense also because the charity says 86% of the funds it raises are supporting organizations outside the capital, and waiting lists for funded projects stand 20% higher in England’s North than in the South.

Hosted by Crystal Raybe and Success Ariyibi, the Awards delivered an important and impactful evening at a time when support for grassroots music has arguably never been more important.

The jury selecting this year’s winners included Myles Smith, BICEP, Sister Bliss of Faithless, ALT BLK ERA, Jess Iszatt, Jodie Bryant and others.

The 2026 ceremony opened with a video from Liverpudlian rapper KOJ and his track “Humble Abode”, exclusively written and recorded to open the Youth Music Awards. It highlighted the national charity’s important messaging, and acted as a rallying cry for better grassroots music support in the industry. KOJ then arrived on stage to perform live renditions of his tracks ‘A Roadman’s Tale of Home’ and ‘Punk Panther’.

Youth Music’s CEO Adam Joolia gave a speech highlighting the vital role grassroots music plays in giving young people confidence, opportunity, connection and pathways. However, with more than a third of grassroots youth music projects still at risk, he called for greater investment and collaboration between government and industry to ensure every young person, regardless of their background, has the opportunity to realize their potential through music.

The evening also featured live performances from nominees Roukaya B who performed ‘Heat’, followed by Cherry Seraph with ‘Slow Punch’, Righteous Reck with ‘You Deserve It All’, Livvy K with ‘Better Man’, and IMVN performing ‘Skin’. BEASTIE closed out the evening’s performances with ‘Pilot’ ahead of the night’s official afterparty.3.

Adam Joolia, CEO at Youth Music, commented, “The grassroots music ecosystem is more than just a talent pipeline. It’s about who gets to take part, empowerment, connection and joy. Grassroots music doesn’t start with a stage or a venue. It starts at the places we celebrate at the Youth Music Awards: the youth clubs, community centers and projects. These are the breeding grounds for new music genres, sounds and movements that shape and evolve UK music culture from the grassroots. It’s an honor to recognize so many inspiring young people at this year’s Awards. Congratulations to all the winners and nominees!”

Youth Music Awards 2026 winners:

**Rising Star Award (Industry) sponsored By Kickers: Rianne Lewis**  
Rianne Lewis is a music entrepreneur, producer and founder of RUN IT, a platform creating opportunities for emerging artists through live events, mentoring and industry partnerships. Over the past 8 years, she has delivered more than 50 events across the UK and internationally, supported over 500 young creatives, and built partnerships with organizations including Red Bull, BBC Radio and PRS Foundation.

**Inspirational Music Leader sponsored By the MU**: **Abi Johnson**  
Abi Johnson is a music leader and sound engineer at The Music Works, where she became the organisation’s first female Black engineer. Starting as a volunteer, she now supports young people through music projects, 1:1 mentoring, disability programmes and school partnerships. Alongside her work with young people, Abi has helped drive racial equity work across the organisation, using music to create inclusive spaces where young people feel seen, supported and able to thrive.

**Young Leader Award sponsored By AQA:****Ria Leah**  
Ria Leah is a cultural leader, creative producer and youth practitioner whose work spans music, film and education. A core leader within Rap Club Productions and The Spit Game, she creates spaces where young people, particularly young women, can develop their creativity, confidence and leadership. From leading school rap programmes to speaking in policy and industry spaces, Ria bridges grassroots youth work and the professional creative industries, helping shape a more inclusive future for young artists.

**Original Track Award (Group) supported by Arts Council England: TRXTH & RHIA**  
TRXTH & RHIA are rising South London artists who first connected through Bollo Studios, collaborating on music that blends personal storytelling with contemporary R&B, soul and rap. RHIA combines influences from R&B, hip-hop and jazz to create a distinctive sound, while Youth Music NextGen Fund recipient Trxth brings honest, reflective lyricism rooted in lived experience. Their track “Stay Alive” pairs an uplifting, memorable hook with a powerful message of resilience, connection and hope.

**Lyricist Award sponsored by PRS for Music: KOJ**  
KOJ is a Liverpool rapper whose powerful blend of grime, rap and punk has made him one of the city’s most exciting emerging voices. Known for his raw storytelling, social commentary and emotionally honest lyricism, he creates music that speaks to the realities of modern Britain. Supported by BBC Radio 1Xtra, Spotify, Apple Music and major music publications, KOJ’s acclaimed debut EP ‘Villain With A Conscience’ established him as an artist unafraid to challenge, connect and inspire through his music.

**Music Video Award supported By Notion: Hillarynx**  
Hillarynx is a South London singer-songwriter whose music blends jazz, soul and deeply personal storytelling. Rooted in Black feminist thought, she creates work that explores grief, identity and resilience with honesty and emotional depth. Supported by BBC Three, Rinse FM and Jazz FM, she has performed at venues including the Southbank Centre and Jazz Cafe. Her video for “The 3rd” was a community-led project inspired by lived experience, raising powerful conversations around loss, disability, culture and affirming life.

**Music Producer Award supported by Elephant Music: IMVN**  
IMVN is a London-based artist, producer and violinist creating genre-blurring electronic music that explores identity, transformation and self-discovery. Combining striking visual storytelling with experimental sound, her work has earned support from BBC Introducing, COLORS and Mixmag. The video for “Skin”, co-directed while touring with FKA twigs, draws on contemporary dance, queer identity and themes of resilience. Created with an all queer, women and people of colour creative team, it showcases IMVN’s bold artistic vision and collaborative spirit.

**Outstanding Project Award sponsored by Rocksteady: Elevation Music by Conscious Youth CIC**  
Conscious Youth CIC, Elevation Music is a youth-led music program created in response to what young people in Kirklees said was missing from their local music scene. Delivered by Conscious Youth C.I.C through HD Sounds, it gives 16 to 25-year-olds access to songwriting, production, recording, live performance and music industry skills. Young people have created a community EP with more than 54,000 streams, performed at major events and gained access to professional studios and industry networks. More than a program, Elevation Music is creating lasting pathways into music for young people who have traditionally faced barriers to opportunity.

**Original Track Award (Solo) sponsored by Skiddle: BEASTIE**  
BEASTIE is a London-based artist, producer and vocalist who writes, produces and engineers his own music. “Pilot” is a hypnotic blend of African disco influences, psychedelic production and alternative R&B, capturing the thrill of letting go and living in the moment, pairing lush, immersive soundscapes with effortlessly flowing vocals. Inspired by the idea of a “rave in the jungle”, the song balances freedom and escapism with emotional depth.

**Social Action Award sponsored by Hal Leonard, A Muse Group Company: TiPP**  
TiPP’s Rhythm and Renewal project uses music and the arts to support young people involved in the youth justice system, creating opportunities for self-expression, skill development and positive change. Working across the North West, the project provides high-quality creative program that help young people build confidence, strengthen relationships and explore new futures. Through music-making, mentoring and accredited qualifications, Rhythm and Renewal challenges barriers to participation and creates meaningful pathways into education, employment and the arts, showing how creativity can be a powerful force for social justice and lasting change.

**Rising Star Award (Artist) sponsored By Empire: Righteous Reck**  
Righteous Reck is an artist, producer and creative facilitator whose work bridges music, education and community. After teaching himself music production as a teenager, he built an independent career spanning performance, content creation and youth engagement. Alongside growing a substantial online following and gaining recognition for his music, he has delivered workshops for hundreds of young people, using music as a tool for confidence, creativity and self-expression. By creating opportunities both on and off stage, Righteous Reck is helping shape a more inclusive and connected future for UK rap and grassroots music.
