# HYBE and CAA Form Global Partnership With Major Live Entertainment Ambitions

## Summary

HYBE (BTS, KATSEYE, SEVENTEEN) and Creative Artists Agency announced a strategic global partnership spanning live events, brand deals, sports, licensing and content across South Korea, Japan, China, India and the Americas — but disclosed no financial terms, duration, or whether it covers exclusive touring representation or ticketing arrangements. The deal lands as HYBE's concert business hits record scale: Q3 revenue topped $1B for the first time, with $512M in record concert revenue (nearly 48% of total revenue) driven by BTS's ARIRANG tour, which continues through H1 2027; CAA already represents a roster of Korean acts (ROSÉ, aespa, NCT 127) and will reportedly represent HYBE's artists, IP and brands, per Deadline, though the booking/ticketing implications remain unconfirmed.

### Key Points
- Partnership spans live experiences, brand/sports/licensing/content deals across 5 regions; no financial terms or exclusivity disclosed.
- HYBE Q3 revenue surpassed $1B for the first time (more than double YoY); concert revenue hit a record $512M (~48% of total revenue), led by BTS's ARIRANG tour.
- Deadline reports CAA will represent HYBE's artists/IP/brands, but neither company confirmed whether this replaces existing booking agents or affects ticketing relationships.
- Comes amid continued scrutiny of HYBE/BTS ticketing — fan complaints over Ticketmaster price transparency and a Mexico consumer-protection intervention earlier in 2026.
- CAA already operates a Seoul office and represents other major K-pop acts (ROSÉ, aespa, NCT 127, Red Velvet, RIIZE, EXO).

### Highlights
- HYBE's concert revenue ($512M, ~48% of total Q3 revenue) now rivals its recorded-music/merch business, making its touring/ticketing relationships increasingly consequential.
- The CAA deal's scope explicitly includes "live experiences" but leaves booking/ticketing exclusivity unresolved.
- Recurring BTS ticketing friction (Ticketmaster price-transparency complaints, a Mexico regulator intervention) frames the stakes of any shift in representation.

### Industry Problem
As K-pop/global touring revenue scales rapidly, talent-agency consolidation around major labels concentrates booking and commercial leverage, which can affect how ticketing/primary platforms negotiate access to high-demand tours — and the recurring pricing-transparency complaints around BTS underscore unresolved patron-trust issues in that pipeline.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor whether the CAA partnership changes HYBE's touring/ticketing relationships (exclusive booking, territory control) as a leading indicator of how agency consolidation could affect primary-platform access to major international tours; no immediate build/improve action, but a trigger to watch is any announcement of exclusive CAA-brokered ticketing or venue deals.

HYBE, the South Korean entertainment company behind BTS, KATSEYE, ENHYPEN and SEVENTEEN, has entered a strategic partnership with Creative Artists Agency (CAA) aimed at expanding the global reach of its artists and entertainment businesses, including new opportunities in live events, content development and commercial partnerships.

The agreement, announced Monday, November 16, brings together one of the world’s most influential music companies and a leading Hollywood talent agency, with plans to collaborate across live experiences, brand partnerships, sports, licensing, merchandising and content.

The companies said the partnership will support business initiatives across South Korea, Japan, China, India and the Americas, leveraging HYBE’s artist roster and intellectual property alongside CAA’s international representation network and commercial relationships.

While the companies have outlined an expansive collaboration, they have not disclosed its financial terms, duration or whether it involves exclusive artist representation, concert booking or ticketing arrangements.

## HYBE’s Record Concert Business Creates Major Opportunity

HYBE reported record third-quarter revenue of 1.53 trillion Korean won (approximately $1.1 billion) earlier this month, more than doubling its revenue from the same period last year.

Concert revenue reached 726.8 billion won, approximately $512 million, driven largely by BTS’ ongoing ARIRANG world tour across North America and Europe. Live performances accounted for nearly 48% of HYBE’s total quarterly revenue, underscoring the growing financial importance of touring to the company’s broader operations.

## Touring and Ticketing Questions Remain

Although the partnership specifically identifies live experiences as an area of cooperation, the announcement leaves important questions unanswered about how the companies will work together within the concert industry.

[Deadline reported](https://deadline.com/2026/11/hybe-caa-artist-representation-1237226005/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) that CAA will represent HYBE’s artists, intellectual property and brands. However, neither company’s public statements specify whether CAA will assume exclusive touring representation for individual artists, replace existing booking agents or take responsibility for particular international territories.

The distinction is significant given the scale of HYBE’s touring operations and the existing relationships involved in bringing major international concert tours to market.

There has also been no indication that the agreement changes HYBE’s existing relationships with concert promoters, venues or ticketing platforms.

Those relationships have received considerable public scrutiny during BTS’ current world tour. Earlier this year, [fans criticized Ticketmaster over pricing transparency](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/01/bts-fans-call-out-ticketmaster-hiding-prices/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) ahead of the group’s presales, while ticketing disputes in Mexico prompted intervention by consumer protection authorities.

Although those issues are separate from the CAA agreement, they illustrate the commercial scale and consumer implications surrounding major HYBE concert productions.

The partnership also comes amid HYBE’s broader international expansion. The company established operations in China and India in 2025, adding to an existing global footprint that includes the United States, Japan and Latin America.

HYBE Chairman Bang Si-hyuk and CAA CEO and co-chairman Bryan Lourd discussed the partnership during HYBE’s Global Leadership Summit on November 13, participating in a companywide conversation about artist relationships and the future of entertainment.

HYBE CEO Jason Jaesang Lee said the companies intend to combine their global networks to expand opportunities for artists across content, brands and live experiences.

For the concert business, the next significant development will be whether the broad partnership produces specific touring representation agreements, new live-event ventures or changes to the commercial relationships supporting HYBE’s expanding global roster.

Neither company has announced individual projects, new concert dates or ticketing arrangements resulting from the agreement.
