# Rochelle Fleming, Disco Legend, Dead At 72

## Summary

Rochelle Fleming, lead singer of disco group First Choice, has died at 72.

### Key Points
- Died Nov. 5 in Pennsylvania; services to be announced
- Founded First Choice in 1971 in Philadelphia, known for hits "The Player," "Doctor Love" and "Let No Man Put Asunder"
- Later released solo singles and reunited with the group in 1987 and 2014

## Transcript

![GettyImages 570510489](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/GettyImages-570510489-scaled.jpg)

American Soul musician Rochelle Fleming, of the vocal trio First Choice, performs onstage at the Chicago Stadium, Chicago, Illinois, June 21, 1979. (Photo by Paul Natkin/Getty Images)

Rochelle Fleming, lead singer of the disco group First Choice, has died. She was 72.

George Iz Correia, Fleming’s manager, shared the news on Instagram through Fleming’s official account, saying the artist died on Nov. 5 in Pennsylvania. “It is with a heavy heart to share the passing of Rochelle Fleming-Finney. She was the founder of the R&B group, First Choice,” he said. “She \[brought\] light, laughter and love to the world.”

Correia also mentioned that services to celebrate her life would be announced at a later date.

First Choice formed in 1971, while members of the group were students at Philadelphia’s Overbrook High School. The all-girl trio recorded hits including “The Player,” “Doctor Love,” and “Let No Man Put Asunder” before parting ways in 1980.

From there, Fleming would release music as a solo act. She released singles like “Love Itch” (1985), “Danger!” (1994), and “Suffer (The Consequences)” (1995). She later reunited with the group in ‘87 with two different singers and again in 2014 for a performance at a Salsoul Records event.
