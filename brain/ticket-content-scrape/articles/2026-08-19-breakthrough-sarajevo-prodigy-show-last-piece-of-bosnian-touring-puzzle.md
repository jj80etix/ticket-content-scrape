# Breakthrough Sarajevo Prodigy show ‘last piece’ of Bosnian touring puzzle

## Summary

The Prodigy's Aug 13 concert at Grbavica Stadium was Bosnia's first international stadium show since U2's 1997 PopMart tour, with promoter Ivan Milivojev calling it the "last piece of the puzzle" for opening a viable Balkan touring route linking Belgrade, Sarajevo, and Zagreb. Local ticketing ran primarily through Ulaznica.org, while unclear alcohol/public-gathering licensing law created last-minute uncertainty for organizers.

### Key Points
- First Bosnian stadium concert since U2's 1997 PopMart show; promoted via Serbia's EXIT Festival veteran Ivan Milivojev and Sarajevo's Garden of Dreams.
- Positioned as connecting Belgrade-Sarajevo-Zagreb into a regional touring circuit.
- Ticketing handled via Bosnian platform Ulaznica.org plus regional platforms.
- Bosnia's unclear public-gathering/alcohol licensing law let police discretionarily restrict alcohol service, a lingering operational risk for organizers.
- Momentum continues with Solomun (Bosnian-rooted DJ) playing a free Sarajevo show the following weekend.

## Breakthrough Sarajevo Prodigy show ‘last piece’ of Bosnian touring puzzle

Promoters behind the concert tell IQ how the first event of its kind since 1997 "opens up Bosnia for future touring plans"

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Prodigy-Sarajevo.png)

The Prodigy in Sarajevo © Adnan Lingo

Sarajevo has held Bosnia’s first international stadium concert since 1997 last weekend (13 August), with organisers saying the Prodigy’s performance at Grbavica Stadium marks the “last piece of the puzzle” for unlocking a surge in international touring in surrounding Balkan countries.

Not since U2’s now-famous *PopMart* tour concert had a Western international act visited a stadium in Bosnia, with promoter Ivan Milivojev saying: “ this kind of open‑air show last happened in 1997, when U2 played. They didn’t have anything similar to this since.”

Milivojev, a veteran of Serbia’s EXIT Festival, reached out to Sarajevo producer Garden of Dreams following the [fallout from Serbia’s Novi Sad protests](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/serbian-live-sector-under-threat-amid-ongoing-protests/) that saw the live music sector in Serbia constrained.

“ They wanted to go to the next level to do something important and big. And I said maybe The Prodigy could work for you guys. And they said yes,” he recalls.

“So we sent the offer last year, and we waited until March until they confirmed the show, and then we got an exclusive show in the Balkans; that was the only show for The Prodigy in the Balkans,” he tells old *IQ.*

> “Sarajevo is not a small city in a mask — it’s a city with culture, peaceful living, incredible music taste, and awareness of quality”

With this success, Milivojev says the show acts as proof of concert for Sarajevo becoming a crucial third stop on a viable and efficient future regional touring route through the north west of the Balkan Peninsula. He explains: “ I’ve worked 30 years in this market and this is the last piece of the puzzle we missed. It’s a couple of hours from Belgrade and a couple of hours from Zagreb. It’s literally one dot in between; a perfect match.”

Asked of this potential route would be a significant boost for attracting more international touring to the region, he says, “ maybe not immediately — people take time — but this is a very good sign.”

Reflecting after the concert, the promoter said the occasion proved emotional for long-time observers of Bosnia’s live music scene. “The show felt like Yugoslavia — seeing all different nations coming to this show. We don’t need English to understand each other. Everyone can speak their native language. Seeing many different cultures inside melted my heart,” he said.

In terms of practicalities, Bosnian ticketing platform Ulaznica.org was primarily used for the concert, in addition to a handful of regional platforms also selling tickets. The lead-up to the show also saw “huge pressure” from local media and some portions of the public over fears that configuring the stadium for a concert could damage the playing field.

And so long after the city’s last concert at this scale, Milivojev also says licensing laws unsuited to modern stadium concerts threatened to cause issues.

“The situation in Sarajevo is that the legal framework regarding public gatherings and alcohol service is not clearly regulated. As a result, the police have the authority to make a subjective decision to prohibit the serving of alcohol and the organiser is obliged to comply with that decision,” he says.

He added that police were “afraid of what might happen” with such crowd volumes, but that the event passed without issue.

> “ It had a feeling this was super special because it opened Bosnia for future tour plan”

Asked what factors have prevented stadium concerts returning to Bosnia since 1997, Garden of Dreams communications manager Iman Kunovac cites “the infrastructure — the lack of places where you can pull off a concert this size — but also a little bit of the political situation in the beginning”.

“But,” she adds, “it’s definitely not a lack of enthusiasm from Sarajevo and Bosnia. People were very excited to finally have a band like The Prodigy. Unfortunately, for the last 30 years, infrastructure was the worst part of this.”

Milivojev tells *IQ* he is hopeful that this signifies a wave of momentum for live events in Sarajevo, pointing to another large-scale live event coming up during the weekend following the Prodigy’s show. “Solomun, one of the top DJs in the world, is playing on Friday for free in the main square in Sarajevo,” he says. “The reason is because Solomun’s roots are from Bosnia.”

This continued momentum is no surprise for the promoter after the atmosphere of last weekend’s concert: “It had a feeling this was super special because it opened Bosnia for future tour plans. Agents and promoters see a new opportunity to do shows there.”

Meanwhile, for Kunovac and Garden of Dreams, the headline-grabbing concert from an international name like The Prodigy is only the culmination of years of local work which slowly sought to strengthen links between the Bosnian and international industries.

“ For the last 11 years, Garden of Dreams has been working on building up the electronic scene, bringing new names, and it took a lot of interest because it’s a small city. It took courage. It took established interest from international booking agencies and artists,” Kunovac said.

She concludes: “Sarajevo is not a small city in a mask — it’s a city with culture, peaceful living, incredible music taste, and awareness of quality. That’s why, with this concert and other things, they welcome these concerts. They understand the quality of the music they bring.”
