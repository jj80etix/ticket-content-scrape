# The Biz: Gen Z Flexes As Live Music Consumers; House Dems Call StubHub CEO To The Carpet; UMG Earnings & More

## Summary

Pollstar's Biz roundup covers three threads: Luminate data showing Gen Z now outspends Millennials on concerts ($101/month vs. $94), even as cost-driven attendance resistance has eased (75% to 57% citing price as the top barrier since 2024); House Oversight Committee Democrats demanding answers from StubHub CEO Eric Baker over his ownership stake in Andro Capital, a hedge fund that resells millions of tickets on StubHub's own platform; and Universal Music Group posting $3.83 billion in Q2 revenue, up 13.3% year-over-year.

### Key Points
- Gen Z concert spend now exceeds Millennials': $101/month vs. $94/month (Luminate U.S. Music 360 report).
- Share of Gen Z citing cost as the top barrier to attending shows fell from 75% (2024) to 57% (Q1 2026).
- Rep. Robert Garcia (House Oversight) sent StubHub CEO Eric Baker a letter demanding investigation into his stake in Andro Capital, a hedge fund reselling tickets on StubHub's own marketplace — alleging potential market manipulation and self-dealing.
- UMG Q2 revenue: €3.294B ($3.83B), up 13.3% year-over-year.

### Highlights
- Gen Z's price-sensitivity as the #1 attendance barrier dropped 18 points (75%→57%) in roughly two years, even as their per-capita concert spend now leads all generations.
- House Oversight Democrats formally requested an investigation into StubHub CEO Eric Baker's undisclosed hedge-fund ownership stake that resells tickets on his own platform.

### Industry Problem
Congressional scrutiny of StubHub's CEO profiting from a hedge fund that scalps on his own platform highlights an unresolved conflict-of-interest and self-dealing risk baked into vertically integrated resale marketplaces — a structural credibility problem for the secondary market broadly.

### Proposed Solution
No product fix from the item itself; this is a monitor-only signal — watch whether the Oversight inquiry produces disclosure requirements or enforcement action that reshapes resale-marketplace governance norms, and note the softening (but still dominant) role of price sensitivity in patron demand as grounding context for pricing/affordability positioning.

Gen Z is now spending more on concerts than Millennials, long the biggest-spending demographic.

[Luminate’s U.S. Music 360 report](https://luminatedata.com/reports/live-music-2026) found that Gen Zers who attend live events spend an average of $101 on concerts monthly, ahead of the $94 average for their Millennial counterparts.

Interestingly, despite the constant sturm und drang around ticket prices, Zoomers’ resistance has softened significantly. While cost, as it always has been, is listed as the top factor keeping fans from attending shows, the level of pushback has declined. In 2024, 75% of Gen Z said it was the most significant factor preventing concert attendance. In Q1 of 2026, that was down to 57%. Interestingly, the percentage of Gen Z who reported attending multiple concerts a year is increasing and the percentage of those who say travel is the biggest factor working against concert attendance has cratered.

Much of this could be attributed to the inexorable march of time. The oldest Zoomers are nearing 30 and the youngest are entering high school. Travel burdens are less as a generation ages and earns their own income.

**House Democrats Say StubHub CEO Has Some Explaining To Do**

After [revelations](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/07/21/the-biz-stubhub-ceo-tie-to-scalpers-ln-settlement-comments-open-the-stones-join-roblox/) that StubHub CEO Eric Baker is a part-owner and managing director of Andro Capital, a hedge fund that sells millions of tickets on StubHub’s platform, House Democrats on the Oversight Committee want answers.

Robert Garcia, the top Democrat on the committee, sent [a letter](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_mr_eric_hbakerceostubhubholdingsinc.pdf) to Baker July 24 saying the committee’s Democrats want an investigation into whether Baker has a hand in manipulating the secondary ticket market.

”Reports suggest that you have financed and profited from the very scalpers that are locking fans out of affordable ticket sales in the first place, and that you are further ripping off  
consumers and smaller sellers by giving yourself preferential treatment on the StubHub platform,” Garcia wrote. “The CEO of the marketplace  
also financing scalpers—and serving as a mass scalper himself—opens the door to a whole new level of advantage. The affiliate agreement between StubHub and these hedge funds poses ethical concerns that one industry expert described as ‘very deceiving.’ Your alleged collusion with these funds also raises serious concerns about whether you have engaged in market manipulation and selfdealing at the expense of consumers.”

**UMG Reports $3.8B In Q2**

Universal Music Group reported revenues of €3.294 billion ($3.83 billion) in the second quarter of 2026, a 13.3% increase over the same quarter last year.

“We’re delivering on our strategic plan, and working to further sharpen our execution, while capitalizing on the opportunities presented by new technologies and the ever-evolving music ecosystem, CEO Lucian Grainge said. “Our unique combination of global reach, local expertise, artist development, vast audio and visual IP and entrepreneurial culture positions UMG to deliver long-term growth, sustained value creation, and creative and commercial success for our artists and songwriters.”
