# AEG Presents Acquires Auguri Productions in Latest European Expansion Move

## Summary

AEG Presents has acquired French concert production and artist development company Auguri Productions outright from RIVAJ Group, expanding AEG's French operations through a company with nearly three decades of experience developing artists including Stromae, Angèle and David Guetta. The deal follows AEG's recent investment in Benelux promoter Greenhouse Talent and continues a broader push by AEG to deepen regional (non-Paris) French touring infrastructure as public cultural funding pressures squeeze smaller venues.

### Key Points
- AEG acquires 100% of Auguri Productions from RIVAJ Group; financial terms undisclosed. Founder Charles Bensmaine stays on as president, and Auguri retains its name/leadership.
- Auguri, founded in Nantes in 1997, has developed artists including Stromae (incl. Madison Square Garden shows), Angèle, Louane, David Guetta and Vanessa Paradis.
- Follows AEG's September investment in Greenhouse Talent (Belgium/Netherlands, ~1,500 events/year) and its existing involvement with We Love Green and Rock en Seine festivals in France.
- Executives frame the deal as artist-development consolidation rather than simply adding event volume, citing increasing French live-industry consolidation and funding pressure on regional venues.
- No changes to ticketing arrangements or new concert dates were announced in connection with the acquisition.

AEG Presents has acquired French concert production and artist development company Auguri Productions, expanding its presence in France and adding another established European promoter to its international network.

The acquisition, [announced October 15](https://aegworldwide.com/press-center/press-releases/aeg-presents-drives-future-growth-france-acquisition-auguri-productions?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), strengthens AEG Presents’ French operations through a company with nearly three decades of experience developing artists and producing concerts and tours. The deal follows AEG’s recent investment in Belgium- and Netherlands-based Greenhouse Talent, continuing a series of moves to expand its European concert business.

AEG acquired 100% of Auguri Productions from RIVAJ Group, according to an [interview published by Billboard France](https://fr.billboard.com/aeg-acquiert-auguri-les-dessous-du-rachat/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). Financial terms were not disclosed.

Auguri will retain its name and existing leadership, with founder Charles Bensmaine continuing as president.

Founded in Nantes in 1997 under the name Olympic Tour, Auguri Productions relocated to Paris in the late 2000s and developed into an established producer of French and French-speaking artists. Its history includes work with Stromae, Angèle, Louane, Julien Doré, David Guetta, Vanessa Paradis, Jain and Dominique A, among others.

The company has been particularly active in developing artists over extended periods, supporting their careers from early performances through larger concert tours. Auguri helped produce Stromae’s international concerts, including performances at Madison Square Garden in New York.

For AEG Presents, the acquisition provides additional expertise in discovering and developing French-speaking artists while creating opportunities to connect those performers with the company’s international touring operations.

Adam Wilkes, president and CEO of AEG Presents Europe, said the acquisition “deepens our roots in France” and brings Auguri’s local artist development expertise together with AEG’s international resources.

Bensmaine said joining AEG would help Auguri expand its activities in France while giving its artists “even greater access to AEG Presents’ international network and opportunities.”

Arnaud Meersseman, president of AEG Presents France, also emphasized the company’s interest in Auguri’s experience identifying emerging artists and supporting their careers over the long term.

In the Billboard France interview, Meersseman explained that Auguri would help AEG develop a stronger presence in regional French concert markets beyond Paris and the country’s largest cities.

Meersseman said the acquisition would allow AEG to strengthen that part of its business, particularly as it develops more French artists whose touring careers depend on reaching audiences throughout the country.

## AEG Continues Building Its European Network

The acquisition follows several investments and partnerships that have expanded AEG Presents’ presence across Europe.

In September, [AEG Presents announced an investment in Greenhouse Talent](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/aeg-presents-invests-in-greenhouse-talent-to-expand-european-footprint/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), an independent concert promoter operating in Belgium and the Netherlands.

Greenhouse Talent promotes approximately 1,500 events annually, ranging from club performances to stadium concerts and festivals. The investment formalized a relationship between the companies that had developed over roughly two decades.

Unlike the Auguri acquisition, AEG did not disclose the size of its ownership stake in Greenhouse Talent. The Benelux promoter continues operating under its existing leadership and identity.

AEG has also expanded its French festival business. In July 2025, TicketNews reported on the [strategic partnership involving AEG Presents, Combat and the We Love Green festival](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/07/we-love-green-festival-partnership-combat-aeg-presents/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) in Paris.

The company is also involved with Rock en Seine, another major French music festival.

Together, the investments give AEG a broader presence across several segments of the European concert industry, including festivals, international touring, regional promotion and artist development.

The Auguri acquisition adds another dimension to that strategy by bringing an established French artist development company directly into AEG’s operations.

In their Billboard France interview, Meersseman and Bensmaine discussed the increasing consolidation of the French live entertainment industry. Meersseman argued that the acquisition was intended to deepen AEG’s artist development capabilities rather than simply increase the number of artists and events under its control.

Both executives also raised concerns about the challenges facing emerging artists and smaller venues in France, particularly as reductions in public cultural funding put additional pressure on regional performance spaces.

Bensmaine noted that developing touring artists has become increasingly difficult as smaller venues face financial constraints. The regional concert circuit remains important for performers building audiences before moving into larger theaters and arenas.

The acquisition could provide additional resources for those efforts by combining Auguri’s existing artist relationships and regional touring experience with AEG’s international reach.

For AEG Presents, the deal represents another step in its broader European expansion, following investments in local promoters and festival partnerships while strengthening its ability to develop artists for domestic and international markets.

Neither company announced changes to ticketing arrangements or specific new concert dates in connection with the acquisition.
