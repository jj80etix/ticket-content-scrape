# Victoria expands festival funding initiative with $640k round

## Summary

The Australian state of Victoria is expanding its Live Music Festivals Fund with a third grant round totaling over A$640,000 across 16 events, delivered in partnership with nonprofit Music Victoria. Contemporary festivals with capacity over 1,000 are eligible for up to A$50,000, with Untitled Group's Ability Fest receiving the maximum grant among recipients that include Port Fairy Folk Festival, Riverboats Music Festival, and Queenscliff Music Festival.

### Key Points

- Third funding round totals A$640,000 (~€391,700) across 16 festivals.
- Individual festivals (1,000+ capacity) are eligible for up to A$50,000; the program initially rolled out in 2024.
- Untitled Group's Ability Fest received the maximum grant; other recipients include Port Fairy Folk Festival, Riverboats Music Festival, and Queenscliff Music Festival.
- Newer festival recipients include LuliePalooza, The DOT, and Our Survival Day; several other 2026 festivals were affected by January bushfires.
- Prior two funding rounds supported 32 festivals; the fund sits alongside Victoria's 10,000 Gigs and Music Works programs.
- Separately, the Australian Festival Association launched an industry-led best-practices guide (Festival Blueprint) for organizers, funded via Sound NSW.

The Australian state of Victoria is expanding its grant funding for music festivals, with the third round of its Live Music Festivals Fund set to total over A$640,000 (€391,700) across 16 events.

Delivered in partnership with non-profit Music Victoria, the funding is intended to create opportunities for artists, boost local economies, and draw audiences to the southeastern state, which includes major cities Melbourne, Geelong, Ballarat, and Bendigo.

Contemporary live music festivals with capacity over 1,000 people are eligible for up to A$50,000 (€30,600) in government funding, which initially rolled out in [2024](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/02/victoria-announces-50k-grants-for-music-festivals/).

Untitled Group’s Ability Fest – Australia’s first fully inclusive and [accessible music festival](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2024/09/fil-palermo-ability-fest-australia/) – recieved the maximum amount, with other recipients including Port Fairy Folk Festival, Riverboats Music Festival, and Queenscliff Music Festival.

“Festivals are so important to Victoria’s music ecosystem,” says Music Victoria CEO Fiona Duncan. “They create opportunities for artists and music professionals, bring communities together through shared live music experiences, and play an important role in the cultural and economic life of towns and cities across the state.”

“This investment will help festivals continue to sustain, support local talent, and give audiences across Victoria the chance to experience so much great live music,” she adds.

Newer festivals included in state funding include Abbotsford’s LuliePalooza, Footscray electronic festival The DOT and Mt Martha’s First Peoples festival Our Survival Day. Other festivals, like Castlemaine’s Town Folk Festival, Tallarook’s Interstellar Groove Festival and Beechworth’s High Country Hop, were impacted by bushfires in January.

> “It’s one of the reasons Victoria is Australia’s live music capital”

The first two rounds of the programme previously helped support 32 festivals, as part of the Victorian Government’s commitment to supporting the state’s music industry.

Elsewhere in the sector, the government also provides funding to venues to present paid gigs through its 10,000 Gigs: The Victorian Gig Fund, along with its Music Works programme, which supports artists, bands, music organisations, and events.

“The live music supported through this fund brings communities together, creates local jobs and showcases our diverse and amazing talent – it’s one of the reasons Victoria is Australia’s live music capital,” says Vicki Ward, Minister for Creative Industries.

Recently, the Australian Festival Association (AFA) launched the country’s first [industry-led best practices guide](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/australia-launches-first-industry-led-festival-guide-for-organisers/) for music festivals to provide organisers with a centralised resource for delivering large-scale events.

Funded by the NSW state government through its Sound NSW arm, the free-to-access Festival Blueprint is a living resource intended to provide organisers with practical operational guidance, regulatory information, and industry expertise.

It is specifically created for festival suppliers, leaders, contractors, agencies, and industry stakeholders in Australia’s most populous state, New South Wales (NSW), helping support safety and consistency of festivals in the market.
