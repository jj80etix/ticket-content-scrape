# Blurring The Lines Between Cinema & Live Concert: David Bowie’s ‘Isolar II’ To Tour Again On Screens

## Summary

Live-entertainment producer Rob Hallett, the David Bowie Estate and filmmaker Brett Morgen are bringing restored film of Bowie's 1978 "Isolar II" tour to arenas worldwide under the title "Stage," premiering at Berlin's Uber Arena Feb. 14, 2027. (Companion coverage of the same Bowie 'Stage' tour reported by Pollstar.)

### Key Points
- World premiere Feb. 14, 2027 at Uber Arena, Berlin, in association with the Berlinale film festival.
- Uses restored 35mm footage with immersive multi-screen visuals and 7.1 surround audio.
- Designed for venues from 5,000-capacity up; produced by Hallett's Wonderworld.

## Transcript

![GettyImages 2639097](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/GettyImages-2639097-scaled.jpg)

29th June 1978: British pop rock singer David Bowie in concert at Earl’s Court, London during “Isolar II – The 1978 World Tour. Photo by Evening Standard/Hulton Archive/Getty Images

Live entertainment veteran Rob Hallet, through his company Wonderworld, the David Bowie Estate and filmmaker Brett Morgen are collaborating on bringing film footage of David Bowie’s 1978 “Isolar II Tour” to arenas.

In association with the Berlin International Film Festival (Berlinale), the show known as “Stage” will celebrate its world premiere at Uber Arena Berlin, Germany, Feb. 14, 2027. Stage is also the name of the live album Bowie recorded on that 1978 tour.

The show has been assembled from raw, unseen 35mm film, which will be presented across multiple screens, with an integrated lighting show designed by world-renowned lighting designer Patrick Woodroffe (The Rolling Stones, Elton John), and an immersive audio mix by Academy Award winning sound engineer Paul Massey (Bohemian Rhapsody, James Bond – No Time To Die, Moonage Daydream).

The world premiere and the tour that will follow is presented by Rob Hallet and his business Wonderworld along with his world-class team, including Production Director John Pryer, Production Manager George Doherty, Production Coordinator Mia Cartwright, with Media Servers by Tom Wood and suppliers including 24-7 Productions, Clair Audio, and Frame Sync.

Some of the techniques through which the film will be brought to life on stage, include immersive audio mapping, mixed by Massey and integrated into a Clair Global audio setup using advanced DSP algorithms and a spread-out flown array system to deliver pristine 7.1 surround sound to every seat in the venue; the visuals utilize full-range transparent LED screens (70 times 40 displays) provided by Unilumin/Roe to create the illusion that Bowie and his band are physically present on stage.

“The show is designed for scale and staged in auditoriums and venues with capacities starting at 5,000, enveloping audiences in the atmosphere of a full-scale rock concert for an unprecedented live experience,” according to today’s announcement to the press.

David Bowie conceived and produced Stage in 1978 during the “Isolar II” concerts at London’s Earls Court, promoting his iconic Heroes album. The recordings, which have been sitting untouched in the Bowie Archives for almost 50 years, showcase Bowie at the zenith of his powers as a live performer.

“It has taken five decades for technology to develop enough to bring his vision to life with cutting-edge spatial audio and visual tech. The production blurs the line between cinema and a live concert. Fans will achieve the sensations of attending an authentic David Bowie show,” the press release continues.

Filmmaker Brett Morgen (Moonage Daydream, Montage Of Heck, The Kid Stays In The Picture) unearthed the original footage 10 years ago while working on the Grammy Award-winning Moonage Daydream. As early as 2018, Morgen and the Bowie Estate began discussing the possibility of bringing “Stage” to arenas worldwide, but it wasn’t until 2024, when former AEG Live President of International Rob Hallett joined, that the dream became a reality.

Described as “a masterclass in visuals and audio integration,” Morgen has spent almost a decade reconfiguring, editing, color grading, and hand-painting the archival footage. By utilizing longer, uninterrupted takes of Bowie, the show creates an intimate atmosphere that mimics a real-time live performance. Coupled with sound and light design by Wonderworld’s Oscar-winning, world-renowned creative team, it will show Bowie and his band in arenas worldwide.

The Earls Court “Isolar II” performance features Bowie backed by his legendary 1978 touring band, including Adrian Belew (lead guitar), Carlos Alomar (rhythm guitar/musical director), George Murray (bass), Dennis Davis (drums), Roger Powell (keyboards/synthesizer), Sean Mayes (piano), and Simon House (electric violin), performing hits such as “Ziggy Stardust”, “Rebel Rebel”, “Suffragette City”, “Fame”, “The Jean Genie”, and “Heroes” alongside tracks from the visionary Low and Heroes album.

Rob Hallett, Founder of Wonderworld and former President of AEG International, commented, “David Bowie has bestowed upon us an extraordinary treasure — an unprecedented concert experience that has yet to be witnessed. Working hand-in-hand with David Bowie’s Estate and Brett Morgen, we are ensuring this experience is 100% faithful to his legacy while making his genius accessible to global fans and a new generation. After the Berlin show, the David Bowie Stage show will embark on a landmark global tour, taking Bowie’s music, vision and theatricality to the live markets of Asia, the Middle East, Africa and Latin America, some of which, never saw him perform. The production will introduce a new generation of fans to his extraordinary catalogue, also returning to the countries that embraced him throughout his career, including the UK, Europe, North America, Japan and Australia.”

Tickets for the Feb. 14, 2027 premiere in Berlin will go on sale Oct. 28 at 9 a.m. local time via eventim.de. The dates for the arena run of “Stage” haven’t yet been set.
