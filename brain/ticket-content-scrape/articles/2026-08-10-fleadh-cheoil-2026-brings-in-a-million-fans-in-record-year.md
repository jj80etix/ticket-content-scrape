# Fleadh Cheoil 2026 brings in a million fans in ‘record’ year

## Summary

Ireland's Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann drew record footfall topping one million attendees in its first-ever Belfast edition, an eight-day traditional-Irish-music festival held across 31 venues.

### Key Points
- Day-six footfall alone (227,170) had already pushed the running total past 900,000, outstripping organizers' original 800,000 total-attendance estimate for the whole festival.
- Organizers say the figure represents a 67% increase in city-core visitor levels versus the same period in 2025.
- The festival returns to Belfast Aug. 1-8, 2027.

## Transcript

## Fleadh Cheoil 2026 brings in a million fans in ‘record’ year

The 2026 Belfast edition of the Irish traditional music festival made for the "best attended Fleadh ever" across its eight-day span, organisers say

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Gig_Rig_301ff9a1-2cc1-4393-9e01-06cf67566d5c.avif)

© Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann

Ireland’s leading traditional music festival, the Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann, wrapped up its eight-day 2026 edition which welcomed “record footfall”, in its first ever event to be held in Belfast.

The festival, often known as the Fleadh, is held each year in towns and cities across Ireland, and produced by cultural organisation Comhaltas Ceoltóirí Éireann in conjunction with government and host cities. It is the world’s largest live traditional Irish music event, and has as a central feature competitions for All-Ireland titles in music, song, and dance alongside large-scale live events.

Organisers said the festival was the “best attended” edition of the festival since it debuted in Mullingar in 1951, pointing to attendance figures in excess of one million.

“This year’s Fleadh – held in Belfast for the first time – attracted record footfall, with final figures expected to top one million, making it the best attended Fleadh ever. The festival will return to the city from 1-8 August 2027, hosted once again by Belfast City Council, Comhaltas Ceoltóirí Éireann and \[regional Comhaltas\] Ards CCÉ,” organisers said as the event concluded. The festival is also supported by the Department of the Economy and the Department of Culture, Communications and Sport.

> “This year’s Fleadh – held in Belfast for the first time – attracted record footfall, with final figures expected to top 1 million”

The festival took place across 31 venues throughout Belfast, ranging from its main stage in an outdoor auditorium in the grounds of Belfast City Hall, to existing concert halls, arts centres, pubs, and grassroots venues.

“We have had an unprecedented Fleadh of music, magic and memories. Belfast opened its doors and hearts to the multitudes who came from far and near for this amazing celebration, which will have an encore in 2027,” said Dr Labhrás O Murchú, chief executive of Comhaltas Ceoltóirí Éireann.

“One million fans can say to Belfast ‘you are the envy of the world’, with perhaps billions worldwide aware of the cultural history made in August 2026,” he added.

By the sixth day of the festival (6 August), organisers said the festival had surpassed 900,000 attendees, as measured by cameras installed by Belfast One Business Improvement District which counted footfall. 6 August saw 227,170 attendees recorded at events across a square mile radius around City Hall.

This led organisers to say that “figures for the same five-day period in 2025 show footfall of 537,921 – meaning the Fleadh has boosted visitor levels into the city core by 67%, when compared to the same time last year.” The attendance figures outstrip organisers’ estimates at the opening of the festival, when they said they anticipated 800,000 guests.
