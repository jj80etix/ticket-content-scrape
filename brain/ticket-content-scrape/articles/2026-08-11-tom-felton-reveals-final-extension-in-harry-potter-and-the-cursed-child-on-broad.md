# Tom Felton Reveals Final Extension in ‘Harry Potter and the Cursed Child’ on Broadway

## Summary

Tom Felton will extend his run as Draco Malfoy in Broadway's "Harry Potter and the Cursed Child" through January 3, 2027, his third extension since debuting in the role in November 2025. He will sit out select weeks in August, September, and October 2026. The show remains the highest-grossing production in the history of the Lyric Theatre and consistently tops Broadway's weekly grosses chart.

### Key Points
- Felton's role, originally a 19-week engagement starting November 2025, has now been extended a third time through January 3, 2027.
- Felton will not perform August 17-23, September 14-20, or October 12-18.
- "Cursed Child" set a box-office record for a nine-performance week ending December 28, 2025.
- The play is the fifth longest-running production in Broadway history, having arrived in 2018 and surpassed "Deathtrap" (1978).
- Story follows Harry's son Albus and Draco's son Scorpius, set 19 years after "Deathly Hallows."

Tom Felton isn’t done bringing the magic to Broadway *just* yet.

The film star, who portrayed Harry Potter’s nemesis Draco Malfoy in all eight *Harry Potter* films, will continue his reprising role as Malfoy in Broadway’s “Harry Potter and the Cursed Child” through 2027. Felton first returned to the role in November 2025. While he was originally only scheduled for a limited, 19-week engagement, this will mark his [third extension](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/04/tom-felton-extends-stay-in-broadways-harry-potter-and-the-cursed-child/) in the production.

The newly-announced extension will see Felton continue the role through January 3, 2027. As previously revealed, Felton will not be performing on select dates: August 17-23, September 14-20, and October 12-18

Since Felton made his Broadway debut in “Harry Potter and The Cursed Child,” the show has consistently held the No. 1 spot on the Broadway grosses and remains the highest-grossing production in the history of the Lyric Theatre, even setting a box-office record at $.7 million for the nine-performance week ending December 28, 2025.
