# Merch Traffic cracks down on online bootleg merchandise sellers

## Summary

Live Nation subsidiary Merch Traffic has filed a US trademark lawsuit against a group of anonymous online sellers accused of peddling counterfeit merchandise for artists including Bruno Mars, Harry Styles, The Cure, Avenged Sevenfold, The Notorious B.I.G., Easy-E and Nirvana. Filed in the Northern District of Illinois, the suit alleges the sellers — mostly believed to operate out of China or other jurisdictions with weak trademark enforcement — caused licensing-revenue loss and brand dilution, and it seeks injunctive relief, profit turnover and up to triple damages (or statutory damages up to $2 million per mark). The suit is part of a broader wave of touring-industry trademark enforcement, following similar actions by Merch Traffic around a Bruce Springsteen show and by HYBE around BTS's US tour dates.

### Key Points
- Merch Traffic (a Live Nation subsidiary) sued anonymous "Schedule A" online sellers in the US District Court for the Northern District of Illinois (filed August 5) over counterfeit merch for Bruno Mars, Harry Styles, The Cure and others.
- Suit seeks injunctive relief, disgorgement of profits, treble damages, or alternatively up to $2 million in statutory damages per infringed trademark.
- Defendants are alleged to operate from China or similarly lax-enforcement jurisdictions; suit also asks marketplaces (PayPal, Amazon, eBay, Temu, Walmart) to stop facilitating sales tied to the trademarks.
- Filing cites OECD figures putting global counterfeit/pirated goods trade at $467 billion annually (~2.3% of world imports), with China as the primary source.
- Follows a pattern of similar action: Merch Traffic previously sought an injunction around a Bruce Springsteen Prudential Center show, and HYBE filed a nationwide injunction over bootleg merch at BTS's US tour stops.
- Article frames trademark-enforcement litigation as becoming a standard operational feature of major touring, alongside artists like Dua Lipa, A$AP Rocky, Benson Boone and Tate McRae pursuing similar actions.

As bootleg artist merchandise sellers run rampant, Live Nation subsidiary Merch Traffic has filed a trademark lawsuit in the US to combat counterfeit merchandise peddlers selling products online for artists like Bruno Mars, Harry Styles, and The Cure.

Prosecutors filed the suit last week (5 August) in the US District Court for the Northern District of Illinois, alleging that a group of anonymous online sellers “sold products using infringing and counterfeit versions of the federally registered trademarks” belonging to a group of artists, per the filing.

By doing so, prosecutors allege that the sellers – referred to as Schedule A – deprived Merch Traffic of licensing revenue and created “confusion, dilution, and tarnishment” of the client trademarks, which also includes Avenged Sevenfold, The Notorious B.I.G., Easy-E, and Nirvana.

Though the defendants, who may be working independently and in conjunction, are not outright listed, the suit states that they “reside and/or operate in the People’s Republic of China or other foreign jurisdictions with lax trademark enforcement systems”.

Global trade in counterfeit and pirated goods was listed in the filing as worth $467 billion (€546bn) annually, roughly 2.3% of all imports, according to figures from the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD), which points to China as the primary source of the counterfeit products.

> Legal action to prevent trademark infringements is quickly becoming a standard part of today’s touring landscape

Merch Traffic is seeking injunctive relief, turnover of all profits made, and up to triple the amount of damages for copyright infringement. Alternatively, the suit requests damages of up to $2 million (€1.7m) for each and every use of a client trademark.

In addition to the monetary recoup, prosecutors request that online marketplaces including PayPal, Amazon, eBay, Temu, and Walmart stop advertising products in connection with the trademarks.

While this motion looks to combat online sellers and distributors, it follows a widespread crackdown on counterfeit merchandise to prevent bootleg flogging outside live events and major tours.

Earlier this year, Merch Traffic also sought an injunction to prevent unauthorised sellers around Bruce Springsteen’s Prudential Center show in New Jersey. HYBE also filed for a nationwide injunction to stop bootleg merchandise being sold around BTS’s *Arirang World Tour* stops in the US last month.

Legal action to prevent trademark infringements is quickly becoming a standard part of today’s touring landscape, with the likes of Dua Lipa, A$AP Rocky, Benson Boone, and Tate McRae also requesting similar injunctions in recent years.
