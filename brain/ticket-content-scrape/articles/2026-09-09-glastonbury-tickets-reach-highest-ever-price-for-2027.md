# Glastonbury tickets reach highest-ever price for 2027

## Summary

Glastonbury Festival organizers confirmed 2027 general admission tickets will cost £408 (including a £5 booking fee), an 8% increase over 2025's price and the highest in the festival's history, following 2026's fallow year.

### Key Points

- Tickets go on sale Oct. 1 (coach-travel passes) and Oct. 4 (general admission) for the 23-27 June 2027 edition.
- Price has risen ~6% per edition since 2023, after a ~26% jump from 2019 to 2023 spanning the pandemic pause.
- The 210,000-capacity festival's 2026-27 fiscal report showed pre-tax profit rising to £75.2M; organizers say they aim to keep prices "as low as possible" given demand far exceeding supply.
- Fans must pre-register as part of Worth Farm's ongoing anti-touting campaign; tickets typically sell out within an hour.

Following its 2026 fallow year, Glastonbury organisers have confirmed ticket prices will top £400 (€465) for the first time in the famed festival’s history.

Tickets for the UK festival’s next edition, set for 23-27 June 2027, will cost attendees £408 (€475), which includes a £5 (€6) booking fee, a roughly 8% bump in price from 2025’s fee-inclusive price of £378.50 (€440).

Since 2023, the cost of a general Glastonbury ticket has risen around 6% between editions, after rising around 26% from 2019 to 2023 during the [pandemic-caused pause](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2022/10/rising-costs-blamed-for-glasto-ticket-price-hike/).

While the lineup for the 210,000-capacity event is yet to be announced, organisers confirmed tickets will go on sale in October, with passes plus coach travel launch on 1 October, followed by general admission tickets on 4 October.

In line with previous years, tickets for the festival are likely to sell out in less than an hour, with fans made to pre-register as part of the Worth Farm festival’s ongoing anti-touting campaign.

Each year, the UK’s biggest festival features more than 4,000 performances across over 100 stages. The 2025 edition featured The 1975, Neil Young, and Olivia Rodrigo as headliners, along with Sir Rod Stewart in the Sunday Legends Slot.

Demand is expected to be strong after the year off, which is a regular feature of the long-standing festival. It most recently took a break to let the farm site recover in 2018, while the 2020-21 events were cancelled due to the pandemic.

In its most recent financial report, organisers reported a [£7.7 million (€8.9m) pre-tax profit](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/01/glastonbury-profits-rise-to-7-7m-for-2024-25/) boost to £75.2m (€86.8m), up nearly £6m (€6.8m) from over £68m (€78.8m) in 2024.

Its charitable donations totalled £2.7m (€3.1m), supporting partners including Oxfam, Greenpeace, and WaterAid.

At the time, the company announced its intention to keep ticket prices “as low as possible” to safeguard the festival’s accessibility, with demand for tickets “far outweighing supply”.

When Glastonbury began on Michael Eavis’ dairy farm in 1970, tickets cost £1, including milk from the dairy, per the [*BBC*](https://www.bbc.co.uk/culture/article/20250620-the-story-of-the-first-ever-glastonbury-festival-in-1970).
