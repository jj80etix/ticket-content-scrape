# Kanye West sets US rap show attendance record, organisers say

## Summary

Kanye West drew 80,210 fans to Houston's Reliant Stadium on Halloween night, setting a new U.S. stadium rap-concert attendance record and surpassing the mark he set earlier this year at SoFi Stadium.

### Key Points
- Promoter Ikon Concerts and the venue jointly confirmed the record; West also received Texas's Global Icon Award.
- Previous record-holder was Jay-Z, who sold over 63,100 tickets at the Rose Bowl in 2014.
- The tour continues amid ongoing controversy over West's antisemitic remarks, which has led several countries (UK, France, India, Poland, Italy) to block or cancel his shows.
- Next stops: Dallas's AT&T Stadium (Nov. 7) and Glendale's State Farm Stadium (Nov. 21).

## Transcript

The controversial rapper drew over 80,000 people to a Halloween show at Houston's Reliant Stadium, raising a bar he set earlier this year

![Ye in Madrid](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Ye-in-Madrid-©-Vibra-Music.png)

Ye in Madrid © Vibra Music

Kanye West (Ye) drew over 80,000 fans to Houston’s Reliant Stadium over the weekend, which organisers say sets a new attendance record for rap concerts in the US.

Promoter Ikon Concerts and Reliant Stadium (FKA NRG Stadium) both [confirmed](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfLtSTWsdMq/) the new record – 80,210 people – following the show on Saturday (31 October).

“Houston made history tonight,” say organisers. “Ye Live in Houston has officially broken the US stadium rap concert attendance record!”

Ye was also awarded the Global Icon Award from the State of Texas, presented by State Representative Ron Reynolds, after the show.

With the Halloween show, the controversial artist raises the bar he set earlier this year at Inglewood’s SoFi Stadium, where he attracted around 74,000 people nightly for a pair of performances back in April.

Before that show, Jay-Z held the record for over a decade, after selling over 63,100 tickets for a Rose Bowl Stadium show during the 2014 *On The Run* tour with Beyoncé. Attendance figures for Jay-Z’s recent pair of shows at SoFi have not been reported.

Recent years have also seen Travis Scott and Kendrick Lamar pull crowds of more than 60,000 people during their respective stadium shows.

The rapper also unseated country singer Cody Johnson for the venue’s attendance record, which was set at 80,203 people (a difference of seven people) in March.

From here, Ye is due to perform in Dallas, Texas’ [AT&T Stadium](https://www.iqmagazine.com/featured-list/stadium-stars-2026/att-stadium/) (cap. 74,000) on 7 November and Glendale, Arizona’s State Farm Stadium (78,600) on 21 November.

> “I firmly believe in culture and in the freedom of audiences to decide what they want to see”

Despite the controversy, demand for the rapper has been strong in the tour’s respective markets. In Istanbul, he reportedly drew 118,000 people at Atatürk Olympic Stadium in May, a personal best for the artist and one of the world’s most-attended rap shows.

In recent years, West has issued a string of antisemitic, racist, and pro-Nazi comments, including releasing a song titled *Heil Hitler* in May 2025.

Though he has attributed this behaviour to his bipolar disorder and apologised, his actions have led to ethical and practical queries from promoters, audiences, and authorities, with local governments in the [UK](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/04/report-kanye-west-blocked-from-uk-wireless/), [France](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/04/kanye-west-postpones-france-concert-adds-poland-date/), [India](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/05/kanye-wests-india-show-scrapped-over-safety-concerns/), [Poland](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/04/kanye-wests-poland-and-switzerland-dates-cancelled/), and  [Italy](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/06/italys-hellwatt-festival-saga-ends-in-cancellation/) moving to strike down the stadium shows, largely over concerns of public safety and public order.

The widely condemned performer’s return to widespread touring launched with two nights in Mexico City in late January. Since then, his fragmented schedule has spanned shows in the US, Türkiye, the Netherlands, Georgia, [Albania](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/kanye-west-draws-60k-to-purpose-built-albanian-venue/), Portugal, Kazakhstan, and Indonesia.

Speaking to *IQ* about bringing the artist to Spain, Gonzalo Peña of promoter Vibra Music said: “Opportunities like this come along very rarely in life and, when they do, you have to decide whether you are prepared to take the risk. We had no hesitation.”

“I firmly believe in culture and in the freedom of audiences to decide what they want to see and what they want to listen to, always within the boundaries of the law,” he said about [the decision to promote Ye](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/inside-the-decision-to-promote-kanye-west/). “As promoters, our responsibility is not to make that decision on behalf of the public, but to ensure that the shows we produce are delivered professionally, safely, and lawfully. After that, every individual is free to decide whether or not they wish to attend.”
