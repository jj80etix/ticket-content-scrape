# ‘Enduring demand’ for live pushes royalties to record €1.4bn

## Summary

Global royalties for music creators hit a record €14.06 billion in 2025, per CISAC's Global Collections Report, with live-music royalty collections rising nearly 15% to a record €1.4 billion and growing three times faster than background-music royalties since 2019. Asia-Pacific and Latin America are the fastest-growing regions for live royalties, though Western Europe still accounts for nearly 75% of collections.

### Key Points
- Total music royalties: €14.06bn in 2025 (+12%); live music segment: €1.4bn (+15%).
- Live/background royalties (€3.87bn combined) surpassed TV/radio (€3.32bn, -3%) for the first time.
- Asia-Pacific and Latin America live royalty collections grew 113.6% and 107.9% respectively since 2019.
- CISAC cites rising ticket prices, more concerts/attendance, and improved reporting as growth drivers; flags AI as a future risk to creator livelihoods.

## ‘Enduring demand’ for live pushes royalties to record €1.4bn

Globally, royalties for music creators topped €14bn for the first time in 2025, with live performance a rapidly growing segment

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Dua-Lipa-©-Jonathan-White.png)

© Jonathan White

In 2025, global royalties for music creators grew nearly 12% to a record €14.06 billion, driven in part by the rapidly growing live performance segment, according to the latest Global Collections Report.

Issued by the International Confederation of Societies of Authors and Composers (CISAC), the comprehensive report compiles findings from 228 authors’ societies in 111 countries and territories, spanning music, audiovisual, visual arts, literature, and drama.

Last year, the live music category rose to a record level in its own right, up nearly 15% to €1.4bn. The report finds that since 2019, live music royalty collections have grown three times faster than background music collections, underlining the increasing importance of concerts to creators’ royalty income.

Digital music collections make up over 45% of music’s total, having increased by 27.9% last year to €6.41bn.

But live and background royalties passed TV and radio segment for the first time, rising by 7.7% to €3.87bn, roughly a quarter of the music’s total takings as the second-largest source of creator royalties worldwide.

By comparison, the live and background figure was €2.64bn in 2016, an almost 47% increase in under a decade.

TV and radio collections fell in 2025 by 3% to €3.32bn, demonstrating the changing face of music consumption. In the past decade, music collections have grown by 83.5% across the board.

> “It is also increasing their expectation to be paid quickly and accurately”

Broken down by territory, Europe continues to be the largest collection region, with Western Europe accounting for nearly 75% of live music royalty collections in 2025.

But Asia-Pacific and Latin America are rapidly picking up steam, rising by 113.6% and 107.9% since 2019, respectively.

This is due in part to rising ticket prices across the board, though an increasing quantity of concerts and higher attendance have also contributed to growth in certain markets, along with new licensing agreements, revised tariffs, or other reporting improvements.

“The growth of live music is creating significant opportunities for rightsholders, but it is also increasing their expectation to be paid quickly and accurately,” says Sylvain Piat, CISAC director of business and technology.

“A performance can involve creators and publishers represented across several countries, so accurate information needs to move efficiently between different organisations and systems. By making that information more consistent, we can improve the identification of works, reduce unnecessary manual processing, and help societies pay creators more quickly and transparently.”

> “As we enter the age of AI, past growth cannot be taken for granted”

Alongside attempting to create a more universal data and collection process, societies are exploring new ways to capture accurate information closer to the point of performance, including music recognition technology (MRT). This can provide an additional source of information, particularly useful at larger events or when setlists are incomplete.

The topline figure from the report is that royalties collected for creators worldwide across all disciplines reached a record €15.5bn in 2025 (up 11.3%), with global collections nearly doubling since 2016.

“The continued growth in global collections shown in our report is a strong and encouraging signal,” says Gadi Oron, CISAC director general. “It reflects the enduring demand for creative works around the world and the effectiveness of collective management in delivering value back to creators.

“But as we enter the age of AI, past growth cannot be taken for granted. Our priority must be to ensure that technology strengthens, rather than erodes, creators’ livelihoods,” he says.
