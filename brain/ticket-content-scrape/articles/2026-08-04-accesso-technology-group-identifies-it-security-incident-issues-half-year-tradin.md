# Accesso Technology Group identifies IT security incident, issues half-year trading update

## Summary

Accesso Technology Group disclosed a contained IT security incident involving temporary unauthorized access to a limited part of its systems — no downtime, low estimated financial exposure, investigation ongoing — alongside a half-year trading update it called "resilient and in line with expectations." Accesso reaffirmed FY2026 revenue guidance of $146 million and highlighted its strategy of unifying ticketing, F&B/retail, guest experience and virtual queuing around its new AccessoPay payments capability, now live with its first customers transacting this month.

### Key Points
- Security incident: unauthorized access to a "limited part" of internal systems, contained, no downtime; investigation suggests only internal operational data was exposed.
- H1 2026 trading "resilient and in line with the board's expectations"; FY2026 revenue guidance unchanged at $146M, weighted to H2 (summer/Halloween seasons).
- AccessoPay, the company's new integrated payments capability, is now live with first customers beginning transactions this month.
- Strategic push to unify ticketing, F&B/retail, guest experience and virtual queuing into a single connected ecosystem and commercial relationship.
- FY2026 outlook includes milestone-based revenue from Middle East projects, principally Saudi Arabia.

### Highlights
- AccessoPay is now live and transacting with first customers — a concrete, dated competitor payments launch, not just a roadmap announcement.
- Accesso's platform-unification strategy (ticketing + F&B + guest experience + queuing under one payments-anchored ecosystem) is explicitly framed as simplifying vendor relationships and improving ROI clarity for venue clients.
- The concurrent security incident disclosure, while contained, shows expanding platform scope carries expanding breach surface.

### Industry Problem
Ticketing/venue-tech vendors are consolidating previously separate product lines (ticketing, payments, F&B, queuing) into single integrated platforms to simplify vendor relationships and improve ROI clarity for venue clients — while simultaneously carrying growing cybersecurity exposure as system scope expands.

### Proposed Solution
The item itself proposes platform unification (an AccessoPay-anchored ecosystem) as the competitive answer to fragmented vendor relationships; for Etix this is a direct competitor benchmark — evaluate integrated first-party payments as a build/improve opportunity — while treating the concurrent security incident as a reminder to keep incident-response and access-control posture current as platform scope grows.

**Accesso Technology Group reported yesterday (August 3) that it had identified an IT security incident involving temporary unauthorised access to a “limited part of the group’s systems”.**

The technology solutions provider for leisure, entertainment and cultural markets added that the unauthorised access had been contained, resulting in no downtime or major disruption to the group’s operations or services.

Accesso said it immediately enacted its incident response plan, engaged external specialists to support its investigation, and implemented workarounds and additional precautionary measures to mitigate the risk of disruption and further unauthorised access.

The company also said that so far, its investigation suggests the part of the group’s systems accessed only contained internal information relating to its own business operations. The investigation remains ongoing.

Accesso will continue to monitor its network, and has not identified any suspicious activity since the incident. It believes that the risk of financial exposure arising from the incident to be low, and it is communicating with customers and the relevant authorities.

**Half-year trading update**

Accesso also reported its trading update for this six months ending June 30.

The company said trading was “resilient and in line with the board’s expectations” with “encouraging commercial momentum across the group’s growth initiatives”. Accesso said it has benefitted from cost efficiency measures implemented earlier this year, while continuing to invest to fuel future growth, including the expansion of AI use and payment capabilities.

As part of its strategy, Accesso has outlined plans to bring together its products as a single, connected ecosystem, spanning ticketing; food, beverage and retail; guest experience; and virtual queuing. This is underpinned by its payments capability, AccessoPay, which is now live, with the first customers expected to begin transacting this month.

The board’s outlook for 2026 remains unchanged, with an anticipated full-year revenue of $146m (£108m/€127m), and, consistent with the group’s typical pattern, trading is expected to be weighted to the second half of the year which captures the summer and Halloween seasons.

The group’s full-year expectations will also include a number of milestone-based revenues from projects in the Middle East, principally in Saudi Arabia.

“Trading in the first half was resilient and in line with our expectations, and we have made encouraging progress in executing our strategy,” commented Lee Cowie, chief executive of Accesso, who took on the role [earlier this year](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/05/people-on-the-move-aeg-presents-korea-30e-and-more/).

“We are pleased to be seeing growing customer engagement aligned to our integrated platform offering, which brings together solutions across the guest experience lifecycle through a single commercial relationship. Customers are responding positively to the simplicity of the proposition and the clearer return on investment it provides, giving us confidence in the opportunity to drive increased customer value, sustainable growth and margin improvement over time.”
