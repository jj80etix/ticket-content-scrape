# Shakira stadium show marks ‘turning point’ for Kyrgyzstan

## Summary

Shakira will play Kyrgyzstan's new 51,000-seat Bishkek Arena in October, her first Central Asia show, which organizers are framing as a proof-of-concept for opening the region to major international touring. This is a market-expansion/booking story with no ticketing-platform, pricing, or policy content.

### Key Points
- First major Western-pop concert at Central Asia's largest stadium.
- Promoted by a multi-party international booking consortium plus the Kyrgyz presidential administration.
- Organizers frame it as a gateway to future touring across Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and the wider region.
- No ticketing-technology, fee, or regulatory content.

## Transcript

Organisers tell IQ the stop at Bishkek Arena by the Latin megastar will be "the biggest concert that has happened in Central Asia"

![Shakira](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Shakira-©-Chris-Cornejo.png)

Shakira © Chris Cornejo

Shakira is set to inaugurate Central Asia’s largest stadium, Kyrgyzstan’s 51,000-seat Bishkek Arena, creating a moment organisers say will be “much bigger than one concert” as they look to open up the region for major touring.

It was confirmed this week that the Colombian megastar will take her *Las Mujeres Ya No Lloran World Tour* to the market – which is bordered by China, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and Tajikistan – following her bespoke [Madrid residency](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/09/shakira-launches-madrid-residency-with-cultural-takeover/). It will be Shakira’s first show in Central Asia, and will serve as a declaration of intent for organisers looking to establish the market as a live music hub.

The one-off show, set for 18 October, is being promoted by Estonia-based Baltic Music Group, with the help of Mullin’s Horizon Booking, Promoter Syndicate, 2Live Entertainment, a local Kyrgyz team, and the Presidential Administration of Kyrgyzstan.

“Bringing an artist of Shakira’s calibre to Bishkek for her first-ever performance in Central Asia is much bigger than one concert,” Isabelle Mullin of Mullin’s Horizon tells *IQ.*

“It sends a very strong signal that markets outside the traditional touring routes can support truly world-class live entertainment when the right local commitment and international team come together,” she adds.

Mohamed Mia, CEO of 2Live, tells *IQ*: “It’s a great thing. This will be the biggest concert that has happened in Central Asia. It’s the first time a concert of this magnitude is happening in the region. It’s great for tourism. It’s great for people to see that part of the world and realise that entertainment is happening out there.”

The show booker adds that the regional live industry is growing to a point of maturity, aiming to use the Shakira show as a gateway to continued international touring across multiple countries.

> “It’s great for people to see that part of the world and realise that entertainment is happening out there”

“The hope is definitely to continue the momentum and to continue bringing big international artists into the region so that the world can see places like Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan — but specifically Kyrgyzstan,” he said. “They want to attract an audience to the place, especially for the beauty of the country, for a tourism aspect. They want people to come and see the country.”

Asked what the next step needed to consolidate the touring ecosystem in the region, Mia said that “as with anything, it does take time”.

“It’s not something you can do as regularly,” he said of the Shakira booking. “This is the first one. The idea is to continue with it, but it will take a bit of time to continuously do this. You can’t do it every day, you can’t do it as often as you like, but at some point you want to get to that stage where you’re able to.”

The stadium will open its doors later this month with a large-scale concert featuring popular Kyrgyz artists, followed quickly by Shakira as the first international act to perform at the capital city’s brand-new stadium.

Tickets for the engagement went on sale today (1 October), priced between 12,000 KGS (€121) for limited view to 40,000 KGS (€405) for the closest seats, with VIP tickets to go on sale at a later time. A local financial publication, [Akchabar](https://www.akchabar.kg/en/news/bileti-na-kontsert-shakiri-v-bishkeke-mogut-prinesti-bolee-115-mlrd-somov-vzyzqhlvldypikai), estimated the show could generate 1.15 billion KGS (€11.65m) in revenue.

“A show by such a massive global superstar at the region’s largest brand-new stadium marks a turning point, as it has been extremely rare for an artist at the absolute peak of their popularity to visit Central Asia,” Syndicate Promoter co-owner Mladen Patajac tells *IQ*. “A show of such scale serves as a strong indicator that Bishkek – with its state-of-the-art stadium – is looking strongly to host other major stars.”

Following the show, she is set to conclude this year’s activity with a show at Egypt’s Pyramids of Giza on 28 November, her [only remaining date](https://www.iqmagazine.com/?p=472144&preview=true) near the Middle East after stops in Jordan, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE were all scrapped as a result of the outbreak of a regional war at the end of February.

> “A show like this can change the way agents, managers, and promoters look at a market”

But the Latin superstar’s arrival in Kyrgyzstan comes as notable global artists are pushing deeper into the [Caucasus States](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/08/the-caucasus-the-next-frontier-for-touring/), the bridge between Eastern Europe and Central Asia. In recent years, acts including Justin Timberlake, Jennifer Lopez, Will Smith, Imagine Dragons, and Guns N’ Roses have performed in countries such as Georgia, Armenia, and Azerbaijan, tapping into markets traditionally left unturned.

Mehmet Uğur Aydın, CEO of Azerbaijan-based regional promoter iTicket Production, previously told *[IQ](https://www.iqmagazine.com/publications/global-promoters-report-2024/azerbaijan/)* that it is “a challenge to persuade the talent to route through Central Asia, as it’s not long been a traditional stop” but that the tides are beginning to turn, in part due to visibility from the Formula 1 Grand Prix in Baku.

As such, iTicket helped deliver shows from Calvin Harris and Katy Perry for the event at the weekend, which Aydın says demonstrated the country’s viability as an “ [international entertainment destination](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/09/f1-music-programming-raises-azerbaijans-touring-profile/) ” as they position themselves as a stop on international routes.

Kyrgyzstan’s position between burgeoning international touring markets China, India, and Mongolia could be leveraged as an important stop between the strengthening Caucasus route and Asia, empowered by the contemporary stadium.

“For a new venue to open its doors with an artist at this level is incredibly exciting,” Mullin says.

Located on the southwestern outskirts of the capital, the multi-purpose space will also be home to the national football team, though Shakira’s show will set the tone for the yurt-shaped venue.

“A show like this can change the way agents, managers, and promoters look at a market, and hopefully it opens the door for many more major international artists to come to Kyrgyzstan and the wider Central Asian region,” Mullin concludes.
