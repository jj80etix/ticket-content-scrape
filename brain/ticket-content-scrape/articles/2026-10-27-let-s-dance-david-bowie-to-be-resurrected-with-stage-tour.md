# Let’s Dance: David Bowie to be resurrected with ‘Stage’ tour

## Summary

David Bowie will be "resurrected" in a touring arena spectacle called 'Stage', built from previously unseen 1978 Isolar II Tour film restored over a decade by filmmaker Brett Morgen with the Bowie Estate and Warner Music. The show premieres at Berlin's Uber Arena in February 2027 before touring globally, organized by Rob Hallett's Wonderworld.

### Key Points
- Built from unseen 35mm film of Bowie's 1978 Isolar II performances at Earls Court.
- World premiere Feb. 2027 at Uber Arena, Berlin, tied to the Berlinale film festival.
- Produced as a full theatrical tour (not a concert film or ABBA Voyage-style avatar).
- Restoration led by Brett Morgen (director of Moonage Daydream) over roughly 10 years.

## Let’s Dance: David Bowie to be resurrected with ‘Stage’ tour

After premiering in Berlin next year, organiser Rob Hallett tells IQ the arena-level live experience will be taken around the world

![David Bowie on 'Stage'](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/David-Bowie-Stage-©-Blackout.png)

David Bowie on 'Stage' © Blackout

David Bowie will be resurrected with an arena-level spectacle that blurs the line between cinema and concert next year, with organiser Rob Hallett telling *IQ* the immersive experience showcases the star “at the peak of his powers.”

The show will feature a previously unseen filmed take of Bowie’s 1978 *Isolar II* *Tour* performances at London’s Earls Court, with the “huge” production set to tour globally after premiering at Berlin’s Uber Arena in February.

Dubbed *‘Stage’* after the inscription on the film tin found stowed away for decades, the film has been meticulously restored over the past 10 years by filmmaker Brett Morgen, the director behind the award-winning *Moonage Daydream* documentary, in collaboration with the David Bowie Estate and the support of catalogue owner Warner Music Group.

Instead of converting it into a concert film or an ABBA Voyage-like digital avatar, organisers formulated the show into a full-production tour, with the delay between finding the film and executing the vision down to the advancement of technology, Hallett says.

“David always had a vision, I’m told, of it being something along the lines of what we’re doing,” Hallett tells *IQ*. “However, the technology simply wasn’t there, and he moved on.”

“He famously said he never wanted to be an avatar. This is not that. Everyone believes this is what David would have wanted with his footage,” adds the former president of AEG International.

The production will feature three high-definition LED screens, which each measure around 200 by 80 feet, designed to give the illusion that Bowie and his backing band are physically present.

> “The only difference between this and the concert is there’s no one physically alive on the stage”

This is to be complemented by an immersive lighting rig that brings the lights from the film into reality and a Clair Global surround sound system, both intended to draw the audience into the world of a show that took place nearly five decades ago.

“It makes you immersed in the experience, with the sound being immersive and the physical light surrounding the stage and blinding you in the same way as a concert would,” Hallet explains. “The only difference between this and the concert is there’s no one physically alive on the stage. But the sound’s amazing, better than any concert I’ve ever been to.”

He says it won’t take audiences “long to be lost in the concert”, despite the lack of live participation, as “the warmth of his performance just comes flooding out.”

“David looks and sounds immaculate, and the band is on fire,” he says, adding that adding live musicians could tip it into “tribute act” territory.

With a sizable production, it is designed to tour venues above the 5,000-capacity level, with the show boasting over 130 rigging points.

“Even though there’s no musicians, we’ve got 14 trucks and 55 people on the road. It’s not a rinky dink show,” he says. Leading the personnel are lighting designer Patrick Woodroffe, audio mixer Paul Massey, and production director John Pryer.

After the exclusive premiere on 14 February as part of the 77th Berlin International Film Festival (Berlinale), which is expected to draw an audience of around 6,000 people, the ambition is to “be a rock ‘n’ roll world tour”, Hallett says.

> “The plan is not just to sit in one place”

“The plan is to play anywhere and everywhere, from places that David played to rekindle the memories, to places where he didn’t reach. The plan is not just to sit in one place,” he says.

And as there is no live performer element, Hallett says he foresees the development of concurrently running kits in multiple markets as the touring path evolves. He adds that the aim is to offer fans of all generations an “affordable” experience, with tickets priced at an average of €75.

“If David were alive, God knows how much the tickets would be,” he says.

Despite the lack of live performers, *‘Stage’* is ultimately expected to mirror something of a standard touring model, and will come complete with an exclusive merchandise collection.

Hallet says the project is “hopefully the first of this type of experience”, with his new venture Wonderworld poised to span live events and avatar recreations of “classical” musicians.

“To my generation, classical music isn’t Beethoven and Mozart. It’s David Bowie, the Beatles, and the \[Rolling\] Stones. To keep these artists alive, and so new generations can see the greatness of what’s come before, is, I think, important,” he concludes.
