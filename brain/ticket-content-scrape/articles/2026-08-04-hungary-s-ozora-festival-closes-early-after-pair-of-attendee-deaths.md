# Hungary’s Ozora Festival closes early after pair of attendee deaths

## Summary

Hungary's Ozora psychedelic dance festival cut its program short after two unrelated attendee deaths — a cardiac arrest and a fall from a stage structure treated as a suspected suicide. Organizers ended all music at midnight, two days before the scheduled close, and pledged to overhaul dancefloor safety policies for 2027, including restricting camping equipment to designated areas.

### Key Points
- Two deaths: a 39-year-old woman (cardiac arrest) and a 45-year-old man (fall from a stage structure, suspected suicide).
- Festival ended its program at midnight Aug. 2, two days ahead of the scheduled Aug. 4 close.
- Organizers confirmed a paused set resumed only after the injured man was confirmed still breathing; a medical quad (not an ambulance) separately struck and lightly injured a bystander.
- Ozora announced forthcoming changes to dancefloor safety rules, including moving tents/chairs/camping gear to designated camping areas only.

## Hungary’s Ozora Festival closes early after pair of attendee deaths

One fan died after falling from a stage structure in a suspected suicide, while another suffered a cardiac arrest

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/08/Ozora-Festival-Hunfary-scaled.jpeg.webp)

Ozora Festival © Ozora Festival

Hungary’s Ozora Festival shut down part way through proceedings as two attendees died at the event in unrelated incidents.

Organisers of the psychedelic dance event in Dádpuszta said a 39-year-old woman suffered a sudden cardiac arrest and died despite swift medical care at the scene, while the death of a 45-year-old man after a fall from a stage structure is being treated as a suicide. There is no suggestion from authorities of fault on the festival’s behalf in either incident.

The circumstances of the man’s death are being investigated by authorities, the festival said, with reports saying the victim jumped from a high part of the main stage in full view of the crowd. Despite not giving further detail on the death of the woman, the festival reminded fans of protocols in place during an ongoing wave of extreme heat in Hungary.

After multiple reports emerged from attendees that a set by DJ Astrix was instructed to continue after initially being paused after the man fell from the structure, and that an ambulance struck and injured another spectator on its way to the scene, festival organisers told IQ: “The music was paused immediately following the incident. The decision to allow the performance to continue was made after it had been confirmed that the injured man was still breathing and had been transferred into the ambulance. The medical team continued resuscitation efforts inside the ambulance for approximately 30 minutes, in line with standard emergency protocol, but despite their efforts, they were ultimately unsuccessful.”

They continued: “Regarding reports that another festival attendee was struck while emergency services were responding, it was not an ambulance but a medical quad making its way through the crowd to the scene. One woman did not move aside in time and was knocked over. Fortunately, she was not injured.”

Following the incidents, the festival cut its programme short at midnight on 2 August, having originally been scheduled to run until today (4 August).

“We extend our deepest condolences and heartfelt sympathy to the families, loved ones, and friends of the two deceased. Our thoughts are with them during this extremely difficult time. In light of these tragic events, all music programs will end at midnight tonight, including those at the Pumpui stage,” organisers announced on Sunday.

> “We extend our deepest condolences and heartfelt sympathy to the families, loved ones, and friends of the two deceased”

“We would also like to remind all participants that a heat alert is currently in effect across Hungary. Please take extra care to stay hydrated and look after your health. Drinking water is available from every running water tap throughout the festival site. We ask everyone to look out for one another during these difficult hours and to treat each other with special care, compassion, and kindness.”

Festivals across Europe have been introducing measures to combat extreme heat seen across the continent during the summer, with several called off due to concerns for fans’ safety. In the wake of the fatatlities, Ozora announced it would be introducing changes to its policies around “the safety of our dancefloors”.

The festival told fans in a statement following the conclusion of the event: “This year we lost two members of our community. We won’t forget them, and we don’t want to simply move on.”

“We believe that taking responsibility means learning from what happens. Just as we made major changes to fire safety over the years, next year we will rethink the safety of our dancefloors.

Organisers continued: “Several new rules will be introduced. As part of these changes, tents, shade structures, chairs, tables, and all other camping equipment will only be allowed in the camping areas. The dancefloor should remain a place for dancing. Thank you for being with us this year. Take care of yourselves, and take care of each other.”
