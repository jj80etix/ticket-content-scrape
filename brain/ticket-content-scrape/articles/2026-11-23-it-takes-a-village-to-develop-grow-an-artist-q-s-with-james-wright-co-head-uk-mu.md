# ‘It Takes A Village To Develop & Grow An Artist’: Q’s With James Wright, Co-Head, UK Music, UTA

## Summary

Pollstar's year-end Q&A with James Wright, UTA's Co-Head of UK Music, covers a standout 2026 (Take That's "Circus" stadium tour, Rosalia, Bad Bunny) and his view on talent development, agent craft, and rising touring costs. Wright discusses how the cost of touring and production, the decline of grassroots/mid-size venues, and the cost of living are squeezing the path for emerging artists, and how a first-live-experience generation raised on arena/stadium shows is reshaping audience expectations.

### Key Points
- UTA's 2026 highlights include Take That's "Circus Live" stadium tour, Rosalia's arena business, Bad Bunny's record-breaking tour, and Sammy Virji's rise.
- Wright describes "signing better" as using data and a wider team ("it takes a village") rather than one agent driving deals.
- He flags a "perfect storm" of rising touring/production costs, cost-of-living pressure, and the loss of grassroots and mid-size venues as the biggest hurdle for breaking new artists.
- Notes a generational shift: many younger fans' first live experience is an arena/stadium show, raising questions about how to introduce them to smaller-scale live music.
- UTA has tours booked into 2027-2029 and is continuing to expand its team and signings.

UNITED: James Wright joined UTA in 2015 and was named co-head of the agency’s UK music group this summer. Picture by Louise Haywood Schiefer

*Pollstar* speaks with James Wright, Co-Head of UK Music at UTA, to talk about the state of play as another incredibly eventful year for live entertainment is drawing to a close.

UTA itself has had a standout year, from Take That’s “The Circus Live- Summer 2026” stadium tour to Rosalia’s arena business to Bad Bunny’s record breaking tour to Sammy Virji’s rise in the electronic space – to name just the tip of the iceberg.

Wright talked about the appetite of audiences for large-scale spectacle, how that could affect the expectations of the next generation of concertgoers, what it takes for the next generation of talent to break through the noise, and much more.

**Pollstar: Has your day to day changed much [since taking on your new role last summer](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/07/15/uta-promotes-james-wright-to-co-head-of-uk-music/?_gl=1%2Ajy4m40%2A_ga%2ANDgzNDk3NzAyLjE3OTQyMzIyNjQ.%2A_ga_5B6XRECYRM%2AczE3OTU0MjEzODMkbzE5JGcxJHQxNzk1NDIxMzk4JGo0NSRsMCRoMA..)?  
**James Wright: First and foremost, I’m an agent for my clients, present and future. I come to this job with that mentality every day. To that extent, it hasn’t changed. I’m artist first, it’s what excites me about being here at UTA, at the center of cultural moments. That’s always going to be the driving force behind everything.

But it has been exciting to take some of the learnings over the years as an agent, and be able to mentor others coming through; to help the team grow and sign better; spend time focusing on the things that are important to a business, including the cultural aspects of working together with people in an office every day. I’m really passionate about that. I don’t think that, as an industry, we have necessarily got it right over the years, and I’m looking forward to bringing some of that focus back on those areas.

**What does it mean to ‘sign better’?  
**The longer you do this, the better you become at it. I will never not want to sign and develop artists. The opportunities out there to do that are vast, but it’s an incredibly noisy business, and it’s a noisy world, so you need to make sure that you use all the expertise and tools at your disposal to sign as smartly as you can. It takes a village to develop and grow an artist, certainly to break into stardom. Very rarely is it done by one single member of the team, and therefore, you want to be able to hone those skills and take all that experience from doing this since 15-or-so years, and teach other people.

**Anything that stood out to you on the huge “Circus” tour by Take That?  
**It was a hazy summer dream working alongside that project. I’m a very small cog in a very big team around that project: Chris Dempsey and his team and management, Simon Moran and John Cornwell at SJM, everybody put in a lot of painstaking hours to pull that tour together, and it was incredibly memorable. We all knew how special the first one was, but there’s no guaranteeing that just because it worked once it would work again. But being at the first night in Southampton, which was…as you know, British weather being slightly unreliable, outdoor shows are always a nerve-wracking experience. But it was literally a gift from the gods. We had bright blue skies, the perfect start to that tour: the image of the balloon against that bright blue sky, it was just syndicated across every newspaper that weekend, and it just gave the tour that real zeitgeist moment of the summer, and it became its own thing.

Another memorable show would be the Manchester shows, certainly the one that was streamed. I’ve been to a lot of TT shows, and Manchester is always a special show for them. But this run was special, in particular the one that was streamed. I think it was Howard who was choking up on stage just by the sheer response from the audience. It was like nothing I’ve ever seen, absolutely unbelievable. And we’re lucky enough to have them out again next summer. They’re doing their best sales in Europe they’ve ever done, playing some great headline show and some festivals next summer. It just continues to grow for them. I’m so proud to be part of it, but I’m especially happy for them.

**[2025 Impact Intl. UK/Euro Honoree: James Wright](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/11/07/2025-impact-intl-uk-euro-honoree-james-wright/?_gl=1%2Ajy4m40%2A_ga%2ANDgzNDk3NzAyLjE3OTQyMzIyNjQ.%2A_ga_5B6XRECYRM%2AczE3OTU0MjEzODMkbzE5JGcxJHQxNzk1NDIxMzk4JGo0NSRsMCRoMA..)**

**What are defining qualities agents need to bring to the table in 2026, and have they changed over the years?  
**I do think the roles changed over the years. I’ve not been around when the labels dominated the music business, when it felt like agents could sit and wait for the music to connect before considering a tour. I’ve come from the alternative and indie world, and other niche genres, where you’ve had to do the heavy work yourself, as an agent and as part of the artist’s team. I don’t think you’re an effective agent if you are the conduit for the deal. I think you should have ideas and opinions backed by experience, and I think you should be curious, and you should nurture talent and have great communication with the wider team. Because, as I said, it takes a village to break an artist in a world where we’re closer in so many ways, but also quite far apart. Being so connected while it is at the same so noisy out there, just takes everyone working at 110%.

**How do you come up with a plan for new artists, who just don’t pull these large crowds yet, in today’s landscape where touring is so expensive for various reasons?**  
The prospect of touring as a young or emerging artist is an exciting and a huge part of why a lot of the artists I want to work with are doing this. Most of the time they can’t wait to be out performing live, and it can feel like the most challenging time to be doing that at the moment. I do see the cost of touring, the cost of production, the demise of the grassroots and medium-sized venues, plus the cost of living, and the challenges that come with everything around our economy here, as well as the wider global economy – it can seem like a perfect storm. The reality is though, that I do work with artists that have a clear proposition and a clear identity about what they’re offering. We believe that we’re working with the best in the world, so there is a confidence that we can cut through with our artists’ propositions across the board.

The proof’s in the pudding with the year that we’ve had here. We’re working with the most culturally relevant artists on the planet, and we’ve broken a lot of those from day one. We spend a lot of time talking about what the offering and proposition are. We spend a lot of time talking about data. We have a great IQ team here that really helps us know where the audiences are, so we can make the most educated and smartest decision possible for and with the wider teams around these artists. I keep going back to it, but it is a village, and I think we need to be able to use all the resources that we’ve got. We have a great platform opportunity here at UTA, through the digital team we’ve got, through colleagues like \[talent and literary agency\] Curtis Brown or Roof, our sports agency. We have so much offering to bring artists to market in various different ways, traditional and non-traditional. You have to be smart. You need to think outside the box.

But to go back to your initial point: yeah, it is a bit more expensive, and yes, the venue choices are not there, and yes, you have to be price sensitive around ticketing. But when you make sure you understand your audience using all the expertise we have, we feel confident that we’ve got a proposition that will allow more and more artists to break.

**Apart from the blockbuster show, which other trends are shaping the work you do right now?  
**It’s tied to that. I was speaking to a promoter partner the other week and we were discussing the fact that, for a whole generation of people their first live experience will be an arena or stadium show. My first live experience was certainly not that. It was a greenfield festival, or a pub gig, or a club gig. You’ve now got a whole generation of teenagers and young adults whose experience of seeing a show involves the stadium or arena process. What do you do with that? How do you take that new fan and show them the full treasure box that is the live music scene, and not lose them because you’ve decided to do something different to that experience. There’s a lot we can be doing. They clearly don’t mind spending the money, but do they want to go and be dirty in a field for three days? Or do they want to go home to their bed? What do you do with that fan that clearly is very happy to spend money in our sector, and give them an alternative? And should we? From a new artist’s point of view, you want these people to be discovering new things and not relying on just what is big and hot. There’s work to be done around that.

**What’s next for UTA?  
**We’re not resting on our laurels. We’ve got a lot of great tours happening in ‘27 and ‘28 and even ‘29. We’re ambitious to build out our team. We’re ambitious to be signing future stars. There’ll be lots more to come.
