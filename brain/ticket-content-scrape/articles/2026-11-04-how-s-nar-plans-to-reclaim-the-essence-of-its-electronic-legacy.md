# How Sónar plans to reclaim the ‘essence’ of its electronic legacy

## Summary

After a provocative "Sónar is dead" marketing stunt following mixed reaction to this year's format changes, Barcelona's Sónar festival will return to its traditional city-center daytime location for its 34th edition (June 17-19, 2027), folding its Sónar+D conference into the daytime program. CEO François Jozic says the reversal responds directly to fan and team feedback that "format is key" to the festival's identity.

### Key Points
- Sónar by Day returns to central Barcelona; Sónar by Night keeps its four-stage format at Fira Gran Via.
- The reversal follows this year's relocation of the daytime program, which drew public backlash and a funeral-themed marketing campaign in response.
- 2027 lineup includes Bonobo, Yung Lean, Jessie Ware, Carl Cox, Peggy Gou, and Honey Dijon, among ~100 artists.
- International editions return to Tokyo (March) and Istanbul (April) in 2027.

## Transcript

## How Sónar plans to reclaim the ‘essence’ of its electronic legacy

After mixed feedback on format changes this year, CEO François Jozic is plotting a forward-looking return to form for 2027

![François Jozic](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Francois-Jozic.png)

François Jozic

After declaring the Sónar brand “dead” on social media last week, the Spanish electronic music institution is plotting a forward-looking return to form in 2027, CEO François Jozic tells *IQ*.

The festival will return for its 34th edition from 17-19 June, with the Sónar by Day programme returning to its central Barcelona location after a year away. Sónar by Night will continue with its established four-stage format at the further-out Fira Gran Via site, better suited to the festival’s overnight structure.

Meanwhile, the conference portion, Sónar+D, will fold into the daytime offering at the city-centre setting, bringing artists, researchers, innovators, and institutions together alongside the music.

The format changes mark a notable reversal from this year’s edition, when Sónar by Day was relocated to the evening venue due to a “structural situation” affecting its central Barcelona site, Jozic explains.

Feedback from both the internal team and external attendees was swift, with questions over the format prompting some to take to social media to declare Sónar “dead”.

After collaborating with local authorities, who fast-tracked the return to the city centre for the day, the team seized on the online discourse, devising a funeral-themed marketing campaign to signal a rebirth of sorts.

> “This campaign is provocative, and meant to provoke very passionate reactions”

“We didn’t want people to believe it, but we also wanted people to see that it could be a possibility,” Jozic says. “Part of the reason is that if you lose the right format, if you lose the essence of the festival, then you might eventually lose it.”

As part of the campaign, the team opened a condolence book for audiences to share memories. Thousands contributed, giving feedback that is now helping shape the Superstruct-backed event’s next chapter.

“This campaign is provocative, and meant to provoke very passionate reactions. People not only react, but they also start to think about how they feel and what they have to say about it, a bit more than the typical Instagram comment of a red heart or a vomiting face. It was also a way to receive more constructive feedback.”

The clearest takeaway was that the “format is key” to Sónar. As electronic music is increasingly integrated into more mainstream festival lineups, attendees of the long-running event are looking for a more “boutique” and “curated” experience geared toward discovery and experimentation.

In response, the team is looking to make the daytime portion more intimate than before, when attendance could rise to 30,000 people, and mix established stars with fast-rising names.

“Sónar has always been very good at capturing these new talents. But maybe in the last few years it was not visible enough. The goal in ’27 is to really show and make very visible that this is what we want to do. It’s part of our mission. It’s part of our historical DNA. It’s part of our legacy. But maybe we need to show it better.”

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/11/Crowd-at-Sonar-©-Nerea-Coll--430x269.png.webp)

*Sónar © Nerea Coll*

Roughly 100 artists are expected to perform at the 2027 edition, with the first wave of artists confirmed this week, including Bonobo, Yung Lean, Jessie Ware, Frost Children, Carl Cox, Peggy Gou, Romy, and Honey Dijon.

For Jozic, who took over as CEO in 2025 following the departure of the festival’s [three founders](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/10/sonar-founders-step-away-from-festival/) after three decades at the helm, next year’s edition will serve as a springboard for Sónar’s future evolution. He describes his guiding vision as to “preserve and protect the legacy” while adapting to the genre’s growing presence in popular culture.

“Electronic music has become so mainstream in some aspects. Maybe 20 years ago, Sónar could be the place to show everything about electronic music, and now we can’t because it’s a three-day festival and we don’t have a €50 million budget. We see that all the multi-genre festivals propose a lot of electronic music, so we need to start curating a lot.”

The challenge is to maintain Sónar’s position as a “very relevant voice” in the increasingly crowded electronic landscape, with Jozic hinting at new formats or media in the pipeline for the global brand.

> “What is key is Barcelona is Sónar and Sónar is Barcelona”

Next year will also see the return of two international editions, Tokyo in March as part of NU Festival and Istanbul in April, both of which are smaller and shaped around the “local singularities” of the markets. He points out a tatami \[traditional floor covering\] stage planned for the Japanese edition as an example.

“Only in Tokyo,” he says. “We might be tempted to import the idea in Barcelona, but not the other way around.”

There are also plans to take the industry conference element, Sónar+D, abroad, and position it as the “main protagonist” instead of a programme addition.

“We really want to bring the whole experience that Sónar represents, which is not just music. It’s this point of convergence between science, technology, music, of course, but also culture and digital culture.”

Early ticket sales suggest attendance will rebound in 2027, Jozic says, with the team now tasked with delivering on the renewed expectations surrounding the festival. Ultimately, he sees Sónar’s future prosperity as reliant on its Spanish home.

“What is key is Barcelona is Sónar and Sónar is Barcelona,” Jozic affirms.
