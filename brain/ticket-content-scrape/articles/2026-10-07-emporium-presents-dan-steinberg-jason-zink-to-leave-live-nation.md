# Emporium Presents’ Dan Steinberg, Jason Zink To Leave Live Nation

## Summary

Emporium Presents co-founders Dan Steinberg and Jason Zink are leaving Live Nation at the end of 2026, winding down the promotion company entirely within Live Nation after eight years and 4,000-plus shows as a majority-owned (51%) joint venture. A six-month non-compete follows their departure; Steinberg will continue co-hosting the "Promoter 101" podcast with Luke Pierce.

### Key Points
- Emporium Presents (formerly Square Peg Concerts) will cease to exist within Live Nation after Steinberg and Zink depart at year-end 2026.
- Live Nation acquired a 51% stake in Emporium in 2018; the company promoted 400+ concerts annually across the U.S. and Canada.
- A six-month non-compete clause takes effect after departure; both say they have no confirmed next plans.
- "Promoter 101" podcast continues under Luke Pierce and Steinberg.

### Highlights
- A Live Nation majority-owned promotion joint venture is being wound down entirely as its founders exit — a direct consolidation/exit data point for the company's regional promoter portfolio.
- The departing founders' six-month non-compete is a notable detail for tracking where this promotion capacity and its artist relationships (Luke Bryan, Dolly Parton, Foo Fighters, etc.) resurface afterward.

### Industry Problem
This is a market-structure signal rather than a client/patron pain point: it marks a reduction in Live Nation's network of semi-independent regional promoter JVs, which is relevant context for how concentrated or fragmented the promoter landscape remains.

### Proposed Solution
No product response applies; this is a monitor-only signal. Etix should track where Steinberg and Zink resurface after their non-compete expires in mid-2027, since a new independent promotion venture could become a prospective ticketing client outside the Live Nation ecosystem.

## Transcript

![image](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/image-1.png)

Emporium Presents’ Dan Steinberg and Jason Zink are leaving Live Nation at the end of the year, with the promoters saying they are unsure of what the next chapter looks like but are looking forward to it.

“We are focused on finding homes for all our staff and making sure our shows are taken care of,” Steinberg tells *Pollstar*. After eight years and 4,000 shows with as a joint venture with Live Nation, “We are looking forward to our first break ever,” he says.

Zink adds, “We had a great run with Live Nation and collaborating with so many folks there. Now, I’m looking forward to exploring the world and spending time with my son.”

Emporium Presents joined Live Nation in 2018, with Live Nation acquiring a 51% stake. Emporium promoted 400-plus concerts annually in the U.S. and Canada, including concerts by artists ranging from Luke Bryan, Dolly Parton to Straight No Chaser, Brad Williams, Jason Mraz and Foo Fighters.

The company was previously known as Square Peg Concerts, with offices in the Pacific Northwest and Nashville.

![SteinbergZinkPSnow](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/SteinbergZinkPSnow.jpg)

Dan Steinberg and Jason Zink in 2018.

Following the end of 2026, a six-month non-compete clause will take effect, after which Steinberg says he and Zink have no specific plans but are excited for the next chapter. Emporium Presents will no longer exist at Live Nation, Steinberg says.

Affectionately known as “Steiny” and taking part in multiple Pollstar conferences over the years, Steinberg and Works Entertainment’s Luke Pierce formed and host the popular “Promoter 101” podcast, featuring many major industry figures and interviews with promoters, agents and more. “Promoter 101” will continue to be hosted by Pierce and Steinberg.

At Pollstar Live! 2017, [Steinberg was tapped as host and moderator of a keynote Q&A with Live Nation CEO Michael Rapino,](https://news.pollstar.com/2018/02/19/listen-to-michael-rapinos-pollstar-live-keynote-qa-promoter-101-podcast/) which touched on subjects including secondary ticketing, independent promoters, festival blunders like Fyre Festival and more.
