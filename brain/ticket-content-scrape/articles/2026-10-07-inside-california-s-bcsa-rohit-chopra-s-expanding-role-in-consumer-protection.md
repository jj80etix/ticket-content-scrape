# Inside California’s BCSA: Rohit Chopra’s Expanding Role in Consumer Protection

## Summary

A Troutman Pepper podcast crossover examines California Gov. Gavin Newsom's appointment of former CFPB Director Rohit Chopra to lead the state's newly created Business and Consumer Services Agency (BCSA), overseeing the Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI) alongside the state AG. The panel discusses Chopra's track record of aggressive, systemic enforcement at the CFPB/FTC and what that posture could mean for California's consumer financial services industry, plus speculation about a future federal role.

### Key Points
- Rohit Chopra, former CFPB Director, appointed to lead California's new Business and Consumer Services Agency (BCSA).
- Chopra will oversee DFPI, which has broad supervisory/enforcement powers alongside the California AG.
- Panelists note Chopra's CFPB/FTC history of aggressive, systemic enforcement actions as a likely preview of DFPI's posture.
- Discussion flags the appointment as a potential stepping stone to a future Cabinet-level federal role under a Democratic administration.

### Highlights
- California is consolidating broad consumer-protection/financial-services enforcement authority under a regulator (Chopra) known for aggressive, systemic actions rather than case-by-case enforcement.
- DFPI's authority runs alongside, not instead of, the state AG — meaning ticketing/fee practices in California face two potentially active enforcers rather than one.
- The appointment is framed explicitly as a bellwether for whether federal regulators swing back toward stronger consumer-protection enforcement under a future administration.

### Industry Problem
A primary ticketing platform operating in California should expect state-level consumer-protection and pricing-transparency enforcement (the same junk-fee/all-in-pricing terrain as the FTC's click-to-cancel and ticketing rules) to intensify under a regulator with Chopra's enforcement history, independent of federal posture.

### Proposed Solution
No ticketing-specific action is described in the item; this is a monitor signal. Etix should track DFPI's early enforcement priorities and any California-specific junk-fee or all-in-pricing guidance Chopra's agency issues, since California frequently sets a template other state AGs follow.

In this special crossover episode of [*Regulatory Oversight*](https://www.troutman.com/podcast/regulatory-oversight/) and [*The Consumer Finance Podcast*](https://www.troutman.com/podcast/the-consumer-finance-podcast/), [Chris Willis](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/chris-j-willis/), [Stefanie Jackman](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/stefanie-jackman/), and [Mike Yaghi](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/michael-yaghi/) discuss California Governor Gavin Newsom’s appointment of former CFPB Director Rohit Chopra to lead the state’s newly created Business and Consumer Services Agency (BCSA).

The group discusses Chopra’s oversight of the Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI), his broad authority alongside the California attorney general, and what his track record of aggressive, systemic enforcement at the CFPB and FTC might mean for California’s consumer financial services industry going forward. They also get into DFPI’s wide-reaching supervisory and enforcement powers, what companies should be doing to get ready, and whether this move sets Chopra up for a bigger Cabinet-level role down the road in a future Democratic administration.

The episode wraps up with a bigger-picture look at whether federal regulators could swing hard back toward consumer protection enforcement under a future Democratic president.
