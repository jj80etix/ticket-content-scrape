# Peachtree Entertainment Reportedly Shutting Down Operations Amid Staff Layoffs

## Summary

Nashville-based concert promoter Peachtree Entertainment is reportedly winding down operations and laying off staff, per Decatur, Alabama officials and a Decibel report, raising questions about its touring business and venue/municipal partnerships, including a five-year, $5M Decatur agreement for the Rock the South festival.

### Key Points
- Decatur confirmed the closure and is reviewing its five-year Rock the South agreement; 2027 dates are unconfirmed.
- Follows the recent departure of a key executive and comes after a period of rapid expansion.
- LiveCo, a 2024 investor, says it divested its stake in January 2025 and has no current ownership, governance or operational role.
- Founded 2013 by Bradley Jordan; promotes Rock the South, Rock the Country and other festivals.

### Highlights
- A $5M, five-year Decatur municipal agreement for Rock the South is now in question; no city funds have yet been exchanged for 2027.
- Closure follows a 2024 expansion (CEO Nathan Baugh, touring lead Shane Quick, LiveCo investment) and a recent key-executive departure.

### Industry Problem
A mid-sized regional promoter's rapid-expansion-then-shutdown cycle illustrates financial fragility among independent promoters/event operators — a peer-company pain point with knock-on risk for municipalities and venues that signed long-term festival/venue agreements with them.

### Proposed Solution
The item describes no vendor-side fix; it's a monitor/evolve signal. An independent primary platform should watch for contract and ticketing-continuity exposure when a promoter client shows financial-distress signs (executive departures, rapid unexplained expansion), and track whether affected venues/festivals (e.g., Rock the South) need a new promoter or ticketing partner.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/peachtree-website-screenshot.webp)

Peachtree Entertainment, the Nashville-based concert promoter behind major country music tours and festivals, is reportedly winding down operations and laying off employees, raising questions about the future of its touring business and several recently announced venue partnerships.

Officials in Decatur, Alabama confirmed Friday that Peachtree was closing. That news followed close on the heels of [a Friday report from Dave Brooks at Decibel](https://decibel.news/p/exclusive-peachtree-entertainment-is-shutting-down-staff-laid-off?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), which landed just days after the departure of a key executive and follows a period of rapid expansion for the promoter.

Peachtree has not issued its own public statement detailing the closure, the number of employees affected or the status of its remaining business commitments.

In a statement provided to TicketNews, LiveCo, which participated in Peachtree’s 2024 expansion, clarified that its ownership involvement ended nearly two years ago.

“LiveCo divested its stake in Peachtree Entertainment almost two years ago in January 2025 and has had no ownership interest, governance role, or operational involvement in the company since that time,” a LiveCo spokesperson said.

“Going forward, LiveCo has no role in Peachtree’s operations and is not assuming any of its existing contracts, financial obligations, or commitments.”

The spokesperson said Peachtree operates independently and that LiveCo cannot comment on its staffing, events, business decisions or venue and municipal agreements.

[Decatur officials also said](https://www.waff.com/2026/11/20/rock-souths-future-uncertain-after-peachtree-entertainment-decides-close/) the city was reviewing its five-year agreement with Peachtree involving the Rock the South festival and could not confirm dates for the 2027 event. No city funds had been exchanged for the 2027 contract year, according to the city. The five-year agreement, approved in October 2025, called for $5 million in city financial support over its term, making the closure particularly consequential for Decatur’s economic development plans.

Founded in 2013 by Bradley Jordan, Peachtree announced a major expansion in June 2024 involving executives Nathan Baugh and Shane Quick, alongside LiveCo. Baugh became CEO, overseeing the company’s day-to-day operations, while Quick focused on artist relationships and touring. LiveCo’s involvement subsequently ended with the January 2025 divestiture disclosed in its statement to TicketNews.

The company has promoted concerts and festivals including Rock the South, Rock the Country, Bulls, Bands and Barrels and the Auburn Rodeo. It has also credited its early involvement in helping develop the touring careers of artists including Luke Combs, Morgan Wallen and Luke Bryan.

The reported shutdown represents a sharp reversal from Peachtree’s recent growth. The company added multiple executives and expanded its touring, marketing and talent-buying operations throughout 2025 and 2026.

On Tuesday, Nov. 17, [MusicRow reported that Shane Quick had departed Peachtree Entertainment](https://musicrow.com/2026/11/shane-quick-exits-peachtree-entertainment-exclusive/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). Quick, who founded Rock the South in 2012, issued a statement expressing gratitude to the artists, promoters, employees and industry partners he had worked with. He said he planned to take a break, spend time with his family and consider his next professional steps.

No connection between Quick’s departure and the reported shutdown has been independently established.

The reported wind-down creates uncertainty surrounding several business commitments Peachtree has announced in recent months, particularly its involvement in The Rail, a new 2,200-capacity concert venue scheduled to open in Lexington, Kentucky, in early 2027.

In September, [Peachtree was announced as co-owner and exclusive operator of The Rail](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/peachtree-entertainment-named-co-owner-operator-of-lexingtons-the-rail/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), part of the 43-acre Commons Entertainment District. Under the arrangement, Peachtree was expected to oversee venue operations, marketing, sponsorships and certain programming functions.

Earlier this month, [The Rail announced its initial concert lineup](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/11/lexingtons-the-rail-unveils-initial-lineup-for-new-venue/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), including performances by Lynyrd Skynyrd, Umphrey’s McGee, Niko Moon, Cole Swindell and Billy Currington.

The venue’s [Ticketmaster event listings](https://www.ticketmaster.com/the-rail-tickets-lexington/venue/181262?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) remained active Friday, with concerts scheduled beginning Jan. 16.

It is not yet clear whether Peachtree will retain its ownership interest or operational responsibilities at The Rail, whether another company could assume those obligations, or whether the reported shutdown will affect the venue’s opening plans.

Peachtree also entered a multiyear partnership with Auburn University Athletics in July 2025 to produce and book concerts at Neville Arena and Jordan-Hare Stadium. The arrangement, developed with sports and entertainment agency Elevate, was intended to bring more than five concerts annually to the university’s facilities.

The future of that partnership has not been clarified.

The shutdown also raises questions about the future of Furnace Fest, the Birmingham, Alabama, music festival that operated under Peachtree’s management in 2026. Just four days before news of the company’s closure, [Furnace Fest announced plans to return](https://www.lambgoat.com/news/55911/furnace-fest-announce-dates-for-2027-event/?utm_medium=content&utm_source=chatgpt.com) to Sloss Furnaces on Oct. 9–10, 2027. The festival has not announced whether those plans will proceed following Peachtree’s closure.

Beyond its venue agreements, Peachtree’s [official website continues advertising upcoming performances](https://peachtreeent.com/events/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) featuring artists including John Morgan, Greylan James, Hunter Flynn, Josh Turner and Hudson Westbrook, with dates extending into December.

The continued availability of event listings does not establish whether promotion responsibilities have changed, but there have been no verified announcements that those performances have been canceled as a result of the reported wind-down.

For artists, venues and ticket buyers, the immediate concern is whether scheduled events will proceed as planned and which companies will be responsible for fulfilling existing commitments.

TicketNews has also sought responses from Peachtree Entertainment and The Rail regarding the impact of the shutdown on existing concerts, venue operations and contractual obligations. This story will be updated as additional confirmed information becomes available.
