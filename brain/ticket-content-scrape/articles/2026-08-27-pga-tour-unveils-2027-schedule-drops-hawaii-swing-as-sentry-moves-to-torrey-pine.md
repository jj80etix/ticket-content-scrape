# PGA Tour Unveils 2027 Schedule, Drops Hawaii Swing as Sentry Moves to Torrey Pines

## Summary

The PGA Tour unveiled its 2027 FedExCup schedule, dropping its season-opening Hawaii stop for the first time in over 50 years and reshuffling the West Coast swing, with Sentry Insurance moving its title sponsorship from Farmers Insurance's event to a renamed tournament at Torrey Pines. The Sony Open is also leaving the regular Tour for the Champions circuit, and several other stops (Florida swing, a new Napa event) are shifting ahead of the Tour's larger 2028 competitive overhaul.

### Key Points
- Season opens with The American Express (La Quinta) Jan. 18-24, followed by The Sentry at Torrey Pines and AT&T Pebble Beach Pro-Am — no Hawaii stop for the first time since before 1999.
- Sentry Insurance sponsorship moves from the Farmers Insurance event to Torrey Pines; renamed "The Sentry," playing a unique Wed-Sat schedule to avoid NFL conference championships.
- Sony Open leaves the regular PGA Tour for the Champions circuit rather than disappearing.
- Additional Florida-swing and Napa-tournament changes precede the Tour's planned 2028 overhaul.

The PGA Tour has [unveiled its 2027 FedExCup schedule through the Tour Championship](https://www.pgatour.com/article/news/latest/2026/08/26/pga-tour-announces-2027-schedule-through-tour-championship), setting up a season that will begin without a stop in Hawaii for the first time in more than five decades and feature a reshuffled West Coast swing, a new tournament in Napa and several changes ahead of the Tour’s larger competitive overhaul in 2028.

The 36-tournament schedule will instead open with The American Express in La Quinta, California, during the week of Jan. 18-24. The newly renamed Sentry follows one week later at Torrey Pines in San Diego, before the AT&T Pebble Beach Pro-Am completes a three-event California start to the season.

The Hawaii absence is particularly notable. The Tour had played its season-opening tournament at Kapalua’s Plantation Course on Maui from 1999 through 2025, most recently under The Sentry name. The 2026 edition was canceled amid water and logistical issues, and the PGA Tour subsequently confirmed that it would end its tournament presence on Maui.

The Sony Open, meanwhile, is also leaving the regular PGA Tour schedule after a run stretching back decades at Waialae Country Club in Honolulu. Rather than disappearing entirely, the Hawaii tournament is transitioning to the PGA Tour Champions circuit.

Sentry Insurance has moved its title sponsorship to the Tour’s longstanding event at Torrey Pines, replacing Farmers Insurance. The San Diego tournament will now be called The Sentry and will be played on the North and South courses at Torrey Pines.

The 2027 Sentry will also have an unusual Wednesday-through-Saturday competition schedule, concluding Jan. 30 rather than competing against the NFL conference championship games on Sunday. It is the only scheduled Saturday finish on the PGA Tour calendar.

## PGA Tour Shuffles Florida Swing, Adds Napa Tournament

Several other pieces of the calendar are moving.

The Cadillac Championship at Trump National Doral will shift forward to the week of March 1-7, putting the Signature Event immediately before The Players Championship at TPC Sawgrass. The Arnold Palmer Invitational follows The Players, while the Valspar Championship moves deeper into the spring to May 3-9.

The Tour’s eight Signature Events remain the AT&T Pebble Beach Pro-Am, Genesis Invitational, Cadillac Championship, Arnold Palmer Invitational, RBC Heritage, Truist Championship, Memorial Tournament and Travelers Championship.

A new Sompo Championship will debut July 26-Aug. 1 at Silverado Resort’s North Course in Napa, California. Silverado previously hosted a PGA Tour tournament from 2014 through 2025. Sompo’s agreement extends into 2028, when the company will sponsor an event in the Tour’s new Championship Series, although that tournament is expected to move to a different market.

The regular season will then conclude with the newly named GO by Raymond James at Sedgefield Country Club in Greensboro, North Carolina.

The FedExCup Playoffs begin with the FedEx St. Jude Championship at TPC Southwind in Memphis, followed by a notable return to the New York market when the BMW Championship heads to Liberty National Golf Club in Jersey City. The Tour Championship will again be held at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta.

The four majors will send players to Augusta National for the Masters, the new PGA Frisco venue in Texas for the PGA Championship, Pebble Beach for the U.S. Open and the Old Course at St Andrews for The Open Championship.

That means Pebble Beach will host two of the year’s highest-profile events, with the AT&T Pebble Beach Pro-Am in February followed by the U.S. Open in June.

## Final Season Before PGA Tour’s 2028 Overhaul

The schedule also represents something of a transitional year for the Tour.

Beginning in 2028, the PGA Tour plans to split its competitive structure into a PGA Tour Championship Series and Challenger Series, with the Championship Series featuring approximately two dozen event weeks and a greater emphasis on having top players compete against one another more consistently.

The postseason will also be radically different. A Championship Series Finale will first determine the season-long points winner and narrow the field to 32 players. Those 32 will then advance to a separate two-week Tour Championship played in a match-play format at two different venues.

The 2027 FedExCup Fall schedule has not yet been announced, meaning additional events after the Tour Championship will be added later.
