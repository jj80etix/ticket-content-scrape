# Five moments that shaped AEG Presents’ first quarter-century

## Summary

IQ Magazine profiles five deals that shaped AEG Presents' rise over 25 years, from its 2001 founding and 2011 launch of AXS ticketing to recent international partnerships in Japan, Benelux, and Latin America, as told by longtime executives including Gongaware, Meglen, and Schaefer. The piece credits AEG's partnership-over-acquisition strategy and selective deal-making as the key to its growth into the industry's number-two promoter.

### Key Points
- AEG Live founded 2001 from the Concerts West and Goldenvoice acquisitions; AXS ticketing JV launched 2011.
- Growth strategy relies on JVs/partnerships with independent promoters (Bowery Presents, Marshall Arts, CMN, MCT Agentur, Creativeman, Greenhouse Talent) rather than outright acquisition.
- Current focus: mid-size (4,000-5,000 cap) venues bridging clubs and arenas, and expansion into Asia/Latin America.
- Leadership emphasizes selectivity over volume as AEG's defining competitive philosophy.

## Transcript

## Five moments that shaped AEG Presents’ first quarter-century

Since the turn of the millennium, AEG has transformed into a global powerhouse. Here, key players chart the trajectory behind the world-renowned operation

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/11/Credit_-Ben-Bentley-scaled.jpg.webp)

AEG Presents-promoted Elton John concert at Dodger Stadium © Ben Bentley

Since it formed at the start of the new millennium, AEG Presents has steadily built a presence across the globe.

But it’s taken a different approach to other global businesses. Rather than acquisitions, it’s opted from the outset to work in partnerships or through JVs with the best independent promoters in key markets and territories.

But how did a company go from a standing start a quarter of a century ago to become the number two player in the live music business? Therein lies a story of ambition, prescience, and resilience, all underscored by an unwavering belief in the talent it chooses to work with. Here are five moments that shaped the company’s first quarter-century.

You can read *IQ’s* full deep-dive on AEG Presents at 25 [here](https://www.iqmagazine.com/magazine/aeg-presents-at-25/).

> “There were maybe six of us, and then Goldenvoice came in”

**2001 – AEG Live emerges**

AEG (Anschutz Entertainment Group) itself was founded in 1994 by Phil Anschutz, starting out by owning sports teams and venues in the US. It branched out into concert promotion with the official founding in late 2001 of AEG Live, with Brandon “Randy” Phillips as its first chief executive.

The entity actually had a more circuitous history before this, coming about through the acquisition of the long-established Concerts West in 2000, quickly followed by the acquisition of LA-based Goldenvoice in 2001, giving it a strong foothold in the alternative rock space, and, most significantly, Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival.

John Meglen, president and co-CEO of Concerts West, says the support of Irving Azoff, giving them Don Henley and Eagles shows in the early days, helped Concerts West get off the ground. This meant it was swiftly on the radar of AEG, which acquired the majority of the company two years after its launch.

“Phil came to us with an idea, and what he said was, ‘This is your company. You do this or don’t do this. This is just an idea I have,’” recalls Paul Gongaware, co-CEO of Concerts West. “It was an amazing time. They financed us, and they got out of the way.”

They quickly found some cheap office space in LA. “There were maybe six of us, and then Goldenvoice came in,” recalls Meglen of the early days.

Phillips helped the company diversify into the burgeoning pop scene in the early 2000s. “Randy was responsible, really, for us getting Britney Spears,” he says. “That led to Justin Timberlake, Christina Aguilera, and Usher. So we had that whole vein.” Acts like Black Eyed Peas, Mariah Carey, and Rod Stewart followed.

The next major play by the company was when it took a 49% stake in Barrie Marshall’s Marshall Arts in 2006, giving the company a strong presence in the UK, which was to serve as a bridgehead into mainland Europe.

> “It’s definitely not just gobbling up assets”

**2011 – Capturing ticketing  
**The company also moved into the ticketing space in 2011 with the launch of AXS in late 2011, the result of a JV between AEG and ticketing startup Outbox Enterprises (AEG eventually became the sole owner of AXS in 2019 when it bought Outbox’s remaining shares).

Trell explains why this model is so effective: “We put a very high premium on the right partners, on fit, and on culture. It’s definitely not just gobbling up assets. It’s trying to do smart deals, but also the right deals. And when I say right, I mean the right people, people who fit here culturally.”

Jay Marciano, who had been heading the company’s European operations since 2011, took over as chairman in 2013. One of the key early deals under Marciano’s leadership was taking a majority stake (estimated at $40m at the time) in New York-based The Bowery Presents in late 2016.

The New York Times reported on the deal that it would give AEG “a powerful foothold on the East Coast,”with The Bowery Presents – led by co-presidents Jim Glancy and John Moore – having grown significantly since 2004, branching out from New York into Portland in 2006 and Boston in 2010. As reported by *IQ*, Moore and Glancy [stepped aside from their roles at The Bowery Presents in late September](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/09/aeg-owned-bowery-presents-co-presidents-to-depart/).

“We had always been self-funded and had some different partnerships in different markets,”says Glancy of the background to the AEG deal.

“We, like many promoters, would go to a ticketing company and get the big advance to help fund some of what we were doing. Not necessarily a good business model! After a decade of doing this, we were ready to look at a different business model. Part of that was for growth. What more could we be doing if we were properly funded? And some of it was to take the pressure off what we were doing.”

Moore adds AEG helped the company move beyond club and theatre shows, enabling it to take its acts, notably The Strokes, LCD Soundsystem, and Kings Of Leon, to the arena level. “AEG has always been really focused on that artist development, those small venues growing into the big venues,” he says.

> “We get to be selective. Fortunately, that’s the position we’re in.”

**2020s – Focussing on the mid-size segment**  
Brent Fedrizzi is president of North American Regional Offices at AEG Presents, having moved up in 2024 from looking after the Denver operation of the company. The role involves ensuring the dozen US regional offices link together where necessary, as well as overseeing around 110 venues in the US.

“We’re developing new markets, and we’re finding markets that are underserved, or where we think there’s a lane for us to be in,” he explains.

A new emphasis is on opening venues in the 4,000-cap and 5,000-cap bracket in different cities to properly bridge between clubs and arenas, with Fedrizzi citing The Mission in Denver (opened in 2019) and The Pinnacle in Nashville (opened in 2025) as examples to follow.

“There are a lot of artists who live in that capacity,” he says. “They may never get to the arena level. Or maybe they’re on the way down. But there are a lot of artists who can play in that capacity, and we’re really having a lot of success \[there\].

Currently, Austin, Texas has a new room \[the 4,000-cap United Heritage Hall\] coming online in late February 2027 that we’re under construction on. Portland is another that was underserved. Every one of them is curated to the community.”

Fedrizzi believes the real power of AEG, across venues and all entertainment types, lies in only doing the deals and tours it feels are right or that have the greatest growth potential. “We don’t have to do every deal,” he says. “We get to be selective. Fortunately, that’s the position we’re in.”

> “I only like to promote what I want to do, not what I must”

**2024 – Deepening partnerships  
**In March 2024, AEG expanded again, this time through a partnership with Cárdenas Marketing Network (CMN), the Chicago-based Latin music promoter, helping the company to build its presence in one of the truly booming genres globally. CMN’s acts include heavyweights of Latin music such as Marc Anthony, Bad Bunny, J Balvin, Daddy Yankee, Don Omar, Chayanne, Ana Gabriel, and Nicky Jam.

The company bookended that year with another major deal, taking a partnership stake in German promoter MCT Agentur in December 2024.“They bring me their global touring \[roster\], which is run by Rich Schaefer in New York,” says Scumeck Sabottka, MCT Agentur’s founder. “I would have never had a part of that \[otherwise\].”

He adds: “I’m not Germany’s biggest promoter because my philosophy has always been that I only like to promote what I want to do, not what I must. I’m quite picky in working on stuff that maybe doesn’t \[always\] make any money. Because I’m not that young anymore, I don’t find the ins to the new, younger agents. AEG brings that. It’s the big factory, and I’m the supply chain person in Germany. That feels quite good.”

On their growing relationship, and how Concerts West have been important mentors within AEG, Sabottka says, “Gongaware and Meglen are really stroppy gits. I love them. They are crazy motherfuckers, but they’re promoters through and through.”

Among AEG’s recent deals is its strategic partnership with [Creativeman Productions in](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/aeg-and-japans-creativeman-strike-major-partnership/) [Japan,](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/aeg-and-japans-creativeman-strike-major-partnership/) announced in August this year. Founded in 1990, Creativeman operates the Summer Sonic festival as well as Sonicmania, Punkspring, Greenroom Festival, Loud Park, Super Sonic, and Springroove.

And in September, it announced a [partnership with Benelux’s Greenhouse Talent](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/09/aeg-invests-in-greenhouse-talent-to-formalise-longstanding-partnership/) which marks the company’s first official presence in the region, based on its longstanding relationship with Greenhouse founder Pascal Van De Velde.

> “We were trying to sign too many acts; we weren’t narrowing our scope enough”

**The future – Eyes on developing Asian markets  
**Rich Schaefer, president of global touring, picks up on this selectivity point, saying when he took over in his new role, he trimmed back the company’s roster so they could focus on, and over-deliver on, a more focused range of acts.

“I felt when I took over that we were trying to sign too many acts, and I didn’t think we were narrowing our scope enough,” he reveals. “I felt like if we had signed the acts that we were offering on that, we wouldn’t be able to execute at the level that I wanted.”

He adds that the company, when it started, had also been “a little bit North America heavy” in terms of roster, so over the past four or five years, “we definitely started to shift our focus to global.”

He points specifically to Simon Jones, SVP of global touring in AEG’s London office, Frontier in Australia, and CMN in Latin music as being key drivers in that diversification, and he has high hopes for Japan and Asia.

“We’ve obviously done a bunch of stuff in China and with our office in Singapore, too. Asia and Latin America are probably our biggest targets as far as where we want to grow and how we want to do more there.”

Simon Jones, SVP of Global Touring, International at AEG Presents, adds: “We have strong boots on the ground in South Korea. We’ve just opened up an office there with Jonathan Lee, who has a very strong presence with all of the major management companies there. That’s a large export country for music, and we utilise those strong relationships out of that country together with our colleagues in America to expand those artists’ touring ambitions globally.”

Quality over quantity is the guiding principle AEG has grown significantly in the past quarter of a century, but at its own pace. It has done so by identifying a region, a market, or a genre and investing in it, or partnering with established and respected names. This ensures it gets things right, rather than expanding frenetically for the sake of hitting accelerating growth targets, where volume trumps quality.

For Schaefer, what makes AEG different and distinct can be boiled down to two key policies. “We’re not in the volume business, and we’re able to really focus on the artists that we work with,” he says. “And we have the best people. I think the people are really what drives it. We really try to have a ‘no assholes’ rule at our company.”
