# Raskin Probes Whether Trump Ties Shielded FIFA’s World Cup Ticketing Practices

## Summary

House Judiciary ranking member Rep. Jamie Raskin sent FIFA a formal document request (deadline Aug. 9) demanding records of FIFA's communications with the Trump administration and DOJ regarding World Cup ticketing complaints, dynamic pricing, and third-party sellers, alongside anything of value FIFA gave Trump or his associates. The letter is an oversight request, not a subpoena — Raskin can't compel compliance without Republican support — and lands alongside ongoing state AG investigations (NY, NJ, CA, TX) and a proposed class action into FIFA's category-map pricing and phased inventory releases.

### Key Points
- Raskin contrasts FIFA's bid-book projected max price of $1,550 for a final ticket against premium inventory FIFA later sold for nearly $33,000.
- New York and New Jersey subpoenaed FIFA in May over MetLife Stadium ticketing; Texas AG Paxton opened a separate probe; a Frankfurt court already ordered FIFA to halt several sales practices for German consumers.
- Central consumer-protection allegation: FIFA sold "Category 1" tickets without disclosing that premium midfield sections were pre-reserved for hospitality/corporate partners, then adjusted category boundaries after purchase.
- Raskin's inquiry explicitly links FIFA's ticketing conduct to a broader pattern — the same forum (with Sen. Blumenthal) previously scrutinized the DOJ-Live Nation settlement.
- FIFA had not responded to the letter as of publication; Infantino's public statement addressed neither the document request nor the state investigations.

### Highlights
- Aug. 9, 2026 deadline for FIFA's voluntary response — a concrete date worth tracking for regulatory escalation.
- Multi-jurisdictional pattern: state AGs, a foreign court, and now a congressional oversight request are all converging on the same category-map/dynamic-pricing disclosure practices in the same window as the FTC's Elite Events BOTS settlement.

### Industry Problem
Regulators and legislators across multiple venues are converging on the same theme — that variable/dynamic pricing and category-based seat allocation are being used in ways buyers can't verify at purchase time, echoing the same all-in-pricing/disclosure scrutiny driving Q9's state patchwork.

### Proposed Solution
No solution proposed in the article itself; for a primary platform the actionable posture is Monitor — track whether FIFA's category-map disclosure failures harden into new pricing-transparency requirements (seat-visibility guarantees, category-change disclosure) that could extend beyond junk-fee rules to seat-allocation representations generally.

**House Judiciary Committee ranking member seeks FIFA’s communications with federal officials about consumer complaints, pricing and third-party sellers as part of a broader inquiry into the organization’s relationship with President Donald Trump.**

A senior House Democrat is investigating whether FIFA’s close relationship with President Donald Trump helped protect the organization from federal scrutiny while World Cup buyers faced soaring prices, opaque inventory releases and disputed ticketing practices.

Rep. Jamie Raskin, the ranking member of the House Judiciary Committee, sent FIFA President Gianni Infantino a [five-page letter Sunday](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-judiciary.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/2026-07-26-raskin-to-infantino-fifa-re-corruption.pdf) demanding records concerning the organization’s contacts with the Trump administration, Department of Justice and other federal agencies.

The request specifically seeks FIFA’s communications with the U.S. government about consumer complaints, variable pricing, third-party ticket sellers and World Cup ticket prices. Raskin also asked Infantino to appear for a transcribed interview and gave FIFA until 5 p.m. on Aug. 9 to produce the requested information and contact Democratic committee staff to schedule the interview.

## Raskin Seeks Ticketing and Government Communications

Raskin asked FIFA to provide all communications with the U.S. government concerning its ticketing practices, including discussions of consumer complaints, pricing mechanisms, third-party sellers and ticket prices.

A separate request seeks FIFA communications since 2024 with any U.S. official or presidential-transition representative about matters pending before the DOJ or any other federal department or agency.

The letter also demands a list of anything of value FIFA provided to Trump, members of his administration, their families or associated entities such as the Trump Organization. That includes payments, gifts, prizes, awards and public appearances.

Raskin is seeking related records concerning the newly created FIFA Peace Prize awarded to Trump in December and FIFA’s lease of office space in Trump Tower. He also requested visitor logs for FIFA’s Miami and New York offices and instructed the organization to preserve messages exchanged through Signal, Telegram, WhatsApp, X, Truth Social and other platforms that allow communications to disappear.

The inquiry joins two issues that had previously developed on largely separate tracks: FIFA’s political relationship with Trump and the extensive consumer backlash surrounding the organization’s control of the 2026 World Cup ticket market.

Raskin pointed to FIFA’s use of dynamic pricing, the timing of its inventory releases and its ability to change seat assignments after purchase. He also contrasted the North American bid book’s projected maximum price of $1,550 for a final ticket with premium inventory that FIFA later offered for nearly $33,000 per seat.

FIFA controlled primary inventory, release timing, pricing and its official resale platform throughout the tournament. That structure allowed the organization to influence both the apparent availability of tickets and the prices consumers encountered as they moved through the sales process — a system TicketNews previously examined in its coverage of FIFA’s [engineered World Cup ticket market](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/06/world-cup-finally-kicks-off-but-fifas-engineered-ticket-market-is-still-under-scrutiny/).

FIFA continued releasing substantial blocks of inventory shortly before matches, even after tickets had appeared unavailable or prohibitively expensive. Those releases contributed to sharp late price declines for several knockout matches and raised questions about whether buyers had been responding to genuine scarcity or only the portion of supply FIFA had chosen to make visible at the time. TicketNews documented that dynamic as [semifinal and final-round resale prices declined despite heavyweight matchups](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/fifas-world-cup-ticket-scarcity-strategy-fuels-falling-prices-for-final-matches/).

## State Investigations and Lawsuits Outlast the Tournament

Raskin’s demand comes as FIFA faces continuing investigations from state attorneys general over many of the same practices cited in his letter.

New York and New Jersey subpoenaed FIFA in May for information concerning ticket sales and representations made to buyers attending matches at MetLife Stadium. New Jersey Attorney General Jennifer Davenport confirmed after the World Cup final that [the investigation will continue despite the tournament’s conclusion](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/fifa-ticketing-investigation-will-continue-after-world-cup-new-jersey-ag-says/).

Investigators are examining whether FIFA’s category maps, phased inventory releases and variable-pricing system misled consumers or contributed to inflated prices. California and Texas have opened separate reviews involving similar complaints.

Particular attention has centered on FIFA’s broad seating categories. Many early buyers purchased a category without receiving a specific section, row or seat. FIFA later added more expensive “Front Categories,” while some Category 1 buyers were assigned seats in corners, behind goals or in areas that earlier maps appeared to place within lower-priced categories.

FIFA’s U.S. ticketing subsidiary is now [facing a proposed class action over those category maps](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/fifa-hit-with-class-action-alleging-world-cup-category-ticket-maps-misled-buyers/?utm_source=chatgpt.com). The complaint alleges that buyers paid Category 1 prices believing they had a chance to receive premium midfield seats that had already been reserved for corporate partners, VIPs and hospitality customers.

The lawsuit’s central argument is not merely that customers received disappointing seats. It alleges that FIFA misrepresented the pool of inventory from which their assignments would be selected. FIFA’s terms gave it broad discretion to adjust category boundaries and seat assignments, but regulators and plaintiffs are examining what an ordinary consumer would reasonably have understood from the maps shown during the purchase process.

FIFA’s ticketing operation has also faced scrutiny outside the United States.

A court in Frankfurt issued a preliminary injunction [ordering FIFA to halt several ticket-sales practices for German consumers](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/german-court-orders-fifa-to-halt-deceptive-world-cup-ticketing-practices/). The order addressed advertised prices that were unavailable once buyers entered the purchase flow, countdown timers and the failure to identify commercial sellers operating through FIFA’s resale platform.

In Canada, Consumer Protection BC entered into an enforcement undertaking with FIFA’s Canadian subsidiary after alleging that the company failed to comply with provincial ticket-disclosure requirements. Ticketmaster Canada and Vivid Seats were subject to separate undertakings in the same review.

## Inquiry Also Targets DOJ’s Retreat From FIFA Prosecutions

Raskin placed the World Cup ticketing controversy within the history of federal corruption prosecutions involving FIFA officials and sports-marketing companies.

The DOJ’s FIFA investigation began in 2015 and resulted in charges against more than two dozen officials and executives accused of participating in bribery, wire-fraud and money-laundering schemes involving more than $200 million.

Raskin focused on the administration’s subsequent handling of a case involving sports-marketing company Full Play Group and former Fox executive Hernan Lopez. An appeals court upheld convictions in 2025, but the Trump administration later supported vacating those convictions and dismissing related indictments.

The DOJ said continuing the litigation was not an administration priority and that resources were needed elsewhere. Raskin rejected that explanation and alleged that the decision should be examined alongside FIFA’s efforts to build a close relationship with Trump.

That older corruption prosecution is separate from the current World Cup ticketing investigations. Raskin’s theory is that the administration’s retreat from the criminal case and the absence of federal consumer enforcement may reflect the same politically influenced relationship.

The FIFA inquiry also extends a line of ticketing and antitrust scrutiny Raskin has pursued elsewhere. In May, he and Sen. Richard Blumenthal convened a congressional forum examining the Trump DOJ’s settlement of its antitrust claims against Live Nation and Ticketmaster.

Participants at that forum [alleged that political influence contributed to a weak Live Nation settlement](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/lawmakers-witnesses-dissect-corrupt-live-nation-settlement-press-for-breakup-after-ticketmaster-verdict/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and pressed for structural remedies after a jury found the company liable in the state-led portion of the case.

## Raskin Cannot Compel Infantino Without Republican Support

Raskin’s letter is an oversight request, not a committee subpoena.

Because Republicans control the House and the Judiciary Committee, Raskin cannot independently compel FIFA to produce documents or require Infantino to testify. Doing so would require support from Chairman Jim Jordan or other Republicans with authority to issue or enforce a subpoena.

The Aug. 9 deadline will therefore initially test whether FIFA is willing to cooperate voluntarily.

FIFA had not publicly responded to Raskin’s letter as of Tuesday. The Associated Press and The Guardian reported that the organization did not immediately answer their requests for comment.

In a separate statement published Sunday, Infantino accused World Cup critics of “spreading hate and false rumors” and portrayed the tournament as an overwhelming success. He said seven million people attended matches across the three host countries and praised FIFA for delivering a safe and unifying event.

That statement did not address Raskin’s document requests, his allegations about federal enforcement or the continuing state investigations into FIFA’s ticketing conduct.

The World Cup is over, but scrutiny of the market FIFA created around it continues to expand. State regulators, private plaintiffs and foreign courts are already examining what FIFA charged, what inventory it disclosed and what consumers were told.

Raskin’s inquiry raises a broader question: whether federal regulators remained absent because they found no basis to act, or because FIFA’s political relationships discouraged them from looking.
