# Post Malone Shelves Asia and Australia Tour Months After Cutting U.S. Stadium Dates

## Summary

Post Malone has postponed the Asia and Australia legs of his "Big Ass World Tour," shelving stadium dates that were set to begin in Hong Kong in September. It's the second major disruption to his 2026 touring schedule after he cut six US stadium dates earlier in the year.

### Key Points
- Eight-date Asia run (Hong Kong, Kaohsiung, Bangkok, Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Manila, Seoul, Yokohama) postponed with no reason given; all tickets refunded.
- Hong Kong's Kai Tak Stadium show (50,000 capacity, tickets HK$699–HK$3,199) among those canceled; fans reported sunk travel costs.
- Australian stadium dates also affected.
- No replacement dates announced; original tickets won't transfer to future dates.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Post-Malone-World-Tour.webp)

Post Malone has postponed upcoming legs of his “Big Ass World Tour” across Asia and Australia, shelving a run of stadium dates that was set to begin in Hong Kong in September and continue through late October.

The latest change comes only months after Malone cut six stadium dates from the opening stretch of his 2026 U.S. tour with Jelly Roll, marking the second significant shakeup of his touring schedule this year.

Live Nation Asia currently lists Malone’s stadium tour as “postponed.” The [previously announced eight-date Asian run](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/04/post-malone-announces-asia-tour-dates-with-don-toliver/) was scheduled to begin September 16 at Kai Tak Stadium in Hong Kong before visiting Kaohsiung, Bangkok, Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Manila, Seoul and Yokohama. Don Toliver had been announced as special guest for the run.

No reason for the postponements has been announced.

Live Nation H.I.P., which was promoting Malone’s October 6 show at K-Arena Yokohama in Japan, said the concert was being postponed as part of the wider Asia tour delay. Replacement dates have not been set, and all ticket holders are being refunded. The promoter said tickets purchased for the original show will not be valid for any eventual replacement date.

In Hong Kong, Live Nation similarly announced a full refund for the September 16 show without providing a reason for the change. The South China Morning Post reported complaints from fans who had already arranged flights, hotels and time away from work for the concert. Tickets for the 50,000-seat Kai Tak Stadium show had originally ranged from HK$699 to HK$3,199.

The disruption extends beyond Asia. Malone’s planned [Australian stadium shows](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/post-malone-to-bring-stadium-tour-to-australia-and-new-zealand/) in Melbourne, Brisbane and Sydney have also been postponed, while his October 21 concert at Go Media Stadium in Auckland, New Zealand, is also off the schedule.

Malone has additionally withdrawn as a headliner from all three editions of Australia’s Strummingbird festival in Ballarat, Newcastle and the Sunshine Coast. Organizers have added Tyler Childers, Sam Barber and Australian singer-songwriter James Johnston following Malone’s departure.

The international pullback follows a similar disruption to Malone’s North American schedule earlier this year.

In May, Malone pushed back the start of “The Big Ass Stadium Tour Part 2,” which he was scheduled to headline alongside Jelly Roll. Six stadium shows on the original opening stretch — El Paso, Waco, Baton Rouge, Birmingham, Tampa and Oxford — were removed from the initial schedule, with the tour instead beginning June 9 in Charlotte.

Malone said at the time that he needed additional time to finish new music, telling fans that the tour was not ready to begin on its original timetable.

The cancellations nevertheless arrived amid reports of softer ticket sales for some of the stadium dates. TicketNews noted at the time that large amounts of unsold inventory were visible for certain shows, [part of a wider wave of tours being reduced or canceled during the spring](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/the-concert-industry-priced-high-tours-falling-apart/?utm_source=chatgpt.com). Malone did not attribute the U.S. changes to ticket demand, however, and there has likewise been no indication from Malone or Live Nation that ticket sales prompted the newly announced international postponements.

For now, promoters continue to characterize the Asia and Australia dates as postponed rather than canceled. With replacement dates yet to be announced and refunds being issued for affected headline shows, fans will need to purchase new tickets if and when at least some of those concerts are rescheduled.
