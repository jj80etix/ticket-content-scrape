# Canada’s live orgs applaud government’s $67m pledge

## Summary

Canada's live-music trade bodies welcomed a CA$67 million pledge to the Canada Music Fund, part of a CA$600m+ federal package supporting the country's creative economy, including new artist tax credits and infrastructure deductions.

### Key Points
- CA$67m added to Canada Music Fund, split across FACTOR, Musicaction, Starmaker Fund, Indigenous Music Office and RadioStar
- New Canada Creative Credit offers artists tax relief up to CA$45,000
- New Productivity Mega Deduction supports infrastructure investment (sound, lighting, staging equipment)
- CA$15m earmarked for the Canada Arts Presentation Fund (festivals/promoters) and CA$10m for community arts engagement

## Canada’s live orgs applaud government’s $67m pledge

CIMA's CEO says the variety of funding and tax incentives is "a clear indication that the government has listened to the music sector"

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Canada_flags.jpg)

Canada’s live industry has welcomed the government’s CA$67 million (€42.3m) pledge to support the Canada Music Fund as part of a more than $600m (€378m) package in support of the country’s creative economy and cultural sovereignty.

The Minister of Canadian Identity and Culture, Marc Miller, confirmed the funding boost for the initiative yesterday (9 November), which is designed to help domestic artists and industry professionals promote their music and expand their audience both at home and abroad.

He says that the “crucial support” will provide “much-needed stability so that our country’s artists, storytellers, creative entrepreneurs and cultural organisations can continue creating Canadian content that reflects who we are”.

The lump sum will be divided up between five organisations, with FACTOR (Foundation Assisting Canadian Talent on Recordings) receiving $31.5m (€20m) and Quebec’s Musicaction to gain $21m (€13m). The remaining amount will be split between the Canadian Starmaker Fund ($5.7m/€3.6m), the Indigenous Music Office ($5m/€3.1m), and the RadioStar fund ($3.8m/€2.4m).

Both the Canadian Live Music Association (CLMA) and Canadian Independent Music Association (CIMA) have stated support for the effort, with the latter’s CEO, Andrew Cash, saying the funding is “a clear indication that the government has listened to the music sector”. CLMA says it hopes that the fund will be made permanent. It was renewed through 2028 last year.

Several live music endowments were confirmed in the Carney government’s 2026-27 federal budget on Thursday (5 November), including artistic tax incentives, funding initiatives, and long-term investments.

> The new Canada Creative Credit will offer tax relief to “foster creativity, artistic production and cultural entrepreneurship”

The new budget also confirms the government’s previously announced planned investment of $600m (€378m) into the country’s audio/audiovisual sector, primarily poised to support the recorded industry.

One of the most notable is the new Canada Creative Credit, described as tax relief for Canadian artists to “foster creativity, artistic production and cultural entrepreneurship”. Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed domestic acts can benefit from an exception up to $45,000 (€28,400).

There is also a new permanent Productivity Mega Deduction tax incentive, which supports infrastructure investments, including cameras, lighting, sound equipment, projection, and stage infrastructure.

Additionally, $15m (€9.5m) has been earmarked for the Canada Arts Presentation Fund, providing financial assistance to festivals or series promoters, and $10m (€6.3m) for the Building Communities through Arts and Heritage programme to increase local engagement.

The Canada Council for the Arts is also set to receive $20m (€12.6m) over three years to support its domestic efforts.
