# Young Miko Shines On ‘Late Checkout’ (Live Review)

## Summary

A live review of Young Miko's sold-out Oct. 17 show at Inglewood's Intuit Dome, the third stop of her 12-arena "Late Checkout Tour." The review praises her stage presence and minimalist, hotel-themed production design.

### Key Points
- Sold-out Intuit Dome show, 31-song setlist, third of 12 U.S. arena dates.
- Production centers on a large screen and rotating set pieces rather than elaborate staging.
- Framed as evidence of Young Miko's rising star status in reggaeton.

## Transcript

![DSC01835](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/DSC01835.jpeg)

Young Miko performs at the Intuit Dome in Inglewood, California, Oct. 17, 2026 (photo by Joshua Rivera)

Young Miko is, simply, a star.

It may seem hacky and trite to put it so bluntly, but any other attempts to saddle the 28-year-old Puerto Rican reggaeton artist with adjectives underestimate her. “She’s a star” and a shrug feels like a cop-out but it has the advantage of being true.

On Oct. 17, Young Miko sold out Inglewood, California’s Intuit Dome on the third stop of 12 U.S. arenas as part of her “Late Checkout Tour.” She ripped through 31 songs in all-gas set that felt like a 100-minute flirtation.

Miko is charismatic without being glib or manipulative. She is confident without arrogance. She is humble without being self-effacing. And she is a captivating performer who still knows it’s the songs that matter and not the glitz.

Indeed her production is similarly balanced. There’s little to it beyond a massive screen — pre-show it displays Miko’s scorpion symbol which fills in as curtain time gets closer, building anticipation in lieu of an opening act — and the sets, when they are rotated on, evoke the hotel theme of the tour. She has a small group of dancers who often serve as the audience surrogate for Miko’s seduction (although during “Sin Pausa,” she brings an audience member to the stage to sit on a chair as Miko gets very flirty indeed).

It is an arena-level production — minimalist but modern — that doesn’t lose the big attraction (Miko herself) inside myriad distractions. It emphasizes the connection to her audience. Miko is particularly popular among young queer women, notable as reggaeton has not always been a genre that’s been particularly welcoming to LGBTQ people (or women, for that matter).

But Miko preserves the swagger of the genre while balancing it with her inherent sweetness in a way that makes her feel more pop star than genre star. There’s an apt comparison to Billie Eilish — Miko opened dates for her on “Hit Me Hard And Soft” — in that she’s able to hold all of these things at once.

She’s already received the imprimatur of reggaeton’s biggest stars. She opened for Karol G and has singles with Bad Bunny (she was included in Benito’s Super Bowl Halftime show performance), Feid, Cazzu and others; much of the back half of her set is devoted to those songs. She’s also certifiably crossing over. The Eilish support dates brought her new admirers — there was a particularly entertaining genre of semi-viral online photography of Eilish fans experiencing Young Miko for the first time — and Gap saw fit to make her the first Latina lesbian to be the face of an ad campaign. Her feature on a remix of Katseye’s “Gabriella” took the girl group’s best song in an even-better direction.

But most importantly, she has the goods live, with energy that somehow doesn’t wane even at such a torrid pace and setlist construction that allows the audience to be on the journey without ever being jarred. Favorites “Meiomi,” “Likey Likey” and “No debo” come early and in a hat trick that builds a frenzy she’s able to maintain even through deeper and newer songs mid-set.

Miko’s been on the festival circuit, giving her valuable experience playing to larger crowds who may or may not be familiar with her, but the joy of “Late Checkout” is she’s able to deliver a dynamite show to an audience hungry for her.

“I’ve been having some beautiful months of my life, where I’ve been traveling the world. This year, I’ve been getting to know new audiences and I’ve also been seeing some beautiful audiences that have supported me for a long time,” she said during the show, in Spanish, “Today I have more people than ever before and I can’t thank you enough for the support you’ve given me.”

In the parlance of her Gen Z fans, “Late Checkout” feels at times like a bit of a risky text. But it’s really a love letter from a star and the reply from her fans is full-throated devotion.
