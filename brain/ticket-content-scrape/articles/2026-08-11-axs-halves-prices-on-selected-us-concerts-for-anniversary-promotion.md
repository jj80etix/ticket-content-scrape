# AXS halves prices on selected US concerts for anniversary promotion

## Summary

AXS has launched a 50%-off flash sale, the "AXS Birthday Bash," on selected US concert tickets to mark the company's 15th anniversary, running from August 10-21, 2026. The discount applies to face value only (fees and taxes excluded) on a first-come, first-served basis at select theatre, amphitheatre, and sports venues, including The Bomb Factory (Dallas), Miami Beach Bandshell, Pier 17 (New York), and Crypto.com Arena (LA). The promotion follows AXS's early-June wave of new and renewed partnerships with independent venues such as the Troubadour, the Marathon Live portfolio, and First Avenue.

### Key Points

- AXS is discounting selected US concert tickets 50% off face value (fees/taxes excluded) from Aug. 10-21, 2026, to mark its 15th anniversary.
- The offer covers shows occurring after the promotion window closes, not just events within the sale dates.
- Qualifying venues include The Bomb Factory (Dallas), Miami Beach Bandshell, Pier 17 (NYC), and Crypto.com Arena (LA); artists include Muna, Vince Staples, Derek Hough, Nick Shoulders, and Zac Brown Band.
- It follows AXS's early-June announcement of new/renewed partnerships with eight independent venues, including the Troubadour, the seven-venue Marathon Live portfolio, and First Avenue.
- AXS has not disclosed whether the 50% discount affects revenue shares with promoters, artists, or venues.

### Highlights

- A Tier 2 consumer-major competitor (AXS) is running a broad, time-boxed face-value discount promotion rather than a fee or product change, layered on a recent independent-venue partnership push.
- The promotion is explicitly positioned as a growth/loyalty play tied to AXS's independent-venue strategy ("independent venues are essential to the live music ecosystem" — AXS head of NA venues Dean DeWulf).
- Open questions (unresolved by AXS) around whether discounts flow through to promoters/artists/venues matter for how the promotion affects underlying revenue economics.

### Industry Problem

Consumer-major ticketers are using aggressive price promotions and independent-venue partnerships to build habitual traffic and goodwill, raising the competitive bar on price transparency and value perception that smaller and independent-venue-focused platforms also have to contend with.

### Proposed Solution

AXS's response is a blunt price promotion tied to venue partnerships and brand milestones; Etix could consider its own targeted, time-boxed promotional-pricing tooling (face-value discounts distinct from fee structure) for anniversary or partner-venue campaigns, while monitoring whether AXS's independent-venue partnership push (Troubadour, Marathon Live, First Avenue) expands further and pressures Etix's own independent-venue relationships.

AXS has opened a 50% off sale on selected concert tickets in the US, valid until 21 August, to celebrate the company’s 15th anniversary.

Beginning yesterday (10 August), the promotion will be processed as a 50% reduction from the original face value of each ticket, excluding applicable fees and taxes, AXS said. Tickets are available on a first-come, first-served basis while supplies last, with the company noting that “availability may vary by event”.

The promotion applies to tickets bought within the stated time period, including shows that occur after the promotion closes.

The offer applies mostly to theatre and amphitheatre concerts across the US, with notable venues hosting qualifying concerts including The Bomb Factory in Dallas and Miami Beach Bandshell. Also among the notable venues to host shows included in the offer are New York’s Pier 17, and there are also sporting events at LA’s Crypto.com Arena.

Acts with shows included in the offer are the likes of Muna, Vince Staples, Derek Hough, Nick Shoulders, and the Zac Brown Band. It is unclear if the offer affects revenue shares with promoters, artists, or venues.

The move to offer discounts to fans in medium-sized venues comes after AXS announced a series of new and renewed partnerships with eight independent performance spaces in the US in early June.

The move saw the firm strike partnerships with West Hollywood’s celebrated Troubadour (500-cap), the seven-venue Marathon Live portfolio, along with an alliance with Minneapolis-based operator First Avenue, among others.

“Independent venues are essential to the live music ecosystem,” AXS’s head of North American venues Dean DeWulf said at the time, adding: “They’re where artists build careers, fans discover new music, and communities come together.”
