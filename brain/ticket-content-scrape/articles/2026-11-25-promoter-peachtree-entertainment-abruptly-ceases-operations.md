# Promoter Peachtree Entertainment Abruptly Ceases Operations

## Summary

Nashville-based independent promoter Peachtree Entertainment has abruptly ceased operations and begun laying off staff after sustaining more than $20 million in festival-related losses over the past two years. The shutdown follows rapid recent expansion — including taking on the distressed Furnace Fest property and an ownership stake in a new Lexington, Kentucky venue — and comes weeks after the president of its live-events division stepped down. Many of the company's booked shows, including its Rock The South and Rock The Country festivals, remain in limbo while venues and partners scramble to reassign dates.

### Key Points
- Peachtree sustained $20M+ in festival losses over two years despite efforts to "rightsize" the business, making continued operations unsustainable.
- Rock The Country faced political backlash this year (dubbed the "MAGA Festival" over Kid Rock's association), with artists like Creed and Shinedown pulling out and a tour stop canceled.
- Shane Quick, president of live events and Rock The South founder, stepped down just weeks before the shutdown.
- The Rail, a new Lexington venue Peachtree co-owned, will pivot to self-operation by the developer's team rather than Peachtree.
- LiveCo, which held an equity stake and was actively involved in Peachtree operations, has not clarified its position following the shutdown.
- Founded in 2013, Peachtree had built a talent roster including early bookings for Luke Combs, Morgan Wallen, and Luke Bryan.

### Highlights
- $20M+ in festival losses over two years cited as the direct cause of the shutdown.
- Multiple in-progress assets (Furnace Fest, The Rail venue stake, Rock The South/Country festivals) now in transition or uncertain status.
- Leadership departure (Shane Quick) preceded the collapse by only weeks.

### Industry Problem
Independent promoters and festival operators face concentrated financial risk when expanding aggressively into festival ownership — a single bad run of festival-level losses can force a sudden, disorderly exit that leaves venues, vendors, and ticket holders exposed with little warning.

### Proposed Solution
The item describes no structured wind-down (shows are being individually reassigned to other promoters ad hoc). For a primary ticketing platform, this is a signal to monitor promoter financial-health risk on upcoming on-sales and have contingency/refund-handling playbooks ready for abrupt promoter exits rather than building new product now.

## Transcript

![image](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/image-4-1.png)

After recent years of aggressive growth as an independent concert promoter investing in venues, music festivals and working with major artists like Alan Jackson, Peachtree Entertainment has abruptly shut down, leaving many concerts in doubt.

“On Friday, the City of Decatur received word that Peachtree Entertainment has decided to cease operations and close its business,” read a statement from the City of Decatur, which hosts the annual Rock The South Festival, according to Loudwire. “This was a significant and unexpected development for City leadership.”

While the news has not been confirmed publicly, and Peachtree representatives have not issued a statement or confirmed the news to multiple requests from *Pollstar*, the company has informed agencies and staff it would be immediately winding down its operations and laying off staff. Multiple employees have publicly expressed shock and surprise on social media while largely expressuig pride in their role at the company and the events they produced.

The news was first reported by Decibel.

The abrupt ceasing of operations appears to be directly related to losses in the festival space, with a statement to local media reading, “Peachtree Entertainment sustained more than $20 million in festival-related losses over the past two years. Despite extensive efforts to rightsize the business, these losses ultimately made continued operations in their existing form unsustainable.” The company produces the annual Rock The Country festival which this year faced political backlash, mocked in the press as “MAGA Festival” for its association with Kid Rock. Artists including Creed and Shinedown eventually pulled out and the South Carolina stop was canceled.

Shane Quick, the company’s president of live events and founder of Rock The South festival, which announced eight different events for 2026, stepped down from his role just weeks ago, according to Music Row.

The Nashville-based Peachtree in recent years has expanded rapidly, including this year taking on distressed music festival property Furnace Fest in Birmingham, Alabama, and recently announced an ownership stake in the new [The Rail concert venue in Lexington, Kentucky.](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/09/23/peachtree-entertainment-named-as-co-owner-and-exclusive-operator-of-2200-cap-lexington-venue-the-rail-to-open-in-2027/)

A representative of The Rail says that the venue will now be self-operated by a small team on behalf of the developer and operate as an open room, and is on track to have no shows impacted by the Peachtree news. The team is working to transfer shows to other promoters or retain them as in-house promoted shows, with 20-plus shows confirmed for 2027.

In recent years the company had expanded its talent buying and concert promotions operations including [in 2024 announcing a partnership with LiveCo](https://news.pollstar.com/2024/06/25/peachtree-entertainment-expands-with-liveco-collaboration/), the promoter collective that combined long-running promoters like TPR, Icon Concerts and more. *Pollstar* has reached out to LiveCo for more clarity on the company’s relationship with Peachtree, although this summer in an unrelated story told *Pollstar* LiveCo still retained an equity position and was actively involved in Peachtree operations. The status of the Peachtree’s event calendar is uncertain, with many shows still appearing to be taking place.

Peachtree Entertainment was formed in 2013 by founder Bradley Jordan and helmed by CEO Nathan Baugh. Jordan is founder of Painted Rock Management, which manages “Choosin’ Texas” star Ella Langley. Along with the festivals, Peachtree has been active in developing talent including early concerts by major artists such as Luke Combs, Morgan Wallen, Luke Bryan, Gavin Adcock and many more.

This is a developing story.
