# Peachtree Entertainment Named Co-Owner, Operator of Lexington’s The Rail

## Summary

Peachtree Entertainment has been named co-owner and exclusive operator of The Rail, a 2,200-capacity live music venue under development in Lexington, KY, replacing prior operator MassConcerts. The venue, part of a 43-acre entertainment district called The Commons, now targets an early-2027 opening.

### Key Points
- Peachtree will handle booking, operations, marketing and sponsorships; SVP Dustin Labbe cited Lexington's central-touring-market location.
- Opening pushed back from an original October 2026 target; first show bookings expected later in 2026.
- Venue will offer both concert (2,200-cap) and theater-style (1,000+ seat) configurations, plus club nights and private rentals.

Peachtree Entertainment has been named co-owner and exclusive operator of The Rail, a 2,200-capacity live music venue under development in Lexington, Kentucky.

The venue is expected to open in early 2027, with its first shows set to be announced later this year. The concert promoter will oversee operations, marketing and sponsorships and provide support for select programming. Its involvement gives The Rail an operator with national touring relationships as the venue prepares to enter the Lexington market.

The Rail will sit within The Commons, a 43-acre entertainment district about a mile from downtown. The site, a former tobacco processing campus, combines restored warehouses and new buildings housing restaurants, shops, recreation spaces and short-term lodging. More than 30 businesses already operate there, according to the venue announcement.

The venue is designed for several event formats. It will accommodate up to 2,200 people for concerts and offer more than 1,000 seats for theater-style events. Plans also call for club nights, corporate functions and private rentals. Guest amenities include six bars, two outdoor decks, VIP areas and a membership program. Backstage, artists will have access to three dressing rooms and a lounge.

Peachtree Entertainment senior vice president of booking Dustin Labbe pointed to Lexington’s position among other touring markets. He called The Rail “an ideal routing stop for national tours,” citing its proximity to cities including Nashville, Louisville, Cincinnati, Indianapolis, Columbus, Knoxville and St. Louis. The company expects that location to help bring more artists through Central Kentucky.

For The Commons, the venue is intended to extend visits beyond a single performance. Founder Daren Turner said guests could attend a show while also using the district’s dining and hospitality businesses before and afterward. VisitLex President Mary Quinn Ramer said the addition could encourage visitors to spend more time in the city and give local audiences another place to gather.

The revised opening timeline marks a change from the venue’s earlier plans. When The Rail was announced last year, it was targeting an October 2026 opening, and MassConcerts was associated with the project. MassConcerts is no longer involved.
