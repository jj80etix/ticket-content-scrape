# Dispute Over Festivals Between AEG, Danny Wimmer Presents Nears Trial

## Summary

An eight-year legal dispute between AEG Presents and Danny Wimmer Presents over the 2018 breakup of the Rock on the Range and Carolina Rebellion festivals is heading toward a reported February 2027 jury trial. Newly surfaced depositions reveal a failed $18.9 million buy-sell proposal (with a five-year, 250-mile hard-rock noncompete) that collapsed before DWP launched the competing Sonic Temple and Epicenter festivals.

### Key Points
- AEG's Aug. 2018 buy-sell proposal valued either side's 50% festival interest at ~$18.9M, with a proposed noncompete barring hard-rock/metal events within 250 miles for five years; deal was never reached.
- DWP had earlier proposed a 3x-EBITDA buyout or a ~5%-of-profits licensing fee for the festival names; AEG countered at 5x EBITDA.
- Case (LA Superior Court No. 18STCV06655) survived DWP's anti-SLAPP appeal in April 2025; discovery disputes continued into August 2026.
- Decibel reports a February 2027 trial date, not independently confirmed by TicketNews; central question is whether the partners had continuing joint rights in the festival businesses or only shared IP.

A nearly eight-year legal battle between AEG Presents and Danny Wimmer Presents (DWP) over two major rock festivals is moving toward a reported February 2027 jury trial, with newly surfaced testimony detailing an unsuccessful $18.9 million buy-sell proposal and a sweeping proposed restriction on competing events.

The Los Angeles Superior Court case stems from the promoters’ 2018 breakup and DWP’s decision to launch Sonic Temple in Columbus, Ohio, and Epicenter in North Carolina as successors to the jointly promoted Rock on the Range and Carolina Rebellion festivals. AEG argues that DWP improperly took advantage of assets and business opportunities belonging to the former partnership. DWP disputes that characterization and maintains that the annual co-promotion arrangements did not prevent it from organizing new festivals.

[Decibel reported Oct. 9](https://decibel.news/p/aeg-v-dwp-rock-on-the-range-fight-heads-to-trial?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) that recent court filings include deposition testimony and internal AEG communications illuminating the negotiations and the companies’ competing plans. The newly reported details put a dollar figure on a proposed separation and underscore how aggressively both sides were positioning themselves in the rock festival market.

## An $18.9 Million Exit Proposal

Advertisement

[![StubHub](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/StubHub-desktop-1.webp)](https://stubhub.prf.hn/click/camref:1100l9NoF)

According to Decibel’s account of testimony from an Aug. 28 deposition of Danny Wimmer, AEG proposed a deal in August 2018 under which it would either pay DWP $18,895,000 for DWP’s 50% interest in both festivals or receive the same amount for AEG’s interest. The proposal was delivered by Melissa Ormond, then AEG’s chief operating officer of festivals, on Aug. 20, 2018.

AEG attorney Kathy Jorrie reportedly described a $15,895,000 base amount, plus additional money tied to an improved Columbus venue agreement. Wimmer acknowledged knowing the proposed $18.9 million figure at the time, while DWP’s attorney objected to the characterization of AEG’s calculations, Decibel reported.

The figure was a proposed buyout price for one side’s claimed interest, not a court determination of the festivals’ value or a confirmed measure of damages being sought in the lawsuit. The current damages demand has not been independently established by TicketNews.

The proposal also reportedly included a five-year noncompete clause barring the selling party from promoting an event featuring three or more hard-rock or metal bands within 250 miles of either festival. Because the buy-sell agreement was not reached, the reported restriction should not be confused with a noncompete that actually took effect.

The negotiations were not simply a choice between accepting or rejecting AEG’s valuation. A [California appellate opinion issued in April 2025](https://law.counselstack.com/opinion/aeg-presents-productions-v-danny-wimmer-presents-ca28-calctapp-2025?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) recounts an earlier DWP proposal to acquire AEG’s interest at three times earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, or EBITDA. AEG’s subsequent counterproposal used five times EBITDA and offered either party the opportunity to be buyer or seller.

The same opinion recounts DWP’s response: It disputed treating the festivals as continuing businesses available for sale, arguing that the festival names were the only jointly owned assets. DWP instead proposed paying AEG an annual fee to license those names, which the opinion describes as roughly 5% of the festivals’ 2018 profits. AEG rejected that alternative.

That disagreement goes to the heart of the case: whether the parties merely owned particular intellectual property together or had broader continuing rights in festival businesses built over years of joint promotion.

## The Breakup and the Competing Festivals

Rock on the Range began in Columbus in 2007, while Carolina Rebellion debuted in 2011. AEG and Right Arm Entertainment, DWP’s predecessor, were involved in developing the events, which grew into prominent fixtures of the hard-rock festival calendar. [TicketNews reported on Rock on the Range’s ticket sales in 2009](https://www.ticketnews.com/2009/04/rock-on-the-range-festival-sells-out-field-tickets/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), when the festival had already expanded beyond its original single-day format.

The appellate opinion says the promoters executed annual co-promotion agreements from 2014 through 2017 but produced the 2018 editions without new written co-promotion agreements. It also describes provisions giving the parties joint ownership of festival intellectual property and limiting its use without mutual consent. Whether those provisions imposed the wider obligations asserted by AEG remains contested.

After negotiations failed, DWP announced Sonic Temple for the same Columbus venue and traditional May weekend occupied by Rock on the Range, followed by Epicenter in North Carolina. AEG’s lawsuit, filed Nov. 29, 2018, alleges that DWP used partnership resources, relationships and confidential material to establish the replacement events. Among its specific allegations is that DWP used computer-aided design drawings associated with the original festivals in seeking permits for the new ones. Those allegations have not been resolved at trial.

DWP has long argued it was free to operate independent events after the annual arrangements ended. In [2018 comments to Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2018/12/06/danny-wimmer-presents-ceo-responds-to-aeg-lawsuit-over-two-rock-fests-exclusive/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), DWP executives and counsel said the companies could not agree on terms for another year and maintained that DWP had the right to proceed separately. The company also disputed AEG’s suggestion that the new festivals had been secretly developed.

Complicating that account of the breakup, Decibel says recently disclosed AEG emails address plans for a competing Columbus rock event on Rock on the Range’s weekend. The possibility of AEG mounting a rival event was not entirely unknown: Pollstar reported in December 2018 that DWP representatives said AEG had placed a hold on Ohio State University’s football stadium and was pursuing some of the same artists. The newly reported emails could provide further context, but TicketNews has not independently reviewed those communications or established how far the competing event plans advanced.

## Appeal, Discovery and a Possible February Trial

The litigation was filed in Los Angeles County Superior Court under case number 18STCV06655. Proceedings were stayed in December 2022 during DWP’s appeal of a ruling denying its motion under California’s anti-SLAPP law, which is intended to protect certain speech and petitioning activity from meritless lawsuits.

In April 2025, the [California Court of Appeal upheld the denial](https://law.counselstack.com/opinion/aeg-presents-productions-v-danny-wimmer-presents-ca28-calctapp-2025?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). The court concluded that AEG’s challenged allegations centered on the alleged misuse of partnership property and confidential information, rather than on DWP’s protected activity in applying for government permits. That procedural decision allowed the disputed claims to remain in the case; it was not a finding that DWP had violated the agreements.

The resumed proceedings have included document-production disputes. An [Aug. 11, 2026, tentative ruling](https://southlawgpt.com/courthouse_html_pages/Stanley_Mosk_Courthouse_August_11%2C_2026_102.html?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) addressed DWP’s efforts to compel additional production from AEG, including a dispute about documents withheld on privilege grounds. Decibel’s more recent reporting describes deposition transcripts and internal communications appearing in court filings, though TicketNews has not independently obtained those particular exhibits.

Decibel reports that a jury trial is scheduled for February 2027. TicketNews has not independently confirmed the exact trial date or determined the current amount of damages or other relief at stake. The approaching trial could address not only disputed conduct during a high-profile promoter breakup but also the distinction between a company’s right to compete and a former partner’s rights in the assets of an established festival business.

A representative for Danny Wimmer Presents declined to comment on pending litigation in response to TicketNews’ questions. AEG Presents did not respond to a separate request for comment and clarification by the publication deadline. In a statement previously provided to Decibel, AEG said, “AEG Presents has no comment other than we disagree with DWP’s positions.”
