# Late changes to California ticket bill leaves resale restrictions uncertain

## Summary

Last-minute Senate amendments to California's AB 1349 speculative-ticketing bill added a carve-out allowing certain resale listings without upfront ownership proof ("ghost-ticketing"), prompting NIVA — the bill's lead backer — to call for a governor veto even after the bill passed a hurried State Senate vote on August 31. Proponents maintain resellers are still fairly targeted by the bill overall.

### Highlights
- Bill passed the State Senate August 31; now awaits Governor Newsom's signature or veto.
- The disputed amendment reportedly lets large resellers list tickets even without proof of ownership under some circumstances — the "ghost-ticketing" carve-out.
- NIVA, previously the bill's lead advocate, is now urging a veto over this late change.

### Industry Problem
Speculative/ghost ticketing — listing and selling tickets a reseller doesn't yet hold — remains a live regulatory fight in California even at the final-passage stage, with last-minute carve-outs threatening to undercut the bill's core consumer protection as originally designed.

### Proposed Solution
No vendor action needed yet; this is a monitor/watch item pending Newsom's decision. The trigger to reassess is the governor's signature or veto — a signed bill with the ghost-ticketing carve-out intact would set a template other states might follow, worth tracking against Etix's own inventory-verification practices.
