# Brazil Orders StubHub, viagogo and Three Others to Halt New Ticket Resales

## Summary

Brazil's consumer protection agency Senacon ordered StubHub, viagogo and three other resale platforms to halt new ticket resales for Brazilian events within 72 hours, citing failures on pricing transparency and seller-identification disclosures under the country's new "Fan Decree." The action is a significant escalation of months of regulatory scrutiny and coincides with, but extends well beyond, BTS's ongoing São Paulo concerts.

### Key Points
- StubHub, viagogo, TicketSwap Brasil, BuyTicket and Global Entretenimento given 72 hours to stop facilitating new resales; daily fines up to R$100,000 for noncompliance
- Alleged violations: insufficient seller-identification disclosure, incomplete fee/tax disclosure, deficient complaint/cancellation systems
- viagogo disputes the finding, says it has "implemented all platform requirements" and calls it a "misunderstanding"
- Brazil's disclosure-based model (seller self-declared face value) differs from Ontario's stricter documented-original-price resale cap, which has already produced C$47,500 in SeatGeek fines and C$20,000 against StubHub Canada
- Order is precautionary pending administrative proceedings; previously completed purchases must still be honored

### Highlights
- 72-hour compliance deadline with daily fines up to R$100,000 per platform for continued noncompliance
- Follows escalating Brazilian enforcement since July 2026 (viagogo proceeding) and August (BuyTicket/FunBuyNet proceedings)
- Contrasts with Ontario's resale regime, where SeatGeek and StubHub Canada have already paid real penalties (C$47,500 and C$20,000) under stricter original-price-verification rules

### Industry Problem
Resale marketplaces are increasingly being forced to implement jurisdiction-specific pricing-transparency and seller-verification systems, and regulators in multiple markets (Brazil, Ontario) are actively enforcing against the same handful of global platforms (StubHub/viagogo, SeatGeek) for falling short — raising real compliance-engineering burden and legal exposure for any multinational secondary marketplace.

### Proposed Solution
No platform fix is detailed beyond viagogo's assertion of compliance. For Etix, this is a monitoring item: track how transparency/disclosure requirements evolve market-by-market (face-value self-declaration vs. documented-original-price models) since a primary platform with resale features would face the same compliance design choices if it expanded into secondary markets.

Brazil’s national consumer protection agency has ordered StubHub, viagogo and three other ticket resale platforms to suspend new resale transactions for events held in the country, citing alleged failures to comply with consumer protection and pricing transparency requirements.

The [order announced Wednesday, October 28](https://www.gov.br/mj/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/senacon-determina-prazo-de-72-horas-para-cinco-plataformas-suspenderem-novas-revendas-de-ingressos?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) gives StubHub, viagogo, TicketSwap Brasil, BuyTicket and Global Entretenimento (FunBuyNet) 72 hours from notification to stop facilitating new ticket resales. The restriction will remain in effect until the companies demonstrate compliance with Brazil’s consumer disclosure requirements.

In a statement to TicketNews, viagogo said it had already implemented the requirements and would work to resolve what it described as a misunderstanding with regulators.

The enforcement action comes as BTS performs three concerts at São Paulo’s MorumBIS stadium on October 28, 30 and 31. However, the government’s directive extends beyond those performances to new ticket resales for events throughout Brazil.

According to Senacon, the investigation identified potential violations of Brazil’s Consumer Defense Code and Decree No. 13,108, adopted in August and commonly known as the *Fan Decree*.

The agency cited insufficient identification of whether tickets were being offered by individuals or businesses, incomplete disclosure of ticket prices including fees and taxes, and deficiencies in systems for submitting complaints, reporting potentially abusive listings and exercising cancellation rights when applicable.

Senacon also initiated new administrative sanction proceedings against StubHub, TicketSwap Brasil, BuyTicket and Global Entretenimento (FunBuyNet). Allegations concerning viagogo will be incorporated into an existing administrative proceeding involving that company.

The latest action follows several months of regulatory scrutiny.

In July, Senacon [opened an administrative proceeding against viagogo](https://www.gov.br/mj/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/senacon-determina-que-viagogo-informe-ao-consumidor-que-nao-e-canal-oficial-de-venda?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), alleging that consumers might not adequately understand that the platform facilitates secondary ticket sales rather than operating as an official ticket outlet.

The agency also questioned StubHub’s involvement in Brazil after the company reportedly told regulators it did not operate in the country. Senacon cited Portuguese-language services, prices displayed in Brazilian reais and tickets offered for Brazilian events as reasons to investigate whether StubHub was targeting domestic consumers.

Separate proceedings involving BuyTicket and FunBuyNet followed in August.

The new suspension directive represents a substantial escalation of that enforcement activity, moving beyond orders to improve consumer disclosures to a temporary prohibition on facilitating new resale transactions.

## Brazil’s Approach Differs From Ontario’s Ticket Resale Restrictions

As [TicketNews reported when Brazil adopted its new regulations](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/brazil-ticket-rules-resale-bots-price-cap/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the Fan Decree establishes obligations for primary ticket sellers and secondary marketplaces, addressing pricing disclosures, ticket transfers, automated purchasing and other consumer protections.

Unlike some other recent ticket resale legislation, Brazil’s decree does not impose a universal cap preventing tickets from being resold above their original price. Instead, it requires additional transparency about original ticket values and the amount consumers are being asked to pay.

Article 5 of the [Brazilian decree](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/2026/decreto-13108-31-agosto-2026-799872-publicacaooriginal-181137-pe.html?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) requires secondary marketplaces to provide a field where sellers self-declare the ticket’s original face value. Platforms must also inform consumers when tickets are sold for more than face value.

That provision is distinct from the requirement to disclose the complete resale asking price, including applicable fees and taxes, from the beginning of the purchasing process.

It also differs from the resale restrictions introduced in Ontario, Canada, earlier this year.

Ontario generally prohibits tickets from being resold for more than the total amount originally paid to the primary seller, although permitted secondary-market fees and taxes may be added. The province also requires resale platforms to disclose the original purchase price and obtain proof of that amount from sellers before facilitating transactions.

Those requirements have prompted objections from independent resale marketplaces, which argue that original purchase information is not always available to companies operating outside the primary ticketing system.

[TicketNews recently detailed Ontario’s escalating enforcement actions](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/ontario-seatgeek-fines-ticket-resale-compliance/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) against SeatGeek, which has accumulated C$47,500 in administrative penalties under the province’s new rules.

In September, StubHub Canada also received C$20,000 in penalties and announced plans to appeal. Both companies have questioned how secondary marketplaces can reliably establish original purchase prices when ticketing records may be controlled by competing primary ticketing providers.

In a [May statement about Ontario’s legislation](https://newsroom.stubhub.com/2026/05/01/new-ontario-ticket-resale-law-what-buyers-and-sellers-need-to-know/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), StubHub said marketplaces like its own lack visibility into the price originally paid for tickets offered by third-party sellers.

Brazil’s approach addresses part of that information problem differently.

Rather than explicitly requiring a secondary marketplace to obtain documentary proof of the original transaction, Article 5 requires the platform to collect the seller’s self-declared face value. The Brazilian measure also uses face value as its reference point, while Ontario’s restrictions concern the original total purchase price, including fees and taxes.

That distinction could make Brazil’s disclosure model easier for independent marketplaces to implement without direct access to the original ticket issuer’s transaction records.

However, seller self-declaration introduces a separate question about accuracy and enforcement. A marketplace collecting an original price supplied by a seller still faces the possibility of incorrect or misleading declarations, particularly when the ticket has changed hands multiple times or was originally purchased through a package arrangement.

Brazil’s decree separately requires platforms to identify patterns of professional resale activity and remove listings associated with prohibited practices. The presence of a self-declaration field therefore does not eliminate the other compliance obligations imposed on secondary marketplaces.

Importantly, Senacon’s public explanation of the October 28 enforcement action specifically highlights alleged failures involving total-price disclosure, seller identification and consumer complaint channels. It does not separately identify a failure to collect self-declared face values among the listed deficiencies.

The underlying regulatory order invokes broader provisions of Article 5, making the precise deficiencies identified for each platform an important question as the administrative proceedings continue.

## viagogo Says It Has Complied With Brazil’s Requirements

Responding to an inquiry from TicketNews about the Brazilian enforcement action, viagogo – StubHub’s parent company – disputed the suggestion that the platforms was not complying with the applicable requirements.

“We have implemented all platform requirements set out under the decree of SENACON and will work to resolve this misunderstanding promptly,” the company said in a statement provided to TicketNews.

“viagogo is committed to ensuring fans have choice, flexibility and safe access when buying tickets on our platform.”

The statement puts viagogo at odds with Senacon’s determination that there are sufficient indications of potential noncompliance to justify temporarily restricting new resale transactions.

It does not specify which measures the company has implemented or whether it intends to challenge the suspension directive through the administrative process.

The company also did not address the distinction between Brazil’s original-price self-declaration requirement and the more demanding documentation requirements imposed in Ontario, leaving open the question of whether that aspect of Brazil’s framework is easier for independent marketplaces to implement.

For now, the five platforms remain subject to Senacon’s directive to halt new resale transactions involving events in Brazil while continuing to fulfill existing customer obligations. The regulator has said the restrictions will remain in place until the companies demonstrate compliance with the applicable disclosure and transparency requirements.
