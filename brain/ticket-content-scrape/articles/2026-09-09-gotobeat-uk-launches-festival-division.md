# Gotobeat UK Launches Festival Division

## Summary

UK live-entertainment company Gotobeat, which pairs promoter expertise with proprietary AI, has appointed Mike Lightford (ex-Boardmasters/NASS) as head of festivals and will launch its first festival, a two-weekend concert series at Beckenham Place Park near London in July 2027. CEO Max Busin says the company's AI analyzes fan demand, venue data, and touring patterns to inform routing decisions and cut admin overhead, enabling a 20-person team to manage 1,000 shows a year.

### Key Points

- Gotobeat's AI reportedly analyzes fan demand, venue data, and touring patterns to help decide which cities, venues, and timelines to book for an artist.
- CEO Busin frames AI's role as reducing admin work and surfacing insight, while local promoter relationships/creativity remain human-led — a hybrid AI-plus-human-judgment model.
- Company claims the approach lets a 20-person team manage roughly 1,000 shows a year.
- New festival division launches with a 12,000-capacity-per-day, two-stage event at Beckenham Place Park (July 2-4 & 9-11, 2027) and a second festival, Unión, for the UK's Latin American community.

### Industry Problem

Live-entertainment operators face the challenge of scaling booking/routing decisions (which markets, venues, and timing to pick) without proportionally scaling headcount — manual demand forecasting and venue selection is labor-intensive and doesn't scale with growing show volume.

### Proposed Solution

Gotobeat's answer is proprietary AI for demand/venue/routing forecasting layered under human promoter judgment, rather than full automation. This is useful grounding for Etix Assistant's roadmap: a demand-forecasting or venue/market-recommendation layer (not customer-facing dynamic pricing) is a live, in-market analog worth benchmarking as an AI use case beyond fraud detection or support automation.

Gotobeat, a UK-based live entertainment company that by its own admission combines “promoter expertise with proprietary AI technology,” has appointed Mike Lightford as its new head of festivals.

Lightford is the former Director of Music at Boardmasters and NASS Festivals. His appointment reflects Gotobeat’s expansion into the festival space.

Gotobeat, in the company’s own words, “combines on-the-ground promoter expertise with proprietary AI technology to help artists plan and deliver smarter tours. Its custom-built tools analyze fan demand, venue data and touring patterns, helping the team make informed decisions around the right cities, venues and timelines for each artist.”

The expansion into festivals brings this data-led approach to the festival market.

*Pollstar* wanted to know what exactly the AI component of Gotobeat adds to the promoter’s role, and received a reply from founder and CEO Max Busin, who said, “The AI gives us another layer of insight when we’re looking at an artist, a market or a potential venue, but it’s the people on the ground who turn that insight into a successful event. It’s also beneficial for us as AI reduces admin work, which is why we are able to manage 1,000 shows a year with a team of only 20 people.

“Local promoters have the time and capacity to fully understand their audiences, their venues and the relationships that make a show work. For me, the exciting thing about Gotobeat is bringing those two things together, using technology to identify opportunities and reduce the guesswork. This simultaneously allows experienced promoters to focus on the relationships, creativity and local knowledge that are essential to putting on a great festival.”

The first festival by Gotobeat will be a six-show concert series across two weekends in July 2027 (July 2-4 & 9-11, 2027), taking place at Beckenham Place Park, located 30 minutes outside of central London. The park provides a natural amphitheater setting with an established history of hosting large-scale live music. Gotobeat will build on the site’s musical heritage with a new festival experience designed to bring a broad range of artists and audiences together across the two weekends. The lineup for the first edition of the Beckenham Place Park concert series hadn’t been announced at the time of writing, but a press release from Gotobeat promises “more than 10 artists performing each day across two stages and capacity for 12,000 attendees per day.”

Most shows on Gotobeat’s event calendar are club and theater shows, but the company stated it also promotes arena shows. The addition of festivals marks another step forward for the business.

Lightford will be tasked to grow the commercial, partnership and sponsorship opportunities in this new festival vertical, with full operational autonomy, working alongside the CEO.

With more than 20 years’ experience across live music, A&R and brand marketing, Lightford began his career at the Barfly Group before moving into A&R at Warner Music Group. He subsequently held roles at Ministry of Sound and MAMA Group before transitioning into brand marketing, where he led music strategies for major brands including Coca-Cola, Unilever and Superdry.

From 2018, Lightford worked within Boardmasters and NASS Festivals, where he became a key member of Superstruct’s European booking team. During his tenure, both festivals experienced significant growth, reaching peaks of approximately 30,000 and 55,000 daily attendees respectively, while hosting artists including Sam Fender, RAYE, Stormzy, Liam Gallagher and Jamie XX.

Another festival conceived by the team at Gotobeat is Unión, scheduled for July 9, 2027, a contemporary music festival for the UK’s Latin American community. “Running from Reggaeton to Latin Pop, Unión will pioneer current Spanish and Latin American artists, including many debut UK performances,” a press release states.

Headline acts and the wider line-up for Uninón will be announced the coming months.
