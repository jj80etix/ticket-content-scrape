# Spotify Adds Setlists, Venue Info to Reserved Concert Ticket Program

## Summary

Spotify is expanding its Reserved concert-ticket program — which grants eligible Premium subscribers early access to primary-market tickets via Live Nation/Ticketmaster based on listening signals — with personalized share cards, pre-/post-show setlist playlists (via setlist.fm) and a dedicated in-app event hub. The features keep fans inside Spotify before and after a show without turning Spotify itself into the ticket seller; purchases still complete through Ticketmaster. Since its June US launch (expanded to Canada in August), Spotify says nearly 100,000 tickets have been reserved through the program.

### Highlights
- New features: personalized share cards (pre- and post-purchase), tour setlist playlists updated with actual songs performed, and a consolidated in-app event page.
- Reserved uses listening signals (streams, saves, shares, location) to grant selected Premium subscribers a window to buy up to two tickets before general onsale.
- ~100,000 tickets reserved since the June 18 US launch; expanded to Canada in August.
- Recent Reserved artists cited: ROLE MODEL, Miley Cyrus, Jonas Brothers, John Summit, Metallica, among others.

### Industry Problem
Fan-facing platforms with rich first-party listening/engagement data (like Spotify) can now identify and reward "real fans" more precisely than a ticketing platform's own purchase history alone — pressuring primary platforms to either partner with or replicate that data-driven access layer to stay the fan's front door before/after the purchase.

### Proposed Solution
Spotify's model (Build signal): pair transaction data with owned engagement surfaces (setlists, share cards, an event hub) to retain fan attention around the show, rather than ceding it once the ticket is sold. A primary platform could build or deepen equivalent post-purchase engagement (setlists, personalized recaps) directly, rather than leaving that layer to a partner.

Spotify is expanding its Reserved concert ticket program with setlists, venue information and other features designed to keep fans using the streaming service before and after they attend a show.

The company [announced Thursday](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-09-24/reserved-by-spotify-share-cards-setlists) that Reserved users will now get access to personalized share cards, pre- and post-show setlist playlists and a centralized event destination within the Spotify app. The additions build on a ticket-access program [Spotify launched in the U.S. in June](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/spotify-launch-reserved-concert-tickets-superfans/) in partnership with Live Nation and Ticketmaster.

Reserved identifies eligible Spotify Premium subscribers based on signals including streams, saves, shares and location, then gives selected fans a dedicated window to purchase up to two tickets before the general onsale.

The new features extend that experience beyond the original ticket offer.

Once a fan purchases Reserved tickets, Spotify will make a tour setlist playlist available through the event page using information supplied by setlist.fm. Ahead of the concert, the playlist will show songs recently performed on the tour, giving fans an indication of what they may hear at their show.

Afterward, Spotify says the page will update to reflect the actual songs performed at the concert, including tour debuts and surprise additions.

Reserved users will also get two personalized share cards. One is generated when a fan receives a Reserved offer, while another becomes available after tickets are purchased. The cards include the fan’s name, artist and show information and Reserved branding and can be shared on social platforms or through messaging apps.

Spotify is also consolidating Reserved-related information into a dedicated in-app event destination accessible through Home and Your Library. The page will include venue and event information, share cards, setlists and previous performances.

The new features begin rolling out Thursday, with setlist functionality becoming available for participating tours as show dates arrive.

Spotify said recent Reserved offers have included ROLE MODEL, Miley Cyrus, Skillet, beabadoobee, Jonas Brothers, Brett Eldredge, John Summit, Young Thug and Metallica.

The expansion gives Spotify a larger role surrounding the concert experience without turning the streaming company itself into the ticket seller.

Fans selected for Reserved still complete their purchases through Ticketmaster. When a Reserved purchase window opens, the user selects an eligible tour date and is directed to Ticketmaster, where Spotify and Ticketmaster accounts may be linked to verify access.

Two tickets are held for an eligible fan for the duration of the Reserved window, although the specific dates, seating locations and price levels available can change as other customers purchase inventory. Spotify’s terms state that tickets are sold and fulfilled by third-party ticket sellers, with prices, fees, refunds and other ticketing terms controlled by the applicable seller, artist, promoter or venue.

Spotify initially announced Reserved in May as a way of giving some of an artist’s most engaged listeners access to ticket inventory without requiring them to compete for an access code or immediately enter a traditional presale.

The program launched in the U.S. on June 18 for eligible Premium subscribers 18 and older, with Live Nation serving as the launch partner and Ticketmaster powering the ticket purchase process. It subsequently expanded to Canada in August.

Spotify said in its second-quarter update that nearly 100,000 tickets had been reserved through the service since the June U.S. launch.

Reserved is part of Spotify’s broader effort to connect its listening data with live events. The company already operates a personalized Live Events hub and Concerts Near You feature that directs listeners toward upcoming shows.

Reserved differs by using listening behavior to identify existing fans for access to limited allocations of primary-market tickets. The latest expansion gives Spotify another opportunity to keep those fans inside its app after the ticket has been secured, providing show information and music tied directly to the concert before and after the event.
