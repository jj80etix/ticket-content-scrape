# EXIT confirms ‘full-scale’ return in traditional home from 2028

## Summary

Serbia's EXIT Festival has confirmed it will return to full scale at its traditional home, Petrovaradin Fortress, from 2028, after the event left the country in 2025 and planned a "special edition" for 2027. The announcement follows Serbia's recent elections.

### Key Points
- EXIT Festival returning full-scale to Petrovaradin Fortress starting 2028.
- Festival had left Serbia in 2025; 2027 planned as a transitional "special edition."
- Timing tied to Serbia's recent elections.

## Transcript

## EXIT confirms ‘full-scale’ return in traditional home from 2028

The Serbian festival said today it will be back to its previous form for the first time since 2025, after a special edition in 2027

![EXIT Festival](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/EXIT-Festival-©-Milos-Krstic.png)

EXIT Festival © Milos Krstic

EXIT Festival has confirmed 2028 will bring a return to its ‘full-scale festival’ in its former home at the Petrovaradin Fortress in Serbia in the wake of the country’s recent elections.

The festival, which was forced to leave its home country in 2025, previously promised a “special edition” back in Serbia in 2027, but organisers have now announced a full return to its previous form at its traditional site from the following year.

“Today, we are incredibly happy to share that, following the historic victory of Serbia’s students, EXIT is coming home! Our full-scale festival will return at the Petrovaradin Fortress in 2028, preceded by a special edition in July 2027,” festival co-founder Dušan Kovačević said today.

It comes after a student-led movement unseated longstanding president Aleksandar Vučić following a parliamentary election in late October, bringing to an end a government that many in the Serbian live sector had accused of attacking free speech on Serbia’s live music stages.

This included the alleged [blacklisting](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/serbian-live-sector-under-threat-amid-ongoing-protests/) of artists supportive of a years-long student protest movement from some venues and festivals linked to the government, including EXIT’s [relocation out of the country.](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/08/exit-launches-global-appeal-to-secure-future/)

Kovačević first [told](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/10/exit-vows-serbia-return-as-countrys-live-scene-celebrates-election-result/) *IQ* of plans for the 2027 special edition on the morning following Serbia’s election, though details of this event are yet to be confirmed.

> “Our full-scale festival will return at the Petrovaradin Fortress in 2028”

Describing the years since this relocation, Kovačević said the festival “refused to give up” and that “during our darkest moments, receiving the Promoter of the Year award at the European Festival Awards in January was an extraordinary source of strength and encouragement”.

“This has been one of the most difficult years in EXIT’s 26-year history. Without our flagship festival in Serbia, we were forced to significantly reduce our team, including saying goodbye to some dear colleagues who had been with us for more than 20 years. These were among the most painful decisions we have ever had to make,” Kovačević said.

During this time, EXIT produced an international touring series made up of eight events in seven different countries. This included EXIT 2 Montenegro, which hosted nearly 100,000 visitors over three days, and and Starlight Festival, the first-ever multi-day, multi-stage music festival in front of the Pyramids of Giza.

“After everything we’ve been through, this homecoming means more to us than words can express. EXIT was born out of a student movement fighting for freedom 26 years ago, and it is deeply symbolic that another generation of students has now made its return possible,” Kovačević said.

“They have achieved something even greater – turning Serbia into a source of inspiration for people fighting for a better world, showing that courage, unity and perseverance can make even the seemingly impossible possible.”
