# The Kansas City Royals Release Renderings Of New Ballpark

## Summary

The Kansas City Royals and architecture firm Populous unveiled renderings for the team's new $3 billion downtown ballpark and mixed-use district at Crown Center, targeting a 2030 season opening. The open-air, 34,000-seat venue is designed with a fabric canopy shading roughly 65% of seating and exterior elements inspired by Kansas City landmarks, part of an 85-acre development including hotels, retail and residential space.

### Key Points
- $3B project: ~$1.9B for the ballpark/infrastructure, ~$1.1B for the surrounding 85-acre mixed-use district, developed with Hallmark Cards.
- Kansas City has committed up to $600M in public funding; Royals targeting a 2030 season opening.
- Open-air design seats approximately 34,000, with a lightweight fabric canopy shading ~65% of seating during late afternoons.
- Populous, headquartered in Kansas City, is architect of record; more than 90% of the design team is Kansas City-based.
- Design references Kauffman Stadium traditions (the crown, fountains, the "Little K") alongside new entertainment and HQ buildings in the outfield.

## Transcript

Rendering courtesy Kansas City Royals/Populous

The Royals and Populous unveiled renderings of the MLB team’s new ballpark being constructed in downtown Kansas City.

“This is a big, big project for Kansas City,” said John Sherman, Chairman and CEO of the Kansas City Royals, in a recorded message. “This city now is not afraid to take on big things and think big about their future. This is what great cities do.”

“I think the downtown location benefits the entire city,” added Cullen Maxey, President Business Operations, Kansas City Royals. “It is something that will be a point of pride. And when we think about the momentum that downtown already has. The different cultures, the different communities – we can only enhance that with our investment.”

The Kansas City Royals’ proposed downtown ballpark and surrounding mixed-use district at Crown Center represent an estimated $3 billion development. The ballpark and related infrastructure are estimated to cost approximately $1.9 billion, while the surrounding district is expected to account for about $1.1 billion. The 85-acre project, developed in partnership with Hallmark Cards, is planned to include restaurants, retail, hotels, residential space and public areas, creating an entertainment destination around the stadium. Kansas City has committed up to $600 million toward the stadium project, and the Royals are targeting opening for the 2030 season.

Populous is the architect of record and has a decades-long relationship with the Royals. The company’s global headquarters are based in Kansas City.

“We could have searched the world over for the leading sports architecture firm in the world, but it’s right here in Kansas City,” said John Sherman on MLB.com. “We selected Populous because we wanted the best design, the best fan experience, and the best pedestrian experience in Major League Baseball.”

Founded in Kansas City more than 40 years ago, Populous has helped redefine modern ballpark design, creating iconic venues including Oriole Park at Camden Yards, Oracle Park, PNC Park, Yankee Stadium, Target Field and Truist Park. The firm has worked with 29 of 30 Major League Baseball clubs, designing ballparks that reconnect baseball with urban communities and help revitalize city centers.

The company’s history with the Royals includes projects and renovations at Kauffman Stadium and the design of Surprise Stadium, the team’s spring training home in Arizona. More than 90% of the design team for the Royals’ new ballpark will be based in Kansas City.

For Populous Senior Principal Earl Santee, a Kansas City native who grew up attending Kansas City Athletics games at Municipal Stadium, the project also carries personal significance.

“This is where I learned to love baseball,” Santee said. “I learned to love the art of baseball, and what we do is art.”

![KCRoyals1populous](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/KCRoyals1populous-scaled.jpg)

Exterior rendering courtesy Kansas City Royals/Populous

For Kansas City Mayor Quinton Lucas, the partnership represents an opportunity to create jobs and contribute to the city’s development.

“The marriage of the Royals and Populous with Kansas City, with all of our construction teams, our labor unions and beyond is what this is all about: jobs for Kansas Citians, new opportunities for Kansas City, and a Kansas City that is programmed for the future,” said Lucas, when the renderings were released Oct. 15.

The open-air ballpark is designed to feel intimate with seating for approximately 34,000 fans. The venue draws inspiration from the city’s character, landscape and architecture with exterior elements inspired by Kansas City’s landmarks including Municipal Auditorium, Union Station and the Liberty Memorial.

The ballpark’s entrance features limestone framed by a layered screen of light-colored materials, metal and terracotta. Above, a lightweight fabric canopy preserves the tradition of open-air baseball while helping manage Kansas City’s summer heat by shading approximately 65% of seating during late-afternoon.

In the outfield, the right-field entertainment building and left-field Royals headquarters building will connect to the ballpark through two continuous 360-degree concourses, opening the seating bowl to views of Kansas City while creating a distinctive setting for the iconic Royals fountains and CrownVision scoreboard.

Royals right fielder Jac Caglianone offered his thoughts on the new ballpark: “There’s a great foundation being built here in Kansas City and to have a new stadium to break it in seems super exciting and I’m super excited for it.”

The updated design honors the crown, fountains, the Little K and traditions that have made Kauffman Stadium a beloved home for generations of Royals fans while creating a forward-leaning future.

“You know, every day when I walk in the stadium, I walk over those bricks outside of Gate C, and I look down. I see some different names every day. Some of them I know, some I don’t know. But it does remind me how much this place means to so many and what it also means to me personally,” offered John Sherman. “So, we’re gonna make sure we take those memories to our new home.”
