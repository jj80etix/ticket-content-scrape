# Hive Secures $27 Million Investment From Frontier Growth to Expand Live Events Marketing Platform

## Summary

Live-events marketing platform Hive raised a $27M Series B from Frontier Growth to expand its ticketing/CRM data integrations, grow internationally, and build "AI-powered assistants" for event marketers. The company, which already integrates with Etix among 40+ ticketing platforms, frames the raise around a persistent industry problem: ticketing and marketing data living in separate systems. No launch date was given for the AI tools.

### Key Points
- $27M Series B led by Frontier Growth (previously put $30M into atVenu); Hive has raised against 1,600+ venues/promoters/festivals served and >$1B in attributed ticket sales.
- Platform integrates with 40+ ticketing systems including Ticketmaster, AXS, Eventim, Etix, Tixr and DICE; syncs purchase history, geography, and artist/genre preference into marketing audiences.
- Ticketmaster named Hive an official marketing partner in June, letting TM1 Marketing audience lists migrate into Hive.
- Customer case study: ASM Richmond reports $500K+ in ticket revenue attributed to email campaigns in its first three months on the platform.
- Planned next phase: AI assistants to automate campaign prep and surface sales opportunities, funded by this raise; no timetable announced.

### Highlights
- $27M Series B from Frontier Growth, announced Oct. 21; Hive already lists Etix as one of its 40+ ticketing integrations.
- CEO framing: "Live events have always been a data problem disguised as a marketing problem" — directly names the client-side pain point this tool addresses.
- Roadmap explicitly includes AI-powered assistants for campaign automation, with no disclosed launch date.

### Industry Problem
Venues, promoters and festivals struggle to connect fragmented ticketing data with marketing tools, making it hard to identify likely buyers, personalize outreach, or measure which campaigns actually drive ticket revenue — a client pain point Hive is capitalizing on.

### Proposed Solution
Hive's answer is a dedicated marketing layer that ingests ticketing data (via its existing Etix integration, among others) and will add AI assistants for campaign automation. Since Etix is already a connected platform, worth monitoring Hive's AI roadmap as a potential channel partner or benchmark for what venue/promoter clients will expect from Etix's own marketing/data tooling.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Hive-Frontier.webp)

Hive has announced a $27 million Series B investment from Frontier Growth, positioning the live events marketing technology company to expand its ticketing integrations, develop AI-powered tools and grow its presence internationally.

The investment, announced October 21, will support Hive’s next phase of development as it works to help venues, promoters and festivals connect ticketing data with marketing campaigns and customer relationship management tools.

Founded in 2014 by Ian Roberts and Patrick Hannigan, Hive serves more than 1,600 venues, promoters and festivals. The company says its platform has helped customers generate more than $1 billion in ticket sales by connecting fan purchase information with targeted email, SMS, advertising and other marketing campaigns.

Hive’s software is designed to address a longstanding challenge for event organizers: ticketing and marketing data often reside in separate systems, making it difficult to identify potential ticket buyers, personalize outreach and measure which promotional efforts translate into sales.

The platform currently supports more than 40 ticketing integrations, including Ticketmaster, AXS, Eventim, Etix, Tixr and DICE. Those connections allow organizers to build marketing audiences using information such as ticket purchase history, geographic location, artist preferences and musical genres.

Rather than relying solely on traditional marketing measurements such as email open rates and clickthrough rates, Hive enables customers to track ticket revenue associated with individual campaigns.

The company cited a customer case study involving ASM Richmond, which reported more than $500,000 in sales linked to email marketing during its first three months using the platform.

“Live events have always been a data problem disguised as a marketing problem,” said Ian Roberts, Hive’s CEO and co-founder. “For more than a decade, we have built the infrastructure that connects ticket buyers to the people putting on the show.”

Roberts said Frontier Growth’s investment will help the company expand its technology and reach across the live entertainment business.

The investment follows Hive’s growing relationships with major ticketing providers. In June, [Ticketmaster announced Hive as an official marketing partner](https://business.ticketmaster.com/ticketmaster-and-hive-partner-to-power-smarter-event-marketing/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), allowing ticketing activity to synchronize automatically with Hive’s marketing platform.

The partnership also allows Ticketmaster clients to transfer existing TM1 Marketing audience lists into Hive, combining previously collected customer information with new ticketing data to support marketing campaigns.

Hive plans to use its expanded resources to develop AI-powered assistants that simplify campaign preparation, reduce repetitive administrative work and help event organizers identify opportunities to increase ticket sales.

The planned tools will focus on automating portions of campaign development and helping marketing teams act on customer data while retaining control over messaging and fan relationships. Hive also intends to expand its ticketing, advertising and data integrations.

The company has not announced a specific launch timetable for the new AI capabilities.

“Hive has established a strong position in live events with products that are central to how its customers sell tickets, supported by proprietary data and emerging network effects,” said Andrew Lindner of Frontier Growth.

“The live events market is large, underserved by generic tools and moving quickly toward automation. Hive is positioned to lead that shift, and we are excited to partner with Ian and the team as they scale.”

The investment comes as ticketing technology companies place greater emphasis on connecting event transactions, customer data and automated marketing systems. As [TicketNews previously reported following vivenu’s September AI summit](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/vivenu-ai-summit-ticketing-fan-data/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), fragmented customer information remains a significant obstacle for venues and event organizers seeking to take advantage of artificial intelligence.

Hive’s approach is built around connecting multiple ticketing systems to a dedicated marketing platform, allowing customers to work with ticket buyer information from different providers.

The company, which participated in Y Combinator’s startup accelerator program, says it has sent more than 5 billion emails, SMS messages and Meta advertising campaigns on behalf of its customers since its founding.

Frontier Growth, established in 1999 and based in Charlotte, North Carolina, specializes in investments in vertical software and AI companies. The firm works with founders to support expansion through investment capital and operational resources.

Financial terms beyond the announced $27 million investment, including Hive’s valuation, were not disclosed.
