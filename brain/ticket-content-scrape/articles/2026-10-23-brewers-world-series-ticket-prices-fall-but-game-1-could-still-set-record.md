# Brewers World Series Ticket Prices Fall, but Game 1 Could Still Set Record

## Summary

Resale prices for Friday's Brewers-Rays World Series Game 1 in Milwaukee pulled back sharply from mid-week highs but remain on pace to be among the most expensive World Series games ever recorded. Ticket Club's Game 1 get-in price fell 21% from $2,920 (Oct. 20) to $2,302, while TicketData separately clocked a $2,188 get-in — still above the prior all-time World Series record of $1,809 (2016 Cubs-Indians Game 4). Analysts attribute the scale of pricing to Milwaukee's small market size and 44-year title drought, while Tampa Bay's games remain far cheaper in comparison.

### Key Points
- Game 1 get-in price: $2,920 (Oct. 20 snapshot) → $2,302 (Oct. 23), a 21% pullback; average asking price fell 16% to $5,587.
- TicketData's independent Game 1 get-in of $2,188 still exceeds the prior World Series record of $1,809 (2016 Game 4, Cubs-Indians).
- Milwaukee's weighted-average asking price (~$5,222) is nearly 3x Tampa Bay's (~$1,760) across potential home games.
- Brewers' decade-old $1,000 "Timeless Ticket" promotion drew separate complaints over downgraded seating for World Series redemptions; team apologized and let holders keep both the WS seat and a future-game redemption.
- Market remains thin and volatile — comparable swings seen in Friday's Knicks home-opener resale pricing ($1,000 → $2,500 in hours).

### Highlights
- Record-threatening pricing: Game 1 get-in at $2,188-$2,302, versus the $1,809 all-time World Series high.
- 21-29% single-game price declines within three days show how fast secondary-market demand signals move pre-event.
- Milwaukee's per-ticket averages run ~3x Tampa Bay's despite being in the same series.

### Industry Problem
Extreme, fast-moving secondary-market volatility around marquee events makes pricing opaque and unpredictable for fans, and promotional long-dated ticket vouchers (like Timeless Ticket) create fulfillment disputes when demand spikes years later.

### Proposed Solution
None proposed in the piece itself. For a primary platform, the signal is to monitor marquee-event resale volatility as a proxy for willingness-to-pay and to ensure any long-dated voucher/credit programs have explicit, enforceable seating-tier guarantees to avoid Timeless-Ticket-style disputes.

Ticket prices for the Milwaukee Brewers’ World Series opener have fallen considerably from their highs earlier this week, but Friday’s matchup against the Tampa Bay Rays could still become the most expensive World Series game ever recorded on the resale market.

As of press time at noon Eastern on Friday, Oct. 23, 2026, updated Ticket Club estimates provided to TicketNews remained at approximately $2,302 per ticket for the Game 1 member get-in price, down 21% from the $2,920 recorded in an Oct. 20 snapshot. The average asking price across available tickets had also declined, falling from $6,652 to $5,587.

## World Series Prices Retreat From Earlier Highs

The latest Ticket Club figures show a meaningful correction in Milwaukee’s ticket market since [TicketNews examined World Series prices on Oct. 20](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/brewers-rays-world-series-ticket-prices-timeless-ticket/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

At that point, the estimated Game 1 get-in had climbed to $2,920, while Saturday’s Game 2 stood at $2,689.

The updated figures, confirmed unchanged at the noon Eastern publication cutoff, put those prices at $2,302 and $1,917, respectively, representing declines of approximately 21% and 29%.

The retreat extends beyond the cheapest available offers. Game 1’s median estimated asking price declined from $5,975 to $4,925, while the average fell approximately 16% to $5,587.

Pagello said the pullback is less surprising than the extraordinary pricing levels that preceded it.

“I’m actually not surprised that prices have fallen from the $3,000 level,” he told TicketNews. “That’s just an extraordinary amount of money for Game 1 of a World Series, far exceeding anything we’ve ever seen for any World Series game.”

“What’s much more surprising to me is that prices managed to reach $3,000 in the first place, and then were able to hold around that level for much of this week,” he added. “Even after the recent pullback, we’re still looking at potentially record-setting prices. That puts the decline into perspective.”

TicketData’s longer-term tracking reinforces the scale of the initial run-up. Its Friday figures showed Game 1’s get-in price up 101% over the preceding 14 days, with Game 2 rising 61% over the same period.

The two datasets capture different aspects of the market. Ticket Club’s comparison measures changes between inventory snapshots supplied Oct. 20 and Oct. 23, while TicketData’s historical and trend figures reflect its own resale-market tracking. Ticket Club’s figures are estimated member prices derived from wholesale listing data rather than observed completed transactions.

The latest Ticket Club estimates for all seven potential World Series games show that Milwaukee’s early home games have become less expensive while prices for the games in Tampa Bay have moved higher.

The changes are not uniform even within Milwaukee. While the estimated minimum price for Game 1 has declined, the get-in price for a potential Game 7 has increased 18% since Tuesday, reaching $2,651.

Friday’s Game 1 snapshot also shows that the cheapest available offer is now a numbered-section seat rather than the standing-room admission that established the minimum price in the earlier analysis.

## Tampa Bay Remains Much Cheaper, but Still Historically Expensive

The contrast between the two World Series host cities remains substantial.

Across the potential home games in the latest Ticket Club dataset, Milwaukee’s available-ticket-weighted average asking price is approximately $5,222, compared with $1,760 for Tampa Bay.

That puts Milwaukee’s overall average nearly three times as high, although the comparison includes different game dates, seating categories and conditional matchups.

TicketData’s noon Eastern snapshot shows a similar divide. Prices for Milwaukee’s potential home games ranged from approximately $1,915 to $2,188, while Tampa Bay’s three potential home dates ranged from $818 to $856.

Pagello cautioned that the difference should not be interpreted as evidence of weak interest in Tampa Bay.

“I think it’s important to emphasize that Tampa Bay’s lower prices aren’t necessarily a knock on the Rays or their fanbase,” he said. “Their tickets are still incredibly expensive by historical standards.”

He pointed to the Arizona Diamondbacks’ home games during the 2023 World Series, when get-in prices were generally in the $300 range.

“Tampa Bay’s current prices, around $800, would still rank among roughly the 20 most expensive World Series games ever,” Pagello said.

The Rays’ recent championship history also differs significantly from Milwaukee’s.

The Brewers are appearing in their first World Series since 1982, while Tampa Bay last reached the Fall Classic in 2020. However, that pandemic-era championship was played entirely at a neutral site in Texas, meaning Rays fans have not attended a World Series game at their home ballpark since 2008.

Neither franchise has won a World Series championship.

## Timeless Ticket Controversy Adds Another Dimension

Milwaukee’s extraordinary resale prices have also provided context for the controversy surrounding the Brewers’ decade-old $1,000 Timeless Ticket promotion.

The promotion, originally launched in 2014, allowed purchasers to redeem a special ticket for a future Brewers home game, including the World Series.

However, some purchasers complained that the seating options presented for this year’s championship were largely limited to upper-level sections or standing-room admission despite the promotion’s promise of access to the best available seats.

As [TicketNews reported earlier this week](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/brewers-timeless-ticket-world-series-seating-dispute/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), Brewers President of Business Operations Rick Schlesinger acknowledged problems with the redemption process and apologized to affected fans.

The organization subsequently announced that qualifying purchasers could retain their World Series admission without losing the ability to redeem their original Timeless Ticket for another game in a future season.

The dispute illustrates the enormous potential value of a World Series ticket secured years before the event, while also raising separate questions about how the team fulfilled its original seating commitments. There is no evidence in the available pricing data establishing that the Timeless Ticket situation has directly affected resale prices.

## Will Prices Fall Further Before First Pitch?

For fans still considering buying tickets to Friday’s opener, the biggest unanswered question is whether the recent declines will continue as game time approaches.

Pagello said the outcome is exceptionally difficult to predict, particularly when the resale market becomes thin enough that relatively modest changes in buying activity can produce large price swings.

“It’s nearly impossible to predict where prices go from here,” he said. “There are reasonable arguments for movement in either direction.”

He pointed to the New York Knicks’ home opener earlier this week as an example of how quickly prices can change for a high-demand event.

According to Pagello, the Knicks’ get-in price climbed from approximately $1,000 to $2,200 during the afternoon, fell to around $1,800 a few hours before tipoff, then surged to $2,500 roughly 20 minutes before the game began.

Although he considers a similarly dramatic increase unlikely for Friday’s World Series opener, he said Milwaukee prices had been climbing again during much of the morning.

“Momentum can reverse very quickly, and with so few tickets remaining, it doesn’t take much to move the market significantly in either direction,” Pagello said.

The Brewers and Rays are [scheduled to begin Game 1](https://www.mlb.com/news/2026-mlb-world-series-faq-schedule-times-teams-and-more?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) at American Family Field at 8 p.m. ET on Friday, with Jacob Misiorowski starting for Milwaukee against Tampa Bay’s Nick Martinez.

Whether Milwaukee ultimately establishes a new World Series ticket-price record will depend on what happens during the final hours before first pitch, when TicketData takes the measurement used for its historical rankings.
