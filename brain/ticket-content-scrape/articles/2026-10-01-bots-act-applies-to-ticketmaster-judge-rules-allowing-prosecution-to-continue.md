# BOTS Act Applies To Ticketmaster, Judge Rules, Allowing Prosecution To Continue

## Summary

A federal judge denied Ticketmaster/Live Nation's motion to dismiss the FTC's BOTS Act and all-in-pricing lawsuit, ruling the Act applies to platforms (not just individual resellers) and allowing the case — joined by seven state AGs — to proceed to discovery. The FTC alleges Ticketmaster's TradeDesk software lets it identify brokers violating ticket limits but that the company declines to stop them because enforcement would cut into its own resale revenue, citing an internal email describing a policy to "turn a blind eye," and that it previously rejected identity verification in 2021 because it was "too effective." The suit also revives bait-and-switch all-in-pricing allegations predating Ticketmaster's 2023 voluntary switch to upfront pricing.

### Key Points
- Judge Frimpong (C.D. Cal.) rejected Ticketmaster's argument that the BOTS Act doesn't apply to platforms; case proceeds to discovery.
- FTC alleges 5 brokers controlled 6,345 Ticketmaster accounts holding 246,407 tickets across 2,594 events.
- Internal Ticketmaster email allegedly described a policy to "turn a blind eye" to broker ticket-limit violations because enforcement would reduce revenue.
- FTC says Ticketmaster rejected third-party identity verification in 2021 specifically because it was "too effective" at stopping bots/brokers.
- Suit also re-alleges deceptive drip-pricing predating Ticketmaster's 2023 voluntary all-in-pricing switch.
- Joined by AGs of VA, UT, FL, TN, NE, IL, CO; seeks injunction plus monetary relief.

### Highlights
- Ruling clears the way for discovery into Ticketmaster's internal broker-enforcement decisions.
- 246,407 tickets alleged held by just 5 broker-controlled account clusters (6,345 accounts).
- 2021 rejection of identity verification cited as deliberate under-enforcement, not technical limitation.

### Industry Problem
The core allegation is a direct financial conflict of interest: a primary platform that also profits from secondary resale has reduced incentive to enforce bot/ticket-limit protections, because doing so would cut off a second revenue stream from the same inventory.

### Proposed Solution
The suit implies the fix: deploy and actually enforce identity verification and purchase-limit tooling against bots/bulk brokers, rather than monitoring without acting. For an independent primary platform like Etix, this is a trust/positioning differentiator — since Etix doesn't profit from brokered resale on its own marketplace, actively enforcing (not just monitoring) ticket limits and bot defenses is a credible claim competitors facing this suit cannot easily make.

## Transcript

![GettyImages 1367002408](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/GettyImages-1367002408-scaled.jpg)

By Aitor Diago/Getty Images

The Federal Trade Commission’s prosecution of Live Nation and Ticketmaster for violation of the BOTS Act can move forward, a federal judge ruled Monday.

Judge Maame Ewusi-Mensah Frimpong of the Central District of California denied Ticketmaster’s motion to dismiss which argued that the BOTS Act was aimed at individual resellers rather than platforms themselves.

“The BOTS Act does not preclude platforms from facing liability, and the court will not read it as doing so,” Frimpong wrote. The case now moves to discovery.

“Live Nation and Ticketmaster could have implemented measures to curtail continuing violations of ticket purchase limits,” Frimpong wrote. “However, the FTC maintains that Live Nation and Ticketmaster ‘have reduced, discontinued, or declined to implement the use of tools that could effectively enforce ticket limits against brokers,’ thereby enabling circumvention.”

The suit — filed in September 2026 and joined by the attorneys-general of Virginia, Utah, Florida, Tennessee, Nebraska, Illinois and Colorado — claims that Ticketmaster and Live Nation “tacitly worked” with secondary sellers, allowing them to buy tickets in the thousands and then list them for resale at marked-up prices, often on Ticketmaster’s own platform, thus giving Ticketmaster multiple bites at the revenue apple: from the initial purchase, the listing by the secondary seller and the consumer who ultimately buys the resold ticket.

The FTC says an unnamed senior Ticketmaster executive wrote in an internal email that the companies “turn a blind eye as a matter of policy” to brokers’ violations of posted ticket limits and that five brokers controlled 6,345 Ticketmaster accounts and possessed 246,407 concert tickets to 2,594 events.

The suit says that Ticketmaster’s TradeDesk software gives the company the ability to determine which brokers are violating purchase limits, but armed with that information, does nothing to stop it.

“The companies also have consistently declined to deploy additional technology that would more effectively prevent brokers from evading ticket limits because such tactics would decrease their revenue, according to internal company documents,” the FTC said in a statement. “For example, the company in 2021 opted against using third-party identity verification because it was ‘too effective.’”

The suit says Ticketmaster and Live Nation’s “illegal conduct frustrates artists’ desire to maintain affordable ticket prices that fit the needs of ordinary American families, costing ordinary fans millions of dollars every year.”

The lawsuit further alleges bait and switch by Ticketmaster by advertising list prices that didn’t include mandatory fees. Ticketmaster voluntarily switched to all-in upfront pricing in June 2023 for tickets at Live Nation venues ahead of an FTC rule change made in the waning days of the Biden Administration that mandated all-in pricing across the ticketing industry.

The suit also alleges BOTS Act violations, saying that Ticketmaster is complicit in the use of automated bots and deceptive practices by secondary sellers on its site. That echoes a defense by Key Investment Group — a conglomerate that controls numerous resale sites that is also being sued for BOTS Act violation — who said, in short, that it cannot violate the BOTS Act because Ticketmaster is aware of the “common” tactics used by secondary sellers.

“American live entertainment is the best in the world and should be accessible to all of us,” FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson said in a statement. “It should not cost an arm and a leg to take the family to a baseball game or attend your favorite musician’s show.”

The suit asks a judge to permanenly enjoin Ticketmaster and Live Nation from violations of the FTC Act, the BOTS Act and the consumer protection laws of the plaintiff states, as well as undetermined monetary relief and fines for BOTS Act violations.
