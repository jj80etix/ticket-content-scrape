# Conference news: Duo of November events to spotlight Balkan industry

## Summary

IQ Magazine previews two Balkan industry conferences this month: Zagreb's Crowd and Loud (starting Nov. 12), which added Dua Lipa's manager Dugi Lipa as a keynote speaker, and Skopje's PIN Music Conference, now in its 13th edition. Both events focus on the regional live-business landscape, including sessions on surviving global/local market pressures and live-event marketing.

### Key Points
- Crowd and Loud (Zagreb) starts Nov. 12; keynote speakers include Dugi Lipa (Dua Lipa's manager) and Sziget Festival's Tamás Kádár.
- Session themes include regional market conditions ("The Whole Region's Rocking") and live-event marketing.
- PIN Music Conference (Skopje, Macedonia) returns for its 13th edition.
- Coverage also references recent ESNS and ADE conference activity.

## Transcript

Croatia's Crowd and Loud and Macedonia's PIN return this month, plus the latest from ESNS and ADE

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/11/LIVELOUD-proslogodisnja-1-scaled.jpg.webp)

Crowd and Loud, 2025, under the name Live and Loud

In upcoming conference news, Zagreb’s Crowd And Loud has completed its speaker lineup for its event in the Croatian capital next week, with the addition of Dugi Lipa, Dua Lipa’s manager, as keynote speaker.

He joins previously announced speakers including Jim Digby, president of Show Makers and tour director for Linkin Park, and Sziget Festival’s Tamás Kádár, for the conference which kicks off on 12 November. Kádár will deliver a keynote session titled ‘Island of Freedom: How to Survive Global Giants and Local Politics’.

Other sessions include a panel titled ‘The Whole Region’s Rocking’, which will explore the condition of the live industry in the Balkans, and ‘Everything is Brand New’, a look into live event marketing in the region.

“Following last year’s successful edition, the region’s largest event industry conference returns with an even stronger programme and a clear ambition: to bring together, under one roof, the people behind the biggest concerts, festivals, sporting spectacles and events across the region,” organisers said.

**PIN Music Conference  
**PIN will also return for its 13th edition in Skopje in Macedonia from 26-28 November, preparing to welcome over 300 delegates across its three-day event.

The programme includes market specific networking sessions on France and the Balkans, as well as a panel on touring in the Croatian market. Also among its more than 30 panels are ‘Festival Dream or Solo Reality: Who Wins in 2026?’ and a live music ‘PR Clinic’.

Delegates are attending from international booking agencies, festivals, and promoters, including Midnight Mango, Reik Management, Yourupe, SoAlive, and Charmenko.

**ESNS**  
Eurosonic Noorderslag (ESNS) has also named Robbert Baruch from Universal Music Group, European Commissioner Glenn Micallef, music business executive Scott Cohen, and Spotify’s Ben Patashnik among a raft of new speakers for its 2027 edition.

Taking place from January 13 to 16 2027 in Groningen, highlights include Universal Music Group and KLAY examining new AI models and artist opportunities. Micallef, who is European Commissioner for Intergenerational Fairness, Youth, Culture and Sport, will speak on the role of music in Europe’s cultural future. Spotify’s Patashnik is due to hold a session on editorial strategy and international artist discovery.

**Map Point Summit  
**In other conference news, Amsterdam Dance Event (ADE) and EDM festival producer Insomniac Events have announced Map Point Summit, a new electronic music and rave culture conference.

Set to debut across two successive weekends in May 2027 at Virgin Hotels Las Vegas between 14-16 May and 21-23 May, it will coincide with EDC festival.

“Rave culture was built by ravers, for ravers. It started as a grassroots, community-driven movement with its own values, spirit, and way of doing things,” Insomniac founder and CEO Pasquale Rotella said.

He added: “As our scene has grown, I believe it’s become increasingly important to protect what made it special in the first place. That’s why Map Point Summit means so much to me. We need to support independent promoters, share knowledge, create opportunities for the next generation, and work together to build an industry that allows rave culture to thrive on its own terms. I’m excited to partner with ADE to help bring that vision to life.”
