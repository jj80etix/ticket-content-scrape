# Australian Frequency festivals remove Israeli DJs citing ‘violent threats’

## Summary

Australian electronic festival Frequency removed Israeli DJs from its lineup after citing "violent threats," amid ongoing uncertainty over the event's still-undisclosed location; organizer Fomo Events has faced online pressure over both decisions.

### Key Points
- Organizers framed the removal as a safety response rather than a stated political position.
- Fomo Events has fielded repeated public questions about the festival's undisclosed venue location.

## Transcript

## Australian Frequency festivals remove Israeli DJs citing ‘violent threats’

Promoters say security concerns have led to five Israeli acts being removed from NSW and Melbourne events, with the venue and format of both now uncertain

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Frequency-Forest-Australia-2025.jpg)

Frequency Forest 2025 © Frequency Forest

Two Australian psytrance festivals under the Frequency brand have removed Israeli DJs from their lineups, and now face uncertainty around venues, as organisers claim the decisions come as a result of threats of violence from boycott campaigners.

The 5,000-cap Frequency Forest camping festival, set to take place at New South Wales’ Maitland Showground, is now undergoing a safety and threat assessment to determine “the best way forward”, event bosses said, after the five DJs were removed from its lineup. Frequency Forest and its related event Frequency in Melbourne are co-promoted by Old Monk Entertainments and Fomo Events.

In a public statement, the festival said it had “received direct written threats referring to serious violence, including threats involving fire being directed the event and associated venues.” As a result, they added changes are being made to the event format to enable more effective security measures, with details to be confirmed directly in emails to ticketholders.

However, subsequent to the festival’s statement, Maitland Showground said in an online post that Frequency Forest was cancelled, saying that it was “a privately booked external hire event organised by Fomo Events and was not an event organised or operated by Maitland Showground”. No mention of cancellation has been made by promoters as of time of writing.

Festival organisers said the threats have been referred to police and that they are forming “part of the ongoing safety and threat assessment”.

Organisers did not name the DJs who had been removed, but Israeli DJs Astrix, Berg, Ghostrider, Ranji, and Ritmo were on the original bill. None of these artists have commented publicly.

The festival posted an anonymised screenshot of an email it claimed had been received on 15 September from an unknown sender, which spoke of entering the venue with “10-15 litres of gasoline” as well as referring to the venue’s fire precautions.

New South Police said it has launched an investigation into threatening emails received by the Maitland Showground venue, via *Rolling Stone AU / NZ.* Victoria Police are also investigating an incident in which two cars belonging to an event partner were set on fire in a Victoria suburb, though Frequency Forest said it is not at this time connecting this incident to the alleged threats.

Promoters from Old Monk told the *Newcastle Herald Sun* that they estimate the cost of relocations and lineup changes between AUS$1.6-1.8 million (€993,000 – €1.12 million).

> “Frequency Forest became the subject of a sustained boycott campaign connected to the inclusion of Israeli artists”

Meanwhile, sister event *Frequency* *Festival* in Melbourne has told ticketholders in an email, seen by *IQ,* that Astrix, Ranji, Ritmo, and Ghostrider’s performances have all been postponed to a new date. No public statement has been issued to this effect. Berg was not due to play at the Melbourne event.

The remaining six acts in the festival will go ahead on 26-27 September, but the event is yet to announce a venue, only five days out from its start. Many of Fomo Events’ online posts in recent weeks have received comments from users asking for clarity about its location.

Despite the uncertainty around both festivals, Fomo said in its email to Melbourne ticketholders it is still “committed to delivering these events”.

However, the email sent to ticketholders for the Melbourne eventsaid a venue has been selected “within 5km of the Melbourne CBD”, but that it will not be disclosed to fans until 24 hours before the event, “as part of ongoing security measures”.

Speaking of the New South Wales festival, Frequency Forest said in its public statement: “Following our lineup announcement, Frequency Forest became the subject of a sustained boycott campaign connected to the inclusion of Israeli artists.” It continued: “This resulted in significant pressure being directed towards venues and organisations associated with the event.”

The festival said that “until now, we have worked privately with venues, artists, authorities, and stakeholders to find solutions” but that “unfortunately, the situation has moved well beyond venue pressure.”

This comes as online campaign groups have gained traction with calls to boycott both events, as well as the Dreamstate techno and trance event scheduled for the Sydney Showground in February next year.

Frequency Forest festival said: “We respect everyone’s right to peacefully protest, advocate for causes they believe in \[and\] express political views. However, threats of violence, intimidation and deliberate property damage are a very different matter. They cannot be treated as ordinary peaceful protest.”
