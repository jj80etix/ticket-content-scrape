# Live Nation Settles Drakeo the Ruler Estate Lawsuit, but Separate Trial Looms

## Summary

Live Nation, its C3 Presents subsidiary, and several festival-security contractors reached a conditional settlement with the estate of rapper Drakeo the Ruler, resolving negligence claims over his December 2021 backstage stabbing at the Once Upon a Time in L.A. festival. The settlement is contingent on terms to be completed within 45 days and does not establish any finding of negligence. Separate claims from Drakeo's brother and entourage members remain set for a jury trial on Nov. 30; conflicting reports exist on whether a parallel suit from Drakeo's son has also settled.

### Key Points
- Settlement reached roughly five years after the Dec. 18, 2021 stabbing; dismissal depends on undisclosed conditions to be satisfied within 45 days.
- Comes about two weeks after a judge denied the estate's bid for Live Nation/C3 financial records in pursuit of punitive damages (Sept. 24 ruling).
- Consolidated case (LA Superior Court No. 22STCV04087) still has other plaintiffs headed to a Nov. 30 jury trial over alleged inadequate backstage security.
- Live Nation has consistently denied liability, characterizing the attack as an unforeseeable criminal act.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/drakeo-the-ruler.jpg)

Live Nation and several companies involved in the 2021 Once Upon a Time in L.A. festival have reached a conditional settlement with the estate of rapper Drakeo the Ruler, resolving one set of claims over his fatal backstage stabbing while related litigation remains scheduled for trial next month.

The estate, administered by Drakeo’s mother, Darrylene Corniel, agreed to settle its remaining negligence claims against Live Nation Worldwide, its subsidiary C3 Presents and other festival-related companies, according to an Oct. 9 court notice described by [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/drakeo-the-ruler-settles-live-nation-lawsuit-stabbing/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). The settling defendants also include Bobby Dee Presents, Contemporary Services Corporation, Black Tide Events and Hollywood Elite Security, according to [Rolling Stone](https://ca.rollingstone.com/music/drakeo-the-ruler-mom-settles-live-nation-wrongful-death/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

The agreement is described as conditional, with dismissal dependent on “the satisfactory completion of specified terms to be performed within 45 days,” Rolling Stone reported. Neither the settlement amount nor the obligations still to be completed have been disclosed. The agreement does not amount to a court finding that the defendants were negligent.

[TMZ reported the settlement](https://www.tmz.com/2026/10/14/drakeo-the-ruler-estate-settles-wrongful-death-lawsuit/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) Wednesday, nearly five years after the killing. Billboard reported that a Live Nation representative declined to comment on the resolution.

## Separate Claims Still Headed Toward Trial

The estate’s settlement does not resolve negligence claims brought by Drakeo’s brother, Devante Caldwell, who performs as Ralfy the Plug, and members of the rapper’s entourage who were backstage during the attack. Those claims remain part of consolidated proceedings in Los Angeles Superior Court, with a jury trial scheduled for Nov. 30, according to Billboard and Rolling Stone.

There are conflicting reports about a parallel lawsuit filed on behalf of Drakeo’s young son, Caiden Caldwell. Billboard reported that an attorney for Caiden confirmed those claims had also settled, while Rolling Stone reported Thursday that the son’s claims remain pending. TicketNews has not independently confirmed which account reflects the case’s current status.

The related lawsuits were consolidated under Los Angeles Superior Court case No. 22STCV04087. The estate’s agreement does not, by itself, dispose of claims brought by other plaintiffs in the litigation.

## Festival Security at the Center of the Dispute

Drakeo, whose legal name was Darrell Caldwell, was 28 when he was stabbed backstage on Dec. 18, 2021, shortly before a scheduled performance at the festival. The event was held at the then-Banc of California Stadium and surrounding Exposition Park grounds in Los Angeles. The venue has since been renamed BMO Stadium. No one has been arrested or charged in the killing, according to the latest reporting.

In lawsuits filed after his death, relatives and associates alleged that inadequate security, screening and backstage access controls allowed a large group of attackers to enter an area that should have been restricted to performers and authorized personnel. Corniel [blamed festival security failures shortly after her son’s death](https://www.ticketnews.com/2021/12/rapper-fatally-stabbed-at-live-nation-festival-in-california/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), questioning why so many people had been allowed backstage.

Live Nation and C3 have disputed those claims. As [TicketNews detailed in September](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/live-nation-c3-drakeo-punitive-damages-lawsuit/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the promoters argued that they had developed a substantial security plan, retained specialized consultants, screened entrants and coordinated with law enforcement. They also maintained that the attack was an unforeseeable criminal confrontation, a position [Live Nation advanced in its early responses to the lawsuits](https://www.ticketnews.com/2022/07/live-nation-no-liability-for-drakeo-the-rulers-backstage-death/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

In more recent filings, the promoters argued that video showed Drakeo initially escaping the confrontation before returning toward the fight, which they contended severed any causal responsibility on their part. The plaintiffs disputed that characterization, and no jury has resolved the competing accounts of what happened backstage.

## Settlement Follows Punitive-Damages Discovery Ruling

The agreement follows a late-stage legal dispute over whether the estate could obtain the promoters’ financial records while pursuing punitive damages. The estate argued that evidence developed in discovery supported allegations of conscious disregard for performer safety. Live Nation and C3 said the evidence did not meet California’s heightened standard for pretrial disclosure of a defendant’s finances.

On Sept. 24, Los Angeles Superior Court Judge James I. Montgomery [denied the estate’s financial-discovery request](https://www.decisiondepot.legal/cases/331023?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), finding it had not established a substantial probability of prevailing on its punitive-damages theory. The judge also concluded that the evidence cited did not sufficiently support the estate’s assertions about corporate decision-makers and deliberate disregard of safety risks.

That ruling addressed access to financial information, not whether the defendants were negligent in providing festival security. It came about two weeks before the estate filed its settlement notice. Publicly available reporting does not establish whether the ruling influenced the decision to settle.

Once the settlement conditions are satisfied, the estate’s claims would be dismissed. The Nov. 30 trial remains on the calendar for other plaintiffs seeking to hold festival organizers and security companies responsible for what happened backstage, unless another settlement or scheduling change intervenes.
