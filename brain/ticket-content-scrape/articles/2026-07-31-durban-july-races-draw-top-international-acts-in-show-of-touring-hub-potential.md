# Durban July races draw top international acts in show of touring hub potential

## Summary

Durban's Hollywoodbets Durban July horse-racing event featured international touring acts (Bryson Tiller, Masego, Swae Lee) for the first time in its 130-year history, part of a bid by organisers to position Durban as a live-music touring destination rather than the "secondary market" it has long been treated as. The concerts were staged within The Ascots, a premium hospitality complex run by Aline Media Group, and the 2026 edition drew significant projected economic impact for the city.

### Key Points
- Hollywoodbets Durban July hosted three international music artists for the first time in its 130-year run: Bryson Tiller, Masego, and Swae Lee.
- eThekwini Municipality projected ~45,000 attendees, ~R254m in direct visitor spending, ~R880m in total economic impact, and ~1,270 FTE jobs.
- Hotel occupancy across Durban was projected to reach ~80% over the event weekend.
- The Ascots, a 4,000-square-metre premium hospitality space by Aline Media Group, hosted the concerts alongside the racing on the southern lawns of Greyville Racecourse.
- Founder Sibo Mhlungu frames the effort as repositioning Durban within South Africa's touring circuit, complementing rather than competing with Johannesburg and Cape Town.

## Transcript

Organisers say combining music, racing, and lifestyle offerings can showcase Durban as a major live music location after being treated a s a secondary market "for years"

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Masego-1.png)

Masego at Hollywood Bets Durban July

**This year’s *Hollywoodbets Durban July* horse racing event hosted three international music artists for the first time in its 130-year history, as concerts from Bryson Tiller, Masego and Swae Lee led a bid to make live music a core commercial pillar of the lifestyle event.**

According to eThekwini Municipality, the 2026 edition was expected to attract around 45,000 attendees, generate about R254 million in direct visitor spending and deliver an estimated R880 million in total economic impact, while supporting about 1,270 full-time equivalent jobs across tourism, hospitality, retail, transport and entertainment. Hotel occupancy across the city was also projected to reach around 80% over the event weekend.

eThekwini Mayor Cyril Xaba described the event as one of Durban’s most important economic catalysts, saying its successful staging once again demonstrated the city’s ability to deliver “world-class events that drive tourism, stimulate economic growth and create meaningful opportunities,” while cementing Durban’s ambition as one of “Africa’s leading events and lifestyle destination.”

In light of that, the decision reflects a bigger strategy to increase the commercial value of Durban July as a destination event, encouraging greater visitor spending across hospitality, tourism and premium experiences.

While horse racing remains the foundation of the event, the expanded international line-up reflects how organisers are increasingly positioning music alongside fashion, premium hospitality and destination tourism as interconnected drivers of audience engagement and commercial value.

> “ For years, Durban has been treated as a secondary market for large shows”

The performances formed part of The Ascots, a premium hospitality experience occupying 4,000 square metres on the southern lawns of Greyville Racecourse and accommodating around 4,000 guests.

For Aline Media Group, the company behind The Ascots, the ambition extends beyond enhancing Durban July itself. Founder and CEO Sibo Mhlungu sees the concept as playing a role to reposition Durban within South Africa’s live entertainment scene.

“The focus is on the full experience, from arrival to the final act. As someone born and bred in Durban, this is more than just an event to me; it’s a way to uplift and contribute to my hometown. For years, Durban has been treated as a secondary market for large shows and has rarely been considered a destination for international artists or events of this scale,” Mhlungu said.

Rather than attempting to build a standalone international music festival, The Ascots embeds global touring talent within an event that already attracts substantial attention from luxury brands, fashion partners, corporate hospitality buyers and domestic tourism. In doing so, organisers spread the commercial value of international bookings across multiple revenue streams rather than relying primarily on concert ticket sales.

Speaking ahead of the event, Mhlungu described the vision as bringing globally recognised entertainment experiences to South Africa, instead of expecting audiences to travel abroad to experience them. “I don’t want to wait to go see someone at Coachella. I wanna bring Coachella back home,” he said, adding that the objective was to showcase “African exceptionalism on the world stage in our own backyard.”

While Johannesburg remains the country’s primary touring region and Cape Town continues to attract major international productions, Durban has sought to build its appeal through destination-led events that combine entertainment with tourism, hospitality and lifestyle programming. By that, the city has carved out its unique positioning within South Africa’s live entertainment ecosystem, complementing rather than competing directly with major cities like Johannesburg and Cape Town.
