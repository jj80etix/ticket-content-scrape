# Ellie Goulding Lawsuit Says Managers Were Barred From Steering Artists Away From Live Nation

## Summary

Pop singer Ellie Goulding has sued her former management team (TaP Management's Ben Mawson and Ed Millett, plus parent company HNOE Ltd.) in the UK, alleging she was not told Live Nation had controlled HNOE since 2015 and that her managers were contractually barred from steering her business away from Live Nation affiliates — even while owing her a duty to seek the best commercial terms across promoters, merchandisers, and other counterparties. The suit does not name Live Nation as a defendant, but the article ties it to the wider scrutiny of Live Nation's vertical integration, coming months after a federal jury found Live Nation/Ticketmaster liable for illegal monopoly maintenance in the primary-ticketing and live-events markets.

### Key Points
- Goulding was managed by Mawson and Millett from 2018–2025 at a 20% commission; Live Nation acquired 50.1% of TaP parent HNOE in 2015 and the remainder in 2019, before Goulding signed.
- The claim alleges Mawson/Millett were contractually barred from encouraging artists to reduce business with Live Nation group companies while managing Goulding.
- During the management period, Goulding entered agreements with Live Nation companies for touring, merchandise, and a documentary film.
- Live Nation itself is not a defendant; Goulding seeks unspecified equitable compensation/damages for alleged breach of fiduciary duty.
- Context: a federal jury found Live Nation and Ticketmaster liable for illegal monopoly maintenance earlier in 2026 (April), with state AG remedies and a DOJ settlement proposal still pending.

### Highlights
- Live Nation held majority/controlling ownership of an artist-management firm (TaP/HNOE) from 2015–2019+ without, per the claim, the represented artist's knowledge.
- Live Nation's 2025 annual report states its management arm represents ~360 artists — a largely under-scrutinized layer of its vertical integration alongside Ticketmaster, promotion, and venue ownership.
- Lawsuit filed July 24, 2026 in the UK; unadjudicated. Comes ~4 months after the April 2026 federal jury monopoly-maintenance verdict against Live Nation/Ticketmaster.

### Industry Problem
This surfaces a structural conflict-of-interest risk inherent to Live Nation's vertical integration: artist representatives with financial/contractual ties back to Live Nation may be structurally unable to negotiate neutrally across promoters, ticketing platforms, or merchandisers — a dynamic that compounds the market-structure concerns already being litigated in the primary antitrust case.

### Proposed Solution
No remedy is proposed in the item beyond the litigation itself. Watch trigger for Etix: whether the suit surfaces discovery evidence used by state AGs or the DOJ in the parallel antitrust remedies process, and whether it prompts wider scrutiny (or disclosure requirements) around ownership ties between artist management and primary ticketing/promotion — either would strengthen the competitive-openings case for independent platforms.

Ellie Goulding has sued her former managers over their undisclosed ties to Live Nation, alleging that the people charged with negotiating on her behalf were simultaneously subject to contractual restrictions that prevented them from encouraging artists to reduce their business with the live entertainment giant.

The lawsuit, filed in the United Kingdom against former TaP Management executives Ben Mawson and Ed Millett and TaP parent company HNOE Ltd., was [first reported by Variety](https://variety.com/2026/music/news/ellie-goulding-sues-former-managers-live-nation-1236826769/), which obtained a copy of the claim.

While much of the initial coverage has focused on Goulding’s allegation that she did not know Live Nation owned her management company, the contractual restrictions described in the complaint raise a broader conflict-of-interest question.

According to the claim, Mawson and Millett were subject to obligations that prohibited them from encouraging artists under their management “to cease to do business with” companies in the Live Nation group. Other restrictions allegedly prevented conduct that would reduce or diminish an artist’s business relationship with Live Nation or its affiliates.

## Live Nation Already Controlled TaP Parent When Goulding Signed

Mawson and Millett managed Goulding from 2018 through 2025 under an arrangement that paid them a 20% management commission, according to the claim.

But Live Nation’s involvement with their company predated Goulding’s arrival.

According to [Music Business Worldwide’s review of the lawsuit and corporate records](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/ellie-goulding-sues-former-managers-over-live-nation-ownership-of-tap/), Live Nation acquired 50.1% of HNOE, TaP’s parent company, in 2015. The lawsuit says Live Nation acquired the remaining interest in 2019.

That means Live Nation was already the controlling shareholder of Goulding’s management company’s parent when she signed with TaP in 2018.

Goulding alleges that she did not know about that relationship.

Following the 2019 transaction, the lawsuit says Mawson became HNOE’s CEO and that Mawson and Millett reported directly to an individual designated by Live Nation CEO Michael Rapino.

The claim alleges that Live Nation had exercised direct or indirect control over HNOE since 2015 and received dividends through its position as the company’s ultimate controlling shareholder.

Public corporate records support the broader ownership structure described in the complaint.

[Companies House currently lists](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/07323377/persons-with-significant-control) Live Nation Entertainment Inc. as directly controlling more than 50% but less than 75% of HNOE’s shares and voting rights. Another Live Nation entity, Artist Nation Management Limited, controls more than 25% but no more than 50%.

That second ownership path ultimately leads back to Live Nation as well. [Artist Nation Management is controlled](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/07437303/persons-with-significant-control) by Ticketmaster Europe Holdco Limited, while [Ticketmaster Europe Holdco is controlled](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/06704345/persons-with-significant-control) by Live Nation Entertainment.

Companies House records show Mawson and Millett both ceased to be persons with significant control of HNOE on Sept. 8, 2019.

## Goulding Did Business With Live Nation While Managers Took Commission

The alleged conflict was not merely theoretical, according to Goulding.

During the period Mawson and Millett represented her, Goulding entered agreements with Live Nation companies involving concert touring, merchandise and a documentary film.

The lawsuit argues that Mawson and Millett therefore had interests or obligations connected to Live Nation at the same time they were responsible for determining what counterparties offered Goulding the best commercial terms.

In other words, Goulding’s complaint is not simply that her management company had a corporate owner she did not know about. She alleges her representatives were unable to approach negotiations as entirely neutral agents because their own agreements limited their ability to steer artist business away from that owner.

That allegation could prove particularly significant in the artist-management business, where managers routinely help determine which promoters, agents, merchandisers and other companies receive an artist’s business.

Live Nation itself describes managers in much the same way. In its [2025 annual report](https://investors.livenationentertainment.com/sec-filings/annual-reports/content/0001335258-26-000009/lyv-20251231.htm), the company notes that artist managers negotiate on behalf of artists and generally receive a percentage of their earnings.

Goulding is seeking unspecified equitable compensation and/or damages for alleged breaches of fiduciary duty, along with other relief.

Live Nation is not a defendant in the action.

## Another Window Into Live Nation’s Vertical Integration

The dispute also highlights a portion of Live Nation’s sprawling entertainment business that receives considerably less attention than Ticketmaster, concert promotion or venue ownership.

Live Nation’s own annual report says its artist-management companies managed approximately **360 artists in 2025**, and the company describes itself as one of the world’s leading artist-management businesses based on the number of artists represented.

That management operation exists alongside a company that promotes concerts for thousands of artists, owns or controls hundreds of venues globally and operates Ticketmaster, the world’s dominant primary ticketing platform.

Goulding’s allegations therefore touch another layer of the vertical integration that has drawn extensive regulatory and legal scrutiny around Live Nation.

Earlier this year, a federal jury [found Live Nation and Ticketmaster liable for illegally maintaining monopoly power](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/04/live-nation-and-ticketmaster-operate-as-an-illegal-monopoly-jury-finds-in-landmark-antitrust-trial/) in portions of the U.S. live-events and primary-ticketing markets. Among other findings, jurors concluded that Live Nation improperly used its control of large amphitheaters in connection with concert promotion and that Ticketmaster maintained monopoly power through exclusionary conduct.

Live Nation continues to contest that verdict, while state attorneys general are pursuing remedies and the Justice Department separately seeks approval of a proposed settlement with the company.

Goulding’s lawsuit is unrelated to that antitrust litigation, and the allegations against her former managers have not been adjudicated. But the dispute puts a different form of vertical leverage under scrutiny: whether an artist’s supposedly independent representative can freely seek competing offers when that representative’s own contractual and financial relationships run back to one of the largest counterparties in the live entertainment business.

That is ultimately the question Goulding is asking the court to examine.

The claim was issued July 24. Mawson, Millett and TaP had not publicly responded to the allegations in reports published after the lawsuit emerged. Music Business Worldwide said it had sought comment from all three, while Live Nation did not immediately respond to that outlet. Goulding has since returned to Sarah Stennett for management and signed with WME for worldwide representation.
