# Live Nation Confirms Trump Attended DOJ Settlement Meeting in Amended Tunney Act Filing

## Summary

Live Nation amended its Tunney Act court disclosure to confirm President Trump briefly attended the March 5, 2026 meeting where the company and DOJ finalized their antitrust settlement terms, while denying he took part in substantive negotiations. The amendment, filed Oct. 21, also adds detail about a February Trump-Rapino call and names additional officials involved in the talks, as judicial review of the settlement continues.

### Key Points
- Trump "briefly appeared" at the March 5 settlement meeting per the amended filing; Live Nation maintains he didn't join substantive discussions.
- Revised filing specifies Trump personally asked CEO Michael Rapino about the DOJ lawsuit's status during a February call ostensibly about the Kennedy Center.
- Live Nation says the amendment was filed "out of an abundance of caution" following an August ruling (HPE/Juniper) that expanded what Tunney Act disclosures must cover — not an admission its original June filing was deficient.
- New detail: Live Nation submitted an initial settlement proposal in Sept. 2025, a formal proposal in Oct. 2025, and DOJ countered in Jan. 2026.
- Non-settling state AGs continue pursuing structural remedies (including Ticketmaster divestiture) after their separate jury verdict; Judge Subramanian's Tunney Act public-interest review of the DOJ settlement is ongoing.

### Highlights
- Oct. 21 amended filing confirms Trump's presence at the March 5 DOJ settlement meeting — new detail not in Live Nation's original June 22 disclosure.
- Judge Arun Subramanian's Tunney Act public-interest review of the settlement remains open, with no ruling date set.

### Industry Problem
Continued scrutiny over how the Live Nation/Ticketmaster DOJ settlement was negotiated keeps the settlement's terms — and the risk it gets reopened or rejected — unsettled, which matters for how much the deal reshapes competitive access to Ticketmaster's back-end and amphitheater exclusivity.

### Proposed Solution
No product action; monitor Judge Subramanian's Tunney Act ruling and the non-settling states' parallel push for structural remedies, since either could materially change Ticketmaster's competitive position and the openings available to independent primary platforms like Etix.

Live Nation Entertainment has amended its court disclosure about negotiations with the Department of Justice to confirm that President Donald Trump briefly appeared at the March 5 meeting where the company and federal officials finalized the terms of their controversial antitrust settlement.

The October 21 filing also revises Live Nation’s account of a February conversation between Trump and CEO Michael Rapino, adding details about the Kennedy Center and specifying that the president asked about the status of the federal antitrust lawsuit.

The revised disclosure comes as U.S. District Judge Arun Subramanian continues reviewing the proposed settlement under the Tunney Act, which requires judicial approval of federal antitrust consent judgments.

Live Nation’s original June 22 disclosure acknowledged extensive negotiations involving senior Justice Department officials and the Office of White House Counsel, including the March 5 meeting that produced a signed settlement term sheet. However, it did not identify Trump himself as having attended that meeting.

## Live Nation Revises Account of February Trump-Rapino Conversation

A redlined version of the filing submitted alongside the amended disclosure reveals several additional changes to Live Nation’s description of its contacts with the administration.

In its June filing, the company stated that Rapino discussed “a variety of topics related to Live Nation’s business” with Trump in February 2026. The disclosure said the Justice Department lawsuit came up during the discussion but that no substantive settlement terms were discussed.

The revised version is more specific.

“In February 2026, President Donald J. Trump called Mr. Rapino to discuss issues related to the Kennedy Center; the President inquired about the status of the DOJ’s lawsuit against Defendants but no substantive terms regarding any potential settlement were discussed,” the amended filing states.

The revision identifies the Kennedy Center as the subject of the contact and specifically attributes the inquiry about the antitrust lawsuit to Trump.

Those details are particularly relevant given [previous reporting by The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/media/trump-live-nation-antitrust-doj-f82b2c55?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), which described a February 27 Oval Office meeting primarily focused on improving Kennedy Center programming.

As [TicketNews reported in August](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/08/report-doj-settled-live-nation-case-on-trumps-orders-after-oval-office-meeting-with-rapino/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the Journal also reported that Trump subsequently directed a senior Justice Department official to settle the Live Nation case shortly before the antitrust trial began.

The new filing does not address that reported instruction to DOJ leadership. It also does not specify an exact date for the February contact described in the amended disclosure or clarify whether it refers to the same encounter detailed by the Journal.

Live Nation’s acknowledgment of Trump’s brief appearance during the March 5 negotiations is consistent with the Journal’s earlier reporting. The company maintains, however, that the president did not participate in substantive settlement discussions during that meeting.

## Filing Adds Details About Settlement Participants and Timeline

The amendment also provides additional information about the officials and representatives involved in negotiations.

Live Nation now specifically identifies the Office of the Associate Attorney General alongside the offices of the Attorney General, Deputy Attorney General, Antitrust Division and White House Counsel as participating in settlement discussions.

The revised filing also adds that representatives of certain plaintiff states attended settlement meetings in March 2026. It does not identify which states participated or specify the meetings they attended.

Another change concerns the company’s earlier efforts to initiate negotiations.

The original disclosure stated that Live Nation representatives, including outside advisers Mike Davis and William Levi, sought to open settlement discussions with the Antitrust Division between February and June 2025.

The amended version characterizes those contacts as efforts to open a dialogue with division leadership about the case, including settlement possibilities.

It also clarifies that Live Nation submitted an initial settlement proposal in September 2025, followed by a formal written proposal in October, which DOJ countered in January 2026.

The changes provide a more detailed account of the contacts that preceded the settlement, although the filing does not disclose the substance of the competing proposals or explain how the final terms were selected.

Live Nation explicitly connects the amendment to an August 12 federal court ruling involving Hewlett Packard Enterprise’s acquisition of Juniper Networks, another controversial antitrust settlement reached under the Trump administration.

In that case, U.S. District Judge P. Casey Pitts found that HPE and Juniper had failed to disclose certain relevant communications with government officials. The court concluded that the Tunney Act’s disclosure requirements extend beyond conversations directly negotiating settlement terms to communications relevant to the proposed agreement.

The judge also found that DOJ had inadequately disclosed alternative remedies it considered before settling. Despite those procedural deficiencies, Pitts ultimately approved the HPE agreement after determining that the proposed judgment satisfied the applicable public-interest standard.

Live Nation cites [that August decision](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/5:2025cv00951/443490/482/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) directly in the opening paragraph of its amended filing.

The company maintains that its original June disclosure was “complete and compliant with Section 16(g),” but says it submitted the amendment “out of an abundance of caution” following the HPE ruling to clarify communications concerning or relevant to the proposed settlement.

That language is important: Live Nation is not conceding that its original disclosure violated the Tunney Act. It is supplementing the record while maintaining that the earlier filing satisfied its legal obligations.

Nevertheless, the amendment adds information that was not previously included in the company’s required public accounting of settlement-related government contacts.

## Judicial Review of Settlement Continues

The additional disclosures arrive amid sustained opposition to the Justice Department’s agreement with Live Nation and Ticketmaster.

The federal settlement would preserve the companies’ combined ownership while imposing restrictions on ticketing exclusivity, certain venue arrangements and other business practices. It would not require the Ticketmaster divestiture originally contemplated in the government’s antitrust case.

Non-settling state attorneys general continued litigating after DOJ reached its agreement and subsequently secured a jury verdict against Live Nation and Ticketmaster. Those states are pursuing broader remedies, including structural separation.

The states have also [challenged the adequacy of the federal settlement](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/state-ags-live-nation-settlement-ticketmaster-entrench/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) and sought greater scrutiny of the negotiations that produced it.

Subramanian previously declined to authorize a broad discovery request into the settlement process but left open the possibility of narrower, targeted requests for information relevant to his Tunney Act review.

The October 21 amendment does not establish that Trump improperly influenced the settlement or that any undisclosed arrangement existed. It does, however, expand Live Nation’s formal account of presidential and senior administration involvement in the negotiations.

The proposed federal judgment remains subject to Subramanian’s determination that the agreement serves the public interest, while the non-settling states continue pursuing remedies based on their separate jury verdict.
