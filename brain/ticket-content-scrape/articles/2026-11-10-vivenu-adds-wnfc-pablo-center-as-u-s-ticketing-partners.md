# vivenu Adds WNFC, Pablo Center as U.S. Ticketing Partners

## Summary

Ticketing technology vendor vivenu announced two new U.S. partnerships — with the Women's National Football Conference (WNFC) and Pablo Center at the Confluence, a Wisconsin performing-arts venue — pitched around improving customer-engagement analytics and marketing tools. Pablo Center reports a 42.6% ticket-sales increase (comparison period unspecified) since adopting the platform, and WNFC cites 63% YoY attendance growth it wants to convert into repeat stadium attendance.

### Key Points
- vivenu signs multiyear deal with the 16-team WNFC to support ticket sales, fan data and attendance conversion from digital audience growth
- Pablo Center (1,200-seat theater + 400-seat black box) adopts vivenu for analytics/marketing tools, reports ticket sales up 42.6% (comparison period not specified)
- WNFC attendance grew 63% from 2025 to 2026, over 31,000 fans attended games this season
- Follows vivenu's October deal with the Metro Conference to centralize collegiate championship ticketing

### Highlights
- vivenu is actively winning both performing-arts and sports-league clients in the U.S., positioning analytics/fan-data tools as the differentiator
- Reported sales lift (42.6%) is an unverified vendor claim with no stated baseline period — a marketing figure, not an audited result
- Pattern of multiyear, multi-vertical (sports + venues) partnership wins suggests vivenu is scaling its U.S. primary-ticketing footprint

### Industry Problem
Clients (venues, leagues) want more than transaction processing — they want patron-relationship tools (CRM-style analytics, marketing automation) bundled into their ticketing platform to convert digital reach into repeat in-person attendance.

### Proposed Solution
Competitor (vivenu) is shipping bundled analytics/marketing tooling as its wedge into new verticals; worth benchmarking Etix's own analytics/CRM feature set against these wins, particularly for mid-size performing-arts and emerging sports-league clients.

Ticketing technology company vivenu has announced two new U.S. partnerships with the Women’s National Football Conference (WNFC) and Pablo Center at the Confluence, a performing arts venue in Eau Claire, Wisconsin.

The agreements will provide both organizations with ticketing technology designed to improve customer engagement, strengthen audience relationships and support revenue growth.

Pablo Center operates a 1,200-seat Broadway-style theater and a 400-seat black box theater, hosting a year-round schedule of performances, exhibitions and educational programming.

Under the new partnership, the nonprofit venue will use vivenu’s ticketing platform to gain greater control over its customer experience, with built-in analytics and marketing tools designed to help the organization better understand its patrons and develop new revenue opportunities.

“Never have I seen my team as enthusiastic about a piece of technology as they have been in the last couple of weeks,” said Monica Frederick, executive director of Pablo Center at the Confluence.

“We were looking for a partner that would let us do things our way: develop our brand, understand who our patrons really are, and give our team tools that actually make their day-to-day easier.”

Frederick said the venue is already seeing encouraging results from the technology. “Vivenu checked every box and initial results are extremely positive. We are up 42.6% on our ticket sales.”

The announcement did not specify the comparison period for that increase or how much of the growth was attributable to the new ticketing platform.

vivenu has also entered into a multiyear partnership with the WNFC, with the 16-team women’s tackle football league looking to increase attendance, improve fan engagement and strengthen its commercial operations.

The agreement follows a significant year of attendance growth for the league. According to the WNFC, attendance increased 63% from 2025 to 2026, with more than 31,000 fans attending games during the season.

The league plans to use vivenu’s technology to support ticket sales, build stronger relationships with fans and convert its growing digital audience into additional stadium attendance.

“We’ve proven that millions of people want to watch women play football. Now our focus is getting more of those people into stadiums,” said Odessa Jenkins, chief executive of the WNFC.

“Our digital growth has been extraordinary, but impressions alone don’t build sustainable sports businesses.”

Jenkins said encouraging repeat attendance and building connections between fans and their local teams will be important to the league’s continued growth.

“Fans having fun, bringing their families, investing in their local teams, and coming back season after season, that’s how we build the next great sports property. Vivenu gives us the tools to make that happen.”

The two agreements add to vivenu’s growing U.S. customer roster across sports and live entertainment. In October, the company also [secured a partnership with the Metro Conference](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/metro-conference-vivenu-championship-ticketing/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) to centralize championship ticketing and fan data across the collegiate athletic conference.
