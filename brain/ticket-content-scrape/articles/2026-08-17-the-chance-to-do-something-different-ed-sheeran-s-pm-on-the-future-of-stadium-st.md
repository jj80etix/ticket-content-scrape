# ‘The chance to do something different’: Ed Sheeran’s PM on the future of stadium staging

## Summary

A Q&A with Helen Himmons, production manager for Ed Sheeran's tours, comparing the technical and logistical demands of his prior in-the-round "Mathematics Tour" against his current end-on "Loop Tour" staging. Focused on production/staging craft, not ticketing or business strategy.

### Key Points
- Himmons has served as PM on both Sheeran's 360°/in-the-round and end-on stadium productions.
- In-the-round staging requires substantially more off-stage storage and creates load-bearing challenges at the field center.
- Sheeran returned to end-on staging for the "Loop Tour" after four years of in-the-round "Mathematics Tour" shows.

## Transcript

## ‘The chance to do something different’: Ed Sheeran’s PM on the future of stadium staging

From safety procedures to visual impact, Helen Himmons tells IQ about Sheeran's 360° Mathematics Tour and why the singer has returned to an end-on format

![Helen Himmons](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Helen-Himmons.png)

Helen Himmons

After spending four years taking Ed Sheeran’s groundbreaking 360° *Mathematics T* o *ur* around the globe, production manager (PM) Helen Himmons has called the in-the-round staging feat “one of the proudest moments” of the teams’ careers, as she weighs up the pros and cons of returning to the end-on *Loop Tour* production in a Q&A with *IQ*.

Sheeran did not stray from the touring circuit long after closing his four-year *[Mathematics](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/01/sheer-brilliance-the-formula-behind-ed-sheeran-mathematics-tour/)* run last September, picking back up on the stadium circuit in New Zealand in January with a brand new end-on production.

Here, Himmons, who has served as PM on both outings, shares her view on the benefits and drawbacks of each approach – and reveals which format gets her vote from as the future of stadium touring.

**What fundamentally changes for a production team when moving from a traditional end-on stage to an in-the-round setup?**  
The fundamentals! You have to change the way you approach everything, and you can’t make decisions or assumptions based on what you’d normally do.

**What are the biggest technical or logistical challenges unique to 360 productions?**  
You have a lot fewer places to hide and store things. The carts and flight cases that would usually be behind the stage have to go somewhere else, so you need a lot more general storage within the stadium, and usually that’s nowhere convenient.

The majority of the weight of the build goes into the centre of the field, which isn’t where it usually goes. This can present a load-bearing issue as not all pitches/floors can support that weight there as they were designed to have it at one end. Also, with the stage on the pitch, you also have to consider expensive irrigation systems that might be underneath.

You cannot get crew, or the artist, to the stage without going through the audience, as the dressing rooms and offices are not behind the stage.

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2025/08/©-Zakary-Walters-430x225.png.webp)

*Mathematics Tour © Mark Surridge*

**How does the lack of a true backstage area change the way crews and performers operate during a show?**  
The crew are very visible walking to and from the stage and need to be a lot more conscious of their actions and conversations. With a traditional end-on, if there’s an issue you can run to stage, but in the round you have to be a little more controlled as you don’t want to highlight that there might be a problem.

Getting the artist to stage without them being seen is another challenge – and there have been lots of creative ways of dealing with this!

**How do load-ins and load-outs differ compared to a traditional end-on production? Are there operational advantages people don’t realise?**  
In many ways the load-out is harder. Your stage manager has to cover the whole floor; they cannot see around or past the stage, so there is a lot more walking and communication involved. Everything comes in and goes out in various directions. We actually increased our stage management team for our in-the-round shows to make sure we had eyes on all areas at all times.

**What are the biggest safety concerns with 360 staging?**  
From a build perspective, nothing.

The only safety concern we had was, in the event of an emergency, how we would get the artist off stage and to back of house or to his vehicle, and the crew out from around and under the stage and to the safety of our rendezvous point, back of house.

> “With everything now captured on phones and content so widely shared, the aesthetic has become a lot more important”

We had a barricade walkway that remained sterile for the entirety, which provided a safe route to our dressing rooms and offices. In the event of an emergency situation, there were blow-out gates within that barricade which would open to allow the audience to pass through, but they would only be opened once the artist and crew had left to ensure they didn’t become trapped at the stage, which is effectively an island in the middle of thousands of people.

This in itself was a challenge though as we faced a lot of resistance from fire marshals and venue safety representatives, many of whom are just not used to dealing with anything other than a conventional pit barrier across the front of the stage.

**Why do you think more major artists are choosing to tour in the round right now?**  
Maybe because it offers the chance to do something different. Trying to come up with something new for an end-on is a lot harder as it’s been done so many times; however, as there have been far fewer in-the-round productions, there’s a greater opportunity to come up with something new in terms of design.

I think an in-the-round design can also be more visually impactful, and with everything now captured on phones and content so widely shared, the aesthetic has become a lot more important.

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/08/Ed-Sheerans-Loop-Tour-©-Mark-Surridge-430x269.png.webp)

*Loop Tour © Mark Surridge*

**After having worked on both in-the-round and end-on, which would you say is your preference from a PM standpoint?**  
End-on. Although I am so glad we did the in-the-round show and, for many of us involved in it, I think it will remain one of the proudest moments of our careers. We had it out for four years, so we had the opportunity to experience almost all the highs, lows, challenges, and highlights that come with an in-the-round production.

**After working on both styles of staging, do you think in-the-round productions represent the future of stadium touring?**  
I hope not to the point that it becomes the norm. The wonderful thing about stadium shows is the opportunity for variety and to be creative, and that should include both end-on and in-the-round productions.

I also think that for some artists, achieving an in-the-round show is cost-prohibitive, which will result in a lot of artists having to stick with an end-on show.
