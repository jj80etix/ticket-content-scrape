# Bono Defends U2’s Premium Ticket Pricing as 2027 Tour Goes on Sale

## Summary

Ahead of presales for U2's 2027 world tour, Bono told the New York Times' "The Interview" podcast that artists have "very little" control over ticket pricing broadly, but that U2 deliberately keeps some of its best seats affordable while unapologetically charging premium prices to wealthier fans elsewhere ("we have no problem separating rich people from their money"). Brazil presale pricing for the band's March 2027 São Paulo shows ranges from ~$120 (Fan Ticket, with fees) to ~$1,580 (Ultimate VIP Hospitality), illustrating the tiered pricing structure described.

### Key Points
- Bono: artists have "very little" influence over ticket pricing; U2's approach is to keep its best seats affordable while charging a premium elsewhere.
- U2's São Paulo 2027 tickets range from R$500/~$120 (Fan Ticket, w/ fees) to R$7,897/~$1,580 (Ultimate VIP Hospitality).
- Mid-tier options: Grandstand ~$166, GA Floor ~$190, Premium Floor ~$372, Gold Circle ~$420, (RED) Zone ~$662 (all incl. online fees).
- Announcement coincides with U2 being named halftime act for the NFL's first-ever Thanksgiving Eve game (Nov. 25, SoFi Stadium).

### Highlights
- Explicit artist endorsement of tiered/platinum pricing as a deliberate strategy to subsidize affordable seats with premium ones.
- Concrete presale price ladder spanning roughly 13x from cheapest to most expensive ticket tier for the same show.

### Industry Problem
Patrons increasingly encounter wide, deliberately-designed price ladders within a single on-sale, where entry-level pricing is kept visible/affordable while most revenue is captured from premium tiers — a pattern that shapes perceived fairness and trust in primary-market pricing.

### Proposed Solution
Not vendor-specific, but it reinforces the case for primary platforms to make tiered pricing structures clearly legible at point of sale (plain-language tier descriptions, not just price) so patron trust holds up even as price-ladder spreads widen — relevant grounding for Etix Assistant's checkout-experience and pricing-transparency guidance.

Bono says U2 has no problem charging wealthy fans premium prices for concerts, arguing that the band has long sought to balance expensive ticket options with affordable access to some of the best locations in its venues.

The comments come as U2 begins ticket presales for its newly announced 2027 world tour, with prices for the band’s upcoming Brazilian concerts ranging from R$500 (approx. $100 USD) for a special fan ticket to more than R$7,500 (approx. $1,500 USD) for the most expensive VIP hospitality packages before online service fees.

The Irish rock group also announced Tuesday that it will headline the halftime show of the NFL’s first Thanksgiving Eve game on November 25, giving the band another major international platform ahead of its return to stadium touring.

Speaking with David Marchese on [The New York Times’ *The Interview* podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/50-years-in-whats-the-point-of-u2-bono-and/id1624946521?i=1000796785156&utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), Bono discussed U2’s philosophy toward concert pricing and the extent to which artists can influence what their audiences pay.

The R$500 Fan Ticket is particularly relevant to Bono’s description of U2’s approach to affordable admission.

Unlike traditional tickets that allow buyers to select a particular section, the Fan Ticket program reportedly assigns purchasers to a location that is not disclosed until the day of the concert.

According to Brazilian entertainment publication [On Backstage](https://www.onbackstage.com.br/post/u2-sao-paulo-como-funciona-fun-ticket-ingresso-surpresa-2027?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), eligible locations can include premium floor sections, general admission and grandstand areas.

That arrangement potentially gives some lower-priced ticket holders access to more desirable concert locations, although it does not guarantee buyers a spot near the stage. And allocations for such ticket tranches are often minimal and run out immediately during early stage sales.

The more expensive VIP offerings provide additional benefits beyond admission. Hospitality packages include access to exclusive lounges, merchandise, commemorative items and other experiences, making them different products from standard concert tickets.

The (RED) Zone package includes access to an exclusive standing area alongside the stage, with purchases supporting the organization’s work with the Global Fund to combat AIDS.

Ticketmaster also specifies that VIP package tickets are nontransferable, a restriction that could limit purchasers’ flexibility if they are subsequently unable to attend.

The published price structure demonstrates how U2 is attempting to accommodate different categories of ticket buyers, with some fans paying considerably more for premium access and experiences while others have lower-cost admission options.

It does not, however, establish how many of the most desirable concert locations are being made available at discounted prices.

## U2 Has Previously Promoted Affordable Tickets Alongside Premium Inventory

Bono’s comments are consistent with the band’s stated approach during its 2023-2024 residency at Sphere in Las Vegas.

When those performances were originally announced, ticket prices started at $140, with organizers saying approximately 60% of tickets would be priced below $300.

At the same time, the residency offered premium-priced inventory that could cost considerably more.

As [TicketNews reported when the Sphere residency was announced](https://www.ticketnews.com/2023/04/u2-announce-opening-dates-of-sphere-las-vegas-residency/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the band also restricted ticket transfers for general admission floor tickets, limiting how some purchasers could subsequently distribute or resell their tickets.

Those decisions highlight an important distinction in the debate over concert affordability.

Offering some lower-priced tickets in desirable locations does not necessarily mean an artist’s overall ticket prices are low. Similarly, selling expensive VIP packages does not automatically make less expensive admission unavailable.

The more meaningful consumer questions concern how ticket inventory is distributed among price levels, whether buyers understand the differences between offerings, and what restrictions accompany their purchases.

Bono’s assertion that artists have limited influence over pricing also raises questions about how those decisions are divided among performers, promoters, venues and ticketing platforms.

While the singer described U2’s ability to influence the availability of certain affordable locations, he did not explain precisely which broader pricing decisions the band controls.

U2’s [newly announced Greatest Show on Earth World Tour](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/u2-announces-first-2027-world-tour-dates/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) is scheduled to begin February 23 at Estadio GNP Seguros in Mexico City.

The initial schedule includes six stadium concerts across Mexico City, São Paulo and Buenos Aires, with Beck serving as the opening act for all six performances.

The tour supports U2’s forthcoming album, *Carnaval De Luz*, scheduled for release November 13.

Additional North American and European tour dates have been promised, but as of October 20, the band has not announced specific cities, venues or concert dates beyond the original Latin American schedule.

U2 has also confirmed that a return to Ireland is being planned.

The first round of U2.com subscriber presales began October 19, with another subscriber group receiving access October 20.

General ticket sales for the São Paulo and Buenos Aires concerts begin October 22 at 10 a.m. local time. Mexico City’s general onsale follows October 23 at 10 a.m. local time.

Details and updates are available through [U2’s official tour website](https://www.u2.com/pages/tours?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

## U2 Announces Thanksgiving Eve NFL Halftime Performance

In another development Tuesday, U2 was announced as the halftime entertainment for the NFL’s first Thanksgiving Eve game, scheduled for Wednesday, November 25.

The performance will take place at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, California, during the Green Bay Packers’ matchup against the Los Angeles Rams.

The game kicks off at 8 p.m. Eastern and will stream globally on Netflix.

According to [the band’s announcement](https://www.u2.com/blogs/news/halftime-headline-for-nfls-thanksgiving-eve-live-on-netflix?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the performance will celebrate U2’s 50th anniversary and is expected to include special guests, although additional performers have not yet been identified.

The appearance will mark U2’s return to an NFL halftime stage nearly 25 years after the band headlined the Super Bowl XXXVI halftime show in February 2002.

That performance, held just months after the September 11 terrorist attacks, featured a tribute to the victims and remains one of the band’s most widely remembered television appearances.

The Thanksgiving Eve broadcast will provide U2 with a major televised performance ahead of its 2027 stadium tour, while fans in the United States and Canada continue waiting for the announcement of the band’s first North American concert dates.
