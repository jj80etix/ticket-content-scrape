# Tech Stack: Gigantic Tickets and more…

## Summary

TheTicketingBusiness's weekly Tech Stack round-up covers four vendor moves: Gigantic Tickets becomes ticketing supplier and shirt sponsor for Welsh club Cwmbran Celtic AFC; performing-arts ticketing platform Spektrix signs three new distribution/partner integrations (StubHub, TicketNetwork, and dining-reservation add-on Make a Rezzy); and in-seat ordering vendor Skippio goes live at the newly reopened (€52m upgrade) RDS Laya Arena in Dublin, letting guests order food/drink from their seats.

### Key Points
- Spektrix adds three new partners: marketplace StubHub, distribution network TicketNetwork (1,000+ sites), and Make a Rezzy (in-flow dining reservations).
- Skippio's in-seat food/drink ordering technology launches at RDS Laya Arena following a €52m ($60m) venue upgrade and a separate Levy Ireland hospitality redesign deal.
- Gigantic Tickets becomes ticketing supplier and kit sponsor for Cwmbran Celtic AFC (Wales), also backing several independent artists' Music Venue Trust fundraiser kits.

### Highlights
- Spektrix (Tier 1 primary-platform competitor) now distributes into StubHub and TicketNetwork's 1,000+ site network — a concrete secondary/distribution-partnership move to track.
- In-seat ordering (Skippio) going live at a newly renovated major arena is a direct patron-convenience feature comparable to what primary platforms are expected to support.

### Industry Problem
Patrons increasingly expect ticketing to be a gateway to broader in-venue convenience (food ordering, reservations, wayfinding) rather than just a checkout step, and distribution partnerships (Spektrix→StubHub/TicketNetwork) show competitors extending inventory reach beyond their own storefronts.

### Proposed Solution
Improve: evaluate in-seat ordering / venue-concierge integrations as a value-add for Etix venue clients; Monitor Spektrix's marketplace-distribution partnerships as a signal of how Tier 1 primary competitors are expanding reach — a posture change would be warranted if Etix clients start asking for equivalent secondary-distribution options.
