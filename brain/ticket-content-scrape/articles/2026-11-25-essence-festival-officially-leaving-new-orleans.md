# Essence Festival Officially Leaving New Orleans

## Summary

The Essence Festival of Culture, one of the nation's largest Black festivals, is officially leaving New Orleans after more than three decades following stalled negotiations over "contracts and vendor payments." The city's contract with the event expired in September, and organizer Sundial Media Group says it needs a new host partner committed to the festival's growth.

### Key Points
- Festival departs New Orleans after 30+ years; the city contract expired in September 2026.
- Negotiations reportedly broke down over contract terms and vendor payments.
- Sundial Media Group's statement cites 2025's $300M+ economic impact on New Orleans/Louisiana as context for the stakes.
- No new host city has been announced.

The Essence Festival of Music and Culture, one of the nation’s largest Black festivals, is officially leaving New Orleans after more than three decades, officials confirm to *Pollstar*.

“The largest Black festival of its kind has to be where it can grow, where it is valued, and where it serves the people who make it what it is,” a statement from Sundial Media Group read. “We need a partner that is committed to that growth and to the people it serves.”

The shocking announcement comes as negotiations between the city and festival leaders stalled over “contracts and vendor payments.” New Orleans’ contract with the storied event expired in September.

“In 2025, the festival generated more than $300 million in economic impact for New Orleans and Louisiana,” the statement continued. “Behind that number are the vendors, musicians, workers, volunteers and families of this city who built the festival alongside us. Every year, the people of New Orleans made it feel like home, and we are grateful.”
