# The Biz: Warner Tops $2B In Record Quarter; THE•TEAM’s New Board; CDs Are Back!

## Summary

Pollstar's industry roundup covers three items: Warner Music Group topped $2 billion in quarterly revenue for the first time (up 7% YoY, digital revenue up 13%); talent/brands agency THE•TEAM named an 11-member board following Providence Equity's buyout of Casey Wasserman; and distributor Alliance Entertainment reported CD revenue up 22% YoY and vinyl up 14%, citing younger buyers' pivot back to physical media.

### Key Points
- Warner Music Group: first-ever $2B+ quarter, +7% YoY revenue, +13% digital revenue; EPS slightly missed expectations.
- THE•TEAM named its new 11-member board after Providence Equity's buyout of Wasserman; seven members from leadership, four from Providence.
- Alliance Entertainment: CD revenue +22% YoY, vinyl +14%, driven partly by Gen Z/Alpha demand; eyeing expansion into DVDs.
- General music-business financial roundup; no direct ticketing-platform content.

Warner Music Group topped $2 billion in revenue in the fourth quarter, the first time the major has hit that mark.

That’s a 7% increase over the same quarter last year and a beat on expectations. The bottom line, however, missed slightly at $0.34 coming in just below an EPS expectation of $0.34

“Our fourth-quarter and full-year performance demonstrates the success of a multi-year transformation strategy that has established a higher standard for Warner Music Group,” CEO Robert Kyncl said. “Our creative strengths, combined with disciplined capital allocation focused on returns and technology-enabled capabilities, position us to generate sustainable long-term value for our artists, songwriters and shareholders in fiscal 2027 and beyond.”

Digital revenue increased 13% year over year to $1.3 billion, with total streaming revenue up 14%

**THE•TEAM’s New Board**

THE•TEAM has named a new 11-member board of directors following [Providence Equity’s buyout of Casey Wasserman](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/07/29/providence-equity-buys-out-wasserman-takes-full-control-of-theteam-watts-elevated-to-ceo/).

Seven of the board’s members come from THE•TEAM‘s leadership group, including CEO Michael Watt, Chief Financial Officer Elena Rochelli, Elizabeth Lindsey (Global President, Brands and Properties), Jason Ranne (President, Global Talent Representation), Kevin Shivers (Co-President, Music), Matthew Vandrau (President, EMEA & APAC) and Jon Liebman, Chairman and CEO of Brillstein Entertainment Partners.

The remaining members are from Providence: Senior Managing Director and Co-Head of North America Davis Noell, Operating Partner Greg Brown, Managing Director Scott Marimow and Senior Vice President Ahana Maken.

Providence said it plans to add more external members in the coming months.

**CDs Are Back**

Alliance Entertainment, one of the world’s largest distributors of physical media, said in its quarterly earnings report that revenue from CDs grew 22% year-over-year as Gen Z and Alpha continue to pivot to physical media, with growth in new releases, special editions and reissues. Vinyl sales were up 14%.

The turn to tangible has Alliance looking at expanding its presence in physical media beyond music with a return to DVDs.

“Physical music, movies, and collectibles all grew,” said Walker. “For a long time, so much of the conversation was about streaming replacing physical formats. But what we’ve seen consistently is that fans who can listen to an album or watch a movie online still want something special they can own and collect,” CEO Jeff Walker said.
