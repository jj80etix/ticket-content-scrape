# City Focus: Kuala Lumpur

## Summary

Kuala Lumpur is in the middle of its biggest-ever live music expansion, driven by government support, regional connectivity, and a run of blockbuster on-sales (The Weeknd, BTS, My Chemical Romance). Malaysia staged over 450 concerts in 2025 (up from 408 in 2024), generating roughly RM1.72bn in economic activity, with 245 international acts approved to perform through end-2026. Industry voices caution that more shows don't necessarily mean a healthier market — rising promoter competition is pushing artist guarantees higher even as KL competes regionally with Singapore, Bangkok, Jakarta, and Manila.

### Key Points
- 450+ concerts staged in Malaysia in 2025 (up from 408 in 2024); ~RM1.72bn in economic activity; 245 international acts approved to perform by end-2026.
- Recent sellouts include The Weeknd and BTS at the 85,000-cap TM Stadium Nasional, and 120,000 tickets for My Chemical Romance's two-night stadium return.

- Malaysia's touring calendar now aligns with the Oct-March Australia/India season, with Ramadan (Feb-early Mar 2027) further compressing scheduling windows.

## Transcript

A government-supported initiative to encourage more international shows is driving record numbers of concerts in Malaysia’s capital but familiar challenges remain

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Laufey-in-Kuala-Lumpur.jpg)

Laufey in Kuala Lumpur © Nicole Mago

Powered by government support, regional connectivity, and a run of recent blockbuster on-sales, Kuala Lumpur is in the middle of its most ambitious live music expansion yet.

According to Communications Ministry figures reported by Malaysian news agency Bernama, Malaysia staged more than 450 concerts in 2025 – up from 408 in 2024 – generating nearly RM1.72bn in economic activity. By September, *The Straits Times* reported that the government had approved 245 international acts to perform nationwide by the end of 2026.

With recent shows by The Weeknd and an upcoming visit from BTS both selling out the 85,000-capacity TM Stadium Nasional, 120,000 tickets snapped up for My Chemical Romance’s two-night stadium return, and KL Headline Season bringing 25 international performances to six venues, the capital is rapidly evolving from a secondary stop on Asian itineraries into a serious regional centre for touring, concert tourism, and live industry growth.

This positive development also extends beyond multi-genre Western headliners. “The number of stadium shows is obviously encouraging, but I pay more attention to the diversity of the calendar,” says Brian Lowe, Southeast Asia managing director at Tixr and deputy chairman of the country’s Arts, Live, Festival and Events Association (ALIFE).

“We are also seeing K-pop, J-pop, C-pop, and Indonesian artists alongside Malaysian and regional acts. They are not all competing for exactly the same audience.”

However, fuller listings do not guarantee a healthier bottom line. “I wouldn’t use the number of shows alone as a metric of health,” cautions Rohit Rampal, founder and CEO of promoter Hitman Solutions.

“We’re seeing more supply, but that doesn’t necessarily mean every show is selling at the same level. Some shows are doing exceptionally well, while others are having to work harder for the same audience.”

**PROMOTERS**  
KL’s promoter pool now spans Live Nation Malaysia, PR Worldwide, Hitman Solutions, Future Sound Asia, Star Planet, iMe Malaysia, Icon Entertainment, Milestone Production, and new entrants. That depth has improved access to tours, but raised guarantees and intensified competition for dates, sponsors, and audience spending.

> “The key is changing the perception that bringing a major show to Malaysia is difficult”

“The older, more established promoters here have stayed conservative, and honestly, so have we,” says Rahul Kukreja, founder of RK Live Asia. “The newer players in the market are the ones taking the real risk. A lot of them are paying guarantees that are, frankly, a little disruptive, and that’s pushed guarantees higher across the board, not just in Malaysia.”

Malaysia now aligns with the October-to-March touring season for Australia and India. Ramadan in February and early March 2027 will compress the window further; meanwhile, November 2026 already has a concert almost every other day.

“The market is healthy and growing, but that doesn’t necessarily mean it has become easier to be a promoter,” says Future Sound Asia director of entertainment Wan Alman Ariffin. “There are more shows happening, more promoters competing for the same artists and dates, and we’re also competing with other cities in the region, such as Singapore, Bangkok, Jakarta, and Manila.”

KL counters with relatively affordable hotels, strong air links, experienced suppliers, and a multilingual audience. Regulation remains part of every bid, although foreign-artist pre-approvals are now online, and promoters routinely attach PUSPAL’s (Central Agency Committee for Application for Filming and Foreign Artists Presentations) guidelines to offers.

> “The real volume still sits at arena level and below”

“The key is changing the perception that bringing a major show to Malaysia is difficult or complicated,” says Para Rajagopal, managing director of Live Nation Malaysia. “What we need is to build greater confidence among the global touring industry that KL is a tour-friendly market where shows can be delivered smoothly and professionally.”

**LARGE VENUES**  
Bukit Jalil, a lush suburb known for being a bustling hub of activity, houses KL’s three biggest venues: the aforementioned TM Stadium Nasional, Unifi Arena, and the outdoor National Hockey Stadium.

Unifi concerts hold 9,000–13,000, while the National Hockey Stadium accommodates up to 20,000. Unifi and the National Hockey Stadium have become dependable options for arena-scale tours, having hosted the likes of Green Day, Imagine Dragons, and Maroon 5. More recently, 5 Seconds of Summer added a second night at Unifi Arena after demand went through the roof.

TM Stadium Nasional is a more selective proposition. “Very few artists can sell that many tickets here; it’s tough when selling 40,000 tickets gets you a half stadium,” says Kukreja. “The real volume still sits at arena level and below.”

The more pressing gap is indoor capacity. “The real sweet spot we’re missing is that 10- to 15,000-capacity indoor arena,” says Rampal. “A venue like Unifi Arena fits that kind of requirement, but the challenge is availability,” adding that more options would help promoters avoid oversizing or undersizing a show.

> “Artist fees have gone up significantly, and so have production costs, logistics, accommodation”

**MID-SIZED VENUE**  
Mid-size options have improved. Zepp Kuala Lumpur holds 2,414 standing, Mega Star Arena up to 5,000, and idea Live Arena more than 4,000 seated or 6,000 in mixed configurations. These rooms let promoters match developing acts to demand rather than gamble on an arena.

Milestone Production managing director and ALIFE council member Dato’ Grace Lee sees the 2,000-to-7,000 segment as equally important. “Having more professionally equipped and commercially viable venues within this range gives promoters the flexibility to match the right content with the right capacity,” she says.

That infrastructure variety can take an act from 2,000 people on its first visit to 5,000 next time and, eventually, an arena.

**GRASSROOTS & THE NEXT PHASE**  
Below the major headliners, the picture is more fragile. REXKL, Live Fact, Merdekarya, Rumah Api, and Moutou continue to provide stages, but dedicated 100-to-500-capacity rooms remain vulnerable. International productions generate work for Malaysian crews and suppliers, yet that activity does not automatically produce arena-level local artists.

“A sustainable live entertainment industry cannot be measured purely by how many international artists we bring into the country,” says Lee. “The real measure of success is two-way: bringing the best entertainment into Malaysia while creating Malaysian entertainment that can travel out to the world.”

For Rajagopal, the next three to five years will show whether the current boom has changed KL’s position permanently. “The real measure is whether 2026 becomes a baseline rather than a peak,” he says.

“We want to see Kuala Lumpur consistently included in international tour routings, more multi-night shows, stronger concert tourism, more venue options across different capacities, and continued growth across the wider ecosystem.”
