# Germany’s LiveKomm elects new board to tackle ‘enormous challenges’

## Summary

German trade body LiveKomm, which represents more than 830 music clubs, venues, and festivals, elected a new board at its general meeting in Leipzig, with several new members joining and one leadership change on the executive board. The chairman cited tight budgets and political pressure as ongoing challenges, pointing to a national noise-protection funding scheme and a voluntary ticket-levy pilot as current initiatives.

### Key Points
- New board members: Andy Olschar, Marlies Resch, and Zora Brändle; chairman Mankel Brinkmann retained.
- Iris Hinze rises to the executive board as political spokesperson, succeeding Pamela Schobeß.
- Cites a €3m government-backed noise-protection scheme for clubs and festivals, with pilot results pending.
- References a nationwide voluntary ticket-levy scheme ("Club Euro") that advocates say remains "blockaded by major event organisers."

## Germany’s LiveKomm elects new board to tackle ‘enormous challenges’

The trade body represents more than 830 music clubs, live venues, and festivals across the leading European market

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/mankel-brinkmann01-Resized.webp)

Chariman Mankel Brinkmann

German trade body LiveKomm has elected a new board during its general meeting in Leipzig this week.

LiveMusikKommission e.V., the national association of venues and festivals, represents more than 830 music clubs, live venues, and festivals across over 100 cities and municipalities.

New members include Andy Olschar (Association for Pop Culture in Bavaria), Marlies Resch (Zauberberg in Passau), and Zora Brändle (Clubkultur Baden-Württemberg).

They join nine pre-existing Board members: Klaus Bossert, Gunnar Gessner, Kai Schulz, Olaf Möller, Franziska Pollin, Marc Wohlrabe, Andreas Walz, Robin Schellenberg, and Marcel Weber.

Members who did not stand for re-election are Bernd Strieder (Association for Pop Culture in Bavaria), Felix Grädler (halle02 in Heidelberg), and Stephan Benn (Gloria Köln).

Iris Hinze (Clubverstärker in Bremen; also head of the AK Kulturraumschutz) will rise to a leadership role, succeeding Pamela Schobeß (Gretchen in Berlin) on the executive board, where she will serve as political spokesperson.

The executive leadership retains chairman Mankel Brinkmann, deputy chairman Thore Debor, and treasurer Steffen Kache.

> “Club culture continues to face enormous challenges – economically, but also politically and socially”

“Club culture continues to face enormous challenges – economically, but also politically and socially,” says chairman Mankel Brinkmann. “Budgets are tight, the situation for many clubs and festivals remains precarious, and we are experiencing a shift to the right in our country.

“This is precisely why a strong national association remains essential, and we set the course for this in \[the\] election. Specifically, important decisions regarding noise protection funding and building regulations are now pending – issues we have been addressing for years. In addition, there is the question of how funding can be structured more fairly overall.”

LiveKomm managing director Christian Ordon also thanks departing members for their voluntary commitment to advancing club culture and welcomes the newly elected members.

This year saw the nationwide rollout of a €3 million [noise protection scheme](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/12/e3m-soundproofing-scheme-unveiled-for-german-venues/) for music clubs and festivals, supported by the government, Initiative Musik, and LiveKomm. Results of the pilot are to be announced.

National body Initiative Musik is also funding a nationwide ticket levy system, Club Euro, a solidarity-based contribution tested in Cologne to help support the country’s venue ecosystem.

It follows the national roll-out of a [voluntary ticket levy scheme](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/10/further-details-emerge-of-german-grassroots-scheme/), inspired by existing schemes in the UK and France, earlier this year, which advocacy group leaders said was “ [at a crossroads](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/05/german-ticket-levy-at-a-standstill-with-voluntary-scheme/) ” and “blockaded by major event organisers”.
