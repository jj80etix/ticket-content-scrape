# 7 Best White Label Ticketing Software Platforms in 2026

## Summary

A TicketNews buyer's guide ranks seven white-label ticketing software platforms for 2026 — Nuweb Group, vivenu, Ticketor, Eventcube, Ticketsauce, and others — assessed on APIs, pricing, customer examples, and case studies, each recommended for a different operator profile (established ticketing businesses, sports/leagues, self-service launches, festivals, marketing-led organizations).

### Highlights
- vivenu named "Best for professional sports teams and leagues," citing strong headless/API capabilities, season tickets, resale, and enterprise sports operations support.
- Nuweb Group rated best for established ticketing businesses via infrastructure-level white-label with custom frontends and multi-organiser support.
- Ticketor positioned as the lowest-cost, turnkey option for launching a new branded ticketing business.
- Eventcube and Ticketsauce differentiated on branding/Storefront API and marketing-attribution tooling, respectively.

### Industry Problem
White-label ticketing spans a wide spectrum from cosmetic checkout branding to full infrastructure hand-off, and operators evaluating vendors (including competitors positioning against a primary platform like Etix) lack a clear, consistent way to compare API depth, resale/season-ticket support, and go-to-market fit across providers.

### Proposed Solution
The article itself is a comparison guide rather than a product proposal; the competitive signal is that vivenu and peers are actively marketing API-depth and sports/enterprise fit as differentiators. Etix should track how its own white-label/API capabilities and case studies stack up against this comparison set, particularly vivenu's sports-and-resale positioning.

## White label does not mean the same thing on every platform

The main difference between these platforms is how much control they give you over the ticketing operation.

At the lighter end, platforms such as Ticket Tailor let you brand the customer experience. Your team still manages ticketing through the provider’s software.

Ticketor and Ticketsauce go further. They let businesses offer ticketing services to other organisations under their own brand.

Nuweb Group and vivenu sit at the infrastructure end. They give businesses more control over the customer experience. Using APIs, components and headless integrations, businesses can build their own interfaces on top of the underlying ticketing technology.

SECUTIX sits in a different category. It is a broader enterprise ticketing and audience-management platform for complex venues, cultural organisations and rights holders. It is less focused on businesses that want to launch their own ticketing brand.

That distinction matters. Branding a checkout is not the same as running a white-label ticketing platform for organisers. Neither gives you the same level of control as building and owning the customer experience on top of the underlying ticketing infrastructure.

## How we compared these platforms

We focused on platforms that offer a meaningful level of white labelling, rather than those that simply let organisers add a logo to checkout.

We compared each platform across six areas: white-label depth, buyer fit, technical flexibility, operational capability, commercial control, and implementation and cost.

Our research draws on current vendor documentation, developer and API resources, published pricing and terms, customer deployments, and case studies.

We did not test competitor products hands-on or through guided demos. We therefore limited our claims to information that is publicly available and can be verified. Where details such as pricing, merchant-of-record arrangements, or implementation terms are not published, we have marked them as unspecified rather than making assumptions.

## 1\. Nuweb Group – Best for established ticketing businesses

**Best for:** Ticketing providers and platforms that want to run a substantial ticketing operation under their own brand.

Nuweb Group is our pick for established ticketing businesses because it is designed to sit underneath a ticketing proposition, rather than simply provide a branded checkout.

We make Nuweb, so we know this product in more detail than the other platforms in this guide. It is best suited to ticketing businesses that already have organisers, customers and established workflows, but want more control over the technology behind them.

White labelling can extend across the customer, organiser and admin experience, with support for custom domains. Businesses can manage multiple companies, organisers and brands from one platform. They can also keep control of their pricing, customer relationships and payment setup. Nuweb supports more than 25 payment gateways and regional payment methods.

The platform also covers more than online sales. Reserved seating, box office, access control, timed entry, memberships, reporting, mobile apps, ePOS and resale all sit within the same ecosystem. This makes it relevant for businesses that want to bring several parts of their ticketing operation together.

The trade-off is that Nuweb is not aimed at businesses looking for a low-cost, self-service way to start selling tickets. Smaller organisers or early-stage ticketing businesses may not need this level of infrastructure. For sports organisations with complex requirements and the internal digital capability to use its architecture, vivenu is a strong fit.

vivenu is also positioned as an enterprise product rather than a self-service platform. It is better suited to businesses looking for a turnkey white-label ticketing platform than engineering teams building a highly customised ticketing product.

For businesses that want to launch a branded ticketing service quickly, without a large development team or major upfront investment, that simplicity is a clear advantage. Businesses that need more control over custom frontends, workflows or deeper integrations may prefer a more infrastructure-led platform.

For smaller event organisers that mainly want control over their brand, customer data and ticket revenue without a complicated setup, Ticket Tailor’s simplicity is a major part of its appeal.

## How to choose white-label ticketing software

The most important question is how much of the ticketing operation you need to own.

## Are you selling tickets or providing ticketing?

An organiser selling its own events has very different needs from a business providing ticketing to hundreds of organisers.

If ticketing is mainly part of your event operation, customer-facing branding may be enough.

If ticketing is the service you sell, look for multi-organiser management, branded administration, commercial control and the ability to onboard clients under your own proposition.

## Which parts need to carry your brand?

There is a big difference between removing a supplier logo from checkout and running the customer, organiser and administration experience under your own brand.

Define what needs to be white-labelled before comparing platforms. Otherwise, products with very different capabilities can look similar on a feature checklist.

## Do you need to build around the platform?

If your development team wants to create its own website, app or workflows, look closely at API coverage, webhooks, embedded components and whether core features can work outside the vendor’s own interface.

If you mainly want to configure an existing product and start selling, that level of technical flexibility may add cost and complexity without much benefit.

## Self-service or implementation-led?

Published pricing and instant signup can be useful when your operation is simple.

For businesses migrating existing customers, inventory, payments and integrations, implementation support may matter more than speed to launch.

The right choice is not simply the platform with the most features. It is the one that best matches your business model, required level of control and operational complexity.
