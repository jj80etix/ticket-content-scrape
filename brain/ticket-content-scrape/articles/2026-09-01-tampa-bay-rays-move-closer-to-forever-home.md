# Tampa Bay Rays Move Closer To Forever Home

## Summary

The Tampa Bay Rays cleared a major hurdle toward a new $2.36 billion ballpark after Hillsborough County and the city of Tampa approved their financing contributions in late August. The fixed-roof, 28,000+ seat stadium — set to open in 2029 and also host concerts and other events — will be built near Raymond James Stadium.

### Key Points
- Hillsborough County approved $796 million (mostly from tourist/investment tax revenue); Tampa city council approved $80 million; $30 million comes from federal disaster-recovery funds.
- The Rays are financing roughly $1.37 billion plus all cost overruns, and will privately finance a surrounding mixed-use district.
- County will own the stadium; the Rays will pay $4 million/year rent under a 35-year, non-relocation use agreement.
- Next steps are a community benefits agreement and bond/funding validation before construction begins; opening targeted for 2029.

## Transcript

The Tampa Bay Rays received county approval on Aug. 28 on a financing plan that will allow them to build a new stadium, a day after city officials approved their contribution to the $2.36 billion ballpark, which replaces Tropicana Field.

The Hillsborough County Commission voted 5-2 to approve its $796 million share of the new MLB stadium, which will have a minimum of 28,000 seats and be located near Raymond James Stadium, home of the NFL’s Tampa Bay Buccaneers.

Most of the funds will come from the county’s Community Investment Tax (roughly $360 million) and Tourist Development Tax money (approximately $303 million). The Tampa City Council city voted 4-3 on Aug. 27 to approve $80 million. Another $30 million will come from federal disaster recovery funds.

The Rays are set to invest approximately $1.37 billion plus all cost overruns. The club is also expected to privately finance a surrounding mixed-use district. The new stadium is expected to open in 2029.

“The Tampa Bay Rays enthusiastically thank and applaud the Hillsborough County Board of County Commissioners for making history,” said Rays CEO Ken Babby in a statement. “By virtue of its approval of a county investment in partnership with the Rays and the city of Tampa, today our entire region can celebrate the coming of a privately financed and mixed-use neighborhood, a reinvented Hillsborough College campus, and a new ballpark that secures the long-term future of Major League Baseball here in Tampa Bay. …By making the Forever Home a reality, the Rays are now home forever in Tampa Bay.”

Finding a long-term ballpark in the Tampa Bay area was a priority for the new ownership group spearheaded by managing partner Patrick Zalupski.

In October 2025, during the group’s introductory news conference, Zalupski told attendees that finding a new ballpark was their “first and highest priority” adding “It’s going to be challenging but we’re confident that we can be successful here in Tampa Bay.”

Less than a year later, the deal for a new multi-purpose ballpark at Hillsborough College’s Dale Mabry campus in Tampa is closer to reality.

Populous has nearly completed the ballparks schematic design. Following city and county approval, the Rays can proceed to the community benefits agreement process and begin bond/funding validation. Bond validation must occur before major demolition and construction can begin. If everything goes as planned, the ballpark will open in 2029.

“We are incredibly grateful to everyone who invested their time, energy, ideas and trust in getting us to this moment,” Zalupski said in a statement. “We recognize there is still important work ahead, and we look forward to continued collaboration to deliver on the promise of this transformational project. Today is an incredible day for Tampa Bay. …Forever starts now.”

The ballpark and surrounding multi-purpose district will be a few miles from downtown Tampa along Dale Mabry Highway, across the street from the NFL Buccaneers’ Raymond James Stadium and next to George M. Steinbrenner Field, the New York Yankees’ spring training facility.

![Rays2](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Rays2.jpeg)

New stadium will seat at least 28,000 and host concerts and other events. Rendering courtesy of Tampa Bay Rays/Populous

The fixed-roof stadium will be owned by Hillsborough County, with the Rays paying $4 million annually in rent. The initial term of the use agreement, which includes a non-relocation agreement, is for 35 years.

The Rays have said that the facility will host non-baseball events, as well as the broader development’s potential to create a destination for businesses, hospitality and entertainment. As part of the project, the district will also include a revamped and modernized campus for Hillsborough College.

Since 1998, the Rays have played at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, with the exception of 2025, when the team moved to Steinbrenner Field following hurricane damage at Tropicana Field. The Rays returned to Tropicana this season and their lease agreement expires after the 2028 season.

“There’s already been a lot of work going on behind the scenes to prepare for a moment when the county and city said yes,” added Babby on MLB.com. “Even this afternoon, I spent most of the day hitting ‘Go’ on a lot of major decisions, which we’ll be able to talk about over the next few weeks and months. But it’s all systems go for the Tampa Bay Rays in Tampa, and excited about the road ahead.”
