# Superstruct reveals Artistic Code for festival portfolio

## Summary

Superstruct, operator of more than 80 festivals worldwide, unveiled an "Artistic Code" formalizing principles on artistic freedom, safety, and inclusivity across its portfolio, unveiled at the International Festival Forum in London. COO Rebecca Kane Burton says the code was built from team input rather than imposed top-down and allows minor regional variation while keeping core principles consistent globally.

### Key Points

- Four principles: protecting artistic freedom within the law, safe/welcoming spaces, values-aligned programming and partnerships, and inclusivity/fairness.
- Code sets a non-intervention default on flags/banners/protests unless illegal in the relevant market or a specific safety risk is flagged by permit authorities.
- Developed under Superstruct's newer leadership (CEO Alex Mahon, COO Kane Burton, comms director Amber de Botton, all within the last 18 months).
- Code was piloted internally during the 2026 festival season before public launch and covers "advancing" processes — how artist behavior/expression info flows from agents to booking to production teams.

## Transcript

The company is formalising the framework of its principles of creative freedom, saying it will help its 80 festivals make 'confident decisions'

![Rebecca Kane Burton](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Rebecca-Kane-Burton.png)

Superstruct COO Rebecca Kane Burton

Superstruct has unveiled a new Artistic Code, which sets out the company’s principles for protecting artistic expression and creative freedom across its portfolio of more than 80 worldwide festivals.

First unveiled at the International Festival Forum in London, the company said the Code is “a demonstration of Superstruct’s longstanding support for artistic freedom and the creative independence of its festivals”.

The Code sets out four central principles: “protecting artistic freedom within the law”, “creating safe and welcoming spaces”, “ensuring programming and partnerships reflect Superstruct’s values”, and “promoting inclusivity and fairness”.

The company said that in practice, this means “no censorship of artists simply because their views are controversial, zero tolerance for discrimination or behaviour that puts people at risk, and no alignment with campaigns of incitement or movements motivated by intolerance”.

Speaking to *IQ* about the announcement, the company’s COO Rebecca Kane Burton said the launch of the code “a very positive statement for us” in setting out “what we stand for as an organisation, which is systemic freedom of expression”.

Kane Burton described the development of the Code as “from the ground up”, explaining she does not expect transitional issues while festivals implement it. Asked how Superstruct expects festivals to adapt to the Code, she said: “The best answer is that this code came from the teams themselves. They wrote it and rooted it. This isn’t us or anyone else dictating — it came from new leadership talking to teams about challenges they face at every level.”

> “Artists have always been provocative, and we love them for that”

She added that the issues addressed in the code are things festival partners “were already dealing with in various ways, but we didn’t have a framework”.

She explained Superstruct began using the Code during this summer’s festival season prior to its public launch, and that “each territory understood what it meant for them and how they would use it to support what they were already doing”.

The Code also addresses the issue of flags, banners, and protests at festivals, with Superstruct’s position being that it will not intervene unless “a flag or symbol is illegal in the relevant market, or where a specific safety risk has been identified and agreed with permit authorities”.

Kane Burton acknowledged to *IQ* the possibility that festivals could in theory have to apply some scepticism to authorities’ decisions about artistic freedom, saying that to counter this, “there are slight regional variations where needed” though the principles of the Code are “the same everywhere”.

“ We have allowed countries to slightly amend, so it works very specifically for them.  The principles on the Code that you will see that is publicly shared are the same everywhere,” she said.

Asked why now was the right time to launch the Code, Kane Burton told *IQ*: “ We can all see huge numbers of examples of where \[artistic freedom\] comes into conflict, or is challenged, or people ask questions quite rightly. Artists have and always will be provocative. And we love them for that.”

And she said the stipulation of allowing all artistic expression “within the law” enables Superstruct to “proactively use this code everywhere where we trade”.

“When you think about putting something like this down on paper, the obvious challenge that we have to think about is how is it applicable and how does it work in different spaces?  And the reality is, we’ve got decades of experience in this with 80 festivals, across multiple countries in Europe,” she said. “I think we’re in a good position to be able to understand what that means, country by country”.

A focus of the Code is also the processes by which agreements about artists’ behaviour and expression are shared along the chain from agents, to booking teams, through to showday and production teams. Kane Burton said a pillar of how Superstruct will apply the Code is working closely with agents to optimise this communication.

“Artists are artists travel over the world, and as they come into different spaces, and they are briefed by their agents, they understand the culture they’re looking into. And we see that as part of our draw, to be a good host, to facilitate them at the festival where they’re playing for us,” she explained.

“I think agents have the first line of responsibility and management to be briefing and sharing with their artists.  But I do think it is incumbent on us to support with them, so that people feel they’re able to be themselves, to express themselves, to have that freedom, but know when that freedom might sit closely adjacent to something that is unlawful, and it’s our duty to brief the artist on that,” she added.

This process, called ‘advancing’ in the Code, also applied to the principle of creating safe and welcoming spaces, Kane Burton added. Breaking down its commitment to this principle, the Code says there will be “no tolerance for people at risk” and “zero discrimination”.

Asked how Superstruct will ensure the delegation to festival partners is efficient and compliant with the Code, she said the approach will be “a mixture” of new processes and existing procedures.

Kane Burton emphasised that enabling artists and festivals to be as informed as possible is one of the key goals of the Code, which places importance on communicating “the details of any specific controls or risk flags”, the Code says.

> “artists can be booked 12–18 months before appearing on stage, and any number of things can change in that time”

“In any business, the point where a baton is handed from one team to another is critical. Information gets shared, some written down, some informal. When dealing with something as fantastic and popular as an artist’s performance, it’s important that continuity is maintained from booking through to delivery on stage,” Kane Burton told *IQ.*

“Largely, this already happens across our festivals, and I’m sure it happens elsewhere. But things move fast — artists can be booked 12–18 months before appearing on stage, and any number of things can change in that time.”

She continued: “This code builds on that good work by putting clear principles in place that everyone signs up to from the start. If there are variations or changes in the country they’re playing, or in the artist’s own reputation or performance needs, that can be taken into account by the production team. The principles don’t change — they’re the framework everyone works within.”

The Code arrives in the wake of refreshed leadership at Superstruct, with Kane Burton, CEO Alex Mahon, and director of communications Amber de Botton all having joined in the last 18 months.

Describing how the Code fits into the way Superstruct is pitching itself to its festival partners in 2026, Kane Burton said: “There’s a huge change in the three women; we’re all excited to be here driving the course, and I think we’re quite a different festival operator in that sense, with who we’ve got from a leadership point of view.”

“Of course, there’s an opportunity to look on things and understand how we can improve, make better, work with our teams as they are. But the one thing that remains absolutely clear, has always been at the heart of Superstruct, and that’s creative independence,” she said.

She concluded: “That’s how James \[Barton\] built Superstruct; it is what he intended. It’s why all the people who’ve come in to superstructure became part of the group. They signed up for creative independence, and that’s why the code is making that very clear and very public.”
