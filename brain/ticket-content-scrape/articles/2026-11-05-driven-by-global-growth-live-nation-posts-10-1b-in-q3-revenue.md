# Driven By Global Growth, Live Nation Posts $10.1B In Q3 Revenue

## Summary

Live Nation Entertainment reported Q3 2026 revenue of $10.1 billion (+19% YoY), beating analyst expectations, with 61 million fans attending concerts and EPS of $1.74 topping consensus. Ticketmaster revenue grew 9% to $870 million, while secondary (resale) gross-ticket value held flat overall — resale volume rose in sports but fell in concerts, which the company attributes to anti-scalping measures. International markets now outpace the US in both attendance and sponsorship-revenue growth.

### Key Points
- Q3 revenue $10.1B (+19% YoY), beating Zacks consensus of $9.57B; EPS $1.74 vs. $1.50 estimate.
- Concerts segment revenue $8.7B (+20%); attendance 61M (+19%), international +29% vs. domestic +13%.
- Ticketmaster revenue $870M, +9% YoY.
- Secondary (resale) gross-ticket value flat overall; sports resale growing, concert resale declining — attributed to anti-scalping measures.
- Sponsorship revenue $506M (+14% YoY).
- Through October, Live Nation sold 170M tickets (+13% YoY); expects ~180M fans for full-year 2026.

### Highlights
- Ticketmaster Q3 revenue: $870M, +9% YoY.
- Secondary/resale gross-ticket value flat; concert resale down, sports resale up — company credits anti-scalping tech.
- International attendance/sponsorship now exceeds US, a shift in Live Nation's growth mix.

### Industry Problem

Steady Ticketmaster revenue growth alongside flat-to-declining concert resale volume signals that primary-market anti-scalping controls are measurably reshaping secondary-market dynamics — a live data point on how the dominant primary platform's enforcement choices ripple into resale supply, while reinforcing the scale (and associated antitrust scrutiny) a primary platform competes against.

### Proposed Solution

Live Nation's own answer is tighter primary-side anti-scalping/bot controls that suppress unauthorized concert resale. For Etix, the #monitor takeaway is whether anti-scalping effectiveness becomes a client-facing selling point — independent promoters choosing platforms partly on bot/resale-control strength — which would turn this into a #build trigger for Etix's own resale-prevention tooling.

Live Nation Entertainment posted third quarter revenue of $10.1 billion, a 19% increase over the same quarter last year, as 61 million fans attended concerts.

Revenue was a beat over analyst expectations, as Zacks pegged the consensus at $9.57 billion.

On the bottom line, EPS of $1.74 was a strong beat, with Zacks setting it at $1.50.

“In a world of endless screens and AI-generated everything, the one thing that can’t be replicated is being there. Artists are taking their music to more fans in more places than ever, and fans are showing up in record numbers. Through October, we’ve sold 170 million tickets, up 13% and already ahead of all of last year. We now expect to welcome nearly 180 million fans in 2026,” Live Nation President and CEO Michael Rapino said. “This quarter attendance grew 19%, and year-to-date, international markets surpassed the U.S. in both concert attendance and sponsorship revenue — a powerful sign of how global live music has become. We’re investing in more venues around the world, giving artists more stages to build their careers and fans more opportunities to experience the magic of live music. With double-digit adjusted operating income growth across all three segments this quarter, we’re on track to deliver another record year and sustain double-digit growth for years to come.”

In the concerts sector, revenues were $8.7 billion, up 20% against the same period last year. The 19% attendance growth was driven especially by international markets — up 29% year over year, accounting for 60% of fan growth, 75% of fee-bearing ticket growth and a majority of sponsorship revenue.

On the venue side, international arena attendance surpassed domestic amp attendance and overseas venues accounted for 75% of the attendance growth. Venue Nation expects to host 75 million fans by year’s end.

Ticketmaster revenues were $870 million, up 9%. Secondary gross-ticket value is flat. Resale in sports has grown while that for concerts has declined, which Live Nation attributes to anti-scalping measures.

Sponsorship revenues were $506 million, up 14% against Q3 2025.
