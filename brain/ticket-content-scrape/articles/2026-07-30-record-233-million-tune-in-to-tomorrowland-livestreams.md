# Record 233 million tune in to Tomorrowland livestreams

## Summary

Tomorrowland's 2026 edition set a new digital-viewership record, drawing 233 million livestream views across YouTube, TikTok, Facebook, Instagram and other platforms — up over 20% (40 million views) from last year's benchmark of 193 million, even as in-person attendance (400,000 across two weekends) held steady. A new Apple Music Club Live partnership streamed six stages for free with Spatial Audio for subscribers, part of a broader trend of major festivals (Bonnaroo, Lollapalooza, ACL via Disney+; Glastonbury via BBC) signing global streaming deals to extend reach beyond physical attendance.

### Key Points
- 233 million global livestream views in 2026, up from 193 million in 2025 (+40 million).
- 400,000 in-person festivalgoers, 500+ artists, 16 stages; largest-ever mainstage (43m x 140m).
- New Apple Music Club Live partnership streamed six stages free, with Spatial Audio for subscribers.
- Belgian authorities restricted fireworks/pyrotechnics for weekend one due to wildfire risk from dry conditions.
- Tomorrowland brand expanding internationally: China (October), Thailand (November), Australia (CORE), plus France and Brazil dates in 2027.
- Other festivals (Bonnaroo, Lollapalooza, ACL, Fuji Rocks, Primavera Sound, Stagecoach) are adding similar global streaming partnerships.

## Transcript

Global viewership for the Belgian electronic mega-event swelled by over 20% from last year's benchmark

![Belgium's Tomorrowland 2026](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Belgiums-Tomorrowland-2026-©-Julien-Duval.png)

Belgium's Tomorrowland 2026 © Julien Duval

Global viewership for Tomorrowland swelled by over 20% for the Belgian electronic festival’s 2026 edition, raising the bar to a record 233 million views across digital livestreams, demonstrating the value of democratising access for international festival brands.

Running over concurrent weekends, 17-19 & 24-26 July, the Boon-based event welcomed 400,000 festivalgoers and hosted more than 500 artists across 16 stages at this year’s event. Martin Garrix, Dimitri Vegas, Amelie Lens, David Guetta, Armin van Buuren, Steve Aoki, and Charlotte de Witte all performed at the annual mega-festival, with Calvin Harris making his long-awaited debut.

While the in-person attendance was on par with years past, a new bar for online viewership across YouTube, [TikTok](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/08/tomorrowland-2025-makes-tiktok-live-event-history/), Facebook, Instagram, and other platforms was set for a second year in a row, rising by 40 million from last year’s benchmark of 193 million.

“For the millions who could not make the journey to Belgium, the world came to them,” promoters We Are One World say. “In living rooms and on phone screens across every continent, hearts beat in unison with every drop, every melody, every moment of silence before the music takes over again.”

Part of this year’s success was a new partnership with Apple Music through its [Club Live series](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/05/apple-music-establishes-new-live-event-streaming-series/), bringing live audio feeds from the Belgian festival to users around the world. Six stages were livestreamed for free throughout the weekends, with subscribers gaining access to Spatial Audio feeds following the broadcasts.

“Bringing Tomorrowland into Club Live allows us to connect fans everywhere with the energy, creativity, and atmosphere of the festival as it happens,” said Stephen Campbell, Apple Music’s global head of dance, electronic music & DJ mixes, with the partnership [announcement](https://tomorrowlandbelgium.press.tomorrowland.com/apple-musics-club-live-expands-with-tomorrowland-partnership-bringing-livestreams-from-one-of-dance-musics-most-iconic-festivals-to-fans-worldwide).

“Through Apple Music Club radio, we’re continuing to open up these cultural moments by bringing world-class performances from the dance floor to a global audience in real time, while preserving sets for fans to experience again on demand,” he said.

> Last year’s viewership record was set amid a heightened spotlight on the festival

It is worth noting that last year’s viewership record was set amid a heightened spotlight on the festival, after its mainstage was [destroyed in a huge fire](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/07/tomorrowlands-main-stage-destroyed-by-fire/) just two days before the festival’s start. Organisers pulled a replacement stage in just [36 hours](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/07/prg-reflects-on-tomorrowland-main-stage-drama/), allowing the 2025 event to proceed.

The 2026 event featured the largest-ever mainstage at the festival, which began in 2005, measuring 43 metres tall by 140 metres wide.

But the Tomorrowland team had to grapple with unusually dry conditions as Europe continues to suffer from heat-related impacts. Belgian authorities [restricted](https://www.nme.com/news/music/tomorrowland-2026-sees-ban-on-large-scale-fireworks-with-limited-pyrotechnics-for-weekend-two-over-wildfire-fears-3958876) fireworks and pyrotechnics for the festival’s first weekend due to wildfire fears, and while the limits were reduced for weekend two, many fireworks displays were replaced with LED visuals on the mainstage screens.

Looking ahead, the electronic festival brand will return to [China](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/07/tomorrowland-to-host-debut-event-in-china/) with a two-day event in October and [Thailand](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/03/tomorrowland-thailand-debut-sells-out-within-hours/) in November, along with the debut of its [CORE event in Australia](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/08/tomorrowlands-core-launches-global-tour/). Next year, events are lined up in France and Brazil.

The growth in this year’s online audience demonstrates the increasingly worthwhile pathway for organisers to [democratise access](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/08/y-m-d/) to live events through digital channels, especially as festival brands like Tomorrowland expand to new markets worldwide. More major festivals are signing up to bring their events to digital platforms, with Club Live launching earlier this year with EDC Las Vegas.

Additionally, Disney+ announced a [global broadcast partnership](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/05/lollapalooza-bonnaroo-acl-to-make-global-streaming-debut/) with Bonnaroo Music & Arts Festival, Lollapalooza, and Austin City Limits Music Festival, streaming the C3 Presents’ events worldwide for the first time. Amazon Music also boasts several high-profile partnerships, including Japan’s Fuji Rocks, Spain’s Primavera Sound, and the US’s Stagecoach, while Glastonbury can also be heard through the BBC’s various channels.
