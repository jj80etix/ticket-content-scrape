# Asia Pop’s Jay Chou, Katseye, BIG BANG, Reach Milestones Down Under

## Summary

Three major Asian pop acts — Jay Chou, Katseye, and BIG BANG — hit notable milestones in Australia as their audiences there grow larger and more diverse. Jay Chou's October 19 show drew 32,000 fans to Melbourne's Marvel Stadium, making him the first Mandarin-language artist to headline a solo stadium concert there, prompting an added Sydney date.

### Key Points
- Jay Chou: first Mandarin-language solo headliner at Melbourne's Marvel Stadium (32,000 fans, Oct. 19); added Sydney show Nov. 21 at Engie Stadium.
- Article frames Asian pop acts' Australian audiences as widening and diversifying.
- Promoted via Sky Music and Horizon Production.

Three major Asian pop acts reached milestones in Australia, as their audiences widen in size and become more diversified than ever before, and its stars amped up their production.

Following an October 19 show at Melbourne’s Marvel Stadium to 32,000 fans, Mandopop superstar Jay Chou quickly added a Sydney show Nov. 21 at Engie Stadium, with tix on sale Nov. 6. Chou played the venue March 2023 to over 30,000 fans.

The Melbourne show marked the first Mandarin-language artist to headline a solo concert at Marvel Stadium.

Speaking fluent English, he said, “I’d like to thank Australian buskers for playing my music” and struck a chord with international students in the crowd remarking, “Hearing my music can bring comfort to those who are studying or working away from their home (countries).”

The two Australian shows, through Sky Music and Horizon Production, are part of the Taiwanese singer songwriter’s Carnival II world tour which began this year in Hangzhou, China – his ninth since 2000, and sixth visit to Australia.

The epic production catered to the genre audience’s obsession with tech and video, with spectacular visual effects, lasers, a ring-shaped water explosion and a fairy tale setting with a castle, a rotating Ferris wheel and Big Ben.

Katseye’s debut Australian and New Zealand tour Feb/ March 2027 has ballooned to seven. Following what it described as “unprecedented demand”, Live Nation added four extra dates.

The act now plays Melbourne’s Rod Laver Arena (Feb.23, 24, 25); Sydney’s Afterpay Arena (Feb. 28, March 1) and Auckland’s Spark Arena (March 5, 6).

The all-female outfit had only visited Australia once, in May 2026, at a fan fest organised by their record label Universal Music Australia.

At a Q&A session in Sydney before media, fans and competition winners, they teased a tour (“I heard you guys have a really big arena somewhere”, and *Spice World* -type movie) before heading to Melbourne for a free set at the 5,000-seat Festival Hall.

BIG BANG made history at their Oct. 31 show at Sydney’s Accor Stadium by becoming the first K-Pop act to headline at the venue. Frontier Touring does not divulge attendance figures, but it is reported it was to a sell-out 80,000.

In 2015, BIG BANG played three shows Down Under to a total of 35,000 in two cities.

The current run of big ticket Asia-Pop tours began Nov. 2025 with TWICE, who played to 50,000 over two shows, which Live Nation hailed broke K-Pop tour record in the region.

Citing their 360-degree stage. LN’s lead promoter for Asia Pop, Wenona Lok told *Rolling Stone Australia* at the time, “To see this level of excitement from fans right across Australia really speaks to TWICE’s impact and how far K-Pop has come here.

“This tour is shaping up to be one of the most spectacular high-production live experiences we’ve seen on these shores.”

In March 2026, Asian promoter Applewood and Australia’s Untitled Group launched K-Pop festival Hello Melbourne at Melbourne’s Flemington Racecourse, “meeting the growing demand for premium K-pop experiences.” Attendance figures were not released.

In the meantime, industry analysts are predicting BTS’s four shows in Feb. 2027 could reach between 140,000 and 250,000.
