# Online Resellers Sue To Block DC’s RESALE Act

## Summary

SeatGeek, Vivid Seats, Gametime, and TickPick — via the Ticket Policy Forum — sued to block Washington, DC's RESALE Act, which would cap most concert resale prices at 10% above face value starting Jan. 1, 2027. The suit claims Commerce Clause, First Amendment, and DC Home Rule Act violations, arguing original face value is often unknowable to resellers and that the cap will push sales into unregulated, higher-fraud channels; the bill's sponsor, DC Councilmember Charles Allen, says the suit is an attempt to preserve "gouging" markups.

### Highlights
- Cap: 10% above face value for most non-sports/concert resale, effective Jan. 1, 2027; DC joins Maine in adopting hard resale price caps.
- Plaintiffs: SeatGeek, Vivid Seats, Gametime, TickPick, represented by the Ticket Policy Forum.
- Legal theories: Commerce Clause (law could reach interstate transactions with no DC party), First Amendment (compelled disclosure of unknown original price), DC Home Rule Act.
- TPF argues the cap will increase fraud/black-market resale; sponsor argues it stops inflated reseller markups enabled by bulk-buying technology.

### Industry Problem
State/local hard price caps on resale increasingly target the information-asymmetry problem (resellers can't always verify original face value) as a wedge to restrict secondary markets — a recurring flashpoint (also seen in Ontario, Maine) that creates compliance and legal exposure for independent resale platforms.

### Proposed Solution
Litigation is the resellers' chosen response; for a primary platform like Etix, the pattern argues for building clean, verifiable original-price/receipt data at the point of primary sale, which would both ease compliance for any resale channel and strengthen Etix's position if more jurisdictions adopt similar price-cap-plus-disclosure regimes. Monitor outcome of the suit and whether other jurisdictions follow DC/Maine.

## Transcript

By Aitor Diago/Getty Images

A group of secondary ticket market giants — including SeatGeek, VividSeats, GameTime and TickPick — filed a lawsuit to block the District of Columbia’s RESALE Act, which puts a cap on resale prices.

The resellers — represented by the Ticket Policy Forum — say the cap of 10% above face value for most concerts and other non-sports events in DC, set to go into effect at the beginning of 2027, is a violation of the Constitution and the DC Home Rule Act.

The suit says that because the act could theoretically regulate the sale of a ticket between a buyer and seller who are in different states and not in the District at all — for example, a Virginian might list for sale a ticket for a show at DC’s Capital One Arena that is ultimately bought by some in Maryland — it violates the Commerce Clause, which leaves the regulation of interstate commerce to Congress rather than local jurisdictions. Further, it says that it can be impossible to ascertain the original face value of certain tickets, making a 10% markup (or any markup) impossible to determine. The suit also claims a First Amendment violation because it compels disclosure of the original price, which may be information the reseller has no knowledge of.

The suit also claims several statutory violations.

“Under the guise of affordability, the District enacted a law that will drive ticket sales into the shadows, subjecting fans to greater risks from street-side scams and increased fraud,” said Brian Berry, Executive Director of the Ticket Policy Forum. “The legally flawed Act would apply to sales largely outside the District and set arbitrary resale-only price caps tied to the original cost of a ticket, information companies in the secondary market do not have and cannot disclose because they were not part of these initial transactions.”

The TPF said the price cap would increase fraud and black market resale, citing examples from other countries that have tried to implement similar legislation.

DC Councilmember Charles Allen, who sponsored the legislation, [told WUSA](https://www.wusa9.com/article/entertainment/music/ticket-resellers-hope-to-block-price-cap-legislation-with-lawsuit/65-28099521-b21f-4aa0-becb-9c4a8d1502c2) he is not surprised by the move.

“I introduced this law because companies like the ones behind this lawsuit are gouging DC residents using technology to scoop up as many live music tickets as they can and resell them at inflated prices – a scam that’s been extremely lucrative for them for years. Under my bill, that show is over,” Allen said in an email statement to the TV station. “However, it’s not surprising to see them try for an encore to preserve these enormous profits and the status quo. I think their time would be better spent preparing for January 1 when music fans will finally catch a break on the outrageous price mark-ups.”

With the RESALE Act, DC joined Maine in implementing hard caps for resale tickets.
