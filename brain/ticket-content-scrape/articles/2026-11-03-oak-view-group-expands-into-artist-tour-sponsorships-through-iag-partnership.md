# Oak View Group Expands Into Artist Tour Sponsorships Through IAG Partnership

## Summary

Venue operator Oak View Group is expanding its sponsorship-sales business into artist touring through a new partnership with talent agency Independent Artist Group, pursuing tour sponsorships, sponsor-backed presales, brand collaborations and fan activations for IAG's roster (including Metallica, Neil Young, Smashing Pumpkins and deadmau5). It's OVG's first branding partnership with a talent-booking agency, with OVG earning commissions on deals it brings in.

### Key Points
- OVG will pitch its existing North American brand relationships to IAG artists' tours rather than just its own venues.
- The announcement doesn't name specific artists, brands, or sponsorship terms yet.
- Follows other OVG commercial expansions: a PWHL sponsorship-sales agency role (February) and a BIG Digital fan-engagement tech partnership (August).
- Sponsor-backed presales are flagged as the most tangible potential impact for ticket buyers, though no allotments, pricing or eligibility rules have been disclosed.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/Oak-View-Group-e1728427226180.webp)

Oak View Group is expanding its sponsorship sales business into artist tours through a new partnership with Independent Artist Group, positioning its network of North American brand relationships to pursue commercial deals for the agency’s music and comedy clients.

The [agreement announced Nov. 2](https://www.oakviewgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/IAG-OVG-Release-FINAL.pdf?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) gives OVG’s Global Partnerships division a role in securing opportunities that could include tour title sponsorships, sponsor-backed ticket presales, artist-brand collaborations, on-site fan activations and meet-and-greets. OVG will work with IAG’s agents, artist managers and performers to determine which tour assets and promotional opportunities can be offered to sponsors.

Under the arrangement, OVG operates as an independent contractor alongside IAG’s existing Brand Partnership division rather than replacing the agency’s internal sponsorship operation. The companies have not announced specific artists, brand partners or tours participating in the program.

The deal represents OVG’s first branding partnership with a talent-booking agency, [according to The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/music/music-industry-news/oak-view-group-iag-announce-branding-partnership-1236745560/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). The publication also reported that OVG will earn commissions on the partnerships it brings to IAG, although financial terms were not disclosed.

IAG represents major touring performers including Metallica, Neil Young, the Smashing Pumpkins and deadmau5. Their inclusion on the agency’s roster does not establish that any of them has committed to a sponsorship deal under the new arrangement.

The effort is centered on North America, where OVG intends to pitch IAG touring assets to established brand partners and prospective new sponsors. It establishes a framework for multiple potential campaigns rather than a single sponsorship arrangement involving one artist or event.

“Music is a natural fit for OVG,” Dan Griffis, the company’s president of global partnerships, said in the announcement. OVG’s sponsorship operation has long sold naming rights and commercial partnerships for sports teams, leagues and entertainment venues, but the IAG agreement provides a more direct route into the business of individual artist tours.

In an interview with The Hollywood Reporter, Griffis said artists and promoters frequently encounter brands that “don’t know how to spend money in this space.” He suggested that experiential partnerships would be an emphasis, rather than limiting the effort to traditional advertising placements.

Jarred Arfa, IAG’s executive vice president and head of global music, told the publication that agency branding teams often focus on larger commercial campaigns. OVG’s existing relationships could be particularly useful in finding local sponsors for individual tour stops, he said, noting that a venue might have 10 to 15 brand partners already connected to the building.

That creates an opportunity to sell more than an arena sign or a single promotional appearance. A company sponsoring a venue could potentially extend its relationship into a concert or a broader touring campaign, while artists and their representatives gain another source of commercial revenue. OVG, in turn, could pursue sponsorship business associated with tours even when performances take place outside its own venue portfolio.

The arrangement follows other efforts to extend OVG’s commercial reach beyond individual buildings. In February, the Professional Women’s Hockey League [appointed OVG as its exclusive sponsorship sales agency for the league and all eight teams](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/02/oak-view-group-sponsorship-revenue-pwhl/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), giving the company access to a range of team and league marketing opportunities. OVG also [partnered with BIG Digital in August](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/08/oak-view-group-taps-big-digital-fan-engagement-technology/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) to explore interactive displays and branded fan-engagement technology at its North American venues.

For ticket buyers, the most tangible potential development is the inclusion of sponsor-backed presales. Those could give participating brands a role in distributing access to certain ticket offers, but the announcement does not specify presale allotments, pricing, eligibility rules or any particular concerts. It also does not announce a new ticketing provider or changes to primary ticket distribution.

OVG and IAG have not disclosed whether the arrangement is exclusive, how many tours they expect to pursue or when the first sponsorship campaign might launch. Its significance will ultimately depend on the artist and brand partnerships the two companies are able to secure.
