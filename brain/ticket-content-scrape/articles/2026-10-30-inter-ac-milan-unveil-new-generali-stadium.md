# Inter, AC Milan Unveil New Generali Stadium

## Summary

Inter Milan and AC Milan unveiled renderings of Generali Stadium, the new venue that will replace San Siro, set to open in 2031 ahead of Euro 2032. Foster + Partners and MANICA (designers of Wembley) are leading design on the stadium, which the clubs say will support sustained competitiveness; naming rights went to Italian insurer Generali.

### Key Points
- Replaces the historic San Siro ("La Scala del calcio"), built 1926
- Opening targeted for 2031, ahead of the Italy/Turkey co-hosted Euro 2032
- Designed by Foster + Partners and MANICA (Wembley Stadium designers)
- Clubs (owned by Oaktree/Brookfield and RedBird Capital) bought the land last year

FC Internazionale and AC Milan, two of the most historic clubs in Italian professional soccer, unveiled images of their new stadium.

Generali Stadium will replace San Siro but retain the spirit of the beloved stadium known as “La Scala del calcio,” originally built in 1926 and hosting FIFA World Cup matches in 1934 and 1990.

The two Serie A clubs, which are owned by Oaktree, part of Brookfield’s credit platform and Redbird Capital Partners, purchased land for the stadium last year to construct a new stadium, and it’s set to open in 2031—just in time for the 2032 UEFA Euro tournament co-hosted by Italy and Turkey.

Foster + Partners and MANICA, bringing together Lord Norman Foster and David Manica, are working together on designing the new venue, which the clubs say creates “the infrastructure needed to support sustained sporting success, unlock new opportunities for growth and strengthen their ability to compete and win at the highest international level.” The two firms also worked on London’s Wembley Stadium.

The two clubs struck a naming rights deal with an Italian insurance company worth €650 million ($725 million) over 20 years, according to ESPN, making it one of the most lucrative naming rights transactions in sports.

The 71,500-seat stadium aims to deliver a more intimate experience for fans with “two seating tiers form a continuous 360-degree bowl, creating exceptional sightlines and an immersive connection between fans and pitch,” according to a press release from the clubs.

At either end, the Curvas “rise as single, steep walls of support, drawn forward toward the field of play with seating inclinations of up to 37 degrees to amplify both atmosphere and visual impact.”

The club owners also aim to build a cultural district where the public can gather. The 281,000-square-meter project will include green areas, retail destinations and new public destinations where guests can celebrate soccer and Italian heritage.

“The project will create a year-round destination for sport, culture and entertainment while contributing to the attractiveness and international appeal of Milano,” the release said. Sustainability will also be considered through natural ventilation, photovoltaic energy generation, and measures designed to improve efficiency, reduce environmental impact, and support long-term operational performance.
