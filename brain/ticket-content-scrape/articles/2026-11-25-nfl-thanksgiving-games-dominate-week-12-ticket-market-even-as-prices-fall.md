# NFL Thanksgiving Games Dominate Week 12 Ticket Market, Even as Prices Fall

## Summary

Ticket-price tracker TicketData's analysis of the NFL's five Thanksgiving-week games shows strong but declining demand: all five rank among the week's six most expensive matchups by get-in price, even though prices for each have fallen over the past month from earlier peaks. The piece highlights how the "Thanksgiving premium" varies sharply by market (Dallas consistently commands a premium, Detroit does not) and how weather, team performance, and halftime-entertainment announcements move secondary-market pricing independent of the holiday itself.

### Key Points
- All five Thanksgiving-week games rank among the top six priciest NFL matchups of Week 12, despite 3%-43% price drops over the past 30 days.
- Dallas has a consistent Thanksgiving pricing premium regardless of opponent quality; Detroit's Thanksgiving prices run below its season average.
- Pittsburgh's Black Friday game price fell from $263 to $133 after consecutive losses, showing on-field performance directly moves resale pricing.
- Rams-Packers Thanksgiving Eve game saw a ~10% price bump after U2's halftime show was announced; Wu-Tang Clan, Miranda Lambert, and Goo Goo Dolls halftime announcements produced no measurable price effect.
- Analyst recommends against waiting for further price declines on games already near their pricing floor.

### Highlights
- Get-in prices as of Nov. 24: Packers-Rams $283, Bears-Lions $330, Eagles-Cowboys $147, Chiefs-Bills $275, Broncos-Steelers $142 — all down 3%-43% month-over-month.
- U2 halftime-show announcement drove a measurable (~10%) price increase; other halftime acts did not.

### Industry Problem
Secondary-market pricing for marquee live events is driven by a complex mix of factors beyond simple scarcity — team performance, weather, and entertainment add-ons — making demand and pricing difficult for any ticketing or resale platform to forecast reliably.

### Proposed Solution
Illustrates the value of real-time dynamic-pricing and demand-signal tooling (of the kind third-party trackers like TicketData sell); a primary platform could watch for similar demand-signal integrations as a monitoring input rather than build its own, since this item concerns secondary-market resale pricing, not primary sales.

## The Thanksgiving Premium Isn’t Universal

Few NFL traditions are as established as Thanksgiving football in Detroit and Dallas. But despite decades of hosting games on the holiday, the Lions and Cowboys have experienced markedly different ticket pricing trends in recent years.

For Dallas, Thanksgiving has consistently been one of the strongest dates on the home schedule over the past two seasons, even when the quality of the visiting opponent might not otherwise warrant especially high ticket prices.

Last year’s matchup against Kansas City was the most expensive Cowboys home game of the season by a considerable margin, according to Pagello. The previous Thanksgiving also produced one of the team’s priciest tickets, despite the visiting New York Giants entering the game with a 2-9 record.

“So the holiday seems to carry Dallas on its own,” Pagello said.

The opposite has been true in Detroit, where Thanksgiving tickets in each of the previous two seasons ended up being less expensive than tickets to the average Lions home game.

According to TicketData’s historical analysis, the Lions’ Thanksgiving get-in price finished approximately 14% below their home-game average in 2024 and 20% below that average in 2025, despite hosting a Green Bay Packers team that entered last year’s matchup with a 7-3-1 record.

The contrasting results suggest that there is no automatic Thanksgiving premium across the NFL. The holiday may elevate a matchup in one market without producing the same effect elsewhere, even in a city with an equally established Thanksgiving tradition.

This year, Detroit is commanding the highest get-in price of the five holiday games, with tickets to its matchup against the Bears costing more than twice as much as tickets to the Cowboys-Eagles game as of Tuesday evening.

Whether that price ultimately represents a premium over Detroit’s other home games will depend on where the market settles by kickoff.

## Cold Rain and a Black Friday Discount

Perhaps the most intriguing ticket market of the holiday slate is in Buffalo, where the Bills will host their first-ever Thanksgiving home game at the new Highmark Stadium.

A nationally televised primetime matchup between the Bills and Chiefs would appear to have virtually everything going for it from a ticket demand standpoint. The weather, however, may be working against it.

On Tuesday, forecasts called for temperatures in the upper 30s and a high likelihood of rain at kickoff. Pagello noted that the distinction between rain and snow could be particularly meaningful for a fan base accustomed to attending games in winter conditions.

“Snow is rare enough that it’s difficult to definitively say whether it leads to a boost in ticket prices,” Pagello said. “That being said, rain absolutely pushes prices down, and the data confirms that. I certainly think a game in 30-degree temperatures with snow is much more appealing than one in 38-degree temperatures with rain.”

Despite the anticipation surrounding the matchup, Pagello said the get-in price for Thursday’s game was running below the average of Buffalo’s five previous home games at an equivalent point before kickoff.

“The rain forecast definitively appears to be having an effect on prices for the Chiefs-Bills game,” he said. “It’s arguably one of the 2 most anticipated games on Buffalo’s schedule this year, yet the current get-in price is actually below the average of their 5 previous home games at the same point before kickoff, which I think is quite telling.”

The [Chiefs-Bills ticket market](https://www.ticketdata.com/events/75571032?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) reached a new low of $275 on Tuesday afternoon, down from a recorded high of $525 in April.

In Pittsburgh, meanwhile, fans considering attending the NFL’s Black Friday game are finding considerably lower prices than they would have paid earlier this month.

Tickets for the Broncos-Steelers matchup reached a high of $263 on Nov. 15, according to TicketData. By Tuesday, the get-in price had fallen as low as $133.

Pagello believes the deterioration in pricing is largely attributable to Pittsburgh’s recent on-field struggles, including a 38-18 loss to the division rival Cincinnati Bengals followed by a 19-16 loss to Philadelphia.

The holiday spotlight has not insulated the game from the kind of price movements that routinely accompany changes in a team’s fortunes during the regular season.

## U2 Gives Thanksgiving Eve Tickets a Boost

The NFL’s growing emphasis on marquee musical entertainment during its holiday broadcasts raises another interesting question for the ticketing industry: Can a halftime performance measurably increase the price of attending a football game?

In at least one instance this year, TicketData found evidence that it can.

Netflix and the Rams [announced on Oct. 20](https://www.therams.com/news/rams-thanksgiving-eve-halftime-show-matchup-green-bay-packers-streaming-netflix?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) that U2 would headline the halftime show of the inaugural Thanksgiving Eve game at SoFi Stadium, with the Irish rock band celebrating its 50th anniversary.

“U2 did indeed have an effect, though nothing tremendous,” Pagello said. “We saw about a 10% jump in ticket prices when U2 was announced in October. Not a massive increase, but certainly not insignificant.”

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/image-4-466x600.png)

The other three major halftime entertainment announcements did not produce similar results, however.

Wu-Tang Clan will perform during halftime of the Bears-Lions game in Detroit, Miranda Lambert will take the stage during the Eagles-Cowboys game in Dallas, and Goo Goo Dolls will perform during halftime of the Chiefs-Bills matchup in Buffalo.

Pagello said TicketData did not observe meaningful or measurable price increases following any of those announcements.

While the entertainment may enhance the experience for fans attending the games, the pricing data suggests that not every high-profile musical addition is necessarily capable of expanding the pool of prospective ticket buyers enough to move the market. U2’s performance appears to be an exception, at least among this year’s Thanksgiving halftime headliners.

## Should Fans Wait to Buy Thanksgiving NFL Tickets?

With prices for all five holiday games having fallen over the past month, prospective attendees might be tempted to hold out for additional savings.

Pagello cautioned, however, that much of the downward movement may already have occurred for some of the matchups, and that waiting until the last possible moment does not necessarily guarantee the best available price.

“That’s not to say there isn’t room for further declines, but for at least some of these games, we’re likely already at or near the bottom, so it’s difficult to recommend waiting much longer,” he said.

Fans can monitor the latest pricing and historical trends for all five games, along with the remainder of the week’s NFL schedule, through [TicketData’s Week 12 ticket price tracker](https://www.ticketdata.com/?category=NFL%2520Week%252012&date_range=ANY&sort_by=date&sort_order=asc&change_window=30day&utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

“As always, I’d recommend keeping a close eye on prices,” Pagello said. “If you start to see the downward trend reverse and prices begin ticking back up, that’s usually a signal that it is a good time to buy.”
