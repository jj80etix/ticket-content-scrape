# Suno Hires Former Ticketmaster VP to Lead Songkick, Years After Hacking Scandal

## Summary

Suno, the AI music company, has named longtime Ticketmaster/Live Nation executive Michael Chua to lead its live-music division and the Songkick concert-discovery platform it acquired from Warner Music Group in late 2025. Chua's background is notable: he consulted for Songkick in 2013, years before Ticketmaster's admitted hacking of Songkick's CrowdSurge-derived ticketing systems (2013-2015), which led to a 2020 DOJ deferred-prosecution agreement and $10M fine, and a separate $110M civil settlement with Live Nation in 2018. Suno says Chua's team will connect Songkick's discovery/recommendation layer with Suno's AI music-creation tools, focused on emerging artists, but has not disclosed any plan to build direct ticketing capabilities — leaving open whether an AI-native entrant could eventually compete for ticket transactions rather than just referrals.

### Key Points
- Michael Chua (ex-VP of Ticketmaster global business development, ran integrations with Google, Spotify, Apple Music, TikTok, Snap) becomes VP/GM of Suno Live Experiences, announced Oct. 8, 2026.
- Suno acquired Songkick (the concert-discovery brand, not the CrowdSurge ticketing business) from Warner Music Group in November 2025 as part of a copyright-litigation settlement and licensing deal.
- The CrowdSurge ticketing business that Ticketmaster illegally accessed (2013-2015) was separate, shut down in 2017, and its remaining assets were acquired by Live Nation in 2018 for an undisclosed sum on top of the $110M civil settlement.
- Suno declined to say whether Songkick will stay limited to discovery/referrals or eventually build transaction/ticketing capability.
- No new ticketing infrastructure, timetable, or vendor relationships have been announced; this is a personnel/strategy signal, not a product launch.

### Highlights
- An AI-native company (Suno) now controls a concert-discovery platform with direct historical ties to a prior Ticketmaster antitrust/hacking scandal, led by a Ticketmaster veteran.
- Suno explicitly left open the question of building "direct ticketing capabilities" via Songkick — a potential new entrant vector into primary ticketing.

### Industry Problem
Discovery-to-purchase handoff (recommendation platforms feeding traffic to incumbent ticketers) is a long-standing structural chokepoint; an AI music platform with its own audience and discovery data is positioned to disintermediate that handoff if it chooses to move downstream into ticketing.

### Proposed Solution
No concrete solution is described by Suno yet. For Etix: monitor whether Songkick/Suno signals any ticketing-infrastructure ambitions or partnership RFPs, since an AI-native discovery platform entering ticketing would be a new competitive vector worth an early relationship or integration play rather than a reactive one.

AI music company Suno has hired longtime Ticketmaster executive Michael Chua to lead its live-music operations and the Songkick concert-discovery platform, bringing a former Songkick consultant back to a business with a remarkable history of conflict with his former employer.

Chua was named vice president and general manager of Suno Live Experiences in an Oct. 8 announcement [reported by Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/suno-hires-ticketmaster-exec-michael-chua-live-experiences/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). The division includes Songkick, which Suno acquired from Warner Music Group last year as part of a broader partnership involving AI-generated music.

The appointment has an unusual connection to Songkick’s past. Responding to questions from TicketNews, a Suno publicist confirmed that Chua consulted for Songkick in 2013, before his years at Live Nation and Ticketmaster. Asked about that work and the company’s ticketing plans, the representative said, “Mike’s just settling into his role so we don’t have anything else to share at the moment.”

Chua’s consultancy preceded Songkick’s 2015 merger with CrowdSurge, the direct-to-fan ticketing company targeted in the illegal access scheme that later led to criminal proceedings against Ticketmaster and former executives. There is no evidence connecting Chua to the improper access or the conduct that was prosecuted.

## A Ticketing Rival, a Lawsuit and a Criminal Case

Songkick began as a service for tracking artists and discovering concerts. CrowdSurge, which merged with Songkick in 2015, offered a different product: technology that enabled artists and fan clubs to distribute concert tickets through their own presales. That business posed a competitive challenge to Ticketmaster, which controlled primary ticketing at many venues through exclusive agreements.

Artist-directed presales were especially contentious because they allowed some tickets to be distributed outside Ticketmaster’s systems. Prosecutors later described how Ticketmaster sought to reclaim that business while monitoring the competing service’s products, customers and planned ticket offerings.

In December 2017, [TicketNews examined an internal Ticketmaster presentation](https://www.ticketnews.com/2017/12/ticketmaster-took-songkick-threat-seriously/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) prepared for senior management in 2014. Its slides included a strategy for “Beating Crowdsurge,” identified the rival as a competitive threat and discussed bringing presale tickets back onto Ticketmaster’s platform. The presentation also included material that raised questions at the time about how Ticketmaster obtained its knowledge of the competitor’s systems.

Those questions became more concrete in the criminal investigation. According to [a 2020 Justice Department announcement detailing Ticketmaster’s admissions](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edny/pr/ticketmaster-pays-10-million-criminal-fine-intrusions-competitor-s-computer-systems-0?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), company personnel repeatedly used credentials retained by a former CrowdSurge executive to enter password-protected systems without authorization between 2013 and 2015. They obtained confidential ticket sales information and business documents and monitored unpublished ticketing pages to identify artists working with the rival.

Federal prosecutors described a 2014 Ticketmaster meeting attended by at least 14 Live Nation and Ticketmaster employees where the former CrowdSurge executive demonstrated access to a confidential artist-sales dashboard. The Justice Department said information collected through the intrusions was used to improve Ticketmaster’s competitive position.

Ticketmaster entered a deferred prosecution agreement in December 2020 and agreed to pay a $10 million criminal penalty, along with compliance and reporting obligations. The Justice Department said the resolution addressed computer-intrusion and fraud charges; it was separate from Songkick’s earlier civil litigation.

Two former Ticketmaster executives, Stephen Mead and Zeeshan Zaidi, pleaded guilty in related criminal proceedings. Mead, who had previously worked at CrowdSurge, was [sentenced in September 2024](https://www.ticketnews.com/2024/09/former-ticketmaster-executive-sentenced-after-hacking-rival/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) to one year of supervised release and ordered to forfeit $67,970. Zaidi, the former head of Ticketmaster’s Artist Services division, pleaded guilty in 2019.

Songkick’s civil action had begun in 2015, alleging anticompetitive conduct and later incorporating claims involving improper access to confidential information. As [TicketNews reported when the dispute was resolved](https://www.ticketnews.com/2018/01/live-nation-songkick-settle/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the direct-ticketing business shut down in 2017, after Warner Music Group acquired the separate Songkick concert-discovery service and brand.

In January 2018, Live Nation agreed to settle the civil litigation for $110 million, [according to its SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1335258/000133525818000004/lyv-20180112x8k.htm?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). That payment was *separate from* the undisclosed amount Live Nation paid to acquire remaining ticketing technology and other assets. The settlement ended the lawsuit without a trial of the civil claims.

That corporate separation matters today. The Songkick Suno acquired from Warner is the concert-discovery operation, not the CrowdSurge-derived ticketing business that Live Nation bought in 2018. The shared name and Chua’s return provide the historical connection, not continuity of ownership of the former ticketing competitor.

## Where Suno May Take Songkick Next

Suno [acquired Songkick in November 2025](https://investors.wmg.com/news-events/news-releases/news-details/2025/WARNER-MUSIC-GROUP-AND-SUNO-FORGE-GROUNDBREAKING-PARTNERSHIP/default.aspx?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) through its agreement with Warner Music Group, which also resolved Warner’s copyright litigation against the AI music company and established a licensing relationship. Warner said Songkick would continue as a fan-facing concert-discovery destination under its new owner.

Now Suno says Chua’s team will connect Songkick’s concert recommendations and live-event discovery capabilities with Suno’s music-creation platform. Chief Music Officer Paul Sinclair said a central focus will be emerging performers, including participants in [Suno’s Spark artist-development program](https://www.suno.com/blog/introducing-spark?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), which provides selected independent musicians with grants, marketing support and opportunities to collaborate.

It is a different business proposition from CrowdSurge’s attempt to control artist-directed ticket allocations, but Chua’s experience makes the next step worth watching. TicketNews [covered his role in Ticketmaster’s Apple Music integration earlier this year](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/03/ticketmaster-integrates-with-apple-music-to-transform-concert-discovery/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), a partnership designed to move listeners from discovering a concert to purchasing a ticket through a connected service.

The unanswered question is whether Suno intends to keep Songkick focused on recommendations and referrals to existing ticket sellers or eventually develop direct ticketing capabilities. A deeper role in transactions could raise familiar questions about access to ticket inventory, distribution partnerships and who controls the relationship between artists and ticket buyers.

Suno has not announced plans to revive CrowdSurge-style direct ticketing, disclosed a ticketing infrastructure strategy or set a timetable for new Songkick transaction features. TicketNews asked the company about those possibilities and its relationships with Ticketmaster and other ticketing providers; its publicist said it had nothing further to share while Chua settles into the role.

For now, Chua takes charge of the part of Songkick that survived the earlier fight, tasked with joining music discovery to live experiences. Whether that eventually extends to selling tickets, rather than helping fans find them, remains an open question.
