# Two Canadian music rights orgs join to streamline royalties

## Summary

SOCAN, Canada's leading performing rights organization, has acquired the Canadian Musical Reproduction Rights Agency (CMRRA) from SoundExchange, consolidating the country's two main music-rights bodies under one roof. The deal adds mechanical rights administration to SOCAN's existing performance-rights and international royalty services, with CMRRA continuing to operate as a standalone unit. Financial terms were not disclosed and the deal is subject to regulatory approval.

### Key Points
- SOCAN represents roughly 200,000 Canadian songwriters, composers, and music publishers.
- CMRRA licenses, collects, and distributes royalties for the majority of songs recorded, sold, and broadcast in Canada.
- SoundExchange, the US-based neighbouring-rights organization, had owned CMRRA since 2017.
- The move is framed as streamlining royalty administration amid streaming and digital-licensing shifts.
- Deal terms undisclosed; pending regulatory approval and customary closing conditions.

In a move aimed at streamlining music royalties in Canada, leading performing rights organisation SOCAN has acquired the Canadian Musical Reproduction Rights Agency (CMRRA), bringing the country’s two main music rights organisations under one umbrella.

With the acquisition from SoundExchange, SOCAN – the Society of Composers, Authors and Music Publishers of Canada – will add mechanical rights administration to its existing performance rights and international royalty services. CMRRA will continue as a standalone unit while benefiting from SOCAN’s resources and network.

“Acquiring CMRRA is about strengthening Canada’s music rights landscape and building a more seamless experience for music publishers and creators,” says Jennifer Brown, CEO of SOCAN. “By bringing together CMRRA and SOCAN, we have an opportunity to harness our respective strengths and support long-term growth.

“SoundExchange has been a strong and reliable home for CMRRA, ensuring continuity and care through a period of significant change in our industry. That foundation in reproduction rights and expertise will play a central role in advancing a more connected and effective music rights management system in Canada.”

SOCAN represents roughly 200,000 songwriters, composers, and music publishers in Canada, while the CMRRA says it licenses, collects, and distributes royalties for the majority of songs recorded, sold, and broadcast in Canada.

The US-based SoundExchange, meanwhile, is the world’s largest neighbouring rights organisation in the world and distributes performance royalties around the world. It acquired CMRRA in 2017 and has overseen its growth for nearly a decade.

This move will help streamline music rights administration in Canada as the industry continues to adapt to streaming, digital licensing, and new technologies.

CMRRA president Paul Shaver says: “As we look ahead, this next chapter with SOCAN represents an exciting opportunity to build on that foundation, drive innovation, deepen our service to music publishers and self-published songwriters, and contribute to a more integrated and effective rights administration ecosystem in Canada.”

Financial terms of the transaction were not disclosed. It is subject to regulatory approval and other customary closing conditions.
