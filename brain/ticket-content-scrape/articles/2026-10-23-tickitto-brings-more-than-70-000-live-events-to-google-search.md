# Tickitto Brings More Than 70,000 Live Events to Google Search

## Summary

UK ticketing-technology company Tickitto has expanded its distribution integration with Google Search, surfacing listings for more than 70,000 of its 90,000+ catalogued live events (concerts, sports, theater) directly in Google's event-discovery features. Tickitto operates as a B2B infrastructure/API layer rather than a consumer marketplace, aggregating curated inventory from venues, promoters, brokers and agents; CEO Dana Lattouf frames the move as part of a "distribution everywhere" strategy that also spans travel-industry partners (InteleTravel, Hickory Global Partners) and, eventually, AI assistants and loyalty programs.

### Key Points
- Google integration surfaces 70,000+ of Tickitto's 90,000+ catalogued events; users clicking through land on Tickitto's own booking platform.
- Tickitto is a B2B API aggregator, not a consumer-facing marketplace — curates (rather than indiscriminately aggregates) primary and secondary inventory to avoid duplicate listings.
- Growth since 2025 acquisition by travel distributor InteleTravel; expanded in April 2026 to corporate travel via Hickory Global Partners.
- CEO Dana Lattouf: next targets for the same distribution model are "AI assistants, loyalty programs" beyond search and travel.
- Piece notes peer moves: Eventbrite's Google Events/Bandsintown discovery expansion, and SeatGeek's "Ask SeatGeek" AI-search feature.

### Highlights
- 70,000+ events now discoverable via Google Search through Tickitto's feed, out of a 90,000+ event catalog.
- Tickitto positions itself as inventory/API infrastructure for any consumer touchpoint (search, travel, AI assistants, loyalty), not just its own site.
- Named peer moves: Eventbrite (Google Events/Bandsintown) and SeatGeek ("Ask SeatGeek" AI referral search) are pursuing similar discovery-distribution expansion.

### Industry Problem
Ticket discovery is fragmenting away from dedicated ticketing sites toward search engines, travel platforms, and (next) AI assistants — platforms that rely only on their own site risk losing visibility to vendors who distribute inventory everywhere fans already are.

### Proposed Solution
Tickitto's approach — a standardized API feed that lets any consumer-facing surface (Google, travel tools, eventually AI assistants) plug in without building ticketing infrastructure — is a template Etix could evaluate for its own distribution: a lightweight, curated inventory feed to high-traffic discovery surfaces (search, AI assistants) rather than relying solely on direct-site traffic.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/tickitto-google-search-featured-1200x628-1.webp)

Tickitto is expanding the distribution of its live-event inventory through Google Search, bringing listings for more than 70,000 concerts, sporting events, theater performances and other experiences into Google’s existing event-discovery features.

The integration, announced October 21, gives the UK-based ticketing technology company a significant new distribution channel beyond its existing travel and technology partners. It also illustrates the growing importance of connecting ticket inventory with platforms where consumers already search for entertainment, rather than relying exclusively on dedicated ticketing websites.

In responses provided directly to TicketNews, Tickitto founder and CEO Dana Lattouf explained how the integration works, what inventory is involved and why the company believes ticket distribution is increasingly moving beyond traditional marketplaces.

“We believe the future of ticketing is distribution everywhere, not discovery in one destination,” Lattouf told TicketNews.

## Curated Inventory From Primary and Secondary Sources

Tickitto’s complete catalog includes more than 90,000 events worldwide, Lattouf clarified, with more than 70,000 included in the Google integration.

That inventory comes from a network of professional suppliers that may include event rightsholders, venues, organizers, professional ticket brokers and authorized agents.

The range of suppliers means Tickitto works across different parts of the ticketing supply chain rather than relying on inventory from a single ticket issuer or marketplace.

“We curate our inventory, rather than indiscriminately aggregating listings,” Lattouf said. “This means we avoid duplicate event listings at the source while bringing together a broad range of live experiences through one API.”

The company did not provide a specific breakdown of primary versus resale inventory appearing through Google. Lattouf said the precise mix depends on which inventory is included in the integration.

For Google, the value of the arrangement is access to event information from multiple suppliers without establishing and maintaining separate relationships and technical integrations across the ticketing industry, according to Lattouf.

“Instead of building and maintaining separate integrations across the supply chain, Google will access a broad catalogue through one connection,” she explained.

Google already operates an [event-discovery experience](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/event?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) that allows participating event publishers and ticketing providers to surface event details and ticket links in relevant search results. Tickitto’s integration expands the inventory available through that existing infrastructure rather than introducing a new Google ticket marketplace.

The announcement did not disclose commercial terms of the arrangement or provide figures on ticket sales attributable to Google searches.

## Expanding Beyond Travel Distribution

The Google relationship represents another step in Tickitto’s expansion since its acquisition by travel distribution company InteleTravel in March 2025.

[InteleTravel acquired Tickitto](https://blog.inteletravel.com/inteletravel-acquires-tickitto?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) to incorporate live-event tickets and experiences into its broader travel distribution business, allowing travel advisors to book entertainment alongside flights, accommodations and other travel arrangements.

That strategy has subsequently expanded into corporate travel. In April, [Hickory Global Partners announced the integration of Tickitto](https://www.hickoryglobalpartners.com/insights/hickory-global-partners-launches-event-and-ticketing-integration?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), enabling participating travel management companies and corporate travel departments to source and distribute tickets for concerts, sports and other experiences.

The company’s focus on building ticket distribution infrastructure predates those relationships. [TicketNews reported in 2020](https://www.ticketnews.com/2020/06/tickitto-hires-stubhub-uk-veterans/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) on Tickitto’s recruitment of two former StubHub UK executives as the startup worked to expand its supplier relationships and API-based distribution platform.

The broader ticketing industry has increasingly pursued similar opportunities to reach customers outside conventional ticket marketplace websites, although the underlying business models differ.

Eventbrite, for example, [expanded its event-discovery distribution](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/06/eventbrite-expands-music-discovery-tools-new-artist-feature/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) through platforms including Google Events and Bandsintown. SeatGeek has also invested in integrations with outside discovery platforms while developing its own conversational search technology, including the [Ask SeatGeek feature launched earlier this year](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/08/seatgeek-launches-ask-seatgeek-as-ai-referrals-convert-at-twice-the-rate-of-traditional-search/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

Tickitto’s approach differs in its emphasis on serving as an inventory aggregation and technology layer that other platforms can use to incorporate ticketing into their existing consumer experiences.

Lattouf sees that model extending beyond search engines and travel distribution into AI assistants, loyalty programs and other services that already maintain relationships with potential ticket buyers.

“Search engines, AI assistants, travel platforms and loyalty programmes already own valuable consumer relationships,” she said. “What they often lack is a simple way to access and distribute fragmented live-event supply.”

For Tickitto, the opportunity is to provide that supply infrastructure without requiring those companies to build their own ticketing operations.

“Our work with Google, alongside our travel distribution through InteleTravel and Hickory Global Partners, reflects this direction,” Lattouf said. “The opportunity extends beyond selling tickets. It is about making live events a native part of how people search, plan trips, use loyalty benefits and decide what to do.”
