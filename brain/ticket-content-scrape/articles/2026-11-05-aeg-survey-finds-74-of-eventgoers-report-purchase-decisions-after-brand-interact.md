# AEG Survey Finds 74% of Eventgoers Report Purchase Decisions After Brand Interactions

## Summary

AEG Global Partnerships released "The Live Effect 2026," a four-country survey of 12,098 adults finding that 74% of live-event attendees made a purchase decision after interacting with a brand at an event. The research, which expands on a 2025 UK-only study, highlights sponsorship as a channel for brand engagement beyond traditional advertising.

### Key Points
- Surveyed 12,098 adults across the US, UK, France, and Germany who attended a live event in the prior 12 months.
- 87% took some action after brand exposure at a live event; 58% found event sponsorships easier to notice than other advertising.
- 56% said event-associated brands were more memorable.
- Study released Nov. 2, 2026 by AEG Global Partnerships.

AEG Global Partnerships has released new international research showing that 74% of live event attendees reported making a purchase decision after interacting with a brand at an event, highlighting the commercial opportunities sponsorships offer beyond traditional advertising and venue signage.

The findings come from [*The Live Effect 2026*](https://aegworldwide.com/press-center/press-releases/live-effect-aeg-research-reveals-how-live-entertainment-turns-attention?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), a study released Nov. 2 that surveyed 12,098 adults across the United States, United Kingdom, France and Germany who had attended a live event within the previous 12 months. The research expands on an initial U.K.-only study conducted in 2025.

According to AEG, 87% of respondents reported taking some form of action following exposure to a brand at a live event, while 58% said sponsorships at live events were easier to notice than advertising elsewhere. Another 56% said brands associated with live events were more likely to remain in their memory.

The findings suggest that sponsorships can create opportunities for brands to engage consumers beyond the immediate event experience, potentially influencing purchasing decisions and longer-term customer relationships.

The research also examined the broader spending associated with attending live events. AEG found that more than half of attendees’ total spending occurred outside the cost of admission, including transportation, accommodations, food, beverages and merchandise.

That spending represents an additional commercial opportunity for companies looking to connect with consumers before, during and after an event. Travel providers, hospitality companies, retailers and other businesses can participate in the broader economic activity generated by live entertainment, rather than limiting their involvement to advertising inside a venue.

The study found that 77% of respondents considered their most recent live event money well spent, while 79% said they would make the same purchase again. Additionally, 80% said the atmosphere of a live event could only be experienced in person, and 78% associated live events with lasting memories.

“Consumers aren’t simply attending live events; they’re investing in them,” said Paul Samuels, president of global partnerships at AEG International.

## Sponsorships Expand Beyond Traditional Branding

The research arrives as major live entertainment companies are expanding their sponsorship businesses to create additional revenue opportunities around artists, tours, venues and fan experiences.

Earlier this week, [Oak View Group announced a partnership with Independent Artist Group](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/11/oak-view-group-iag-artist-tour-sponsorships/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) that will extend OVG’s sponsorship sales capabilities into artist tours. The arrangement allows the company to pursue opportunities ranging from tour title sponsorships and artist-brand collaborations to on-site activations and sponsor-backed ticket presales.

That partnership reflects the broader commercial strategy highlighted by AEG’s findings: brands are increasingly being offered ways to engage directly with audiences rather than relying exclusively on advertising exposure.

AEG has also demonstrated how sponsorship arrangements can incorporate ticket access and customer benefits.

In October, [AEG and O2 extended their naming rights agreement for London’s The O2 through 2038](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/aeg-and-o2-extend-london-venues-naming-rights-deal-through-2038/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). The renewed partnership includes a 50% increase in the number of tickets available through O2’s Priority Tickets program, along with expanded early access for eligible customers to performances at indigo at The O2.

The agreement illustrates how a long-standing venue sponsorship can extend into ticket distribution, customer loyalty programs and other benefits that give participating brands a more direct relationship with eventgoers.

For ticketing companies and event organizers, such arrangements create additional commercial opportunities around the ticket-buying process. Sponsor-supported presales, exclusive offers and loyalty-based access can provide value to participating consumers while creating new ways for brands to reach potential customers.

They can also influence how ticket inventory is allocated among different presale groups, raising practical questions about access for fans who do not participate in a particular sponsor’s program.

AEG’s research does not measure those ticketing-specific effects, but it provides additional context for why companies are pursuing sponsorship relationships that extend beyond the physical event.

The findings also warrant some qualification. The reported purchase decisions and brand interactions are based on survey responses rather than independently verified transactions. AEG’s publicly released summary does not specify how many reported purchase decisions resulted in completed sales or provide conversion rates for individual sponsorship campaigns.

AEG Global Partnerships, which commissioned the research, is also responsible for selling sponsorships across the company’s live entertainment portfolio, giving it a direct commercial interest in demonstrating the effectiveness of event-related marketing.

The survey was conducted in August 2026. While the study expands the geographic reach of AEG’s original U.K. research, the company has not published comparable country-by-country findings in its announcement.
