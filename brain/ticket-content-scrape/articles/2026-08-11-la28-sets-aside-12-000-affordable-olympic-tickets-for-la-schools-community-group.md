# LA28 Sets Aside 12,000 Affordable Olympic Tickets for LA Schools, Community Groups

## Summary

LA28 has set aside 12,000 Olympic and Paralympic tickets for bulk purchase by Los Angeles-area schools, youth sports organizations, chambers of commerce and community groups, with 5,000 of those priced at a flat $28 and the community-allocation average landing around $70. The program has so far reached 28 youth sports organizations, 26 schools and 29 community groups, and sits alongside LA28's broader affordability push (the $28 price tier) as general ticket sales continue through its draw system and Ticket Drop 2. The article also notes registration for a shot at tickets has surged past 12 million, more than double the 5 million reported in February.

### Key Points
- 12,000 tickets set aside for bulk purchase by LA schools, youth sports orgs, chambers of commerce and community groups; 5,000 priced at $28, community-allocation average ~$70.
- Program has reached 28 youth sports organizations, 26 schools and 29 community groups across the LA region so far.
- LA28 has opened a group ticket interest program for organizations seeking future allocation access.
- General public sales continue via LA28's ticket draw system, currently in "Ticket Drop 2."
- More than 12 million fans from 200+ countries and all 50 US states have registered for a chance to buy tickets — over double February's 5 million figure.
- LA28 CEO Reynold Hoover calls it "the most in-demand ticketed event in Olympic history."

### Highlights
- $28 flat price point applied to nearly half of the 12,000-ticket community allocation, explicitly branded as LA28's affordability mechanism.
- Distribution channel bypasses individual consumer purchase — allocation goes to institutions (schools, youth sports orgs, community groups) for bulk buy-in, a structured low-income-access model rather than a discount code or lottery weighting.
- Registered demand (12M+) is running far ahead of available inventory, framing affordability/access as a scarcity problem, not just a price problem.

### Industry Problem
Large-scale, high-demand events price out lower-income patrons and community organizations by default; without a deliberate low-cost, bulk-allocation channel, affordability skews entirely toward whoever can pay market or draw-lottery prices, leaving schools and community groups without a practical path to attend.

### Proposed Solution
LA28's approach is a structured institutional set-aside — a fixed low-price tier sold in bulk directly to schools/community organizations rather than left to general on-sale mechanics. An independent primary platform like Etix could offer organizers a similar built-in "community allocation" ticket type/workflow (capped-price inventory routed to verified schools, nonprofits and civic groups) as a standard event-configuration option, turning ad hoc affordability gestures into a repeatable product feature for clients running large public-interest events.

LA28 is carving out a chunk of its Olympic and Paralympic ticket inventory for Los Angeles-area schools, youth sports organizations and community groups, with thousands of seats priced at just $28.

The effort comes as LA28 continues broader Olympic ticket sales through its ticket draw system.
