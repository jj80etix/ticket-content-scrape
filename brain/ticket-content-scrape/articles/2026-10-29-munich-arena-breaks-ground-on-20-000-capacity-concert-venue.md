# Munich Arena Breaks Ground on 20,000-Capacity Concert Venue

## Summary

Live Nation broke ground on Munich Arena, a 20,000-capacity, purpose-built music venue near Munich International Airport slated to open in 2029. The company is positioning it as Germany's first arena designed specifically for live music.

### Key Points
- 20,000-capacity venue near Munich International Airport, opening 2029
- Features a 270-degree horseshoe seating bowl and production facilities built for large-scale touring shows
- Design incorporates Bavarian-flag-inspired architecture and sustainability features including rooftop solar powering an ice-based cooling system
- Live Nation says the project aims to strengthen Munich's position as a European touring destination

## Transcript

Photo courtesy Live Nation

Construction has begun on Munich Arena, a planned 20,000-capacity concert venue near Munich International Airport. The project is scheduled to open in 2029.

The new facility is being developed specifically for live music, with a design intended to accommodate major international touring productions and provide an improved concert experience for audiences.

The arena will feature a 270-degree, horseshoe-shaped seating bowl designed to bring fans closer to the stage, along with acoustics engineered for live music and production facilities built to accommodate large-scale performances. Live Nation describes the project as Germany’s first arena purpose-built for live music.

The arena’s architecture will incorporate a design inspired by the Bavarian state flag. The project also includes sustainability features, including rooftop solar panels that will power a system designed to freeze water during the day. The resulting ice will be used to help cool the building ahead of evening events.

Live Nation GSA President Marek Lieberberg said the project is intended to strengthen Munich’s position as a destination for major international touring acts.

“We are building the Munich Arena for those magical moments of energy and connection that only live entertainment can create,” Lieberberg said. “Our ambition is clear: when international tours choose their stops in Germany and Europe, Munich should be the first choice.”

The arena is also expected to deliver substantial economic benefits for the surrounding region. According to estimates from Deloitte cited in the announcement, Munich Arena is projected to generate approximately €355 million in annual economic value for Munich and neighboring communities once operational.

The facility is expected to welcome approximately 1.5 million fans, while construction of the project is expected to create an estimated 3,425 jobs.

The groundbreaking ceremony was attended by Bavarian Prime Minister Dr. Markus Söder and Freising Lord Mayor Monika Schwind, who both emphasized the project’s anticipated economic and cultural importance.

“Today’s groundbreaking is a special moment for Freising,” Schwind said. “With the concert arena, a project of international radiance is emerging that can sustainably strengthen our city culturally and economically.”

Schwind also emphasized the anticipated benefits for businesses throughout the community.

“In the future, we will be able to welcome guests from all over Germany and beyond to Freising. Our local hotels, restaurants, and many other businesses will benefit from this,” she said. “After years of intensive planning, I am pleased that today we are taking this important step into the future of our city.”

Söder characterized Munich Arena as a flagship project for Bavaria, highlighting its planned capacity and modern facilities.

“Bavaria knows how to host major events: The ‘Munich Arena’ in Freising, near Munich, will be a flagship venue – Germany’s largest and most modern concert arena, capable of accommodating up to 20,000 fans,” Söder said.

He also pointed to the project’s role in the state’s broader ambitions.

“The Munich Arena fits perfectly with our Bavarian strategy: We want to be leading in high-tech innovation and joy of life,” Söder said.

Lorenz Schmid, managing partner of Munich Arena, said the groundbreaking follows years of planning and development and represents a major step toward realizing the project.

“The groundbreaking marks a significant milestone, the result of a clear vision, entrepreneurial determination and years of sustained effort,” Schmid said. “With Munich Arena, we are creating a landmark project for Munich and Bavaria that will deliver lasting cultural and economic value while setting new standards for live entertainment across Europe.”

Schmid said the development team is now concentrating on bringing the venue to completion.

“Our focus is now firmly on delivery, with a clear ambition: to open one of Europe’s leading concert arenas in 2029.”
