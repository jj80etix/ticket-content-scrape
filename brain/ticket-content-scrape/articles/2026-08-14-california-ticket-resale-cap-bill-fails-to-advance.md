# California ticket resale cap bill fails to advance

## Summary

California's AB 1720 ("Fans First Act"), which would have capped concert/theatre resale prices at 10% above face value, failed to advance out of the Senate Appropriations Committee on August 13. The bill had already been narrowed to apply only to venues under 3,000 capacity, and StubHub reportedly spent $3.4m lobbying against it despite support from NIVA, Live Nation, and artist groups. California joins the list of major markets without a resale price cap, unlike Vermont and other states that have enacted one.

### Key Points
- AB 1720 stalled in Senate Appropriations on Aug. 13, having been amended to cover only venues of 3,000 capacity or less (concerts/theatre/comedy, not sports).
- StubHub reportedly spent $3.4m lobbying against the bill in California.
- Supporters included NIVA, Music Artists Coalition, National Independent Talent Organisation, and Live Nation.
- Sponsor Matt Haney called the outcome disappointing but said it doesn't change the underlying "predatory resale" problem.
- California would have joined Vermont among states capping resale at 10% above face value; a separate Vermont law (effective July 1) already bans speculative ticketing and requires seller-type disclosure.

### Highlights
- AB 1720 (10%-above-face resale cap, sub-3,000-cap venues only) died in CA Senate Appropriations, Aug. 13, 2026 — no effective date now applies.
- StubHub's $3.4m in CA lobbying spend against the bill is a concrete data point on resale platforms' willingness to fund opposition to price-cap legislation.
- Vermont's cap + speculative-ticketing ban + seller-disclosure rules took effect July 1, 2026, and remain the operative state-level model.

### Industry Problem
Resale platforms whose revenue scales with resale price have a direct financial incentive to lobby against price caps, and that lobbying is proving effective even in large, favorably-disposed markets — meaning state-by-state resale regulation will likely stay a patchwork rather than converge quickly.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor state-level resale cap efforts (this is the second failed/weakened attempt after prior amendments) and the states where caps did pass (Vermont, Maine, DC) for enforcement data; Etix's primary-market, fixed-fee model is inherently aligned with the "reasonable resale" framing these bills use, which is a differentiator worth surfacing to prospective clients in states debating similar bills.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**A California bill aiming to place a cap on resale tickets has failed to progress from the Senate Appropriations Committee yesterday (August 13).**

The California Fans First Act, or AB 1720, was originally introduced by state assembly member Matt Haney earlier this year. The bill would have capped resale prices at 10% above face value, in a bid to allow for reasonable exchanges while tackling extreme price gouging.

Rules would have applied to tickets for concerts, theatre shows, comedy, or live entertainment, but not for sports tickets. However, during its journey to the Senate, the law was reduced with an amendment, which limited the rules to smaller 3,000-capacity or lower venues – removing many of the venues and shows that would draw higher levels of resale.

While the bill had garnered support from the likes of the National Independent Venue Association (NIVA), Music Artists Coalition, the National Independent Talent Organisation, Live Nation Entertainment and more, StubHub [reportedly spent](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/music/music-industry-news/stubhub-lobbying-spending-concert-ticket-bill-1236664212/) $3.4m (£2.5m/€2.94m) in lobbying expenses in California fighting the bill.

Following the bill’s failure to advance Haney told *Billboard* in a statement: “AB 1720 would have helped remove the incentives that fuel predatory ticket resale while still allowing someone who can’t attend a show to resell their ticket and recover their costs.

“I’m disappointed the bill did not advance, but this outcome doesn’t change the reality that fans, artists, and venues are losing out while middlemen extract more and more profit.”

The Music Artist Coalition added that this was a “disappointing outcome” and “every fan, at every show, needs to be protected”.

California would have joined other US states that have implemented a cap on resale prices such as [Vermont](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/05/vermont-to-implement-ticket-resale-price-cap/).

The legislation came into effect on July 1, limiting resale prices to 10% above face value while banning speculative ticketing and deceptive URLs. It also requires clear disclosure for buyers so they know if they are buying from a venue or reseller.
