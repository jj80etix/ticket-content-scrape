# Settlement Reached Ensuring The Future Of Forest Hills Stadium Through 2040

## Summary

Forest Hills Gardens Corporation, West Side Tennis Club, and Tiebreaker Productions (operator of Forest Hills Stadium) reached a settlement resolving pending litigation, securing the Queens, NY venue's concert business through 2040 with funds directed toward neighborhood preservation.

### Key Points
- Resolves years of litigation over noise, zoning, and the stadium's concert business versus its residential surroundings.
- A June 2026 appellate ruling had allowed nuisance claims to proceed while dismissing a separate residents' group lawsuit and rejecting a zoning challenge.
- A related dispute with New York City over street closures for concerts was settled in March 2026 ($150,000 payment, improved police-coordination commitments).
- Agreement directs funds toward preservation and beautification of Forest Hills Gardens.

## Transcript

Forest Hills Gardens Corporation (FHGC), West Side Tennis Club (WSTC) and Tiebreaker Productions, LLC (operator of Forest Hills Stadium) announced an agreement that resolves all pending litigation among the parties and sets a collaborative path forward for the century-old Queens, New York, institutions.

Under the agreement, the stadium will continue to put on shows for years to come, with significant funds directed back into the neighborhood – a portion of which is dedicated to the preservation and beautification of Forest Hills Gardens.

The stadium has faced a series of legal disputes over its outdoor concerts primarily involving neighborhood noise, zoning restrictions and whether the venue’s seasonal concert business is consistent with the residential character of the surrounding community.

Residents raised concerns about amplified music, late-night performances, traffic and the impact of large crowds, while Tiebreaker has sought to preserve the stadium as a major live-music destination.

In June 2026, a New York appellate court allowed the FHGC’s nuisance claims to proceed, while dismissing a separate lawsuit brought by Concerned Citizens of Forest Hills. The court also rejected the zoning argument in the FHGC case.

FHGC sued New York City, arguing that authorities improperly closed private neighborhood streets to accommodate concert crowds. The dispute was settled in March 2026, with the city agreeing to pay $150,000 and improve coordination with residents over police patrols.

The litigation didn’t establish a blanket ban on concerts at Forest Hills Stadium, but the surviving nuisance claims left the venue facing continued legal scrutiny over noise levels and the impact of concerts on residents. The new agreement ends pending litigation, establishes an operational framework through 2040, invests in the neighborhood and reinforces community safeguards.

The agreement establishes an initial term of seven years, with renewal options up to an additional seven years. The stadium may host up to 28 concerts per season during the initial term (up to 29 during renewal terms). All performances remain subject to strict operating standards, including a curfew for amplified sound and real-time audio monitoring aligned with City guidelines.

Tiebreaker will provide substantial payments to FHGC, including per-show payments that support the corporation, together with a one-time upfront payment, a portion of which is dedicated to restoration and beautification projects.

Under NYPD guidance, concert attendees will utilize designated routes to minimize disruption to residential corridors. The venue will continue to coordinate with NYPD in addition to deploying its own security personnel and post-show sanitation teams. The venue will also work with a community ombudsman to address resident needs in real time. Pickup zones outside of Forest Hills Gardens will be designated and coordinated with Rideshare providers.

The agreement provides for the voluntary end of all pending lawsuits between the parties, establishing a streamlined dispute-resolution process overseen by a designated Special Referee to ensure smooth ongoing collaboration.
