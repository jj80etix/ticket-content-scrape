# Live Nation Makes Sound Adjustments After Mystic Lake Amphitheater Complaints

## Summary

Mystic Lake Amphitheater (Shakopee, MN), a new 19,000-cap Live Nation-operated venue that sold 500,000+ tickets across 41 shows in its debut season, is adjusting sound systems after neighboring communities complained about audible bass.

### Key Points
- Opened June 2026; hosted Bob Dylan, Mumford & Sons, Guns N' Roses, Chris Stapleton, Iron Maiden, Dave Matthews Band, among others.
- Developed by Swervo Development Corp.; operated by Live Nation.
- Shakopee, Bloomington and Eden Prairie residents reported low-frequency bass audible inside homes; city set up a dedicated complaint webpage.
- Live Nation has added sound-dampening material and adjusted output in response.

## Transcript

![Photo via Live Nation](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/mystic-lake.jpg)

Photo via Live Nation

Mystic Lake Amphitheater is wrapping up its first season in Shakopee, Minnesota, after selling more than 500,000 tickets across 41 concerts, while Live Nation and city officials work to address noise complaints from surrounding communities.

The 19,000-capacity venue opened in June near Canterbury Park and quickly became a major stop on the Twin Cities concert calendar. The inaugural season included concerts from Bob Dylan, Mumford & Sons, Guns N’ Roses, Chris Stapleton, Iron Maiden and Dave Matthews Band, among others. The venue was developed by Swervo Development Corp. and is operated by Live Nation.

But residents in parts of Shakopee, Bloomington and Eden Prairie have also complained that concert noise, particularly low-frequency bass, can be heard inside their homes.

Shakopee Mayor Matt Lehman told the [*Star Tribune*](https://www.startribune.com/noise-complaints-rock-a-smooth-first-season-at-shakopee-amphitheater/601893489) that his office has received numerous calls and emails about sound from the amphitheater. City Council member Jim DuLaney said most concerts generated little or no feedback, while a smaller number created a noticeable impact in surrounding neighborhoods.

The city has created a dedicated webpage for amphitheater noise complaints and is asking residents to contact Live Nation while concerts are underway so venue personnel can respond and take sound measurements.

According to the city, Live Nation has installed additional sound-dampening material inside the amphitheater, adjusted upper speakers to direct sound downward and reduced volume from speakers in outside gathering areas. Venue staff have also traveled to locations where complaints were reported to measure sound levels.

Live Nation Minnesota Market President Josh Lacey told the Star Tribune that the company monitors conditions throughout concerts and adjusts the system when necessary. “We actively monitor sound levels from multiple locations at the venue during each show,” Lacey said.

Shakopee is also working with the Minnesota Pollution Control Agency, which has offered additional sound-monitoring equipment. City officials have noted that wind, humidity, temperature and other weather conditions can affect how far concert sound travels.

Live Nation and the city are considering additional monitoring equipment for neighborhoods that have received complaints ahead of the amphitheater’s second season. Other adjustments could be made based on information collected during the remaining shows and ahead of next summer.

There are no current plans to shorten the venue’s 11 p.m. curfew, despite requests from some residents. The focus instead remains on speaker adjustments, monitoring and other measures intended to limit sound traveling beyond the venue.

“The issue is complex but solvable,” DuLaney told the Star Tribune.

The complaints come during an otherwise strong opening season for the amphitheater. Live Nation said before the opening that the venue could generate an estimated $138 million in annual economic impact, and some nearby businesses have reported significant increases in traffic on concert nights.

Badger Hill Brewing Co., located near the venue in Shakopee’s social district, told the Star Tribune that sales increased about 45% on concert nights. Owner Chris Cole described the season as a learning experience involving traffic and other issues but said “mostly it was awesome.”

The amphitheater is already booking shows for its second season, with A Perfect Circle scheduled for May 23, 2027, and Stick Figure set for June 9.
