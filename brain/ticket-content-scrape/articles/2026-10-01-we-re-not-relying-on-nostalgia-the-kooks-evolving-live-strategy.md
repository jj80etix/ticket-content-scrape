## ‘We’re not relying on nostalgia’: The Kooks’ evolving live strategy

Agent Charly Beedell-Tuck and frontman Luke Pritchard explore leveraging nostalgia and an active resurgence for the band's anniversary tour

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Charly-Beedell-Tuck-Luke-Pritchard-1.png)

Charly Beedell-Tuck & Luke Pritchard

Off the back of The Kooks’ 20th anniversary run around *Inside In/Inside Out*, agent Charly Beedell-Tuck and frontman Luke Pritchard have told *IQ* that nostalgia and streaming have given rise to a younger core audience, powering the British band’s biggest live campaigns as they set the stage for their next chapter.

This year saw the indie rockers tour across a dozen European markets and a host of open-air shows in the UK and Ireland, making scenic stops including Halifax’s The Piece Hall, Dublin’s Trinity College, and London’s Old Royal Naval College, as part of the 20th anniversary of their 2006 album that’s sold over 2 million copies. It served as a continuation of the band’s [biggest headline tour](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/10/how-the-kooks-became-bigger-than-ever-after-20-years/) in 2025, where they rose to arena level across seven cities in their native UK.

“Things have really come together in the last year,” Luke Pritchard tells *IQ*. “We’ve played some of the best shows we’ve done; I can’t believe it sometimes. People have found this record – young people – and it feels exciting.”

Both Pritchard and Beedell-Tuck credit streaming as the reviving force behind the band’s catalogue, with 18 to 24-year-olds now The Kooks’ key demographic, making the timing of launching an anniversary run a balancing act of nostalgia and active resurgence.

“The fact that it continues to connect so strongly with a new generation shows its cultural impact, so it deserved its own moment and a live experience built specifically around it,” Beedell-Tuck tells *IQ*.

“Streaming has been hugely important in introducing The Kooks to a new generation,” the Solo head continues. “It has allowed songs from *Inside In/Inside Out* to live far beyond their original release cycle, and we can now see younger audiences discovering the catalogue in real time. That insight feeds directly into our live strategy – from the markets we prioritise and the rooms we play, to the setlist, content, and timing of campaigns. It means we’re not relying on nostalgia, but rather we’re responding to a genuinely active and growing audience.”

> “The shows reinforced the scale of their current momentum”

The succession of the campaigns can be demonstrated by a dual-pronged approach, designed to “show two very different sides of the band at scale,” she says. In London itself, the band played their biggest headline show to date – a sold-out night at The O2 (cap. 20,000) in October – and quickly returned to the UK capital nine months later to play to another 10,000 fans with a sell-out show with the [Labyrinth on the Thames](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/we-dont-take-this-for-granted-how-labyrinth-on-the-thames-is-navigating-success/) series, coincidentally located 10 minutes away.

“Announcing another major Greenwich show so soon after The O2 might have seemed like a brave move,” Beedell-Tuck says. “But selling it out within an hour of going on sale showed both the strength of the band and the genuine engagement of their fans. The shows reinforced the scale of their current momentum, while the Royal Naval College felt like a true moment in the band’s history that won’t be forgotten.”

For Pritchard, the album and the touring career it helped spark “surpassed everything I thought it could do,” he says, noting that the audience’s sentimentality for *Inside In/Inside Out* continues to surprise him.

“I see it in gigs a lot, when a song switches on, you’ve almost encapsulated someone’s memories. No one really tells you that when you start touring. It gives quite a lot of meaning to the whole thing,” he says.

> “There’s no denying the anniversary creates a strong platform for the next chapter”

Giving a changing audience that kind of experiential opportunity was the key strategic motivator for Beedell-Tuck, she says, in part as a springboard for what comes next.

“For me, it’s about celebrating the history and timelessness of the record, but with a very clear eye on the future. *Inside In/Inside Out* feels more relevant 20 years on, but so does the band. These shows give it the recognition it deserves in its own right. At the same time, there’s no denying the anniversary creates a strong platform for the next chapter – leading into a new album and campaign with an energised, growing audience already engaged.”

The same holds true for Pritchard: “We just play songs that we love, and we play songs that we know people will want to hear,” he says. “People will have strong feelings towards that one album, and for now it’s about that. I’m ready to put a little ribbon around 20 years, and that’s what’s nice for us – we can draw a line in the sand for this amazing thing, but we’re going to come back with something even better.”

The Kooks will round out the year with a slot at Mexico City’s Corona Capital in November, with the agent confirming there is a “huge amount coming down the pipeline” as they plan for the future.
