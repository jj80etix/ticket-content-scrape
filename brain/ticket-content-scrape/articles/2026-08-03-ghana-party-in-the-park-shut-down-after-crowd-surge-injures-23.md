# Ghana Party in the Park shut down after crowd surge injures 23

## Summary

London diaspora festival Ghana Party in the Park was shut down mid-event on August 1 after a crowd surge near the exit injured 23 attendees, three of whom were hospitalized. Organizers and police say the surge followed a small vendor-area fire that prompted a temporary gate closure, and the resulting crowd buildup surged when gates reopened.

### Key Points
- 23 people received medical care, some requiring CPR; 3 were hospitalized. Incident occurred at Copthall Playing Fields, Hendon, North London.
- Police attended just after 6pm and confirmed the event was cancelled due to an "overcrowding issue at the entrance."
- Trigger sequence: a small fire involving vendors led to temporary gate closures, and a crowd that had built up surged when gates reopened.
- The festival, running since 2005, typically draws 8,000-10,000 attendees from the UK's Ghanaian diaspora; this year's edition was headlined by Shatta Wale and co-produced with ShattaFest.
- Organizers apologized and said they would work with authorities to establish the full circumstances.

## Transcript

The long-running London diaspora event thanked emergency services and apologised to fans injured in "one of the most difficult days" in its history

London-based diaspora festival Ghana Party in the Park was forced to shut down part way through its event this past weekend (1 August) after “surging crowds” caused injuries to 23 fans.

Authorities confirmed 23 fans were given medical care, including some needing CPR, while three fans were hospitalised, after what appears in footage shared online appeared to be a crowd crush by the exit of the outdoor venue. In the footage, spectators can be seen falling underneath a rush of people heading for the exit.

Police confirmed officers attended the site at Copthall Playing Fields in Hendon, North London, just after 6pm, and that the event had been cancelled “after an overcrowding issue at the entrance saw several people sustain non-life threatening injuries”.

The crowd surge was caused initially by a small fire on the festival site, which event organisers said “involved vendors”. Police said the fire required gates to the site to be closed “temporarily”, only for a crowd which had built up around the gates to surge when they were reopened.

“The decision to close the festival was made solely on the grounds of health and safety. Officers from the London Ambulance Service and the London Fire Brigade responded to a major incident on site,” organisers said in a statement.

> “The safety and wellbeing of our attendees, staff, artists, contractors, and partners remains our utmost priority”

“Following discussions with the emergency services and our health and safety team, it was agreed that closing the festival was the safest option in order to protect all attendees. The safety and wellbeing of our attendees, staff, artists, contractors, and partners remains our utmost priority. We thank everyone for their cooperation and understanding during this difficult situation.”

“We wish all those who sustained injuries a speedy recovery, and safe journey back home to everyone. Further updates will be shared through our official Ghana Party in the Park channels as more information becomes available,” organisers added.

First staged in 2005, Ghana Party in the Park attracts between 8,000 and 10,000 attendees each year, largely drawn from the Ghanaian diaspora in the UK. This year’s event was headlined by Ghanaian dancehall artist Shatta Wale.

[Speaking to *IQ* in April](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/04/how-ghana-party-in-the-park-became-a-launchpad-for-ghanaian-artists-abroad/), organisers of Ghana Party in the Park said the event operates as a full-day cultural gathering featuring food vendors, craft sellers, businesses, and community activities.

The festival, which this year was produced in conjunction with ShattaFest, said it had welcomed fans to celebrate Ghanaian culture for over 20 years in a “safe and positive environment”, adding it is “committed to learning from this incident and will continue to work closely with the relevant authorities as they establish the full circumstances around what occurred.”
