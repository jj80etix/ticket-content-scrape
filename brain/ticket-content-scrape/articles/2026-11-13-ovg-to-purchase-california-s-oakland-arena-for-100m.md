# OVG to purchase California’s Oakland Arena for $100m

## Summary

Oak View Group has agreed to buy and take over operations of Oakland Arena for $100 million, with the deal brokered by the Oakland Acquisition Company and targeted to close by January 1, 2027. OVG will replace current operator Legends Global, which had separately bid $102.5 million for the arena; more than 1,600 workers across the arena and the neighboring Oakland Coliseum face layoffs as the broader complex sale completes, though OVG says it will rehire those who reapply.

### Key Points
- $100m purchase price; OVG takes over operations from Legends Global starting January 1, 2027.
- Part of a larger $240m sale of Oakland Arena and Coliseum from the city/county to private investors (AASEG/Loop Capital via the OAC).
- City of Oakland will receive 6% of future event ticket-sale revenue from both venues.
- 1,600+ workers (stagehands, hospitality, admin) face layoffs in the transition; OVG says it will hire those who reapply.
- Arena joins OVG's portfolio alongside Climate Pledge Arena, UBS Arena, Moody Center, and Acrisure Arena.

### Highlights
- $100m deal, closing targeted for January 1, 2027; OVG replaces Legends Global as operator.
- City of Oakland retains a 6% cut of future ticket-sale revenue from both the arena and Coliseum.

### Industry Problem
Consolidation among venue operators/developers continues to concentrate management of major arenas in a small number of global groups (OVG, Legends, ASM, Live Nation/CTS Eventim), reshaping which ticketing providers can partner with a given venue market by market.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor how OVG's operating model and exclusivity terms affect which ticketing providers can service the venue; no immediate build action, but a trigger to revisit the Oakland Arena relationship once the ownership transition completes.

## OVG to purchase California’s Oakland Arena for $100m

The global developer will also operate the music-forward venue, with the deal to be finalised in the coming weeks

![Oakland Arena](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Oakland-Arena.png)

Oakland Arena

Oak View Group (OVG) is set to purchase and take over operations of California’s music-forward Oakland Arena next year, the company confirms to *IQ*.

The global developer is set to buy the 19,200-capacity arena for $100 million (€90.5m) through a deal brokered by the Oakland Acquisition Company (OAC), and is aiming to take over operations from 1 January 2027.

“Oak View Group is excited to work with Oakland Acquisition Company, the City of Oakland, and Alameda County on the ownership transition of the Oakland Arena,” an OVG spokesperson tells *IQ*. “OVG is committed to building on the arena’s legacy as a premier destination for live music and entertainment in the Bay Area.”

With the deal, the company will take over from current operators, Legends Global, which had also submitted a $102.5m (€92.7m) bid to buy the arena outright earlier this year, in addition to offering a portion of advertising revenue to the city and county.

This comes after OAC chief Raymond Bobbit [told](https://www.sfchronicle.com/eastbay/article/oakland-coliseum-layoffs-22509681.php) the *San Francisco Chronicle* earlier this week that the venues’ staff “will transition from Legends to Oak View Group”, as reports emerged that they face layoffs.

The global firm has managed both venues since 2012, shepherding the arena to its new chapter after the departure of three major sporting tenants in five years.

> “Our opportunity is to respect what works \[and\] invest in what could be better”

Oakland Arena and the neighbouring 65,000-capacity Oakland Coliseum are owned by the city of Oakland and Alameda County, which are in the final stages of selling their 50% respective stakes in the complex and its venues.

More than a year ago, the two parties agreed to sell both to a group of private investors, the African American Sports & Entertainment Group (AASEG) and financier Loop Capital, through its purchasing coalition, the OAC, for a combined $240m (€217m).

In May, amidst efforts to raise capital to purchase and redevelop the 112-acre complex, OAC and the county agreed to spin off the sale of the arena for $100m (€99.5m).

With the arena separated, the term sheet outlined that the OAC will enter into a $115m (€104m) payment plan for the Coliseum complex over several years. When the purchase of both properties is completed, projected by the end of the year, the entity is preparing plans to sell arena ownership to OVG, with the aim of taking effect from 1 January.

As part of the deal, the city will receive 6% of future event ticket sale revenue from both venues as long as they are operational.

The Coliseum will remain separate and [be part of the AASEG](https://www.aasegoakland.com/), with early plans to redevelop the site as a sports and entertainment destination. Legends Global’s management agreement for the Coliseum will also expire at the end of the year.

> “This is not a rescue mission in itself”

When it does, more than 1,600 workers – stagehands, hospitality, administration, janitorial, and other workers – are set to be laid off, according to reports. OVG has indicated it will hire those who reapply for arena positions.

On the matter, OVG tells *IQ*: “We value the experience, dedication, and contributions of the arena’s workforce. We look forward to working with the community of Oakland and the unions on a smooth transition for the arena’s employees, so fans and artists enjoy a first-class experience.”

Oakland Arena is a hotspot for touring in the US market, with major tours from Ariana Grande, Olivia Rodrigo, J. Cole, Enhyphen, Gorillaz, and John Summit coming through this year. It is a rare example of a music-forward arena, having no sports tenants, giving it wide flexibility and an open calendar.

OVG founder Irving Azoff previously [spoke publicly](https://www.kqed.org/news/12090665/oakland-approves-125-million-coliseum-sale-clearing-way-for-irving-azoff-overhaul) about plans to revitalise the arena, saying: “Our opportunity is to respect what works, invest in what could be better, invest in the community and bring our unique artist relationships, both as a management company and as OVG, to get to the next chapter of this great building.”

He added: “This is not a rescue mission in itself; it’s just an opportunity to take what I consider to be a B experience and make it an A-plus experience.”

When the deal is complete, the arena will join spaces like Seattle’s Climate Pledge Arena, New York’s UBS Arena, Austin’s Moody Center, and Palm Springs’ Acrisure Arena in OVG’s portfolio.
