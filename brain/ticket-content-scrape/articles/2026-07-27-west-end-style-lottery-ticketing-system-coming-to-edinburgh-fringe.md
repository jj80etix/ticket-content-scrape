# West End-style lottery ticketing system coming to Edinburgh Fringe

## Summary

Edinburgh Festival Fringe is piloting a West End-style ticket lottery for a limited number of sold-out shows via the Sold Out Board website, giving fans a chance to buy standard-price tickets to shows with no remaining capacity. Unlike West End lotteries, which often offer discounted tickets, the Fringe pilot will only offer full-price tickets.

### Key Points
- First-time pilot for Fringe 2026, covering a limited set of sold-out shows.
- Entrants win the chance to buy a ticket at standard (not discounted) price.
- Modeled on the West End's long-running sold-out-show lottery system.
- Sold Out Board frames the lottery as a demand-signal and audience-relationship tool for producers, not just a consolation mechanism for fans.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**For the first time, Edinburgh Festival Fringe is set to introduce a “lottery” ticketing system for sold-out shows similar to that used in London’s West End.**

Audience members will be able to use the Sold Out Board website to enter a draw to get the chance to buy final tickets for Fringe shows which have no remaining capacity.

It is commonly seen in the West End, with a small number of tickets for shows held back for the lottery system, often at discounted prices.

The initiative is set to be piloted at this year’s event with a limited number of sold-out shows.

“At the Fringe, the highest praise is not a five-star review,” said Celia Dugua, founder of Sold Out Board, as reported by [The Scotsman](https://www.scotsman.com/arts-and-culture/west-end-lottery-ticketing-system-to-come-to-fringe-for-first-time-8813059).

“It’s your name written up in chalk on the sold-out board that every box office keeps, a running list of the shows that filled every seat that day. To be chalked up is to have arrived.

“In a world where AI algorithms and the biggest PR budgets are increasingly shaping what people are booking, Sold Out Board is a recommendation signal that is a pure reflection of human demand.

“Behind each sold-out board is an act of waste, because the people who tried to book those shows and could not are lost. And the next time the company performs, they start again from nothing.

“A show’s real asset is the relationship with the people who love it, or wanted to and could not get in. That is how a Fringe hit grows into something bigger. A sold-out board should be more than a trophy, it is an opportunity to speak to a group of patrons telling us exactly what they want to see next.”

Unlike on the West End, where tickets are often sold at discount prices, the Fringe lottery will only give audience members a chance to get their hands on a standard-price ticket which would otherwise not be available.
