# Exclusive: DEAG founder Peter Schwenkow plots return to live business

## Summary

DEAG founder Peter Schwenkow, two years after stepping down, is launching a new pan-European buy-and-build platform with €100M in committed funding from Swiss investor Ufenau Capital Partners (a longtime DEAG backer), aiming to consolidate mid-sized promoters/producers/operators — primarily in family entertainment, with music promoters also of interest — while explicitly excluding ticketing from its near-term ambitions. Schwenkow will serve as CEO alongside Grandezza Entertainment partner Thomas Schütte and Ufenau's Ralf Flore, folding in his existing Grandezza business and targeting at least two acquisitions announced by year-end; acquired companies are expected to retain their identities and operational autonomy.

### Key Points
- Peter Schwenkow (DEAG founder, led it 1978–2024) launches a new pan-European buy-and-build platform with €100M available funding from Ufenau Capital Partners.
- Focus: mid-sized promoters, producers, and operators, primarily family entertainment (cited as higher-margin than "pop and rock"), with music promoters also targeted; ticketing is explicitly excluded from near-term plans.
- Structure: Schwenkow as CEO, Grandezza Entertainment partner Thomas Schütte and Ufenau's Ralf Flore rounding out leadership; Grandezza folded into the new venture.
- At least two acquisitions targeted to be announced by end of 2026; acquired businesses are expected to keep their brand identity and operational autonomy.

### Industry Problem
Live-entertainment consolidation capital is actively hunting for mid-sized promoter/producer/operator targets in Europe, a segment where independent primary ticketing platforms often have existing client relationships that could be disrupted or strengthened depending on who acquires them.

### Proposed Solution
No product response is described — this is an M&A/consolidation signal to monitor: track which promoters/operators the new Schwenkow-Ufenau vehicle acquires and whether any are current or prospective Etix clients, since new ownership often triggers a ticketing-vendor review.

## Transcript

Two years after stepping away, he has announced plans to build a pan-European network of mid-sized promoters, producers, and operators

![Peter Schwenkow](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Peter-Schwenkow.png)

Peter Schwenkow

Two years after stepping away from the institution he created, DEAG founder and German stalwart Peter Schwenkow has revealed plans to create a pan-European company with €100 million in available funding, in an exclusive conversation with *IQ*.

The to-be-named umbrella company aims to establish a network of mid-sized promoters, producers, and operators in the live and family entertainment domain across Europe. Launching with €100 million in available funding from Swiss-based Ufenau Capital Partners, it is poised for a rapid buy-and-build strategy that mirrors both DEAG and Ufenau’s business approach, the latter of which is expanding its hand in the live domain after having invested in DEAG over 20 years ago.

The announcement is a high-profile return for [Schwenkow](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2024/02/the-deutsche-entertainer-peter-schwenkow/), who founded DEAG in 1978 and led it for nearly five decades until [handing over the reins](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2024/03/deag-announces-management-handover/) to Detlef Kornett in March 2024, stepping back to an advisory role that concluded with the [sale of Grandezza](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/11/deag-sells-family-entertainment-company-to-peter-schwenkow/) to Schwenkow late last year.

“After 46 years at the front, I thought I needed a break,” the 72-year-old tells *IQ*. “I have a big family – five children, eight grandchildren – and I needed to figure out what I wanted for the next decade. After two years enjoying myself, I found that in the end, the entertainment gene is in my blood,” he says, joking about the new venture’s “in-house research institute” in his grandchildren.

With the new venture, the trio are aiming to build an umbrella company with a 330° offering, omitting ticketing from their near-future ambitions. While the primary focus will be on family entertainment, something with margins “much healthier than the pop and rock business”, he says, Schwenkow confirms interest in music promoters as well.

> “We’re in a people’s business, and in the end, it’s all about the people”

“Anything that entertains people at a certain level of quality, in the long run, deserves an investment. If a festival comes along and we think it fits, we’ll buy it. It’s a load of money that is available, but we will not throw it out the window. I was always a careful CEO, and this is the way it’ll stay, but it’s definitely a sector that should be invested in,” he says.

Grandezza will be folded into the operation, with plans to announce at least two acquisitions by the end of the year. Acquired businesses are expected to retain their identities and operational autonomy. Schwenkow will serve as CEO, with his Grandezza Entertainment partner Thomas Schütte and investor Ralf Flore of Ufenau rounding out the starting cohort.

“The underlying spirit is that I’m an entrepreneur, and I have great respect for entrepreneurs, so I want to find ones that fit into our new organisation. The identity of the companies that we want to invest in is made by these entrepreneurs,” he says.

“We’re in a people’s business, and in the end, it’s all about the people. If we can offer a bigger and stronger organisation to those who’ve developed their own products, then we can help them bring their products to other territories,” he asserts.

The venture will begin with a European focus, though Schwenkow is encouraged by the globalisation of the entertainment industry and exporting market-specific content to new horizons.

> “In the end, we may compete with whoever is in the market”

“In the end, the philosophy of our business is simple: it’s the people’s business. They make other people happy, and if you have the right product, then you can bring that product anywhere else in Europe,” he says.

That philosophy carries through to their competitive ambitions as well. Though Schwenkow will carry on as a shareholder in the market-leading company he helped build, he says he’s not afraid to compete with other players in the space.

“We will focus more or less on business where fewer people are involved. In the end, we may compete with whoever is in the market,” he says. “The focus is on supporting entrepreneurs, helping them grow their businesses, trying to keep a double-digit margin, and keeping promoters and audiences happy.”

“If you have €100 million equity capital at hand, and you put the appropriate financing on that, then you can imagine that we try to play an important role in the next three to four years,” he says.

But for Schwenkow, the core ambition is having and promoting fun, as well as doing great business.

“I don’t have to prove anything anymore,” he says. “So I’m expecting a mixture of responsibility and fun for the next few years.”
