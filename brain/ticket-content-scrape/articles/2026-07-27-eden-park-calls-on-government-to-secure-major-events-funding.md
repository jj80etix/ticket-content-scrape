# Eden Park calls on government to secure major events funding

## Summary

Eden Park is lobbying the New Zealand government to establish permanent, multi-year major-events funding after hosting a record-setting 40,112-attendance football match between Tottenham Hotspur and Auckland FC. The stadium's CEO argues major events should be treated as strategic national investment rather than discretionary spending.

### Key Points
- Record attendance (40,112) for a men's football match in New Zealand at Eden Park.
- CEO calls for a permanent major-events fund rather than the current time-limited Major Events and Tourism Package.
- Independent research estimated NZD$37.2M in net additional GDP for Auckland from 25 events held at Eden Park in 2025/26.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**Eden Park is calling on New Zealand’s government to secure the future of major events funding and establish a long-term approach to attracting international events.**

The plea follows the national stadium’s hosting of a fixture between Tottenham Hotspur and Auckland FC which drew a crowd of 40,112, a record for a senior men’s football match in New Zealand.

Eden Park chief executive Nick Sautner says the event demonstrated the value of sustained government investment in securing “internationally significant” events for New Zealand.

“More than 40,000 people had the opportunity to see one of the world’s most recognised football clubs play at our national stadium this afternoon,” he said.

“It was a significant occasion for football, for Auckland and for New Zealand, and it would not have happened without support from the government’s major events funding.

“We are calling on all political parties to support a permanent, multi-year major events fund that enables New Zealand to build a strong pipeline of international sport, entertainment, cultural and business events.”

Sautner states that the current government’s Major Events and Tourism Package has successfully helped bring new content to New Zealand which otherwise would not have happened.

However, if the country is to continue attracting major events and competing globally, a permanent approach that provides certainty beyond the current programme is needed, according to Sautner.

He believes major events should be treated as strategic national investments rather than discretionary expenditure.

“Events generate immediate benefits across our accommodation, hospitality, transport, tourism and retail sectors,” Sautner said.

“They support jobs and local businesses, but their value extends well beyond the direct economic return.

“Events showcase New Zealand to international audiences, strengthen our reputation as a visitor destination, build national pride and create experiences that New Zealanders would otherwise have to travel overseas to enjoy.”

Independent University of Auckland research found 25 events held at Eden Park during 2025/26 generated an estimated NZD$37.2m (£16.1m/$21.5/€18.9m) in net additional GDP for the Auckland region.
