# TicketNetwork, vivenu Partner on Expanded Ticket Distribution

## Summary

Ticketing platform vivenu launched a distribution partnership with TicketNetwork, letting vivenu clients selectively push inventory across TicketNetwork's network of 1,000+ resale, travel and affiliate distribution channels while retaining pricing, branding and first-party customer-data control within vivenu. The integration supports real-time inventory sync and consolidated reporting back into vivenu, positioning it against traditional "closed silo" outsourced-ticketing models.

### Key Points
- vivenu (Tier 1 primary-platform competitor) opens optional, granular, opt-in distribution to TicketNetwork's 1,000+ channel network (OTAs, travel agencies, affiliates, resale marketplaces).
- Organizers retain full control of pricing, branding and customer data; sales data flows back into vivenu for consolidated reporting rather than fragmented multi-system tracking.
- Real-time inventory broadcasting is designed to prevent overselling or double-selling the same tickets across channels.
- Positioned explicitly against "closed ticketing silo" models — vivenu frames its API-first Sales Channels architecture as the differentiator.

### Highlights
- vivenu's distribution is granular and opt-in per event/ticket-type/quantity — organizers choose exactly what inventory leaves their own storefront.
- TicketNetwork serves 1,000+ organizers in 50+ countries, giving vivenu clients access to corporate, travel-agency and affiliate buyer segments an organizer's own site rarely reaches.
- All TicketNetwork-driven sales reconcile back into vivenu's own reporting rather than requiring organizers to manage separate inventory systems.

### Industry Problem
Event organizers want broader distribution reach (travel, affiliate, resale channels) without ceding their primary ticketing relationship, branding, or first-party data — a tension traditional outsourced-ticketing or closed-platform models handle poorly.

### Proposed Solution
vivenu's answer is an opt-in, API-first distribution layer that keeps the organizer as system of record; a primary platform like Etix could evaluate similar optional third-party distribution integrations (or a comparable partner network) as a build/improve opportunity to compete on reach without sacrificing the direct-organizer-relationship model.

***The integration will allow vivenu clients to place selected inventory across TicketNetwork’s network of more than 1,000 distribution partners while retaining control over pricing, branding and customer data.***

TicketNetwork and ticketing technology provider vivenu are [launching a distribution partnership](https://corporate.ticketnetwork.com/2026/08/04/ticketnetwork-and-vivenu-announce-partnership-to-expand-ticket-distribution-for-event-organizers/) that will give event organizers another option for reaching buyers beyond their existing direct sales channels.

Through the integration, eligible vivenu clients will be able to make selected ticket inventory available across TicketNetwork’s distribution ecosystem, which includes more than 1,000 ticketing websites, travel platforms, affiliate networks and other consumer and business-to-business channels.

The partnership is structured to expand the reach of an organizer’s inventory without replacing vivenu as its primary ticketing platform. Participating clients will continue to manage inventory, customer data and ticketing operations within vivenu while determining which events, ticket types and quantities are made available through TicketNetwork.

That balance between wider distribution and organizer control is central to vivenu’s approach to the partnership.

“Open distribution has been part of our architecture from the start,” Julia Ahaus, partnerships manager at vivenu, told TicketNews.

Through vivenu’s Sales Channels architecture, organizers can decide where and when inventory is distributed, as well as how many tickets are allocated to an individual channel. A client could dedicate a limited contingent of tickets to TicketNetwork, make a larger share available or choose not to participate for particular events.

“Organizers remain the absolute system of record for their data and their brand, choosing exactly what inventory funnels outward,” Ahaus said. “It is the exact opposite of a closed ticketing silo, and it perfectly reflects the core position vivenu is built around: open, API-first infrastructure that puts the organizer in charge of every sales path.”

Founded in 2018, vivenu provides customizable, white-label ticketing technology for sports organizations, venues, festivals, performing arts groups, attractions and other event operators. Its platform is designed to allow clients to retain control over their ticketing storefronts, branding, customer journeys and first-party data while connecting with outside technology and commerce systems.

The TicketNetwork integration extends that model into a distribution network serving both consumer-facing marketplaces and less visible business channels.

TicketNetwork’s distribution operation connects inventory with ticketing websites, affiliates, travel businesses and other partners that surface event listings to their respective customers. The company also provides point-of-sale technology, white-label marketplaces and other ticketing infrastructure.

For organizers, broader distribution can provide access to audiences that may not visit an event’s own website or existing ticketing storefront. That can include buyers shopping through travel agencies, corporate programs, affiliate sites, resale marketplaces and other platforms that combine live-event inventory with separate products or services.

Ahaus said vivenu is seeing demand from organizers that want that additional reach but do not want to surrender control of the customer relationship or move their operations into a different ticketing environment.

“Organizers are looking for reach without sacrificing anything,” she said. “They want to expand their audience through OTAs, resellers and secondary marketplaces, but often they want to keep the primary customer relationship, pricing and brand experience anchored to their own shop.”

Under the partnership, inventory distributed through TicketNetwork will remain connected to vivenu. Sales activity will flow back into the platform, allowing organizers to track transactions through consolidated reporting rather than operating separate inventories and attempting to reconcile sales across disconnected systems.

The integration is expected to support real-time inventory broadcasting and dedicated ticket allocations, reducing the risk that the same inventory is sold through multiple channels or that organizers must update availability manually.

Organizers will also retain the ability to decide which inventory is offered externally. That distinction is important for event operators that may want to use broader distribution selectively, such as allocating tickets for particular performances, lower-demand dates, designated seating areas or events with an opportunity to attract tourists and other buyers outside the organizer’s established audience.

“Distribution on vivenu is granular and opt-in by design, and that pattern runs through every partnership we sign,” Ahaus said.

The partnership also illustrates the difference between adding an outside distribution channel and entering a traditional outsourced ticketing agreement. TicketNetwork will not replace vivenu as the organizer’s central ticketing system. Instead, it will function as another sales endpoint connected to the organizer’s existing infrastructure.

That model allows organizers to pursue incremental sales through external platforms while keeping their first-party ticketing environment intact. Pricing, branding and the extent of the inventory allocation remain determined by the organizer rather than the distribution partner.

“A distribution ecosystem like TicketNetwork unlocks deep reach into buyer segments an organizer’s own site rarely touches: corporate channels, travel agencies, resale markets and affiliate networks,” Ahaus said. “Yet, because every single one of those sales tracks back into vivenu, reporting stays consolidated on a single platform.”

For TicketNetwork, the agreement adds a direct connection to a ticketing provider serving more than 1,000 organizers in over 50 countries. Vivenu’s clients operate across sports, festivals, music and entertainment, attractions, conventions and the performing arts.

For vivenu, the integration adds another distribution option to an infrastructure strategy built around allowing clients to select their own combination of sales channels and technology providers.

The companies said the integration will be available to vivenu clients immediately, with implementation and participation details provided directly to eligible organizers.
