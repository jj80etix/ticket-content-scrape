# Live Nation Backs Creator Nation to Buy and Scale Creator Management Firms

## Summary

Live Nation is backing a new creator-economy venture, Creator Nation, co-founded by Scott and Alexis Fisher, that will manage talent and acquire other creator-management firms rather than simply signing creators to management deals. Live Nation is providing capital and acting as strategic backer but has not disclosed the investment size or acquisition targets; the first acquisitions are expected within months. The launch extends Live Nation's earlier stake in Timeline Management and signals a push to invest further upstream in businesses that develop talent and audiences, which can eventually feed into Live Nation's touring, ticketing and sponsorship pipeline.

### Key Points
- Creator Nation plans to both manage talent and acquire creator-management/adjacent businesses; first acquisitions expected "in the coming months."
- Live Nation is providing capital and serving as strategic backer; investment size and acquisition targets undisclosed.
- Co-founder Scott Fisher previously built and sold Select Management Group (500+ creators, 1B+ combined followers) to Propagate Content in 2025.
- Timeline Management (Live Nation's existing creator-talent stake, led by Alexis Fisher) continues operating separately from Creator Nation.
- Live Nation frames the strategic fit as moving digitally-established talent into touring, ticketed experiences and sponsorships via its existing promotion/venue/ticketing infrastructure.

### Highlights
- Live Nation is the capital backer of a dedicated acquisition vehicle for creator-management firms, not a single acquisition.
- First acquisitions targeted within "coming months" — a near-term consolidation signal to watch.
- Builds on the Dec. 2024 Timeline Management stake, showing a repeated/expanding pattern rather than a one-off deal.

### Industry Problem
Creator-led audiences increasingly want to convert online followings into durable, multi-channel businesses (touring, brand, commerce), but the management infrastructure around individual creators hasn't scaled with that demand — leaving a fragmented market of smaller firms ripe for consolidation by a company that already controls downstream touring and ticketing infrastructure.

### Proposed Solution
Live Nation's answer is vertical integration: fund a platform (Creator Nation) to acquire and professionalize creator-management firms, then route that talent into Live Nation's existing promotion, venue and ticketing pipeline. For Etix, the signal to monitor is whether this deepens Live Nation's primary-market lock-in via creator-originated touring deals; the trigger to revisit posture is Creator Nation's first disclosed acquisition and any exclusivity terms tying creator tours to Ticketmaster.

Live Nation is backing a new creator-economy company built not only to support talent but to acquire and scale creator-management firms, extending the concert giant’s push to turn large online audiences into broader entertainment businesses.

“Creators have proven they can build enormous audiences, but the infrastructure around them has not always kept pace,” Scott Fisher said. “We’re building Creator Nation to give creators and the teams supporting them the resources to turn that influence into businesses designed to last.”

Live Nation’s interest in the creator economy increasingly centers on the possibility of turning digital followings into businesses that extend well beyond social-media content.

Alexis Fisher has already pursued that model through Timeline, including bringing the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders, the cast of *The Secret Lives of Mormon Wives* and children’s brand Gracie’s Corner to live stages.

“Bringing creator-led shows to the stage showed us how a strong relationship with fans can open the door to entirely new opportunities,” Fisher said. “Creator Nation gives us a platform to do that for many more creators, across every part of their business.”

The company’s founders elaborated on that approach in a separate [Live Nation interview about the launch](https://news.livenationentertainment.com/news/5-questions-with-scott-and-alexis-fisher-co-founders-of-creator-nation?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). Alexis Fisher described the goal as strengthening the relationship between creators and their audiences across products, projects, brands and live appearances, while Scott Fisher said Creator Nation would support the businesses operating around those creators.

For Live Nation, the strategic fit extends beyond simply signing creators to management deals. The company already operates across concert promotion, venues, ticketing, sponsorship and other areas of live entertainment, potentially giving Creator Nation and the companies it acquires access to infrastructure for moving digitally established talent into touring and other in-person experiences.

Live Nation CEO Michael Rapino said creators are increasingly developing careers that resemble broader entertainment businesses, spanning live events, content, brands and commerce.

“Scott and Alexis understand how to help talent build those businesses for the long term,” Rapino said, “and we see a lot of opportunity to support that growth.”

## Acquisitions Are Central to the Strategy

The planned acquisition of management firms makes Creator Nation a broader bet than Live Nation’s earlier investment in Timeline.

Scott Fisher brings his own experience building and selling a creator-management business. He founded Select Management Group in 2010 and led the company through its full acquisition by Propagate Content in 2025. According to Live Nation, Select has worked with more than 500 creators whose combined audiences exceed 1 billion followers, while also developing television programming and consumer brands.

Creator Nation is now positioning itself to provide capital and operating support to other businesses serving that market rather than building solely through an individual roster of creators.

The company said it is already spending to assemble its team, with hiring underway in creator strategy, commerce, touring, data, operations and marketing. It also plans to work directly with brands and marketers on campaigns involving creators and companies in its future portfolio.

Live Nation and the Fishers have not yet disclosed how much capital will be available for acquisitions, how Creator Nation will structure investments in outside management businesses, or how large a portfolio it ultimately intends to build.

Those details should become clearer as the first deals are announced. But the launch establishes another avenue for Live Nation to invest further upstream in the businesses developing talent and audiences that can eventually move into tours, ticketed experiences, sponsorships and other parts of the live-entertainment economy.
