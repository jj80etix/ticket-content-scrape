# Mass Arbitration: Managing the Risk for Payments and Fintech Companies

## Summary

In a Troutman Pepper Locke "Payments Pros" podcast episode, litigation partner Justin Weber and host Carlin McCrory discuss how mass arbitration has become a significant risk for payments and fintech companies due to filing-fee leverage, and outline how updated JAMS/AAA rules and early-resolution clauses can manage that exposure. The episode also compares consumer-facing versus B2B arbitration provisions and contrasts arbitration's flexibility against litigation's broader appeal rights.

### Key Points
- Mass arbitration risk stems largely from filing-fee leverage against companies with broad arbitration clauses.
- Updated JAMS and AAA mass-arbitration rules are reshaping how claims are filed and resolved.
- Companies can mitigate risk with meet-and-confer and other early-resolution provisions built into agreements.
- Consumer-facing and B2B arbitration clauses face different enforceability and unconscionability standards.

In this episode of *[Payments Pros](https://www.troutman.com/podcast/payments-pros-podcast/)*, host [Carlin McCrory](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/carlin-a-mccrory/) is joined by [Justin Weber](https://www.troutman.com/professionals/justin-g-weber/), a business litigation partner at Troutman Pepper Locke, to discuss arbitration and the growing challenge of mass arbitration for payments and fintech companies.

Their conversation covers what arbitration is and how it differs from litigation, why companies use arbitration provisions, and what separates an enforceable clause from one vulnerable to unconscionability challenges. Justin and Carlin also discuss the differences between consumer-facing and B2B provisions, why mass arbitration has become a significant risk because of filing-fee leverage, and how companies can manage that risk, including by understanding the updated JAMS and AAA mass arbitration rules, and building meet-and-confer and other early-resolution provisions into their agreements. The two wrap up their conversation by comparing arbitration with court litigation, highlighting arbitration’s flexibility and efficiency as well as its limited appeal rights.
