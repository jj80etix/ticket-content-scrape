# Ticketmaster targets ‘AI-powered’ future with trio of hires

## Summary

Ticketmaster has named former Amazon/Zalando/Permira executive Jim Freeman as CTO, Rachel Smith (ex-Block/Square/Cash App/Tidal) as its first SVP of data science and machine learning, and Chuck Mortimore (ex-Disney/Stripe/Visa/Salesforce) as SVP of product, while folding in the team from Ragie.ai (acquired July 2026). Global president Saumil Mehta frames the hires as building "a new generation of agentic, AI-powered tools" and making "every function AI-first," with Smith's group centered on fraud detection, bot mitigation and fan experience. The push follows roughly 350 layoffs earlier in 2026 as Mehta narrows the company's focus to fewer, deeper initiatives.

### Key Points
- Jim Freeman (ex-Amazon Prime Video, Zalando CTO, Permira) joins as CTO; Rachel Smith (ex-Block) as first SVP data science/ML; Chuck Mortimore (ex-Disney/Stripe/Visa/Salesforce) as SVP product.
- Ragie.ai team (acquired July 2026) folded into the new AI organization.
- Mehta: strategy is to make "every function AI-first"; Smith's remit centers on fraud detection, bot mitigation, fan experience.
- Mortimore will lead fan-facing account-access and anti-bot product strategy.
- Hires follow ~350 layoffs earlier in 2026 as Ticketmaster narrows to fewer, deeper initiatives.

### Highlights
- Three senior AI/ML/product hires plus an acquired startup team (Ragie.ai) consolidated under a stated "AI-first" company strategy.
- Fraud/bot mitigation explicitly named as a core charter for the new data-science org, not just personalization.

### Industry Problem
The largest primary ticketing platform is making a structural, leadership-level bet that agentic AI is now core infrastructure — for fraud/bot defense as much as for fan-facing personalization — raising the competitive bar for what "AI deployment" means in ticketing.

### Proposed Solution
Direct competitive signal for Etix: benchmark the AI roadmap (support, fraud/bot detection, personalization) against Ticketmaster's new agentic-AI organization, and specifically track what Freeman's team ships, since bot-mitigation ML is the area most directly transferable to Etix's own fraud posture.

Ticketmaster has made a trio of senior appointments in its quest to build “a new generation of agentic, AI-powered tools”.

Former Amazon executive Jim Freeman will join the company as chief technology officer, alongside Rachel Smith as the platform’s first SVP of data science and machine learning, and Chuck Mortimore as SVP of product.

In addition, the team behind startup Ragie.ai, which was [acquired](https://www.ragie.ai/blog/ragie-joins-ticketmaster) in July, will be folded into the operation.

“Reliable, secure and personalised experiences depend on how effectively we use technology and data,” says global president Saumil Mehta. “Jim, Rachel, Chuck, and the Ragie.ai team will help us move faster, build better tools and deliver better outcomes for fans, artists, venues and clients.”

In his new role, Freeman will be tasked with the technology behind the Live Nation ticketing arm’s products for fans and clients, including the “agentic, AI-powered tools”, steering a company-wide push to make “every function AI-first”, the company says.

He brings with him experience from roles at Amazon, where he helped build and launch Prime Video, and marketplace Zalando and private equity firm Permira.

Within this wider push, Ticketmaster has also carved space for the data science and machine learning segment, to be led by Smith. The operation will put data at the centre of product and business development, centring on fraud detection, bot mitigation, and the fan experience, Ticketmaster says.

She brings experience from Block, where she helped lead data science teams behind Square, Cash App, and streaming service Tidal.

Meanwhile, Mortimore will lead the firm’s product strategy for fans, helping them access their accounts while combating bot issues. He will build on experience from leadership roles at Disney, Stripe, Visa, and Salesforce.

The tech-forward appointments arrive around the one-year mark for Mehta, who joined Ticketmaster last November after spending nearly a decade at point-of-sale business Square.

He previously told [*IQ*](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/01/ticketmasters-new-global-president-unveils-tech-forward-vision/) that integrating AI into Ticketmaster’s operation is a key pillar of his strategy, as the technology will “fundamentally change how software companies work.”

Earlier this year, Mehta said the company was “evolving its organisation to increase focus and invest more deeply behind fewer initiatives” in response to [350 layoffs](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/05/ticketmaster-confirms-around-350-layoffs/).
