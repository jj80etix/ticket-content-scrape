# Asia Focus: Expanding Global Reach, World’s Most Populous Continent Continues To Flourish

## Summary

Pollstar's Asia Focus feature documents a touring market still expanding broadly, led by K-pop (SEVENTEEN topped the 2026 chart at $97.4M gross) but increasingly two-way: Western artists are touring Asia in greater numbers while Japanese, Chinese and Indian artists build international audiences. Promoters across Japan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Indonesia and Dubai describe sustained demand growth, new/renovated venues, and rising sophistication in routing and ticketing infrastructure, with multiple executives citing venue availability — not demand — as the binding constraint on further growth.

### Key Points
- SEVENTEEN led Pollstar's 2026 Asia Top Touring Artists chart ($97.4M, 661K tickets); Hong Kong's Kai Tak Stadium topped Asia venues ($258.6M, 1.46M tickets).
- Live Nation's Asia-Pacific touring president and others say the constraint on growth is venue/infrastructure availability and routing efficiency, not artist demand.
- Japan's Creativeman Productions entered a strategic partnership with AEG, aiming to keep developing artists (who previously moved to other promoters for global tours) within its network longer.
- Multiple markets cited as emerging growth points: Indonesia (world's 4th-largest population, localization-dependent), Dubai (shifting from occasional shows to year-round live-entertainment hub), and secondary Japanese cities beyond Tokyo.
- Executives explicitly flag "ticketing infrastructure" development as a named enabler of continued regional growth (Live Nation APAC), alongside venue supply.

### Highlights
- Direct executive quote identifies ticketing infrastructure, alongside venue supply, as a named bottleneck/enabler for Asia's touring growth — a specific hook for platform expansion interest.
- Market-level data (K-pop dominance, two-way touring flow, Dubai's shift to year-round programming) gives concrete evidence of where international touring volume is growing fastest.
- Creativeman/AEG's strategic tie-up signals continued consolidation among regional promoters as international demand scales.

### Industry Problem
As international touring volume shifts toward Asia and the Gulf, ticketing/event-management infrastructure in several of these markets is explicitly cited as lagging venue and artist supply — a structural gap rather than a demand problem.

### Proposed Solution
No specific ticketing solution is proposed in the piece itself; it is a market-opportunity signal. Etix's posture here is evolve/monitor: track whether international promoters or venues in these named growth markets (Hong Kong, Indonesia, Dubai, secondary Japanese cities) begin issuing RFPs for ticketing infrastructure as volume scales, since that would be the trigger to evaluate entering or partnering in the region.

AEG’s Wilkes offers insight into the blossoming Asian touring market. “The most striking change has been the evolution of the audience,” he says. “Fans are more connected, informed and discerning; they discover artists without borders and increasingly travel across them.”

Live Nation’s Argenzia notes the possibilities in summing up the region.
