# Dolly Parton estate announces ‘party of a lifetime’ DollyFest

## Summary

Following Dolly Parton's death, her estate announced DOLLYFEST, described as a "party of a lifetime" — a two-day tribute festival staged across successive weekends in Nashville and London next year.

### Key Points
- Two-day tribute events held on consecutive weekends in Nashville and London in 2027.
- Organized by Dolly Parton's estate as an "authentic" celebration of her legacy.

## Transcript

## Dolly Parton estate announces ‘party of a lifetime’ DollyFest

The two-day 'authentic' tribute for the glittering star will be staged across successive weekends in Nashville and London next year

![Dolly Parton](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Dolly-Parton-©-Creative-CommonsTimothy-Wildey.png)

Dolly Parton © Creative Commons/Timothy Wildey

In the wake of country icon Dolly Parton’s death, her estate has announced back-to-back festivals celebrating her life and music, set to take place in Nashville and London in 2027.

DollyFest: The Celebration of a Lifetime will take place across successive weekends next year, with the dates, locations, and participants of the two-day events to be announced.

The Nashville edition was initially planned around the star’s 80th birthday this past January, but was pushed back to 2027 due to her initial health challenges. While Parton was slated to participate in the initial configuration of DollyFest, it will now “morph into a global double-day, double-weekend, double-continent party of a lifetime,” organisers say.

“What Dolly started, we will ensure is brought to life,” says Danny Nozell, Parton’s longtime business partner and founder/CEO of CTK Enterprises. “A lot of people always say, WWDD (What Would Dolly Do?), and, well, at this moment, DollyFest is WDWD (What Dolly Would Do!).”

Both events are expected to serve as a lively, “authentic” public tribute for the glittering star, with Nozell saying that “as far as a traditional memorial goes, that is not something Dolly wanted.”

“It feels more appropriate for us to take a beat, let all the tributes that immediately make sense occur naturally, and then see through this festival-style concept that was truly dreamed up by Dolly,” he adds.

Parton’s passing last week was met with an [outpouring of tributes](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/tributes-paid-to-country-legend-dolly-parton/) from the live industry and beyond. Her agents, CAA’s Rod Essig and UTA’s Neil Warnock, spoke to *IQ* about the [star’s legacy](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/dolly-partons-longtime-agents-open-up-about-the-working-with-the-singer/) and rhinestone-embellished live career that spanned nearly seven decades, including her business prowess, memorable Glastonbury slot, and affinity for tour buses.

“Dolly was a very, very smart businesswoman,” Warnock said. “The great thing about dealing with Dolly was, you always told Dolly exactly how it was.”

Of her character, longtime agent Essig added: “Her talent was unparalleled, but it was her heart, generosity, and the way she treated people that made her so special.”
