# Colombia rapidly expands venue network to capitalise on booming demand

## Summary

Colombia is rapidly building out its concert-venue infrastructure, led by a planned 50,000-capacity rebuild of Bogotá's El Campín stadium (2027/2028), Medellín's new 17,200-cap DAVIarena (opening November with Juanes), and a new 6,000-cap Vive Claro Music Hall adjacent to Bogotá's 40,000-cap Vive Claro. The buildout aims to capture growing regional touring demand alongside Argentina, Brazil, and Chile.

### Key Points
- El Campín redevelopment: 50,000-cap roofed stadium plus a 2,300-seat auditorium, e-sports arena, and 200-room hotel; existing stadium stays open until the new one opens.
- DAVIarena (Medellín, 17,200-cap) opens November with Juanes; projected 600K+ guests and 75 events annually, with a forecast 30% tourism boost.
- Vive Claro Music Hall (Bogotá, 6,000-cap) opens with Daniel Caesar Nov 29, bridging the venue-tier gap next to the 40,000-cap Vive Claro.
- Vive Claro (opened 2025) has hosted Green Day, Ed Sheeran, Shakira, Guns N' Roses; Maná, Iron Maiden, Stray Kids booked this year.

## Transcript

A trio of new venues from 6,000- to 50,000-capacity are set to give promoters more reasons to route through the integral LatAm market

![El Campín rendering](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/El-Campin-rendering-©-BDP-Pattern.png)

New El Campín rendering © BDP Pattern

One of Latin America’s foremost entertainment markets, Colombia is rapidly expanding its venue infrastructure across the touring ladder, with new venues from 6,000- to 50,000-capacity set to give promoters more options in a market that has become increasingly integral to international touring.

Having produced global stars including Shakira, Karol G, J Balvin, Maluma and Feid, Colombia is now looking to develop a new generation of venues to match its broadening cultural reach.

Estadio Nemesio Camacho El Campín ([El Campín](https://www.iqmagazine.com/featured-list/stadium-stars-2026/estadio-el-campin/) for short) has been a defining landmark of Bogotá since its inauguration in 1938, evolving to become a 39,500-capacity venue that balances football fanfare and major concerts, such as BTS and Karol G this year.

Despite its legacy, El Campín is now scheduled for demolition as part of a redevelopment plan to capitalise on the capital’s cultural cachet. Developed by BDP Pattern in collaboration with Sencia Colombia and engineering partner WSP, the project will transform the site of the existing stadium into a mixed-use cultural and commercial complex, anchored by a new 50,000-capacity roofed stadium.

Also to be known as El Campín, the venue is currently being constructed on a site adjacent to the active stadium. Once the venue comes online, which is projected to be in late 2027/2028, the existing stadium will be demolished, meaning there will be no lapse in concert-hosting capacity.

“This project is a once-in-a-generation opportunity to deliver a world-class stadium while harnessing the passion and support of two of Colombia’s biggest football clubs,” said Lindsay Johnston, BDP Pattern director and co-founder, with the design announcement.

> “We’re creating a place that will \[…\] help define a bold new identity for Colombia”

“By placing football and live entertainment at the centre of an exciting new district, we’re creating a place that will transform not just Bogotá’s urban and cultural landscape but help define a bold new identity for Colombia on the global sports stage,” she added.

The wider area is also expected to boast a 2,300-seat auditorium (to be the Bogotá Philharmonic Orchestra’s home), an e-sports arena, and a 200-room hotel, in addition to commercial and retail spaces, transforming El Campín into one of Latin America’s most significant cultural and entertainment hubs in the coming years.

Later this year, the country’s second-largest city, Medellín, will see the country’s newest arena open its doors with a show from homegrown star Juanes in November.

The 17,200-capacity DAVIarena, formerly known as Arena Primavera, is designed to be a state-of-the-art building and will be operated by 360-degree firm BeatHub Entertainment. The venue is expected to host over 600,000 guests and 75 events annually, which BeatHub predicts will boost the city’s tourism by 30%.

“DAVIarena is a sign of confidence for promoters, artists and all stakeholders in the industry,” said Eduardo Olea, CEO of BeatHub Entertainment, earlier this year. “It’s the infrastructure that allows Colombia to compete at a higher level and continue to solidify its position as a key destination for international tours.”

> “It’s the infrastructure that allows Colombia to compete at a higher level”

It will become a premier space in Medellín, strengthening the country’s arena options for regional touring outside of Bogotá, which is home to key arenas [Coliseo MedPlus](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2022/08/latams-largest-multipurpose-arena-opens/) (cap. 24,000) and [Movistar Arena](https://www.iqmagazine.com/featured-list/arena-stars-2026/movistar-arena-bogota/) (14,000), the latter of which is also located in the El Campín grounds.

Further down the touring ladder, OCESA Colombia recently unveiled a 6,000-capacity venue adjacent to the one-year-old 40,000-capacity Vive Claro in Bogotá.

The [Vive Claro Music Hall](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/ocesa-colombia-unveils-6k-cap-space-next-to-estadio-vive-claro/) will offer multiple configurations for concerts and family shows, scalable to artists and shows of varying calibres as its operators look to bridge the gap between the market’s venue tiers.

Located near the 40,000-capacity space, the new venue will amplify the offerings of the Vive Claro Distrito Cultural, with artist and audience development at the heart of the entertainment destination, operator OCESA says. The first confirmed show is Daniel Caesar, who will bring his [*Son of Spergy* *Tour*](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/tour-news-megadeth-expands-farewell-outing-with-eu-uk-dates/) to the venue on 29 November.

> “Audiences deserve spaces designed for memorable experiences”

“Great artists and projects need venues that grow alongside them, and audiences deserve spaces designed for memorable experiences,” says Luz Angela Castro, CEO of OCESA Colombia. “Vive Claro Music Hall closes a gap in the market and continues to position Bogotá as one of Latin America’s leading entertainment destinations,” she adds.

Major open-air venue Vive Claro opened its doors just last year, and has already hosted artists like Green Day, Ed Sheeran, Shakira, and Guns N’ Roses, with Maná, Iron Maiden, and Stray Kids all set to come through this year.

“With a venue like Vive Claro, artists have the confidence of world-class production, strong audience capacity, and solid business support that make Colombia a profitable and essential part of their global tours,” Castro [previously told *IQ*](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/08/vive-claro-colombia-no-longer-optional-market/). “Colombia is no longer an ‘optional’ market – it is now a must-stop.”

Colombia’s appeal as a touring market has grown simultaneously alongside its South American neighbours – Argentina, Brazil and Chile – but the country’s increasingly diverse venue network is giving promoters the infrastructure to accommodate more talent, bigger shows, and wider touring routes, positioning the country for sustained growth.
