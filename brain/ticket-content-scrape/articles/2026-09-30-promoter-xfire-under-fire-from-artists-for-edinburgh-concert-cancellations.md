# Promoter XFire under fire from artists for Edinburgh concert cancellations

## Summary

UK promoter Xfire Live has had four shows canceled in quick succession (The Fratellis/James Morrison in Falkirk in early September; Red Hot Chilli Pipers and Ministry of Sound: Classical at Edinburgh's Royal Highland Centre in early October) after allegedly failing to meet contractual conditions, with the venue and artists blaming the promoter and ticketholders left without timely cancellation notice. Red Hot Chilli Pipers said refunds would be issued directly by the promoter, but the venue noted it does not manage ticketing for these events, and tickets for the Ministry of Sound: Classical show were reportedly still on sale on some sites even as the cancellation was being confirmed.

### Key Points
- Four Xfire Live shows canceled in weeks: The Fratellis/James Morrison (Falkirk, early Sept.) and Red Hot Chilli Pipers/Ministry of Sound: Classical (Edinburgh's Royal Highland Centre, Oct. 2 and Oct. 4).
- Royal Highland Centre says Xfire Live failed to "meet contractual conditions required... within the required timeframe."
- Ticketholders reportedly did not receive cancellation confirmation from Xfire Live until days after artists/venue announced it.
- Tickets for the Ministry of Sound: Classical show were still listed for sale on some sites even as cancellation was being finalized.
- Xfire Live's own site still advertised shows into November despite the cancellation pattern; the company did not respond to IQ's request for comment.

### Highlights
- Four cancellations in rapid succession from one promoter is a pattern, not an isolated incident, with venue explicitly attributing it to contractual/financial failure by Xfire Live.
- Ticketholders went days without cancellation notice directly from the seller of record (the promoter), relying on artist/venue announcements instead.
- Tickets remained purchasable on some channels after the event was effectively dead — a live risk of selling tickets to an event that won't happen.

### Industry Problem
When a promoter becomes financially or operationally unable to deliver, patrons bear the communication and refund risk (delayed/no cancellation notice, refunds promised "directly by the promoter" with no guaranteed timeline or backstop), while venues disclaim ticketing responsibility — leaving a gap between who sold the ticket and who patrons can actually hold accountable.

### Proposed Solution
The article describes no structural fix beyond the promoter's unfulfilled refund promise; an independent primary platform could differentiate by owning cancellation communication directly to ticket buyers in real time (rather than relying on artist/venue social posts) and by halting/flagging sales automatically once a promoter-side contractual failure is known, reducing the window where tickets remain purchasable for a dead event.

## Transcript

Artists and Edinburgh's Royal Highland Centre have accused promoter Xfire Live of not meeting contractual conditions for the concerts

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Red-Hot-Chilli-Pipers.png)

Red Hot Chilli Pipers were due to perform in Edinburgh © Red Hot Chilli Pipers

Two concerts at Edinburgh’s Royal Highland Centre have been cancelled in quick succession, with artists and the venue expressing disappointment at what they said were decisions of promoter Xfire Live.

Uncertainty has arisen around the cancellation of concerts from Celtic rock band Red Hot Chilli Pipers and *Ministry of Sound: Classical*, with ticketholders for both shows saying they had not received confirmation of cancellation from Xfire Live until several days after it was announced by artists and the venue.

The two cancellations come only weeks after two other Xfire Live shows by The Fratellis and James Morrison in Falkirk were also cancelled in early September. At the time, The Fratellis said: “Unfortunately the promoters have been unable to fulfil their commitments to the band to perform and they have had to therefore take the decision not to appear at the event.”

The Red Hot Chilli Pipers show was due to take place on 4 October while *Ministry of Sound: Classical* was set for 2 October. Xfire Live have been approached for comment.

When checked by *IQ,* tickets for the *Ministry of Sound: Classical* event are still available for purchase on some sites.

The Royal Highland Centre has since confirmed neither event will take place at the venue, saying Xfire Live failed to “meet contractual conditions”. The showground venue on the outskirts of Edinburgh can be configured to hold events ranging between 6,000 and 35,000 in capacity.

Following the *Ministry of Sound: Classical* cancellation, the Royal Highland Centre said: “Unfortunately, the event promoter was unable to meet the contractual conditions required to allow the necessary arrangements for the event to be finalised within the required timeframe.

> “We made every effort to work with the promoter and explore whether a way forward could be found”

“We made every effort to work with the promoter and explore whether a way forward could be found. Despite these efforts, the necessary arrangements could not be put in place and, as a result, the event is no longer able to go ahead as planned.”

Xfire Live’s website still lists shows reaching into November on its home page, but its ‘All Shows’ page show only past events. The company is still advertising future events on social media.

Announcing the cancellation of their performance, Red Hot Chilli Pipers wrote in an online statement: “It is with regret that we need to inform you that our show at the Royal Highland Centre, Edinburgh, on Sunday 4 October has been cancelled by the promoter. Refunds will be issued directly by the promoter, and we are extremely disappointed that the situation has reached this point.”

In the wake of the band’s announcement, the Royal Highland Centre said it had received enquiries from ticketholders about the event’s status, saying: “We know a number of people have been in touch with us looking for confirmation, and we understand the disappointment this cancellation will cause. The Royal Highland Centre does not manage the ticketing for these events.”
