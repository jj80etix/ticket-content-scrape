# Live Nation’s Pukkelpop deal approved with ten-year independence safeguard

## Summary

Belgium's competition authority approved Live Nation's acquisition of the Pukkelpop festival after the company made "binding" commitments to leave creative, programming, and ticket-pricing control with founder Chokri Mahassine's organization for at least ten years. The BCA closed its probe — opened in November 2025 over concerns that Live Nation already promotes three of Belgium's largest festivals — without finding any infringement, while Live Nation retains formal ownership and can still sell Pukkelpop standalone services on a non-exclusive, market-rate basis.

### Key Points
- BCA probe launched Nov. 2025 over Live Nation's existing ownership of Rock Werchter, Dour, and Graspop, Belgium's other major festivals.
- Pukkelpop organizers retain independent control of artist booking, programming, ticket/service pricing, venue, staffing, and suppliers for 10+ years.
- Operating rights transfer to an entity controlled by founder Chokri Mahassine, who may later pass them to an independent third party.
- Live Nation keeps formal festival ownership and can still provide standalone services at market rates, non-exclusively.
- The 66,000-cap festival achieved its fastest-ever sellout in 2026 with headliners including Tyler, The Creator and Florence + The Machine.

### Highlights
- Regulator-approved 10-year binding independence commitment covering artistic, commercial, and pricing decisions.
- Live Nation already controls three of Belgium's largest festivals, which triggered the original competition probe.
- Cleared via voluntary commitments with no infringement finding, rather than a contested ruling.

### Industry Problem
Regulators are increasingly scrutinizing Live Nation's festival-acquisition strategy for its effect on independent promoter and programming diversity in local markets, echoing (on a smaller scale) the antitrust scrutiny Live Nation/Ticketmaster face domestically.

### Proposed Solution
No direct product response; the signal to watch is whether other European competition authorities adopt similar "independence safeguard" conditions as a template for approving future Live Nation festival acquisitions, which would shape the competitive landscape for independent promoters and platforms in those markets.

## Live Nation’s Pukkelpop deal approved with ten-year independence safeguard

Live Nation will retain formal ownership of the Belgian festival while making "binding" commitments to keeping creative control with its founder for a ten-year period

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Pukkelpop-2025-Sfeer-23u38m05s-@farahlieten.jpg)

Pukkelpop 2025 © Farah Lieten

Live Nation’s acquisition of Belgium’s Pukkelpop Festival has been approved by the country’s competition authority, after the company made “binding” commitments to keep creative direction of the event with the festival’s founder for at least ten years.

A new decision by the Belgian Competition Authority (BCA) will mean that Pukkelpop organisers will keep full independent control over “all artistic, commercial and other decisions relating to the organisation of the festival”, the regulator said. This includes the choice of artists, booking agents, programming, pricing of tickets as well as services, suppliers, staff, venue, dates, duration, capacity, staging suppliers, and more.

However, this arrangement does not prevent the possibility of Pukkelpop “procuring standalone services from Live Nation at market terms and on a non-exclusive basis,” BCA said.

Operation of Pukkelpop, along with all related assets, will be transferred to an organisation controlled by its founder Chokri Mahassine, with the possibility to pass on these exclusive operating rights to an independent third-party. However, Live Nation will retain formal ownership of the festival.

Live Nation has made “voluntary commitments” to keeping the festival’s creative direction independent, which are in line with its plan “from the outset” of the purchase, it said. The decision from the regulator does not suggest any infringement by Live Nation.

However, the company declined to disclose precise details of these commitments, but said: “Following the Belgian Competition Authority’s decision regarding Live Nation and Pukkelpop, Live Nation confirms that Pukkelpop will continue to be run by its own team, with the festival’s identity and day-to-day organisation remaining firmly in place.”

> “The decision “preserves diversity and independence within the Belgian festival scene”

The BCA’s [probe](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/11/live-nations-pukkelpop-takeover-sparks-competition-probe/) began in November 2025, after it was announced Live Nation was to become the festival’s parent company. Following this, the Belgian minister for economic affairs instructed the BCA to open a probe into the deal, on the grounds that Live Nation already provides artist booking services and organises three of Belgium’s largest festivals in Rock Werchter, Dour, and Graspop.

Held in the city of Hasselt, the 66,000-cap festival [this year](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/03/pukkelpop-achieves-fastest-sell-out-to-date/) hosted headliners including Tyler, The Creator, Florence + The Machine, Deftones, and Yungblud.

“The BCA has concluded its investigation without any finding of infringement following a set of voluntary commitments. These reflect the approach Live Nation and Pukkelpop had envisaged from the outset: preserving the way Pukkelpop is run, while allowing Live Nation to continue supporting the festival’s long-term development,” Live Nation continued.

Meanwhile, Herman Schueremans, president of Live Nation Belgium, said: “Pukkelpop is Pukkelpop because of the people behind it and the identity they have built over many years. That will remain the case. These commitments confirm the approach we had in mind from the start, while Live Nation continues to support the festival’s future. We are pleased that the \[BCA\] process has concluded and that the focus can now return to the festival itself.”

BCA prosecutor general Damien Gérard said the decision “preserves diversity and independence within the Belgian festival scene by allowing Pukkelpop to continue operating as a distinct festival with its own artistic identity and organisational autonomy. The BCA is grateful for the many contributions to its investigation by a broad variety of industry participants and remains at their disposal in the future.”
