# SM Entertainment revenue driven by concerts, merchandise

## Summary

South Korean entertainment agency SM Entertainment reported Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 349.6bn ($246.6m), driven by growth in concerts and merchandise/licensing even as net income dipped on investment-valuation losses.

### Key Points
- Parent-company revenue rose 9.2% year over year to KRW 240.6bn; concert revenue alone grew 23.6% year over year to KRW 41.6bn.
- Merchandising/licensing revenue rose 22% year over year to KRW 77.9bn on IP-based monetization and pop-up tie-ins.
- Concert-production unit Dream Maker's revenue jumped 178.5% year over year to KRW 41.6bn.
- Physical album/digital music sales fell 8.2% year over year to KRW 90.9bn.

**South Korean entertainment agency SM Entertainment posted a revenue of KRW 349.6bn ($246.6m/£182.7m/€213.4m) for the second quarter of 2026, driven by continued growth in concerts and merchandising/licensing business.**

Growth across the company’s subsidiaries also helped to boost revenue for Q2, with the group’s consolidated operating profit increasing by KRW 5.3bn year-over-year to total KRW 52.9bn.

However, consolidated net income for the quarter was KRW 29.2bn, a drop of KRW 1.7bn, which the company has attributed to valuation losses on equity investments, impairment losses on investment assets, and higher income tax expenses.

For the parent company, SM Entertainment itself, revenue reached KRW 240.6bn, representing a 9.2% year-over-year increase, driven by concerts, appearances, and merchandise/licensing. However, there was a drop in revenue from physical album/digital music sales, which dropped 8.2% to KRW 90.9bn compared to this time last year.

**Concert boost**

Concert revenue grew by 23.6% year-over-year to KRW 41.6bn, driven by the expansion of tours by SM Entertainment’s legacy IPs such as Super Junior, who concluded their 20th anniversary counter tour – which began last year – with three encore performances in Seoul during the second quarter.

Aespa held a total of five performances across Jakarta and Japan for their latest tour, while TVXQ! played two shows at Nissan Stadium in Japan. EXO also continued their sixth solo concert tour with a streak of sold-out shows during Q2. NCT Wish held a total of five concerts in the second quarter as part of their first concert tour, which started last year.

Additionally, performances from SHINee in Japan and Seoul, five solo concerts from IRENE, five shows from YESUNG in Japan, and one solo concert by TAEYONG contributed to the growth of SM Entertainment’s concert business in the second quarter.

During the second quarter, artist comeback and concert activities were effectively integrated with various pop-up events, expanding fan engagement. Driven by ongoing IP-based monetisation, merchandising/licensing revenue increased 22% year-over-year to KRW 77.9bn. Concert merchandise sales associated with artists’ tours also remained strong during the quarter.

Elsewhere, concert production unit Dream Maker recorded revenue of KRW 41.6bn, an increase of 178.5% year-over-year, due to an increase in the number of domestic concert productions.
