# 49 State AGs Tell FCC: Vet Every Robocall Mitigation Database Filing Before a Provider Can Carry Calls

## Summary

A bipartisan coalition of 49 state attorneys general urged the FCC to strengthen vetting of its Robocall Mitigation Database, asking for pre-listing review of filings, more public disclosure and faster removal of bad actors, building on the 2025 "Operation Robocall Roundup" enforcement push.

### Key Points
- AGs want every filing checked for accuracy before a provider is listed or allowed to carry calls.
- They flagged 61 providers sharing one Sheridan, WY address as evidence of weak foreign-provider screening.
- Proposed new certifications, proof-of-financial-stability requirements (surety bonds), and public disclosure of providers' third-party vendors.

This is general telecom/robocall regulatory news with no ticketing-industry nexus (not pricing, antitrust, resale, or BOTS-Act related); no research question is substantively informed, so no tags are applied.

[A bipartisan coalition of 49 state attorneys general (AGs)](https://www.naag.org/press-releases/49-attorneys-general-urge-fcc-to-strengthen-robocall-mitigation-database/) urged the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) to turn its [Robocall Mitigation Database](https://fccprod.servicenowservices.com/rmd?id=rmd_welcome) from a formality into a real checkpoint. In reply comments filed November 9, 2026, the states largely back the [FCC’s proposals](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/09/2026-18366/improving-the-effectiveness-of-the-robocall-mitigation-database-call-authentication-trust-anchor) but want it to go further: check every filing before a provider is listed, make more of what providers report public, and remove bad actors faster.

### What Is the Robocall Mitigation Database?

The Robocall Mitigation Database is the FCC’s public list of voice service providers allowed to send calls across the U.S. phone network. Any provider that starts, passes along, delivers, or brings in calls from abroad must file. A filing includes basic business information, a certification about the provider’s robocall practices, and a plan for stopping illegal robocalls. A provider may accept calls directly only from providers in the database.

### Why Do the AGs Say the Database Falls Short?

- Downstream providers can currently accept calls directly from any of the more than 11,000 providers in the database. The AGs say that limit is “barely a niggling obstacle, if that,” to protecting the U.S. phone network from illegal call traffic.
- Foreign providers can avoid added scrutiny with a nominal U.S. presence, the AGs say. They point to 61 providers listing the same suite at a Sheridan, WY address, only two of which identify as foreign.

### What Did the AGs Ask the FCC to Do?

- **Check filings before listing them.** Review every filing for accuracy and completeness before a provider appears in the database or can send calls.
- **Require more disclosure.** Make providers disclose their use of third parties, related companies, claims of a U.S. presence, and access to phone numbers.
- **Add certifications.** Require providers to certify that they have not submitted false or misleading information, that they follow FCC robocall rules, and that they will answer traceback requests (requests to trace a suspect call to its source) within 24 hours.
- **Require proof of financial stability.** The FCC asked which filers should have to show they can pay their debts. The AGs say all of them, because bad actors can be foreign or domestic, new or established. They also want the proof verified before the FCC publishes a filing.

### Where Do the AGs Go Further Than the FCC?

The AGs endorse most of what the FCC proposed. Three of their asks go further:

- **Public disclosure of outside vendors.** The FCC proposed collecting which third parties a provider uses for call analytics, call signing, customer vetting, and database filings. The AGs want that information public or, at a minimum, open to state and federal law enforcement, not limited to FCC staff.
- **A listing is a credential.** A database listing already carries an “imprimatur of legitimacy,” the AGs say, and the body that oversees STIR/SHAKEN, the system that verifies caller ID information, indicated that it relies on the database to judge a provider’s identity. The FCC should either fully vet filings or stop letting a listing unlock those credentials and call traffic.
- **Surety bonds, not just letters of credit.** The AGs say a bond may prove financial stability better than the FCC’s letter of credit, because a bonding company is likely to check that the business is real and its mitigation plan works.

### How Does This Fit the AGs’ Broader Robocall Enforcement?

The filing builds on [Operation Robocall Roundup](https://www.troutman.com/blog-post/ags-demand-end-to-unlawful-robocalls-through-operation-robocall-roundup/), launched in 2025 by the Anti-Robocall Multistate Litigation Task Force, a bipartisan group of state AGs led by Indiana, North Carolina, and Ohio. [Phase 2](https://ncdoj.gov/attorney-general-jeff-jackson-leads-phase-2-of-operation-robocall-roundup-announces-investigation-of-major-service-providers-inteliquent-bandwidth-lumen-and-peerless/) expanded the effort to four of the country’s largest intermediate voice service providers.

The reply comments were co-sponsored by the AGs of Colorado, Illinois, Indiana, North Carolina, Ohio, and Pennsylvania. The coalition has pressed the FCC on related fronts before, including [access to phone numbers](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/07/bipartisan-coalition-of-state-attorneys-general-ask-fcc-to-strengthen-rules-against-illegal-robocalls/), [Know Your Customer rules for originating providers](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/08/bipartisan-ag-coalition-urges-fcc-to-strengthen-know-your-customer-rules-to-combat-illegal-automated-calls/), and [Know Your Upstream Provider rules](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/09/49-state-attorneys-general-urge-fcc-to-mandate-upstream-provider-vetting/).

### What Happens Next?

These are proposals, not final rules. The FCC adopted its [further notice of proposed rulemaking](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-proposes-measures-stop-robocallers-access-us-networks) on July 22, 2026, and [published it in the Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/09/2026-18366/improving-the-effectiveness-of-the-robocall-mitigation-database-call-authentication-trust-anchor) on September 9. The AGs ask the FCC to make the new rules effective as soon as practicable, and say a phased rollout would be acceptable.

### What Should Companies Do Now?

- **Check your partners.** Confirm that the providers you take calls from, or send calls to, appear in the database and have not been suspended or removed. Recheck on a schedule.
- **Have a backup if your carrier is removed.** Other providers cannot accept calls from a removed provider, so a removal can cut off its customers. If your business relies on one carrier, review your contract and line up an alternative.
