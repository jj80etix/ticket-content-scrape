# Hive Launches AI Marketing Assistants for Live Event Venues and Promoters

## Summary

Hive launched five AI Marketing Assistants (Just Announced, Onsale, Newsletter, Countdown, Post Show) that auto-prepare email/SMS campaigns for venues and promoters using ticketing data, event schedules, and prior campaigns, with every send requiring human review/approval in a "Hive Inbox." Hive integrates with more than 40 ticketing systems — including Ticketmaster, AXS, Eventim, Etix, Tixr, and DICE — and serves 1,600+ venues/promoters/festivals; pricing starts at $50/month for 5,000 credits, charged only on approved campaigns. The launch follows Hive's $27M Frontier Growth raise and early adoption at Belly Up, whose marketing manager reported faster, more thorough audience segmentation.

### Key Points
- Five AI assistants cover distinct event-lifecycle marketing moments; nothing sends without human approval.
- Hive explicitly integrates with 40+ ticketing systems, including Etix, Ticketmaster, AXS, Eventim, Tixr, and DICE.
- Credit-based pricing ($50/mo for 5,000 credits) charges only for approved campaigns; drafts, edits, and rejections are free.
- Early customer Belly Up reports faster, more thorough segmentation and on-brand messaging via the feedback-learning loop.
- Hive did not disclose quantified lift in ticket sales or conversion attributable to the new assistants.
- Follows Hive's $27M Frontier Growth investment announced in October.

### Highlights
- Hive integrates directly with Etix's own platform, meaning Etix clients may already be exposed to this third-party AI layer.
- Hive serves 1,600+ venues/promoters/festivals and has historically helped generate over $1B in ticket sales.
- The human-approval gate ("Hive Inbox") is the core trust/control mechanism, not full autonomy.

### Industry Problem
Smaller marketing teams at venues/promoters/festivals lack the staff time to manually produce the recurring campaign types a touring/onsale calendar demands, and existing tools don't integrate event, audience, and brand-voice data into ready-to-approve drafts.

### Proposed Solution
Hive's lifecycle-stage AI drafting assistants (human-approved) address this directly, and since Hive already integrates with Etix, this is both a competitive AI benchmark (Q14) and a partner-integration/feature-parity consideration (Q1) — worth assessing whether Etix Assistant should offer comparable marketing-draft automation natively or lean further into the existing Hive integration.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Hibe-AI-marketing-assistants.webp)

Hive has launched five AI-powered Marketing Assistants designed to prepare email and SMS campaigns automatically for venues, promoters and festivals, introducing a new layer of automation intended to reduce the repetitive work involved in promoting live events.

The new tools, announced Wednesday, November 18, use ticketing information, event schedules, previous marketing campaigns and brand preferences to generate promotional messages for staff to review, edit and approve before they are distributed.

Rather than requiring marketers to initiate each campaign from scratch, the assistants work in the background to prepare messages based on an organization’s upcoming events and promotional calendar. The system pulls together artist information, show dates, ticket availability and relevant links, while drawing on past campaigns to maintain an organization’s established messaging and tone.

Hive says the technology is particularly intended for smaller marketing teams managing busy event calendars, where announcing new shows, promoting ticket onsales, sending reminders and maintaining regular audience communication can require substantial manual work.

- **Post Show:** Drafts follow-up communications after an event.

The assistants use information already connected to Hive’s marketing platform, including ticketing data and previous audience communications, to build campaigns tailored to each organization’s schedule.

Importantly, the system does not independently send marketing messages. Completed campaigns appear in a team’s Hive Inbox, where staff can review, revise, approve or reject the proposed communications. Nothing is distributed without human approval.

The assistants also incorporate feedback from those decisions, with Hive saying that previous campaigns, edits, approvals and rejected drafts help the system adapt to each organization’s brand and marketing preferences over time.

“Live-event marketers know their audiences and their shows. What they often need is more time to get the work done,” said Ian Roberts, CEO and co-founder of Hive. “Our Marketing Assistants bring together the event data and brand context already in Hive to prepare campaigns for their teams to review. Marketers stay in control, and their feedback helps shape the next draft.”

Hive pointed to early adoption at Belly Up, where senior marketing manager Alexa Carcamo said the technology has improved the efficiency of the venue’s email marketing operations.

“Marketing Assistants has made our email marketing at Belly Up faster and more efficient,” Carcamo said. “It builds segmentation lists more thoroughly than I could on my own and explains the logic behind each one. It has also learned from our feedback to keep our templates on-brand, which saves me a lot of time.”

Carcamo said she is also looking forward to potential improvements involving audience-segment integration with Meta advertising and more streamlined analytics and reporting.

The company did not provide specific measurements of time savings, increased ticket sales or conversion improvements attributable to the new assistants.

The product launch follows [Hive’s $27 million financing announcement in October](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/hive-frontier-growth-27-million-investment/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), when the company outlined plans to expand its artificial intelligence capabilities and automate portions of live-event marketing.

Hive’s existing platform connects ticket purchase information with email, SMS, advertising and customer relationship management tools, allowing event organizers to segment audiences and track the ticket sales associated with particular marketing campaigns. The company maintains integrations with more than 40 ticketing systems, including Ticketmaster, AXS, Eventim, Etix, Tixr and DICE.

Founded in 2014, Hive says it serves more than 1,600 venues, promoters and festivals and has helped customers generate more than $1 billion in ticket sales.

The launch also comes as [ticketing technology providers increasingly look to artificial intelligence](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/vivenu-ai-summit-ticketing-fan-data/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) to make customer information more accessible and automate operational tasks. Hive’s approach focuses specifically on campaign preparation, using existing event and audience data to generate marketing materials while keeping final decisions with venue and promoter staff.

Pricing for Hive’s AI Marketing Assistants starts at $50 per month for 5,000 credits, which the company estimates will cover approximately five approved campaigns. Credits are charged only when a campaign is approved, while drafts, edits and rejected campaigns do not consume credits. Email and SMS sending costs are billed separately.

Hive is also offering a 30-day free trial that includes 5,000 credits and does not require a credit card. Additional credit-purchasing options are expected later in November, and the company plans to introduce more specialized assistants beyond the initial five.

Additional information is available through [Hive’s Marketing Assistants product page](https://www.hive.co/features/marketing-assistants?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).
