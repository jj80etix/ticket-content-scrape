# Ticketbud Rolls Out All-in-One Credentialing for Complex Events

## Summary

Ticketbud launched an all-in-one credentialing suite for large festivals, conferences and venues, merging ticket sales, attendee intake, approval workflows and on-site badge production into a single platform to replace disconnected credentialing tools.

### Key Points
- Assigns tiered access levels (staff, vendor, sponsor, media, VIP) across tickets, badges and wristbands.
- Supports custom application/approval workflows with photos and role assignment before credential issuance.
- Field-tested at high-profile events before wider rollout; also offers wristband ordering and on-site badge printing services.

### Highlights
- New credentialing suite unifies public ticketing and staff/production credentials, addressing fragmented access data at complex events.

### Industry Problem
Event organizers commonly run separate platforms for public ticketing and for staff/vendor/media credentialing, so access data doesn't sync with ticket-scanning systems — creating security gaps and manual reconciliation work.

### Proposed Solution
Ticketbud is bundling credentialing directly into its ticketing platform as a single integrated system; this is a concrete competitor feature to benchmark — Etix could evaluate whether a similar unified credentialing module would resonate with complex/multi-day-event clients.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/ticketbud.webp)

Ticketbud is expanding beyond traditional ticketing with a new suite of credentialing tools designed for large-scale festivals, conferences, venues and other operationally complex events.

The upgraded platform brings ticket sales, attendee intake, approval workflows, access permissions, credential printing and on-site support into one system. Organizers can manage credentials for staff, vendors, sponsors, media, talent and VIP guests without relying on separate platforms that may not communicate with their ticket-scanning technology.

“Every event is different, and we needed to build out a robust and customizable system for supporting the many different needs of our growing client base,” Ticketbud CEO Kayhan Ahmadi said.

Ticketbud field-tested the tools at high-profile, heavily attended events over the past year before refining the system for a wider rollout.

Organizers can now assign specific access levels across tickets, badges and wristbands, including permissions for individual venues, restricted zones, sponsor activations and tiered VIP areas. Credentials can be printed in advance or produced on demand at the event.

The system also supports customized application and approval processes. Event teams can collect names, photographs, company details, roles, venue assignments and other information before approving or denying credential requests.

According to Ahmadi, many major events currently use one platform for public ticketing and another for staff and production credentials, leaving access data fragmented.

“Most frequently, simple generic credentials are printed en masse in advance and are not assigned to a specific individual,” he said. “We wanted to give event organizers a better solution: credentials and ticketing as an integrated solution.”
