# ADE & VibeLab Launch Nighttime Culture Index, Measuring Nighttime Culture In Six Cities

## Summary

ADE and VibeLab launched the first Nighttime Culture Index, measuring nightlife culture across Amsterdam, Lagos, New York, São Paulo, Seoul, and Sydney via six research dimensions and 30 indicators. Findings highlight large disparities in nightlife affordability and venue density between cities.

### Key Points
- Index covers Creative Ecology, Space & Infrastructure, Diversity & Representation, Community & Belonging, Policy & Civic Health, and Sustainability & Environment.
- Club-night entry costs range from ~1 hour of minimum-wage work in Sydney to ~25 hours in Lagos.
- Venue density varies widely: ~50 venues per million people in New York/Amsterdam vs. ~1 in Lagos.
- Women and gender-diverse artists remain under a third of festival line-ups.
- Research is framed as a baseline for cities/policymakers, not a ticketing-industry study.

The first Nighttime Culture Index (NCI) launched at the 30th Anniversary of Amsterdam Dance Event (ADE).

A joint initiative of ADE and VibeLab, with research led by VibeLab and local coordinators in each city, the Index measures nighttime culture in Amsterdam, Lagos, New York, São Paulo, Seoul and Sydney, aiming to provide “shared evidence for a global network of nighttime culture, and a baseline for tracking progress and challenges for local scenes over time,” according to the launch release.

It gives cities, policymakers, cultural organizations and industry a shared evidence base for understanding nighttime culture, its challenges and where to act.

Nighttime culture within these cities is measured through six research dimensions: Creative Ecology, Space & Infrastructure, Diversity & Representation, Community & Belonging, Policy & Civic Health, and Sustainability & Environment. Across these six dimensions there are 30 individual indicators which include the scale of cultural activity at night, how safe nighttime culture feels in these cities and for whom, price accessibility of participating in nighttime culture and how effectively cities support and protect their nighttime culture ecosystems.

The Nighttime Culture Index research combined desk research, expert interviews, data collection, a public survey and focus groups across the six NCI cities: Amsterdam, Lagos, New York, São Paulo, Seoul and Sydney. The research team analyzed 12,000-plus event line-ups, assessed more than 3,000 venues and researched close to 30,000 non-music cultural events; and heard from more than 700 people via the public survey, 67 focus group participants and 14 nighttime culture experts.

Key findings:

- A night out costs far more in some cities than others. Measured in hours of minimum-wage work, entry to a typical club night costs approximately one hour of work in Sydney, 1.3 hours in Amsterdam, 1.7 hours in Seoul, 1.8 hours in New York, 9 hours in São Paulo and approximately 25 hours in Lagos. These figures exclude transport, drinks and other costs associated with an evening out.
- The physical infrastructure of nightlife also varies considerably. New York and Amsterdam have approximately 50 dedicated music venues per million people, compared with around 12 in Seoul, seven in São Paulo and just over one in Lagos.
- Women and gender-diverse artists make up under a third of line-ups, and men still run the venues. Female and gender-diverse artists make up between 27% and 32% of line-ups across the six cities, and are booked for fewer events on average. Even where artists, collectives and frontline staff have become more diverse, venues are still run primarily by men. Experts in five of the six cities rated the representation of women, LGBTQIA+ people and other marginalised communities in venue ownership and leadership as poor, and experts in Amsterdam and Seoul said diversity behind the scenes is getting worse. A growing number of cities now have an office or official government appointment overseeing the night, including Sydney, New York, Amsterdam and, more recently, Seoul. But their remits are still mostly framed in economic terms, focused on business, tourism and turnover. Amsterdam’s 2021 vision for night culture is an exception.
- Participants in São Paulo and Seoul called for the night to be governed as part of cultural life, not only as a matter of economic policy or noise control. The research also found that night governance is not only the work of government: experts and participants called for nightlife operators and artists to take a greater role in advocating for the night in city policy, and for more collaboration, solidarity and resource-sharing within scenes.
- Research participants across all index cities expressed that night culture is thriving, but what varies by city is the scale, support for night culture, and who is able to take part in the culture.
- The NCI also highlights the importance of informal, DIY and public spaces, which provide lower cost opportunities for emerging artists and collectives to experiment where conventional venues are inaccessible or unaffordable.
- Despite the ongoing challenges in sustaining nighttime culture, its resilience presents many opportunities in all cities to build more inclusive systems to protect its nighttime culture and support its growth.
- Nighttime venues do serve as a “safer space”, but by contrast, feelings of safety drop sharply once outside venues. Across all six cities, respondents reported feeling less safe in nightlife-area streets than inside venues. The difference is particularly stark in São Paulo, where 73% of respondents feel safe inside venues, compared with just 11% in the streets. In Lagos, the figures are 74% and 20%, respectively. These concerns affect whether people go out at all: 84% of respondents in Lagos and 70% in São Paulo reported avoiding going out or avoiding a particular place in the previous year because of safety concerns. These findings also reveal significant gender imbalances. In Amsterdam, just 28% of women reported feeling safe travelling home at night, compared with 61% of men. In Sydney, the figures were 48% of women and 75% of men. At the same time, grassroots, alternative and queer scenes are often leading the way in developing more inclusive events, safer spaces and new approaches to audience wellbeing. The research found that these practices are particularly vulnerable when rising costs put pressure on budgets.

Comments:

Mirik Milan, Co-Founder, VibeLab, “Behind the numbers is a night culture that is thriving, even as the business around it is under immense pressure. New ideas often come out of crisis. Let’s give nightlife culture the space to do what it does best: bring people together.”

Jan-Willem van de Ven, Managing Director, Amsterdam Dance Event, “You can’t have a nighttime industry without a nighttime culture, and that culture depends on people being able to take part. When a club night costs a day’s wages or more in some cities, that is a question for all of us, including the industry.”

Jane Slingo, Director Asia Pacific, VibeLab, “What I value most about the NCI dashboard is the stories it lets us tell. Behind every indicator are real people: the collective running a party in a borrowed space, the promoter covering costs out of their own pocket, the artist building a community from scratch, the performer experimenting and staging work that doesn’t fit any category. The data gives us a way to see them, understand their experiences in their own city, and bring their work into the conversations where decisions are made.”
