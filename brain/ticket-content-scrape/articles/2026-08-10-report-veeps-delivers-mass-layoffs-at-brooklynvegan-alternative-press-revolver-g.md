# Report: Veeps Delivers Mass Layoffs at BrooklynVegan, Alternative Press, Revolver, Goldmine

## Summary

Veeps, the livestreaming platform majority-owned by Live Nation, reportedly laid off nearly all editorial staff at music publications BrooklynVegan, Alternative Press, Revolver and Goldmine, per a Pitchfork report.

### Key Points
- Veeps quietly bought the four publications from Project M Group in 2024/2025; Live Nation took a majority Veeps stake during the 2021 pandemic.
- All BrooklynVegan staff emails reportedly bounced except founder Dave Levine's.
- Publications' websites remain live but none has publicly commented on the layoffs.
- The story is developing; the future of the outlets is unclear.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/layoffs.jpg)

The editorial staff at music publications [*BrooklynVegan*](https://www.brooklynvegan.com),[*Alternative Press*](https://shop.altpress.com/?srsltid=AfmBOoq6dNLX_0v_BaJikFniOEqzrf80l0Umu5KrgvfYMbThm2nvVBM8), [*Revolver*](https://www.revolvermag.com), and *[Goldmine](https://www.goldminemag.com)* were reportedly gutted by Veeps last week, taking a huge hit against the music journalism industry.

According to *[Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/story/brooklynvegan-alt-press-hit-with-mass-layoffs/)*, nearly all staff at each publication were laid off. When the publication tried to reach out via email, all of *BrooklynVegan* staff emails bounced back with the exception of founder Dave Levine.

Veeps, the livestream platform majority-owned by Live Nation, was founded by Good Charlotte members Benji and Joel Madden in 2017. The company quietly purchased the four music publications from Project M Group in either 2024 or 2025. During the COVID-19 pandemic in 2021, Live Nation acquired a majority stake in Veeps.

*Goldmine* readers were able to enjoy a newspaper from the publisher since the mid-1970s, followed by a print magazine. *Alternative Press* was first founded in Cleveland in 1985, followed by *Revolver* in 2000 and *BrooklynVegan* in 2004. Each publication acted as a pillar in the music scene, helping uplift underground artists.
