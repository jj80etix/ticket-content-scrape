# OVG Among Gather Brisbane Consortium To Deliver $1.4 Billion Arena Project

## Summary

Queensland's government has awarded the Gather Brisbane consortium — Oak View Group, Live Nation, Plenary and Webuild — the contract to design, build, finance and operate a $1.4 billion, 17,000-seat indoor arena in Brisbane. The project, part of the Gabba Entertainment and Housing Precinct, drew eight bids and is expected to begin construction in early 2027 with completion targeted for April 2031. The government will contribute $600 million (AUD) toward the project on completion.

### Key Points
- $1.4B (AUD) arena, 17,000 fixed seats / up to 18,700 for concerts; construction starts early 2027, completes April 2031.
- Winning consortium: Oak View Group, Live Nation, Plenary, Webuild — design-build-finance-operate model.
- Queensland government contributes $600M on completion; eight bids were submitted for the project.
- Part of a broader precinct with ~4,000 new homes, retail and transit upgrades.

## Transcript

The Queensland government today granted Gather Brisbane – a consortium made up of Oak View Group, Live Nation, Plenary and Webuild – the contract for the $1.4 billion (US $948.08 million) indoor Brisbane Arena.

The highly-anticipated decision was announced during a Nov. 15 media call by Deputy Premier and Minister for State Development, Infrastructure and Planning Jarrod Bleijie.

“To get the acts to come to Queensland, to get the sporting events, to get the music events, the concerts to come to Queensland, you need a venue that rivals the rest of the venues around the world,” he said.

Bleijie said the selection of Gather Brisbane marked a major milestone for the precinct after a decade of flip-flopping and project failures by the former Labor Government.

The 17,000-seat arena is part of the Gabba Entertainment and Housing Precinct in Woolloongabba, Brisbane. The project drew eight bids, with two on the shortlist. The contract was awarded to Gather Brisbane following a competitive selection process led by the Queensland Government, which owns the land until 207. The Queensland Government will make a one-off $600 million ($406.32 million) contribution on completion in April 2031.

“The stage is now set for something special at Woolloongabba. This will be transformational for the area and generational for millions of people who’ll come to live, visit and enjoy this new precinct,” added Bleijie in the announcement.

Gather Brisbane brings together Plenary, Live Nation, Oak View Group (*Pollstar* ’s parent company) and Webuild, combining global expertise across infrastructure development, venue design and operations, live entertainment and major events. The consortium will design, build, finance, maintain and operate the new arena. Construction is anticipated to begin in early 2027.

The landmark project features the new arena purpose-designed for live entertainment and sports, with a capacity for up to 18,700 for concerts.

The arena serves as the centerpiece of a broader precinct that will bring together nearly 4,000 new homes, public spaces, dining, retail and improved transportation and pedestrian connections.

“We are incredibly excited to be part of bringing this landmark venue to life for Brisbane and its surrounding community,” added Chris Granger, CEO of Oak View Group in the announcement. “Developing and operating world-class venues is at the core of who we are at OVG. Working alongside our Gather Brisbane partners, we look forward to combining our collective expertise to create an exceptional destination that attracts the biggest events, elevates the live entertainment experience and becomes a source of pride for Brisbane for generations to come.”

Designed with the artist, fan and live entertainment experience at its core, the arena will strengthen Brisbane’s position as a leading destination for major concerts, touring productions and sporting events. Its location adjacent to the new Woolloongabba Cross River Rail station will provide direct access for fans and visitors, while the broader precinct is envisioned as a year-round destination that connects entertainment, housing, public space and community.

The project will also play an important role in Brisbane’s preparations for the 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games. Brisbane Arena, as commonly referred to, will host basketball during the 2032 Olympics and Paralympics. The nearby aging stadium is schedule for demolition following the Games to make way for additional housing and public open space.

The selection marks another significant step in OVG’s continued international growth and reflects the company’s integrated capabilities across venue development, programming and operations. Through its work with leading partners around the world, OVG helps create destinations that bring people together, strengthen communities and deliver extraordinary live experiences.

Live Nation Australia and New Zealand Venue Development Senior Vice President Guy Ngata outlined how the arena would change the city: “Brisbane is a city with a deep love of live music and major events. This arena will attract more touring artists to Brisbane, give Queensland talent a bigger stage and bring fans closer to the moments they love. It’s a long-term investment in Brisbane that will create jobs, bring more visitors to the city and support local businesses for decades to come.”

Plenary Group head of development, Sergio Calcarao, added, “It’s an honour to be selected to deliver this impressive venue; one that will prove to be an iconic piece of legacy infrastructure for Brisbane and a diverse range of housing and community infrastructure that will be integrated into the master planned precinct.”

Webuild Senior Executive Vice President, Operations Oceania, Marco Assorati, called the

Gabba Entertainment and Housing Precinct a defining project for Brisbane’s future growth and connectivity.

“With a strong pipeline of work and a clear vision for growth and infrastructure delivery, Queensland is a very important market for Webuild and we look forward to working collaboratively with the Queensland government and our partners to bring their vision for Woolloongabba to life,” said Assorati.

Brisbane Arena was first mooted in 2016 by Harvey Lister, then chief executive of ASM Global Asia Pacific and later Legends Global, as an 18,000-seat venue atop the new Roma Street underground train station.

In June 2023, it was predicted Brisbane Arena, by then moved by the government to its current site, would attract up to $2.5 billion ($1.69 billion) in real gross regional product over 2026-2048, and $90 million ($60.9 million) in investment each year. In his media conference, Deputy Premier Bleijie stated: “This is a 40-year deal and the maintenance \[and\] the obligations to maintain the arena will be \[on\] the consortia and the operator. There will be a commercial arrangement going forward with the operators, but as I said, it’s over 40 years. That will be locked in before the end of the year, before construction starts on the new arena in January.”
