# Ticketmaster’s New Identity Verification Process Leaves Customers With Privacy Concerns

## Summary

Ticketmaster has begun requiring some customers to pass an identity-verification "Security Check" — a selfie plus government ID checked by third-party vendor Persona — framed as an anti-bot/anti-scalping measure to give "real fans, not bots" a fair shot, but the requirement is drawing public pushback over privacy given the collection of biometric and government-ID data. Ticketmaster/Persona disclose that biometric data from successful verifications is retained up to 60 days, while failed verifications may be retained up to three years for fraud prevention, with data not shared with third parties beyond what's needed for verification/fraud purposes.

### Key Points
- New "Security Check" via Persona requires a selfie plus government ID for some account verifications.
- Ticketmaster frames it as bot/scalper mitigation to give "real fans" fair access.
- Persona retains biometric data ≤60 days for successful checks; up to 3 years for failed/fraud-flagged checks.
- Ticketmaster says data isn't shared with third parties beyond verification/fraud-prevention needs.
- Fans have voiced privacy concerns on social media; Ticketmaster Fan Support has issued only a generic templated response.

### Highlights
- Retention: 60 days (pass) vs. up to 3 years (fail/fraud-flagged).
- Verification requires both a selfie and a government-issued photo ID for some accounts.

### Industry Problem
Bot/scalping mitigation increasingly requires collecting sensitive biometric and government-ID data from ordinary patrons, creating direct tension between fraud prevention and patron privacy trust — and Ticketmaster's blanket, non-specific customer-support response isn't resolving that tension.

### Proposed Solution
Ticketmaster's approach is third-party biometric verification (Persona) with disclosed but lengthy fraud-flagged retention; an independent platform like Etix could differentiate with lighter-weight verification (device/behavioral signals before falling back to ID checks) and clearer, non-templated privacy communication to reduce patron distrust while still deterring bots.

Ticketmaster is hoping to combat mass scalping with its new identity verification process, but many customers are feeling weary about giving up their privacy.

The identity verification process requires some customers to submit selfies and their government issued ID. Ticketmaster noted on its website that the software company Persona checks the submitted photo against the ID photo and looks for indications that the ID may be fake. The ticketing giant’s said this process is to “help give real fans — not bots — a fair shot at getting tickets.”

The company’s website also disclosed that Persona retains biometric data for no more than 60 days for successful verifications, while unsuccessful verifications may lead to data being retained for up to three years for fraud prevention purposes. Ticketmaster’s customer disclosures added that Persona did not share data with third parties except to the extent necessary to perform identity verification and prevent fraud and abuse.
