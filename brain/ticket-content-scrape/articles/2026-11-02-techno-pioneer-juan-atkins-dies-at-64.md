# Techno Pioneer Juan Atkins Dies At 64

## Summary

Juan Atkins, the Detroit DJ/producer widely credited as a founder of techno, died Oct. 31 in Mexico City at 64 after being found unresponsive before a scheduled show. Tributes came from his agency Greensmith Artists, fellow Belleville Three member Kevin Saunderson, and Detroit Mayor Mary Sheffield.

### Key Points
- Formed Cybotron in 1980, later released solo work as Model 500 and founded the Metroplex label.
- Part of "The Belleville Three" (with Derrick May and Kevin Saunderson), credited with inventing techno.
- Last performed Oct. 23 in Amsterdam; was en route to a show at Mexico City's YuYu Cine Club when he fell ill.

## Transcript

![GettyImages 851234656](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/GettyImages-851234656-scaled.jpg)

Juan Atkins, a Detroit DJ and producer who helped pioneer techno, died Oct. 31 in Mexico City. He was 64.

The longtime musician was found unresponsive in a hotel room prior to performing at YuYu Cine Club in Mexico’s capital, according to *[Resident Advisor](https://ra.co/news/86327)*. A representative from Greensmith Artists, the agency that represents Atkins, told the outlet that the artist was rushed to a hospital, where he was pronounced dead.

Greensmith Artists confirmed Atkins’ death with a post on Nov. 1 and followed up with a tribute the following day.

“There are people whose contribution to music can be measured in records, performances, and the generations they inspired. And then there are people whose presence becomes part of your own life. Juan Atkins was both,” Greensmith Artists wrote on Instagram. “Music brought us together. It took us around the world, through almost every corner of Europe, across the Americas, Asia, Australia, New Zealand and Africa. We shared the stages of legendary clubs and festivals, extraordinary nights and countless ordinary ones, and more of our lives than we could ever measure in years.

“Juan became family. He was our brother,” the agency continued. “We knew the visionary who changed electronic music, but also the man behind that history. His humour, his stubbornness, his warmth, his contradictions. We laughed together, argued, worried about each other, drove each other mad, and somehow always ended up planning another journey.

“The world has lost one of the people who gave techno its sound, a man whose imagination changed the course of music,” the agency continued. “His children have lost their father, and our hearts are with them and the entire family.”

Born Sept. 12, 1962, Atkins was around music at an early age because his father was a concert promoter, putting on shows for artists like Barry White and Michael Henderson. He played guitar and bass. Atkins formed Cybotron with Richard Davis in 1980 and split five years later, which paved the way for him to release his own music in 1985 under the alias Model 500.

Atkins, widely known as “the godfather of techno,” was also part of the group The Belleville Three, which included Derrick May and Kevin Saunderson. The trio are credited for inventing the techno genre in Belleville, Michigan, and redefining the electronic music scene in the 1980s.

“Juan was the originator of Techno, a true visionary who opened the door for so many of us, myself included,” Saunderson said in an online tribute. “I’ll never forget how he showed me how to finish my first record, *Triangle of Love*. That meant a lot to me then, and it still does today. We shared a journey that changed music forever, and I’ll always be grateful for his friendship, guidance and everything he gave to our scene.”

The music innovator was also celebrated by Detroit Mayor Mary Sheffield, who referred to Atkins as one of the city’s “true originals.”

“Juan Atkins was born in this city, and he gave the world a new sound. As a teenager with a synthesizer and a big idea, he imagined what the music of the future could be. Then he built it,” Sheffield said in a statement. “With Cybotron, with Model 500, and through his Metroplex label, Juan laid the foundation for techno. He even gave the music its name.

“Alongside his friends Derrick May and Kevin Saunderson, Juan took a sound born in Detroit basements and bedrooms and carried it to clubs and festivals on every continent,” Sheffield added. “Today, millions of people dance to music that traces back to him and to our city. That is a Detroit story: genius, grit, and invention, from neighborhoods the world too often overlooked. Juan never stopped creating. He kept recording, touring, and pushing the music forward for more than four decades. Just as important, he opened doors for generations of Detroit artists who followed him. … Detroit will honor his memory, and his music will keep moving us forward.”

Atkins was active on the road and last performed at Q-Factory in Amsterdam on Oct. 23. He performed an intimate sold-out show at Number 90 Bar & Kitchen in London last year and grossed $5,743 off 178 tickets sold, according to Pollstar Boxoffice.
