# Ed Sheeran Fans Delayed Over Palestine Jerseys at Chile Concert, Club Demands Answers

## Summary

Fans attending Ed Sheeran's Nov. 21 Santiago, Chile concert say security initially barred entry to attendees wearing Club Deportivo Palestino (a Chilean soccer club founded by Palestinian immigrants) jerseys, with at least four confirmed cases and the club publicly demanding an explanation. No official venue, promoter (DG Medios, majority-owned by Live Nation) or artist-published entry rules listed clothing or political-symbol restrictions, and as of Nov. 23 none of the parties had responded to the club's or TicketNews' inquiries.

### Highlights
- The incident occurred Nov. 21 at Estadio Bicentenario de La Florida; at least 4 confirmed affected fans missed the show's first 3 songs.
- It follows a similar controversy two months earlier when Macklemore was pulled from Sheeran's US tour over pro-Palestinian remarks.
- Published entry-rule documents (Sheeran's own FAQ, the promoter's restricted-items list) named no clothing/political-symbol bans.
- Promoter DG Medios is majority-owned by Live Nation; as of Nov. 23 no party had responded to the club's request for an explanation.

### Industry Problem
Undocumented, inconsistently enforced entry restrictions imposed at the door — outside any published policy — create patron confusion, delays and perceived discrimination, and expose platforms/promoters to reputational and legal risk when restrictions aren't transparently communicated pre-event.

### Proposed Solution
The source describes no fix; a primary platform like Etix could differentiate by ensuring any venue/promoter-imposed entry restrictions (attire, political symbols, etc.) are captured and surfaced to ticket holders pre-event (confirmation emails, app notices) rather than left to door-level discretion.

Fans attending an Ed Sheeran concert in Santiago, Chile, say they were initially prevented from entering the venue because they were wearing clothing from Club Deportivo Palestino, a Chilean professional soccer team founded by Palestinian immigrants. The incident prompted the club to condemn the reported restriction and demand an explanation from concert organizers.

The incident comes roughly two months after rapper Macklemore was removed from the U.S. leg of Sheeran’s tour following controversy over pro-Palestinian statements he made onstage, which led to the other supporting acts quitting the tour in solidarity and cancellation of the singer’s performance at Gillette Stadium.

The confrontation occurred Saturday, November 21, at Estadio Bicentenario de La Florida during the first of Sheeran’s two scheduled performances at the stadium. According to accounts shared on social media and subsequently [reported by Chilean news outlet BioBioChile](https://www.biobiochile.cl/noticias/sociedad/debate/2026/11/22/fans-de-ed-sheeran-acusan-que-no-los-dejaban-entrar-a-concierto-por-llevar-indumentaria-de-palestino.shtml?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), security personnel stopped concertgoers wearing Palestino jerseys and sweatshirts, telling them they could not enter with the clothing.

The affected fans said they challenged the restriction because neither the event promoter nor the ticketing provider had previously announced a prohibition on such apparel. They were eventually admitted after disputing the decision with security personnel, but said they missed Sheeran’s first three songs.

The club called on promoter DG Medios and the event’s organizers to publicly explain what happened and identify the criteria behind the alleged restriction. It also requested a response from Sheeran’s representatives.

## Concert Admission Rules Raise Questions

The controversy raises questions about whether security personnel were enforcing an established admission policy or applying an undocumented restriction at the venue.

The [official Chile concert FAQ published on Sheeran’s website](https://www.edsheeran.com/sites/g/files/g2000021856/files/2026-04/Ed%20Sheeran%20Chile%202026%20-%20FAQs_m9ac57RG9ivvkg.pdf?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) outlines several admission requirements, including the use of dynamic QR codes through the Quentro ticketing app, age restrictions and limitations on professional cameras and recording equipment.

The document does not identify soccer jerseys, political symbols or clothing associated with particular countries or organizations as prohibited items. It does, however, direct attendees to DG Medios for the full list of restricted items.

A separate [November 19 report from Chilean outlet Rock&Pop](https://www.rockandpop.cl/2026/11/ed-sheeran-en-chile-2026-confirman-horarios-teloneros-y-objetos-prohibidos-para-sus-conciertos/amp/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), citing the promoter’s pre-show instructions, listed restrictions on bottles, aerosols, laser pointers, professional cameras, selfie sticks and other items. That list likewise did not include restrictions on clothing or soccer team merchandise.

The absence of such a restriction from the published materials does not establish whether additional instructions were issued privately to security personnel. It does, however, leave unanswered why ticket holders reportedly encountered a clothing restriction only after arriving at the venue.

DG Medios is one of Chile’s leading concert promoters. The company, which is [majority-owned by Live Nation](https://dgmedios.com/nosotros?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), has been responsible for bringing numerous major international tours to the country.

It remains unclear who directed security personnel to challenge the fans, whether the reported restriction applied throughout the stadium or whether the attendees were affected by an isolated decision at a particular entrance.

Nabzo clarified that Club Deportivo Palestino is not considering legal action of its own but has made legal assistance available to affected attendees who wish to pursue the matter.

“The club is available to provide legal assistance to those affected,” he told TicketNews, translated from Spanish.

Nabzo also confirmed that, as of November 23, the club had not received any response from DG Medios, the stadium administration or Ed Sheeran regarding its request for an explanation.

TicketNews separately contacted DG Medios, the stadium administration and representatives for Sheeran to ask who authorized the reported restriction, whether it was an official admission policy and whether affected fans would be offered any remedy. None of those parties had responded to TicketNews as of this update.

## Incident Follows Earlier Ed Sheeran Tour Controversy

The dispute comes roughly two months after Sheeran’s Loop Tour became embroiled in controversy over the removal of rapper Macklemore from scheduled appearances following his public statements supporting Palestine.

Macklemore was removed from the tour after objections from venues and promoters. Several other supporting performers subsequently withdrew, while Sheeran said the decision had been made by the promoters and venues rather than by him.

[In September](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/ed-sheeran-venue-controls-macklemore-loop-tour/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), Sheeran addressed the controversy during a Philadelphia concert, criticizing efforts by venues to control performers’ speech and expressing his desire for concerts to be inclusive spaces.

The latest incident raises a different question about the boundaries of venue and promoter authority, this time involving the clothing worn by paying attendees rather than the statements made by performers onstage.

There is no established evidence that Sheeran or his management directed security personnel to restrict Palestino merchandise at the Santiago concert, nor has any connection been demonstrated between the November incident and the earlier tour dispute.

The identity of those responsible for the reported admission restriction remains unresolved. Palestino has called on the event organizers to disclose what happened, explain the justification for the security decision and clarify the artist’s position.
