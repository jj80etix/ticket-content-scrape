# Movers & Shakers: Aviva Arena, SeatGeek, WME

## Summary

IQ's latest Movers & Shakers roundup covers appointments at Bristol's incoming Aviva Arena, SeatGeek, WME, New West Records and Live Nation. The most notable move is SeatGeek naming its first chief business officer as the company expands AI investment across fan and rightsholder experience and looks for growth beyond core ticketing.

### Key Points
- SeatGeek elevates Sara Providence to newly created chief business officer role, tasked with identifying where the company grows next
- Move comes as SeatGeek "expands its investment in AI across the fan and rightsholder experience," per CEO Jack Groetzinger
- Bristol's 20,000-cap Aviva Arena (opening 2028) names Dave Gaydon as live programming director
- WME adds two agents to its contemporary/electronic music team; Live Nation hires a UK/EMEA communications director

Bristol’s incoming arena has named its live programming director, SeatGeek has a new chief business officer, and WME has added two new agents, leading the latest movement for the live music industry.

**Aviva Arena  
**Dave Gaydon is joining the senior team of Bristol’s forthcoming Aviva Arena as live programming director, effective immediately.

A highly respected industry figure, he joins having most recently served as part of Live Nation’s outdoor promotions team, delivering shows including South Facing Festival and the Colchester Castle Summer Series. He also spent nearly a decade as Roundhouse’s Head of Music and worked as a consultant with LW Theatres.

He will lead programming on the “ [game-changer](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/02/inside-bristols-gamechanger-arena-opportunities-are-limitless/) ” 20,000-capacity arena currently under construction in Southwest England. It is projected to contribute £1 billion to the local economy in its first decade, as it will serve roughly 16 million people within a two-hour drive of the venue. It is slated to open in 2028.

“What excites me is starting with a very clear ambition but also the freedom to build a dynamic programme,” Gaydon says. “We want to become an essential stop on major touring schedules serving this part of the UK in a way it never has been before. It’s not every day you get to join a team as the venue is built before your very eyes, and I am thrilled to be part of that.”

He will report to YTL Live and Aviva CEO Andrew Billingham, and work alongside non-executive director Miles Leonard.

“We’re bringing together a team with the expertise, experience and imagination to deliver a venue that can completely transform the live events landscape in the West of England for both fans and artists,” Billingham says. “Dave has an impeccable track record, and having him join our team will help us create a distinctive calendar that combines the world’s biggest touring music artists with comedy, sport and entertainment.”

*IQ* sat down with Gaydon about his plans for the role for our next issue, out 23 November.

> “I can’t think of anyone better to lead this work”

**SeatGeek  
**Ticketing platform SeatGeek has elevated Sara Providence to the newly created role of chief business officer.

In the executive role, she will lead company strategy, business development, and corporate communications, and will be responsible for identifying where SeatGeek grows next, both within ticketing and in new businesses adjacent to live entertainment.

She joined the company as chief of staff in 2023, and was later promoted to VP. The appointment comes as SeatGeek expands its investment in [AI](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/11/ticketers-turning-to-ai-to-offer-tour-planning-services/) across the fan and rightsholder experience.

“We’ve always said that SeatGeek is a technology company that happens to sell ticket,” says Jack Groetzinger, SeatGeek co-founder and CEO. “We want to grow faster in ticketing while finding new opportunities beyond it, and that work needs a dedicated owner on the executive team. Sara has been at the centre of our biggest decisions for the past three years.

“She’s shaped our company priorities and led the executive team and Board through some of the most important decisions we’ve made. She knows how this company works and where we can go next. I can’t think of anyone better to lead this work.”

As part of the new structure, SeatGeek is hiring a chief of staff, a head of PR & communications, and a business development manager.

Providence adds: “During my time at SeatGeek, this team has pioneered things that didn’t exist in live entertainment before, and we’ve only scratched the surface of what our technology makes possible for fans and partners. Figuring out where that takes us next, and how we can truly reshape live events for the better, is the most exciting job I can imagine. I’m grateful for the trust and can’t wait to get moving.”

> “The future of music is global, and their additions to our team continue to strengthen WME’s position”

**WME  
**Global talent agency WME has hired two new agents, Aleks Ljubojevic and Sophia Simon, for its contemporary music team.

Ljubojevic joins from Amsterdam’s Ace Agency, where he represented Martin Garrix, Sam Feldt, Don Diablo, and many others in the electronic music space. He will focus on expanding WME’s electronic music roster and driving growth opportunities for talent. He brings with him producer Steve Angello of Swedish House Mafia fame to WME’s roster.

He will be based in London and will report to Stephanie LaFera, partner and head of EMD.

Spain-based Simon is joining the team from the agency she founded in 2024, So.Connected, which focuses on developing artists in the Brazilian market.

She has worked with artists including Martin Garrix, Ne-Yo, Jason Mraz, Maddix, Sean Paul, Alesso, and Soulja Boy, and has helped develop BRESH’s presence in the country. Simon will report to Rob Markus, senior partner and head of international.

“Aleks and Sophia both have strong track records of developing talent, building international touring businesses, and creating opportunities for artists across key markets worldwide,” says global head of music Kirk Sommer. “The future of music is global, and their additions to our team continue to strengthen WME’s position as the leading agency for long-term global success.”

> “This year has brought a few unexpected detours for me professionally”

**New West Records  
**After a stint with TEG Live, Brad Turcotte has now been named general manager at Nashville’s New West Records, a country-focused independent label.

He brings more than 30 years of industry experience, spanning independent and major record labels, international marketing, artist management, and live entertainment to the role.

Turcotte joined [TEG Live](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/02/teg-live-zeroes-in-on-country-music-with-new-office-svp/) as SVP of country, as part of a new dedicated touring brand for the genre, back in February, which has since been [disassembled](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/teg-pulls-plug-on-country-festival-one-month-after-announcing/).

“This year has brought a few unexpected detours for me professionally, but I truly feel like the road has ended at the best home possible,” he says. I can’t thank John Allen and George Fontaine Sr. enough for trusting me with this role.”

**Live Nation**  
Hannah Stennett has joined Live Nation as director, communications UK & EMEA.

She brings experience from a variety of communications roles, having most recently spent nearly seven years at AEG International. Stennett remains based in London.
