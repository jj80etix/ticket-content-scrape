# Live Nation makes third Australian venue move in a month

## Summary

Live Nation has confirmed Surfers Music Hall, a 2,500-capacity venue on Australia's Gold Coast projected to open mid-2028, its third Australian venue announcement in a month (following the 18,700-cap Brisbane Arena with Oak View Group and the 5,000-cap Arden Central in Melbourne). The city-owned, Live Nation-operated venue will be open to a "range of promoters," is projected to contribute $20M annually to the local economy, and will include a Community Benefit Reserve for local talent and community use.

### Key Points
- Surfers Music Hall: 2,500-cap, Gold Coast's first purpose-built venue of its scale, opening ~mid-2028.
- Third Live Nation Australia venue announcement in the past month (after Brisbane Arena w/ OVG, and Melbourne's Arden Central).
- City-owned, Live Nation-operated; open to multiple promoters, not Live Nation-exclusive.
- Projected $20M/year local economic contribution; includes a Community Benefit Reserve.
- Comes alongside Live Nation CEO Michael Rapino's comments on record Q3 results ($10B+ quarterly revenue) and stated ambition to add stages across venue tiers.

### Highlights
- Three new Australian venues announced by Live Nation within one month (Surfers Music Hall, Brisbane Arena, Arden Central), spanning 2,500–18,700 capacity.
- Comes directly off record Q3 revenue (~$10B) and explicit CEO messaging about expanding venue-tier coverage.

### Industry Problem
Live Nation's rapid, multi-tier venue buildout in a single market (ownership/operation across small, mid, and large capacities) deepens its vertical integration across the live-event value chain — the same structural pattern at the center of antitrust scrutiny of Live Nation/Ticketmaster, and a competitive-moat concern for independent platforms trying to win venue and promoter relationships.

### Proposed Solution
The article describes no ticketing-specific solution; it's a venue-development play. For Etix, this is a monitor/evolve item: track whether Live Nation's venue-tier expansion (and resulting promoter/venue lock-in) extends into markets where Etix competes for venue ticketing contracts, and whether it strengthens the case independents can make around neutrality/promoter choice.

The 2,500-cap Surfers Music Hall on the Gold Coast follows closely behind the Brisbane Arena and Arden Central in Melbourne

![Surfers Music Hall rendering](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Surfers-Music-Hall-rendering-©-Live-Nation.png)

Surfers Music Hall rendering © Live Nation

Live Nation is doubling down on its Australian venue development, today confirming a 2,500-capacity concert hall on the Gold Coast, its third venue announcement in the past month.

Surfers Music Hall will become the city’s first purpose-built music venue of this scale when it opens, projected for mid-2028. The space will feature a multi-tiered mezzanine designed to give fans good sightlines and bring them closer to the stage.

Owned by the city and operated by Live Nation, the venue is slated to host both international and domestic acts and be open to a “range of promoters”, the company says.

“The Gold Coast has a strong music community and a growing audience for live events,” says Guy Ngata, SVP, venue development, Live Nation Australia and New Zealand.

“This venue will give artists a high-quality room to play, fans more shows close to home and promoters a venue that makes the Gold Coast a more viable stop on national and international tours,” he adds.

Once it is open, it is expected to contribute $20 million annually to the local economy. Live Nation is also set to establish a Community Benefit Reserve to support local talent and community use of the venue, with the funds able to cover operating costs or help with production expenses.

It will be part of the wider Surfers Paradise precinct, which will include public space, improved pedestrian connections, and be utilised for cultural and civic events.

> “Surfers Music Hall is an important investment in the Gold Coast’s cultural life”

Gold Coast Mayor Tom Tate says: “Surfers Music Hall is an important investment in the Gold Coast’s cultural life. It will bring more quality artists to the city, create more reasons for people to spend time in Surfers Paradise, and support the businesses that make the precinct vibrant.”

It is the latest in a string of announcements from Live Nation in the past month, following closely behind the 18,700-capacity [Brisbane Arena](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/11/live-nation-and-ovg-venture-to-build-music-first-arena-for-brisbane/) collaboration with Oak View Group (OVG) and the 5,000-capacity space in North Melbourne, [Arden Central](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/10/venue-news-live-nation-reveals-new-5k-cap-melbourne-for-100-events-a-year/).

Like other music markets, Live Nation is moving rapidly to develop options across the venue tiers, as part of the company’s ambition to give “artists more stages to build their careers and fans more opportunities to experience the magic of live music”, CEO Michael Rapino said regarding [record Q3 results.](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/11/stadium-attendance-jump-drives-live-nation-to-10bn-q3-revenue/)

Last year, the company also announced it was building the [Northbridge Music Hall](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/06/live-nation-to-launch-mid-size-venue-in-perth-wa/) in Perth, with a projected opening date of late 2026 or early 2027. No further details or timeline have been announced on the project.

Outside of the latest developments, Live Nation also operates Fortitude Valley’s [Fortitude Music Hall](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2018/09/brisbane-fortitude-void-festival-hall/), Brisbane’s The Triffid, Melbourne’s [Festival Hall](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2023/03/ln-australia-to-run-melbournes-festival-hall/), and Adelaide’s [Hindley Street Music Hall](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2022/03/live-nation-teams-on-new-1800-cap-australia-venue/).

The company has also helped revitalise heritage entertainment venues, including St Kilda’s Palais Theatre and Thirroul’s Anita’s Theatre.

Over in New Zealand, the company also operates Auckland’s Spark Arena, which recently had a record-breaking set of shows from [Zara Larsson](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/10/zara-larsson-smashes-new-zealand-arena-attendance-record/).
