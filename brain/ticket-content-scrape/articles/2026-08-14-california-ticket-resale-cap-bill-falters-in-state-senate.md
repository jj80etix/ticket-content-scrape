# California ticket resale cap bill falters in State Senate

## Summary

A second report (IQ Magazine) on California's AB 1720 resale-cap bill stalling in the State Senate Aug. 13 adds detail on the broader multi-state trend: Maine, Vermont and Washington D.C. have already enacted resale caps, Massachusetts is preparing a similar proposal, and resale platforms StubHub, SeatGeek and Vivid Seats — whose fee revenue scales with resale price — pushed back against the bill; StubHub reportedly spent $2.6m lobbying against it (a different figure than the $3.4m cited elsewhere).

### Key Points
- AB 1720 would have capped resale at 110% of face value and limited platform transaction fees, for venues under 3,000 capacity only; it stalled in the State Senate Aug. 13.
- Maine, Vermont and Washington D.C. have already enacted resale price caps; Massachusetts Gov. Maura Healey said in July she'd propose a similar cap via the state's supplemental spending bill.
- Music Artists Coalition called the outcome "disappointing" and reiterated fan-protection framing.
- StubHub is reported to have spent $2.6m lobbying against the bill (vs. the $3.4m figure reported by TheTicketingBusiness on the same story).
- Live Nation and NIVA supported the bill, framing it as targeting "predatory resale sites."

### Highlights
- AB 1720 (110%-of-face resale cap, sub-3,000-cap venues) failed in the CA Senate Aug. 13 — confirms the TheTicketingBusiness report with added context on lobbying spend and multi-state momentum.
- Massachusetts is the next state to watch: Gov. Healey signaled a resale-cap proposal via the supplemental spending bill in July 2026.
- Resale platforms (StubHub, SeatGeek, Vivid Seats) are named as active opponents of price-cap legislation given fee revenue tied to resale price.

### Industry Problem
Same structural conflict as the companion story: resale platforms' fee-based revenue models create a direct incentive to lobby against price caps, slowing convergence toward the fan-protection framing that primary platforms and artists (Live Nation, NIVA) are increasingly aligned behind.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor Massachusetts' forthcoming resale-cap proposal as the next test case; continue positioning Etix's primary, fixed-fee model against the "predatory resale" narrative in client-facing materials for markets considering similar legislation.

A bill that proposed to introduce a ticket resale cap in California has failed to pass, with live industry figures calling it a “disappointing outcome”.

The bill (AB 1720) stalled in the State Senate yesterday (13 August), meaning its aims to limit resale price to 110% of face value, as well as limiting transaction fees charged by platforms, will not take effect. The proposed regulations would have applied only to tickets for events in venues below 3,000 capacity.

Three US jurisdictions have introduced limits to how much resellers can charge for live event tickets as part of a recent wave of momentum. behind consumer protection laws. These are Maine, Vermont, and Washington D.C., with several other states currently proposing similar actions.

> “very fan, at every show, needs to be protected. Period”

However, the failure of the law in California, one of the world’s leading live entertainment markets, means some of the biggest concerts in the global industry will remain without capped resale.

Reacting to the news, the Music Artists Coalition said: “Resale price caps aren’t a new concept. Maine, Vermont, and Washington D.C. have all recently enacted similar laws. In July, Massachusetts Governor Maura Healey announced she would also be presenting a price cap law for review in the state’s supplemental spending bill.

It added that “these caps have received significant pushback from resale platforms and brokers such as StubHub, SeatGeek, and Vivid Seats, whose business models depend on revenue from fees attached to ticket sales. The higher the ticket’s sale price, the higher the fee they collect”.

“This is a disappointing outcome,” its executive director Ron Gubitzadded. “Every fan, at every show, needs to be protected. Period.”

The bill had also received support from the National Independent Venues Association (NIVA), as well as Live Nation, which said when the proposal was tabled in February: “We applaud California Assemblymember Matt Haney’s efforts to protect concert fans and artists. AB 1720 targets a core problem in live music: predatory resale sites that profit from excessive markups and manufactured scarcity.”

The failure of the bill comes as reports emerged that StubHub’s had spent $2.6m on lobbying activities in opposition to the regulations.
