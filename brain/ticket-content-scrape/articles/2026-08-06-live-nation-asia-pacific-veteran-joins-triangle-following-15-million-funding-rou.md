# Live Nation Asia-Pacific Veteran Joins Triangle Following $15 Million Funding Round

## Summary

Asia-Pacific live entertainment operator Triangle Group has raised $15 million ($10M equity seed led by DSG Consumer Partners plus $5M venture debt from Genesis Alternative Ventures) and hired former Live Nation Asia Pacific president Roger Field as executive director and operating partner. Triangle is positioning itself as a regional alternative to global concert operators, betting on continued growth in Asia-Pacific's live entertainment market, which it estimates could reach $75 billion by 2033. The company plans to expand touring/festival operations and add consumer offerings like travel and hospitality packages.

### Key Points
- Triangle Group raised $15M total: $10M equity seed (DSG Consumer Partners, Golden Gate Ventures, Oberoi Family Office, angels) plus $5M venture debt (Genesis Alternative Ventures) — called the largest institutional investment in APAC's experience economy by a regional operator.
- Roger Field, former president of Live Nation Asia Pacific and CEO of Live Nation Australia/New Zealand (13 years, 11 markets, 400+ employees), joins as executive director and operating partner after a year advising/investing in the company.
- Triangle's pitch is a market-by-market (not standardized global) touring model, covering artist offers, routing, production, promotion, and financial risk.
- Company cites APAC live entertainment market potentially reaching $75B by 2033, with nearly 60% of the world's Gen Z living in the region.
- Plans include expanding touring/festival operations and adding travel and hospitality packages for consumers.

### Highlights
- $15M raise ($10M equity + $5M venture debt) announced August 6, 2026, described as the largest regional-operator institutional investment in APAC's experience economy.
- Roger Field's move from Live Nation (13-year veteran, ran 11 APAC markets) to a regional independent operator signals executive talent flowing toward APAC-focused challengers.
- Triangle explicitly frames itself as a regional alternative to "global concert operators" (i.e., Live Nation), citing 60% of global Gen Z population and $75B APAC market projection by 2033.

### Industry Problem
The item evidences a market-structure trend rather than a client/patron pain point: capital and senior talent are flowing into regional challengers positioning against global-scale incumbents like Live Nation, particularly in the fast-growing, Gen Z-heavy Asia-Pacific live entertainment market.

### Proposed Solution
Triangle's own answer is a regionally-tailored, market-by-market touring and promotion infrastructure rather than a one-size-fits-all global playbook. For Etix, this is a monitor-and-evolve signal: APAC's growth trajectory and the emergence of well-funded regional operators are worth tracking as a long-horizon market-entry or partnership consideration, with the trigger being further APAC-focused funding rounds or executive moves from Ticketmaster/AXS/SeatGeek.

Triangle Group is gearing up for a major expansion across Asia-Pacific after raising $15 million and bringing former Live Nation regional president Roger Field into its leadership team.

The live entertainment operator secured a $10 million equity seed round led by DSG Consumer Partners, with participation from Golden Gate Ventures, the Oberoi Family Office and several angel investors. Genesis Alternative Ventures provided an additional $5 million venture debt facility.

Triangle described the financing as the largest institutional investment in Asia-Pacific’s experience economy by a regional operator.

Alongside the raise, Field has joined Triangle Asia as executive director and operating partner. He previously served as president of Live Nation Asia Pacific and CEO of Live Nation Australia and New Zealand, overseeing 11 markets and more than 400 employees during his 13 years with the concert giant.

Field had already spent a year advising and investing in Triangle before taking on the full-time role.

“We set out to build seamless, end-to-end infrastructure for artists and festivals coming to Asia,” Triangle CEO and co-founder Greg Hargrave said in a press release. “A raise this size in this market, and the region’s most senior executive choosing to join us, are the clearest proof yet that vision is real, and ready to scale.”

Triangle is positioning itself as a regional alternative to global concert operators, with a model built around market-by-market touring strategies rather than a standardized international playbook.

“Running Live Nation across this region taught me one thing: Asia-Pacific doesn’t need a global template; it needs an operator who understands every market individually,” Field said. “That’s the business we’re building at Triangle, where the standards of a global player are applied with a regional focus.”

The company manages the full touring and festival cycle, from artist offers and routing to production, promotion and financial risk. Triangle said it intends to provide consistent execution across the region while tailoring each campaign to the artist and local audience.

“We build around the specific artist in terms of who their audience is, which markets are ready, when to step up and when to hold,” said Zaran Vachha, Triangle’s managing director of culture and creative and a company co-founder. “We’re not booking a one-off; we’re routing a region.”

Hargrave, a former CEO of a multibillion-dollar company listed on the Australian Securities Exchange, brings experience in fintech, public markets and scaling businesses. Vachha has spent years working across Asia’s touring and festival sector, while Field adds three decades of experience spanning ticketing, venues, concerts and festival promotion.

Triangle is betting on continued growth in Asia-Pacific’s live entertainment market, which the company said could reach $75 billion by 2033. Nearly 60% of the world’s Gen Z consumers live in the region, creating a rapidly expanding audience for concerts, festivals and other live experiences.

The company also sees opportunity in increasingly global fan behavior. The worldwide rise of K-pop and Latin music has shown that artists can develop large audiences far beyond their home markets, while declining reliance on recorded-music revenue has made touring and international expansion more important.

Triangle plans to use the new capital to expand its touring and festival operations while developing additional consumer offerings, including travel and hospitality packages.
