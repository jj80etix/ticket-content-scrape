# ‘Going to a festival alone is no longer a compromise’

## Summary

Denmark's Smukfest is expanding community features for solo festival-goers, including a new in-app "Find a Concert Companion" matching tool and an expanded solo-camping ground, as new festival research finds roughly 4.5% of its 60,000 attendees this year (2-9 August) are attending alone and nearly 90% of them value the freedom of shaping their own experience.

### Key Points
- ~2,700 of Smukfest's expected 60,000 attendees are solo this year; ~90% of surveyed solo-goers cite valuing independence.
- New app feature "Find a Concert Companion" matches attendees (solo or in groups) around shared shows they want to see, allowing up to three matches per performance.
- Solo camping ground "Modigheden" ("The Courage"), which debuted last year, has been expanded.
- Trend echoes Ticketmaster's 2025 State of Play: Festivals report, which found 29% of Brits have attended a festival alone.

## ‘Going to a festival alone is no longer a compromise’

Denmark's Smukfest is expanding its communal and camping initiatives for solo festival-goers as more look to go it alone

![Smukfest](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Smukfest-©-Kristian-Appelon.png)

Smukfest © Kristian Appelon

As more individuals go it alone at festivals, Denmark’s Smukfest is expanding its community options for solo festival-goers.

Of the 60,000 people expected to attend the long-standing forest festival in Skanderborg this week (2-9 August), roughly 4.5% of them (2,700) will attend on their own.

New research from one of Scandinavia’s largest music festivals finds that audiences are increasingly making the choice to attend solo, with nearly 90% of surveyed solo attendees saying they value the freedom of shaping their own experience.

This aligns with the growing trend identified by Ticketmaster in their 2025 [State of Play: Festivals report](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/08/music-festivals-becoming-the-new-family-holiday/), with almost one in three Brits (29%) attending a festival alone, and nearly one in five (18%) saying they would consider it in future.

“Festivals are among the largest social gatherings we have, and it’s wonderful to see more people feeling confident enough to experience them on their own,” says Trine Bang, Smukfest head of content and spokesperson. “Going to a festival alone is no longer a compromise. It represents a new way of being part of a festival. Today’s festival community is no longer defined only by who you arrive with, but also by the people you meet along the way.”

In response to the expanding segment, Smukfest has launched a new initiative within its official festival app to encourage community building among its audience, called the Find a Concert Companion feature.

> “Some come with lifelong friends, others arrive hoping to meet new people”

It is designed to connect attendees – both solo and travelling in groups – based on the performances they want to attend, making it easier to find others with similar musical interests. Guests can match with up to three people for each show, and can chat directly to find a meeting place in person.

“Good hosting isn’t about making everyone experience a festival in the same way,” Bang says. “It’s about recognising that people participate in communities differently. Some come with lifelong friends, others arrive hoping to meet new people. Our role is to create space for both.”

Additionally, Smukfest organisers have expanded their solo camping option, called Modigheden (‘The Courage’), which debuted last year and offers a community-focused ground for those attending alone.

Smukfest’s 2026 lineup includes names like Robbie Williams, Lewis  
Capaldi, Macklemore, Melanie C, Bebe Rexha, and Lukas Graham, among many others.

“We believe music has always been one of the easiest ways for people to connect,” says Trine Bang. “Now we’re simply making it easier for people to find each other around the artists and experiences they’re already excited about.”
