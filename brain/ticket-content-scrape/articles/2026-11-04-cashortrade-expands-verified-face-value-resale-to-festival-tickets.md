# CashorTrade Expands Verified Face-Value Resale to Festival Tickets

## Summary

CashorTrade has extended its verified face-value resale technology to Front Gate Tickets, a Ticketmaster subsidiary that powers major festivals including Lollapalooza, Austin City Limits and Ultra, building on its earlier Ticketmaster integration and adding support for RFID wristband-based admission. The system authenticates resale listings against Front Gate's original transaction records, capping resale prices at the verified original purchase amount (including primary fees) while buyers still pay CashorTrade's platform and processing fees. Participation requires individual event organizers to opt in, so the integration won't be available for every Front Gate event immediately.

### Key Points
- Front Gate (Ticketmaster subsidiary) tickets can now be resold on CashorTrade at a verified, authenticated face-value price, including for RFID wristband-based festivals.
- Resale price is capped at the original paid amount (fees included); buyers still pay CashorTrade's 5-10% platform fee and 3-5% processing fee (waived platform fee for Gold members).
- Three wristband-handling models: pre-ship sync with Front Gate, deactivate-and-replace at will-call, or physical ship/exchange.
- Participation is opt-in per event organizer, not automatic across Front Gate's portfolio.
- CashorTrade frames this as deepening direct data-sharing partnerships with primary ticketing providers rather than relying on seller-submitted receipts.

### Highlights
- Integration announced Oct. 30, 2026, extending CashorTrade's April 2026 Ticketmaster integration into the festival/RFID-wristband market.
- Resale fee structure mirrors the existing Ticketmaster integration: 5-10% platform fee, 3-5% processing fee for non-Gold members.
- Front Gate's festival portfolio includes Lollapalooza, ACL, Bourbon & Beyond, Lost Lands, and Ultra Music Festival.

### Industry Problem
Fans reselling festival tickets have lacked a trusted way to prove the original purchase price, especially for physical RFID wristbands, leaving resale markets vulnerable to scalping above face value and disputes over authenticity.

### Proposed Solution
CashorTrade's direct-integration model lets a primary platform's own transaction data enforce a face-value ceiling and reissue credentials automatically; an independent platform like Etix could pursue a similar authenticated-resale partnership (own-built or third-party) to capture secondary-market trust and fee revenue currently flowing to resale specialists.

## Handling Festival Wristbands

One of the more significant aspects of the Front Gate partnership is its ability to accommodate physical festival wristbands, which create different resale and fulfillment challenges from electronically transferred concert tickets.

Rich outlined three approaches depending on when a transaction occurs and how the festival manages admission.

For tickets resold before wristbands have been shipped, CashorTrade transactions synchronize with Front Gate’s system, allowing the new buyer to receive the wristband through the festival’s regular fulfillment process, as though the ticket had been purchased directly from the primary seller.

When wristbands have already shipped, the original seller’s wristband can be deactivated and the buyer can collect a replacement at the festival’s will-call location. This allows the original admission credential to be invalidated rather than requiring the buyer to rely on the seller delivering the existing wristband.

For events that prefer to reuse physical wristbands, CashorTrade also supports shipping and in-person exchanges between fans.

“We support RFID wristband events at every stage leading up to the show, so fans are covered no matter when they buy,” Rich said.

The options give participating organizers flexibility in how they handle resale while maintaining a process for verifying the underlying ticket purchase.

## What Buyers Pay

Although the integration prevents sellers from marking up tickets beyond their original purchase price, buyers may still pay additional fees when completing a transaction.

Rich confirmed that selling on CashorTrade is free and that the resale listing price is capped at the original checkout amount, including the fees paid to the primary ticketing company.

Buyers, however, pay platform and payment-processing fees. According to [CashorTrade’s published fee schedule](https://help.cashortrade.org/hc/en-us/articles/12918261373069-Understanding-Fees-on-CashorTrade?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), non-Gold members generally pay a 5% to 10% platform fee and a 3% to 5% payment-processing fee. Gold members generally avoid platform fees, although certain high-value transactions and partnered events carry exceptions. Processing fees still apply.

CashorTrade also notes that some primary-ticketing integrations may carry additional fees, which are disclosed at checkout.

Rich said the buyer fee structure for Front Gate is the same as the existing Ticketmaster integration.

The distinction is important because a ticket sold at its verified original price can still cost the second buyer more than the first purchaser paid once resale transaction fees are included.

It also means that face value is not necessarily a single, fixed price for every admission to an event. Where original ticket prices vary, the verified resale ceiling reflects the amount paid for that particular ticket.

## Building a Network of Primary Ticketing Integrations

Founded in 2009 by brothers Dusty and Brando Rich, CashorTrade began as a fan-to-fan ticket marketplace centered on the principle that sellers should not profit by charging more than the original price of admission.

The company has since worked to move beyond community enforcement of that policy by developing direct integrations with the companies that issue the tickets.

“When Brando and I started CashorTrade in 2009, we knew artists and festivals would want the reassurance that the face-value of a resold ticket was strictly enforced,” Rich told TicketNews. “That could only be 100% possible if their primary ticketing company stood behind it. Now it does.”

The broader strategy presents an interesting development in the relationship between primary and secondary ticketing companies. CashorTrade remains independently owned while gaining access to original transaction records that primary ticketing providers have traditionally controlled.

That access makes it possible to enforce verified resale-price limits and automate ticket replacement without requiring every transaction to occur within the primary ticketing company’s own resale marketplace. At the same time, participation remains subject to the primary provider’s integration arrangements and individual event organizers’ decisions.

CashorTrade has not yet disclosed transaction volume or growth figures associated with the integrations, although Rich said the company expects to share more information about adoption.

He also indicated that additional major artist-tour partnerships will be announced in the coming weeks as CashorTrade continues expanding its verified resale technology to selected events.

“Our goal is to bring verified face-value resale to every corner of the primary ticketing industry, so that CashorTrade becomes an essential part of the ticketing process for fans and event holders alike,” Rich said.
