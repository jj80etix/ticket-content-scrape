# Kennedy Center Board Votes to Restore Trump’s Name After Renovation Closure

## Summary

The Trump-backed Kennedy Center board voted to close the venue's main building for a $250 million renovation and to re-inscribe President Trump's name on the exterior, after a court previously ordered his name removed. A federal judge is reviewing whether the board did adequate diligence before the renovation vote, and the venue continues to face fallout — including nearly 20 cancelled acts and a separate court order to pay $250,000 in fees to a musician whose lawsuit over the earlier name dispute was dismissed.

### Key Points
- Board approved a $250M "renewal" plan, including $11.8M in acoustical upgrades, and voted to add Trump's name back to the building's exterior.
- A federal judge previously blocked the closure temporarily, calling the initial vote "ill-informed and seemingly preordained"; Judge Cooper is now reviewing board diligence.
- The Center is appealing an earlier ruling that ordered Trump's name removed from all signage.
- Nearly 20 acts have cancelled Kennedy Center performances since the leadership takeover began.

The Trump-backed board for the John F. Kennedy Memorial Center for the Arts has voted on a resolution to close down the venue for renovations and re-add the President’s name to the building’s exterior.

Per the *[New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/13/arts/design/kennedy-center-closure-board-vote-trump.html)*, the venue’s board voted on Thursday to close the Kennedy Center’s main building, but keep the newer addition to the campus open for programming. The board was presented with a 163-page plan for a $250 million “renewal” project, which includes structural repair work, new marble floors, and $11.8 million in “acoustical improvements.” According to the resolution, the decision to close the venue was based on an analysis by an outside consulting firm, who determined it would be too costly to keep the main building open during renovations.

While the venue closure was initially announced earlier this year — and was unanimously agreed upon by the President-backed board — a Democratic lawmaker on the board sued over the decision, which caused a federal judge to temporarily block the closure, noting the initial vote was “ill-informed and seemingly preordained.”

Additionally, as a part of the resolution, the board voted to inscribe President Trump’s name on the building beneath the main signage. The inscription would read: “Restored and Renovated by President Donald J. Trump.” While Trump’s name was added to the building last year, renaming the venue “The Donald J. Trump and John F. Kennedy Memorial Center for the Arts,” a judge ordered the Center to remove Trump’s name, ruling that the Kennedy Center board violated the law when they added Trump’s name to the venue’s exterior, as well as its website and marketing materials.

Judge Christopher Cooper will review the decision to renovate the venue, considering if the trustees performed enough due diligence ahead of their vote.

This is the latest twist in the Center’s tumultuous saga; earlier this week, a [court ordered the Center to pay over $250,000 in fees](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/08/kennedy-center-ordered-drummer-250k-fees/) to jazz drummer Chuck Redd after a lawsuit failed to materialize.

When Trump and his allies tried to change the venue’s name, Redd backed-out of his annual Christmas concert, which he had held annually at the Center since 2006. Then, in March, the venue sued Redd, claiming that the musician broke a contract when he cancelled the Christmas concert in protest of the name change. However, Judge Bosier threw-out the lawsuit in June after Redd showed evidence that there was never a contract between himself and the venue.
