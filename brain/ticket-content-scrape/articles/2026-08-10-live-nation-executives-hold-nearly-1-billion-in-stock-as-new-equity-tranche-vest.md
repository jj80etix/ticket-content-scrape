# Live Nation Executives Hold Nearly $1 Billion in Stock as New Equity Tranche Vests

## Summary

Restricted stock vested Aug. 6 for four top Live Nation executives (Rapino, Berchtold, Hopmans, Rowles), pushing their combined direct Live Nation equity holdings to roughly $993 million, even as the company continues absorbing significant legal costs tied to antitrust litigation.

### Key Points
- Rapino alone directly holds about 4.2 million shares worth roughly $761 million; the four executives together hold about 5.47 million shares.
- Rapino's 2025 total compensation was about $32.6 million; Live Nation's companywide stock-comp expense hit $94.8 million in H1 2026, up from $86.1 million a year earlier.
- Q2 2026 revenue was $7.7 billion (up 9% year over year), with Ticketmaster generating $331 million in adjusted operating income.
- Live Nation has recorded a $450 million accrual tied to government investigations/litigation; a federal jury previously found the company operated as an illegal monopoly, and disputes continue over remedies and the DOJ settlement's scope.
- Shareholder support for executive pay rose to about 87% at the June annual meeting, up from about 74% a year earlier.

### Highlights
- $450 million litigation/investigation accrual disclosed alongside the stock-vesting filings.
- A federal jury verdict found Live Nation/Ticketmaster operated as an illegal monopoly; remedies and the scope of the DOJ settlement remain disputed.
- Ticketmaster posted $331 million in adjusted operating income for Q2 2026 even as the parent company absorbs the litigation accrual.

### Industry Problem
Live Nation/Ticketmaster's core ticketing business remains highly profitable even as it operates under an active antitrust monopoly finding and unresolved remedies -- illustrating the scale of legal/financial overhang competitors must factor into how they read the market's competitive-opening timeline.

### Proposed Solution
No product fix here -- this is a monitor signal. The trigger that would change posture is resolution of the remedies phase / DOJ settlement scope, which could reshape ticketing exclusivity arrangements and open venue/promoter relationships to independents like Etix.

Live Nation’s top executives saw another multimillion-dollar tranche of previously awarded stock vest last week, adding to equity holdings that now approach $1 billion across four members of the company’s senior leadership team.

[Securities filings show](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1335258/000133704126000019/xslF345X06/wk-form4_1786134551.xml) restricted shares vested on Aug. 6 for CEO Michael Rapino, President and CFO Joe Berchtold, Executive Vice President of Mergers and Acquisitions and Strategic Finance John Hopmans and General Counsel Michael Rowles.

The vesting was accompanied by automatic share withholding to cover taxes, rather than discretionary stock sales by the executives. Rapino had 16,251 shares withheld at a reported value of $181.77 per share, while Berchtold had 10,834 shares withheld. Hopmans and Rowles had 3,970 and 1,084 shares withheld, respectively.

The transactions provide another window into the scale of equity compensation at Live Nation at a time when the concert and ticketing giant remains under unusually intense legal and regulatory scrutiny.

The remaining ownership stakes are considerably larger.

Berchtold held 901,617 shares worth nearly $164 million. Rowles’ direct holdings were valued at roughly $36.5 million and Hopmans’ at about $31.7 million.

Together, the four executives directly owned approximately 5.47 million Live Nation shares valued at roughly $993 million. Much of the stock was awarded under long-term incentive programs designed to tie executive compensation to company performance and share-price targets.

The August vesting dates were scheduled portions of earlier awards, including performance-based grants made after Live Nation met specified stock-price thresholds. Similar tranches have vested throughout the year. In May, for example, Rapino had another 17,464 shares withheld for taxes following the vesting of part of an earlier award, with those shares valued at nearly $3 million.

The continued vesting comes after another lucrative compensation year for Live Nation’s senior management.

According to the company’s proxy filing, Rapino received approximately $32.6 million in total compensation for 2025. That included a $3 million salary, about $10 million in stock awards and more than $17 million in non-equity incentive compensation. Berchtold received approximately $6.2 million in total compensation, while Hopmans and Rowles each received roughly $3 million.

Rapino’s compensation package also included more than $2 million in other compensation, including costs associated with personal security and use of company aircraft.

Stock-based compensation extends well beyond the company’s top executives. Live Nation reported $94.8 million in companywide stock-based compensation expense during the first six months of 2026, up from $86.1 million during the same period last year.

Shareholders have nevertheless shown greater acceptance of the company’s executive-pay structure than they did a year ago.

At Live Nation’s June annual meeting, approximately 188 million shares were voted in favor of the company’s executive compensation proposal, compared with 28.7 million against. That represented about 87% support among shares voted for or against the proposal. A year earlier, support stood at roughly 74%, with more than 56 million shares voting against the compensation package.

The latest equity vesting also comes as Live Nation continues to report strong operating results while simultaneously absorbing significant legal costs.

The company reported second-quarter revenue of $7.7 billion, up 9% year over year, while Ticketmaster generated $331 million in adjusted operating income during the quarter. Live Nation has also recorded a $450 million accrual connected to government investigations and litigation, weighing heavily on its year-to-date operating results.

That legal backdrop remains unusually significant for the company. Live Nation and Ticketmaster continue to face the consequences of a federal antitrust case in which [a jury found the company operated as an illegal monopoly](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/04/live-nation-and-ticketmaster-operate-as-an-illegal-monopoly-jury-finds-in-landmark-antitrust-trial/), while separate disputes continue over the scope of potential remedies and the Department of Justice’s settlement with the company.

Against that backdrop, the latest filings show that Live Nation’s senior leadership remains heavily exposed to the company’s share price — with Rapino alone holding a stake worth more than three-quarters of a billion dollars and the four executives collectively approaching $1 billion in directly owned Live Nation stock.
