# Phoebe Bridgers Delivers Memories On ‘The Lost Tour’ (Live Review)

## Summary

A Pollstar live review of Phoebe Bridgers' sold-out "The Lost Tour" closing shows at Inglewood's Intuit Dome, arguing Bridgers has fully arrived as an arena-level rock star. The review highlights the tour's phone-free Yondr-pouch policy and a surprise Billie Eilish cameo.

### Key Points
- North American leg sold out instantly with added dates; European leg starts later this month.
- Bridgers cited her own nerves about performing songs about her late father as the reason for the device-free policy.
- Staging features a UFO/flying-saucer main-stage set piece and a satellite-themed b-stage.
- Guest appearances included Billie Eilish, Julien Baker, Lucy Dacus, and MUNA.

## Transcript

![PhoebeBridgers LA1 1](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/PhoebeBridgers_LA1_1.jpg)

Phoebe Bridgers performs at Intuit Dome in Inglewood, Calif., Oct. 30 (Photo by Wes Ellis / Instagram: @atwes)

Maybe Phoebe Bridgers has been a rock star all along.

The 32-year-old is rightly respected as one of the best songwriters of the last decade or so, since her 2017 full-length debut *Stranger in the Alps*. Her songs are whipsmart, catchy, deeply personal reveals into her life and her struggles, peppered with detail, but approachable and universal enough to win over listeners who, perhaps, have never had the actual experience of needing to make an international call to their sometimes-estranged father to tell him about the wonders of Japanese 7-11s and pay phones.

But intelligent, intimate songwriting can be pigeonholing. We have been trained to see our singer-songwriters — particularly woman singer-songwriters — as understated, single-spotlight-on-a-stage-with-a-stool types. Bridgers has never really been that. All three of her records — *Punisher* came in 2020 and *Lost Weekend* was released earlier this year — have featured plenty of shout-it-out moments and electric guitars and thundery drums. She’s certainly played smirkingly with the preconceived notions about what she *should* be. One of her most popular t-shirts features her name in what can only be described as “metal font.” She famously Pete Townshended a guitar on a 2021 episode of “Saturday Night Live,” causing much Discourse. Her supergroup boygenius had [tons of fun](https://news.pollstar.com/2023/05/01/some-element-of-magic-boygenius-is-having-a-helluva-good-time-conquering-the-world/) subverting the tropes of the ’90s dude-fronted band.

Let there be no doubt, though: Bridgers is a full-fledged, full-throated, full-house-drawing arena-level rock star. The proof’s in the pudding, of course: her ongoing “The Lost Tour,” [her first run promoted by Messina Touring](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/04/17/phoebe-bridgers-joins-messina-touring-group-roster/), was a near-instant sell-out, with dates added in several cities on the North American leg, which concluded this weekend with three shows at the Intuit Dome in Inglewood, California (the tour’s European leg starts later this month).

And the concert itself is, frankly, a banger, with all the bells and whistles of production and spectacle that meet the moment.

You have to take the word of attendees for it: the concerts are fully phone and device free — any social media clips you see were taken illicitly; the photo leading this story was provided by PR, the rest of the illustrations were drawn in my reporter’s notebook by my 14-year-old daughter. Having been around long enough to remember when all shows were like this — there were no smartphones to be had in those halcyon days — I found the experience refreshingly nostalgic. For the Gen Zer beside me, it was totally novel. In the lulls before the show and between the opener — frequent Bridgers collaborator Alex G — and the headliner, there was widespread murmuring in the crowd. That may sound like a small thing and it was once commonplace, but that indistinct rumble has been gone from the concert experience for so long now that its return was noticeable. It was people…talking to one another. Of course, people have conversations at concerts even with the tiny supercomputer in their pocket, but this was nearly everybody, just chatting (not everybody: at least one showgoer spent the downtime doing the *New York Times* crossword).

For all the talk since the end of the pandemic that the Great Return to live entertainment was spurred by a renewed importance to experience actual things in-person and together, it’s still rare to actually *experience* things without the filter of the phone and the app and to actually experience it *with* other people, to share thoughts and feelings and expectations and anticipations about the performance with the people who are right next to you and not the 10,000 people who follow you on Instagram.

For her part, Bridgers said the choice to dole out Yondr pouches was mostly because of her own nerves about singing the songs from *Lost Weekend*, a record that is largely about her late father, who died in early 2023.

“My dad died four years ago. He was a complicated guy,” Bridgers said during the Oct. 30 show. “When the album came out, he died for the rest of the world.”

Bridgers admitted the show would be totally different if there was a chance the moments from it would end up on TikTok. If that’s the case, she made the right choice, because the show is a mesmerizing two hours.

On the main stage looms a sort of child’s-imagination version of a flying saucer and above the b-stage hangs a satellite that evokes the early Space Age. Bridgers has used UFO imagery in the past, though really leaned into it with this tour — the underplays that preceded the tour began with a date in Roswell, New Mexico, and featured stops in cities near other famous and not-so-famous UFO sightings, though her agent — High Road Touring’s Dave Rowan — told *Pollstar* for [a September cover story](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/08/18/creative-genius-phoebe-bridgers-finds-pop-ups-planetariums-dollar-msg-show-arenas-for-the-lost-tour/) that was just a happy coincidence.

The show begins with “The Outside” and when the UFO lifts from the stage (rotating to serve as a screen to complement the larger projection curtain behind) Bridgers and her nine-piece band — befitting the Halloween weekend, all were dressed as characters from Peter Pan; Bridgers’ brother Jackson, who emceed an audience costume contest won by a woman dressed as a Yondr pouch, wore a headpiece that was a model of Captain Hook’s flagship the Jolly Roger — launched in “Motion Sickness,” a crowd pleasing rumble that is great fun to sing along with and dance to and that, of course, is about Bridgers reflecting on the toxicity and power imbalances that were present in her relationship with a much older and (at the time, at least) much more famous man.

Then it’s into a nice run of songs from the new album: “Kill Me” and “Governor’s Waltz” — both a gear-down from the rollicking “Motion Sickness” and the latter, like “Motion Sickness,” a not-terribly-thinly-veiled story about a past relationship and its end (all of this gossip is Googleable, but these are Bridgers’ stories to tell). The hat trick wraps with the very fun (and Alex G co-written) “Lost Boys,” the album’s lead single (and ostensibly inspiration for the costumes).

Bridgers’ band — an amalgam of standard rock set-up and bluegrass quartet — is game and loose. The performance has a feel of an impromptu picking party. Some players come and go seemingly casually, hopping back in for their parts, adding subtle harmony. It’s all too well thought out to be a noodley jam session, but it carries the spirit of improvisation anyway. There was even some light choreo on the danceable “Liberty Tree.”

The UFO set piece — in addition to its role of defining the space of the stage and serving as a production element — also builds and releases narrative tension as it raises and lowers.

The b-stage — it with the satellite that also served as a lighting element for designer Davey Martinez — allowed for more intimate moments. Bridgers began that portion of the show solo with her album’s title track and then played the extraordinarily mournful “Funeral” before the string players joined her for two of her most overtly country- and bluegrass-inspired tracks: “Haunted” and the sweet, redemptive “Graceland Too.” And then, poignantly, bathed in a cone of light swirling with ghostly smoke, she dedicated to her late father the intense, personal “Still Standing,” so revealing and open and devastating a story of their relationship that it can be uncomfortable in the way great art often is.

There have been lots of special guest moments, must-see (but don’t-record) moments on the tour. Hopes of a boygenius reunion for the Friday show were dashed, as Julien Baker was playing across the country (Baker and Lucy Dacus did reunite with Bridgers for the Halloween show). Still the crowd got a pair of very LA treats for what was essentially a homecoming show for the Pasadena-reared Bridgers. The crowd seethed with joy as Bridgers joined local — and increasingly, national — favorites MUNA on “Silk Chiffon.” Brother Jackson joined, as he has on most dates (though this was surely the only one with a ship on his head) for “I Can’t Wait.”

And then: Billie Eilish, in fuzzy pink pajamas, earned a volcanic response for her duet on “Savior Complex.”

“Ha ha. You don’t have your phones,” Bridgers lovingly needled the crowd.

Everything after that could be a let down for a lesser act — how do you top a Billie Eilish cameo? — but Bridgers tore through the jingly “Kyoto” and wrapped with “I Know The End” (appropriately) with the crowd enthusiastic and loud joining in the song’s distinctive screams and “The end is here!” reprise.

Back on the b-stage for the lower-key encore: “Lost Weekend” (again) and “Scott Street,” Bridgers was able to ease the heartrates and the chattering tongues of excitement before sending the crowd out into the Inglewood night, taking with them only memories, hoarse throats and tear-stained cheeks, and the knowledge that they saw a real life rock star.
