# New York AG Sues Evolutions Festival Promoters for Allegedly Scamming Ticket Buyers

## Summary

New York AG Letitia James is suing Evolutions Festival's promoters (Evolutions Festival LLC, 845 Vibrations LLC, and organizers Michael and Justin Brown) for allegedly collecting $243,158 in advance ticket/add-on sales and $21,663 in vendor fees for a 2025 festival that was canceled 10 days before it was to begin, then failing to issue refunds as required under New York's ticket-escrow law. Only 11 of 521 buyers recovered a combined $5,006, and that was via credit-card chargebacks rather than promoter refunds. The state alleges the promoters diverted advance ticket revenue to pay performers instead of escrowing it as New York's Arts and Cultural Affairs Law requires, and is seeking restitution, civil penalties of up to $5,000 per violation (1,185 alleged violations), and a $500,000 performance bond before the promoters can run another New York event.

### Key Points
- $243,158.04 in advance ticket/add-on sales from 521 buyers (1,157 tickets/add-ons); $21,662.75 in vendor fees from 28 businesses — festival never occurred.
- New York's Arts and Cultural Affairs Law requires advance ticket funds be escrowed by the next business day or secured via bond/letter of credit; the AG alleges Evolutions did neither, instead using the funds to pay performers.
- Only 11 of 521 buyers recovered money ($5,005.97 total), via card-issuer chargebacks, not promoter refunds; some chargebacks were time-barred.
- AG seeks restitution, civil penalties up to $5,000 per violation across 1,185 alleged violations, and a $500,000 performance bond before promoters can run future NY events.
- Case: *People of the State of New York v. 845 Vibrations LLC, Evolutions Festival LLC, et al.*, Index No. EF2026-3520; allegations not yet adjudicated.

### Highlights
- $243K+ in advance sales and $21.6K in vendor fees collected for an event that never happened; only ~$5K recovered, and only via chargebacks.
- NY alleges a specific statutory violation: failure to escrow advance ticket funds under the Arts and Cultural Affairs Law.
- AG is seeking a $500,000 performance bond as a forward-looking compliance/deterrence mechanism — a potential template other states could borrow.
- Festival's own FAQ reportedly stated tickets were nonrefundable even on cancellation, a term the state argues is overridden by statute.

### Industry Problem
Patrons who prepay for events have no visibility into whether a promoter is actually escrowing or protecting their funds, and "no refunds" FAQ language does not override state ticket-escrow law — leaving buyers exposed when a promoter misuses advance revenue and then can't refund at cancellation.

### Proposed Solution
The complaint itself proposes no product fix beyond legal remedies (restitution, penalties, a forward bond requirement); for an independent primary platform, the response is to ensure its own advance-sale funds are verifiably escrowed/bonded per each state's ticket-escrow statute and to make refund-on-cancellation terms unambiguous and compliant, turning statutory compliance into a trust differentiator versus promoters like Evolutions.

New York Attorney General Letitia James is suing the promoters behind Evolutions Festival, accusing them of scamming ticket buyers by collecting more than $243,000 in advance sales for a three-day music festival that was canceled just 10 days before it was scheduled to begin and allegedly failing to refund the money.

The civil enforcement action targets Evolutions Festival LLC, 845 Vibrations LLC and organizers Michael G. Brown and Justin J. Brown. The state alleges the promoters engaged in repeated fraud, deceptive business practices and violations of New York’s ticketing laws in connection with the canceled 2025 Evolutions Festival.

Evolutions was scheduled for Sept. 5-7, 2025, at the Sugar Mountain Performing Arts Center in Caneadea, New York. Tickets went on sale in March, with three-day general admission starting around $200 and VIP packages reaching roughly $500, alongside camping, parking, transportation and other add-ons.

According to the attorney general’s petition, 521 customers purchased a total of 1,157 tickets and add-ons, generating **$243,158.04 in advance sales**. Another 28 businesses paid **$21,662.75 in vendor fees** to participate in a planned vendor village. The event never happened.

On Aug. 26, 2025, Evolutions announced that the festival was being “postponed,” saying Sugar Mountain had failed to obtain necessary permits. But the venue’s owner disputed that explanation almost immediately, saying Sugar Mountain held a standing special-use permit and alleging that Evolutions instead had failed to provide necessary contracts, funding, insurance documentation and other materials as the event approached.

## Ticket Buyers Left Without Refunds

The attorney general says Evolutions never offered refunds to ticket purchasers after canceling the event.

Of the 521 buyers identified by investigators, only 11 ultimately recovered money — totaling $5,005.97 — and those refunds came from their credit card companies after customers disputed the charges rather than from the festival itself, according to the petition. Other customers allegedly attempted chargebacks only to find that their purchases were already outside their card issuers’ dispute windows.

The state investigation began after the Attorney General’s Office received 42 consumer complaints.

Several buyers cited in the filing had used buy-now-pay-later services to finance their purchases. One Brooklyn customer allegedly spent nearly $1,900 on three VIP passes, a camping pass, taxes and fees, with part of the purchase financed through Affirm and carrying an additional finance charge. Another customer who used Afterpay complained that payments were still scheduled after the festival was canceled.

The festival’s refund language had already attracted attention when the event was postponed. EDM.com reported at the time that Evolutions’ website FAQ said tickets were nonrefundable even in cases including festival cancellation, postponement or rescheduling.

New York now contends that the promoters could not simply retain the money under state law.

## State Says Ticket Money Should Have Been Protected

One of the most consequential allegations centers on what happened to the advance ticket revenue before the festival was canceled.

New York’s Arts and Cultural Affairs Law requires covered ticket distributors accepting advance payments to deposit those funds into an escrow account by the next business day. The law alternatively permits financial protections including a letter of credit, bond or indemnity arrangement designed to protect purchasers if an event is canceled or rescheduled.

The attorney general alleges Evolutions did neither.

Instead, according to documents and representations obtained during the state’s investigation, the promoters said **all advance ticket-sale funds had been used to pay festival performers whose contracts required payment before the event**.

The promoters also allegedly told investigators that refunds would only be issued if they prevailed in pending litigation against Sugar Mountain, the venue. The attorney general argues that left consumers without both their festival and the money state law was intended to protect until the performance occurred.

The filing also alleges that vendors were left unpaid. One Rochester-area food business paid $1,250 for a booth and was told after the cancellation that refund requests could be submitted and would be processed quickly. The owner had still not received a refund when she filed a complaint with the state in October 2025, according to the petition.

“Concertgoers and vendors who spend their hard-earned money in New York should know that my office has their back,” James said in announcing the case, accusing the promoters of taking advantage of consumers and small businesses.

## AG Seeks Refunds, Penalties and $500,000 Bond

The state is asking a New York Supreme Court judge in Ulster County to order restitution for affected ticket buyers and vendors and impose civil penalties of as much as **$5,000 for each of 1,185 alleged violations** of New York General Business Law.

James is also seeking an injunction that would prevent the promoters from planning, promoting or operating another arts, cultural or music event in New York unless they first obtain a **$500,000 performance bond** intended to secure compliance with the court’s order and provide funds for restitution if necessary.

The case is *People of the State of New York v. 845 Vibrations LLC, Evolutions Festival LLC, Michael G. Brown and Justin J. Brown*, Index No. EF2026-3520.

The allegations have not yet been adjudicated.
