# Prince George’s County Approves National Harbor Sphere Financing Despite Labor, Taxpayer Concerns

## Summary

The Prince George's County Council approved up to $215 million in special-obligation bond financing supporting Sphere Entertainment's proposed $1.4 billion, 6,000-seat venue at National Harbor, Maryland, clearing a key hurdle toward a targeted 2030 opening despite unresolved construction-labor disputes.

### Key Points
- Oct. 27 vote authorizes up to $215M in special-obligation bonds (CB-111-2026).
- ~$130M of financing is tax-increment financing projected to require ~$400M in debt service through 2056.
- Council added community-benefits and labor-peace agreement requirements but rejected a stronger project labor agreement.
- Project still needs final development and financing arrangements ahead of a 2030 target opening.

The Prince George’s County Council has approved a public financing package supporting Sphere Entertainment’s proposed $1.4 billion venue at National Harbor, Maryland, clearing a major legislative hurdle for the planned 6,000-seat immersive entertainment destination.

The council’s October 27 action advances legislation authorizing up to $215 million in special-obligation bonds connected to the development, according to [WTOP’s reporting on the vote](https://wtop.com/business-money/2026/10/prince-georges-county-votes-to-pass-sphere-venue-at-national-harbor/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

Although lawmakers added requirements addressing community benefits and workers’ organizing rights, they previously rejected proposals that would have required stronger union labor commitments during construction.

The action represents an important step toward Sphere Entertainment’s targeted 2030 opening, although the project still requires final development and financing arrangements.

## Public Financing Moves Forward

The approved legislation, CB-111-2026, authorizes Prince George’s County to issue up to $215 million in special-obligation bonds associated with National Harbor.

That authorization should not be confused with the amount of new financial assistance being provided directly to Sphere.

As [TicketNews previously reported](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/national-harbor-sphere-renderings-traffic-financing/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), approximately $130 million of the financing would support the venue through tax-increment financing, or TIF, which relies on designated future tax revenues to repay borrowing.

The broader $215 million authorization also includes refinancing existing National Harbor debt, establishing reserves and covering interest and other financing expenses.

The development’s incentive package additionally includes approximately $40 million for a publicly financed parking garage and $13.5 million in state funding.

Unlike general-obligation bonds, the special-obligation borrowing is intended to be repaid from specified revenues associated with the development district. Nevertheless, the arrangement commits future tax revenue to debt service and carries financial risks if projected revenues fail to materialize.

County legislative staff previously raised questions about how long it would take the government to recover its investment and the potential financial consequences of lower-than-expected revenues, construction delays or cost overruns.

During September discussions, staff estimated that approximately $130 million in TIF borrowing could entail roughly $400 million in combined principal and debt-service payments through 2056.

That distinction between the initial public financing commitment and its longer-term repayment costs remains central to evaluating the project.

## Construction Labor Dispute Remains Unresolved

The financing package also advances despite continued disagreement over labor protections for workers who will construct the venue.

During its October 5 consideration of the legislation, the County Council adopted amendments requiring a community benefits agreement and a labor peace agreement.

The community benefits agreement is intended to establish commitments involving local hiring, minority-owned businesses, workforce training, educational programs and other community investments.

The labor peace agreement addresses workers’ ability to organize and participate in collective bargaining activities, with requirements applying to developers, tenants, contractors and subcontractors as applicable.

However, these measures are distinct from the project labor agreement sought by construction unions.

A project labor agreement establishes employment terms and labor standards for contractors and building trades working on a specific construction project. Proposals that would have imposed additional construction labor requirements failed during the council’s earlier deliberations.

Labor representatives criticized that outcome, arguing that a development receiving substantial public support should provide stronger guarantees for the workers responsible for building it.

County Executive Aisha Braveboy has defended the project as an opportunity to generate employment and economic activity in Prince George’s County, particularly following the closure of Six Flags America and the Washington Commanders’ planned departure from the county.

The final financing approval does not, by itself, resolve the disagreement over construction labor arrangements.

## Economic Benefits and Community Questions

Sphere Entertainment and county officials have promoted the National Harbor development as a major potential economic engine.

An Ernst & Young analysis commissioned in connection with the project projected approximately $1.3 billion in annual economic activity in Prince George’s County once the venue reaches stabilized operations.

The analysis also estimated that the venue and associated visitor spending could support approximately 7,100 direct and indirect jobs throughout the county.

Those projections represent estimates of economic activity and employment rather than guaranteed government revenue or a calculation of the county’s net financial return.

A separate proposal, CB-132-2026, would establish a Sphere Community Impact Fund, reserving 15% of certain county tax revenues generated by the venue for communities within a six-mile radius.

Under that proposal, half the money would support community services and programs, while the other half would be allocated to infrastructure improvements. Tax revenues already committed to certain bond repayments would be excluded from the calculation.

The legislation was introduced separately from the financing authorization, and its final disposition remains to be confirmed.

Meanwhile, residents have continued raising questions about traffic congestion around National Harbor, particularly near the Woodrow Wilson Bridge, Maryland Route 210 and surrounding roads.

Recent [TicketNews coverage of updated Sphere renderings](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/national-harbor-sphere-renderings-traffic-financing/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) examined proposed pedestrian connections, parking facilities and rideshare access while noting that comprehensive traffic-impact studies remain an important part of the development process.

The planned National Harbor Sphere would be the company’s second U.S. venue and its first smaller-scale design, following the larger Sphere in Las Vegas.

Plans call for an approximately 6,000-seat facility featuring an exterior LED display, an immersive 16K-by-16K interior screen, advanced sound systems, haptic seating and environmental effects.

With the county’s financing legislation approved, attention now turns to final development agreements, the structure of the public financing, construction planning and the remaining questions surrounding community benefits and labor commitments.
