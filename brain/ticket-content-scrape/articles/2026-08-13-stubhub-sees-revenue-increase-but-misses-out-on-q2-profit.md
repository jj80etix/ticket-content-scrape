# StubHub sees revenue increase but misses out on Q2 profit

## Summary

StubHub's Q2 2026 revenue rose to $573m (up from $430.3m a year prior), driven by strong demand including the FIFA World Cup, and adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled to $105.7m. However, net income attributable to common stockholders was a $40,000 loss, narrower than last year's $75.9m loss but still not a profit. The quarter was also marked by World Cup ticket-delivery complaints and a proposed class action alleging tickets were never delivered or were cancelled last-minute.

### Key Points
- Q2 revenue: $573m (vs $430.3m in Q2 2025); GMS: $3.1bn (+34% YoY); adjusted EBITDA: $105.7m (+94% YoY).
- Net income totalled $14.6m but net income attributable to common stockholders was a $40,000 loss (vs $75.9m loss prior year).
- Free cash flow reached $309.7m (vs $9.7m prior year); cash and equivalents at $1.7bn, with $1.2bn owed to sellers.
- CEO Eric Baker credited "record-setting" World Cup demand for the results.
- A proposed class action alleges "hundreds" of World Cup ticket buyers received tickets that didn't exist, were revoked without notice, or were erased due to FIFA's "poor digital infrastructure."

### Highlights
- StubHub Q2 GMS $3.1bn (+34% YoY), adjusted EBITDA $105.7m (+94% YoY) — resale volume and profitability both climbing despite a net loss to common stockholders.

### Industry Problem
Marketplace-side revenue growth is decoupled from the buyer experience during high-demand events: the same World Cup surge that drove StubHub's best quarter also produced patron complaints about tickets that never materialized or were cancelled last-minute, exposing fulfillment/verification gaps in large-scale resale during peak demand.

### Proposed Solution
StubHub points to its FanProtect Guarantee (replacement or refund) as its existing mitigation, but the underlying inventory-verification problem persists. For Etix, this is grounding material on patron trust risk during major/high-demand onsales — worth benchmarking Etix's own delivery-guarantee and inventory-verification posture against what's now generating litigation risk for the largest resale marketplace.

**StubHub saw its revenue increase during the second quarter of 2026, which the ticket resale marketplace attributes to strong demand for live events including the FIFA World Cup. However, the company was unable to turn a profit.**

Net cash provided by operating activities was $321.9m, inclusive of net inflows of buyer receipts and seller payments, compared to $19.3m in the prior-year period. Additionally, free cash flow was $309.7m, compared to $9.7m in Q2 2025, and cash and cash equivalents totalled $1.7bn with payments due to sellers amounting to $1.2bn.

“The second quarter demonstrated strong demand for live events, highlighted by a record-setting World Cup. Our results reflect StubHub’s leadership in the resale market and our ability to deliver these experiences to fans at scale,” said Eric Baker, founder, chairman and chief executive of StubHub.

“We remain focused on our vision for long-term growth — enhancing the fan experience, democratising access to live events, and expanding our addressable market.”

This summer, the men’s FIFA World Cup 2026 was held across the US, Canada and Mexico between June 11 and July 19.

Many fans turned to resale marketplaces to try and secure tickets for the event. However, StubHub faced complaints and accusations that tickets purchased to attend the tournament were never delivered, or cancelled at the last minute.

One [proposed class action lawsuit](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/07/stubhub-sued-over-cancelled-world-cup-tickets/) said that “hundreds” of World Cup fans had purchased tickets only “to find out that those tickets either did not exist, were revoked without any forewarning, or had been erased due to what FIFA called ‘poor digital infrastructure’”.

At the time, a StubHub spokesperson said: “Our singular goal is to get fans into events, and if anything goes wrong, our FanProtect Guarantee provides replacement tickets or a full refund.”
