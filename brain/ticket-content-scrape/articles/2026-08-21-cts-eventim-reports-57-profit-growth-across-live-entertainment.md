# CTS Eventim reports 57% profit growth across live entertainment

## Summary

CTS Eventim's Live Entertainment division reported 57.1% year-on-year adjusted-EBITDA growth in H1 2026, with divisional revenue surpassing €1bn (up 18.6%), outpacing the group's overall 12.4% EBITDA growth. The company's Ticketing segment also grew, posting €473.3m in H1 revenue (+13.9%) and a 36.4% adjusted-EBITDA margin — well above group average — with management reaffirming full-year guidance.

### Key Points
- CTS Eventim's Live Entertainment adjusted EBITDA up 57.1% YoY in H1 2026; divisional revenue passed €1bn (+18.6%), the fastest-growing segment by turnover.
- Group-wide H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA: €225.4m (+12.4%); revenue up 16.9% YoY.
- Ticketing segment: €473.3m revenue (+13.9%), €172.5m adjusted EBITDA (+3.4%), 36.4% EBITDA margin — far above the Live Entertainment margin of 5.0%.
- Company reaffirms full-year 2026 guidance, citing "robust position in Europe and expanding global presence."

### Highlights
- Ticketing segment margin (36.4%) vastly outperforms Live Entertainment margin (5.0%), underscoring how much more profitable the core ticketing business is versus promotion/production.
- Full H1 2026 figures: Live Entertainment revenue €1.061bn, Ticketing revenue €473.3m.
- Guidance reaffirmed for FY2026 despite only moderate group-wide margin growth.

### Industry Problem
CTS Eventim's results illustrate the structural profitability gap between pure ticketing (high-margin, scalable) and live-entertainment promotion (thin-margin, capital-intensive) — a data point on where value concentrates in the vertically integrated giants Etix competes against indirectly.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor CTS Eventim's segment-level disclosures each reporting period as a benchmark for ticketing-segment margin health and international expansion pace; no immediate product response indicated, but useful context for pricing/packaging strategy discussions.

## Transcript

Live Entertainment revenue passed €1bn in the first half of 2026, making it the fastest growing division in the group by turnover

![Klaus-Peter Schulenberg](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Klaus-Peter-Schulenberg.png)

CEO Klaus-Peter Schulenberg © CTS Eventim

CTS Eventim’s Live Entertainment division has reported a 57.1% year-on-year increase in profitability (adjusted EBITDA) across the first half of 2026, saying a “strong international event portfolio” saw the unit collect €52.9m in earnings.

This came as the company’s Live Entertainment revenue surpassed €1bn in H1 (€1.061bn), an 18.6% rise. This puts CTS Eventim approximately on track for similar revenue numbers in the live entertainment division to 2025, when it reported €2.15bn in earnings across the entire year.

This growth rate shows the Live Entertainment arm of the German firm outgrowing the company’s overall 12.4% increase in profitability (EBITDA) in the first half of 2026, which took earnings for H1 to €225.4m.

Despite moderate growth in margins across the group, revenue rose 16.9% compared to the first half of 2025. Splitting out the H1 2026 revenue rise, the second quarter of this year saw a 13.9% year-on-year rise.

Across the whole group, an additional boost to earnings was provided by a turn to favourable exchange rate effects, the company said.

> “Out robust position in Europe and our expanding global presence underline the enduring strength of our business model”

The company also reported earnings (adjusted EBITDA) of €172.5m on ticketing, a rise of 3.4%. The firm’s profit margin on its ticketing division stood at 36.4% for the period, considerably above the company’s overall margin. In contrast, Live Entertainment’s profit margin was 5.0% for H1.

“In the first six months of 2026, revenue in the Ticketing segment rose by 13.9% compared with the prior-year period to €473.3m. Adjusted EBITDA advanced by 3.4% to €172.5m. The adjusted EBITDA margin stood at 36.4%,” CTS Eventim said.

This led the company to say it is upholding the forecast published for the 2026 financial year and that it is  
“optimistic about business in the remaining months of the year”.

Following the company’s results in March, CTS Eventim said it was [planning “more conservatively for 2026”](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/03/cts-eventim-surpasses-e3-billion-in-revenue-for-2025/), citing geopolitical factors which it said could impact consumer discretionary spending, and guided towards growth “in line with past performance”.

Announcing the 2026 H1 results yesterday (20 August), the company’s CEO Klaus-Peter Schulenberg said: “Against a backdrop of challenging economic and geopolitical conditions, CTS Eventim is achieving steady and profitable growth.”

He added the firm’s “robust position in Europe and our expanding global presence underline the enduring strength of our business model” and that this “provides the foundations for continuing on our path of long-term growth in the years ahead”.
