# Bipartisan AG Coalition Urges FCC to Strengthen ‘Know Your Customer’ Rules to Combat Illegal Automated Calls

## Summary

A bipartisan coalition of 50 state and territorial attorneys general sent a letter urging the FCC to strengthen "know your customer" (KYC) onboarding rules for voice service providers, aiming to curb illegal robocalls and scam texts, which cost U.S. consumers roughly $2 billion in 2025. The letter responds to an FCC rulemaking proposal from April 2026 and calls for deeper due diligence, uniform standards regardless of provider size, and ongoing monitoring of high-risk telecom accounts.

### Key Points
- 50-state/territory bipartisan AG coalition (led by IL, IN, NJ, NC, OH, PA) submitted the letter July 27, 2026.
- Responds to the FCC's April 30, 2026 Further Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on KYC obligations for originating voice providers.
- Cites 29.6 billion scam robocalls/texts in 2025, causing ~$2B in consumer losses.
- Recommends: deeper due-diligence beyond identity verification, uniform onboarding standards across provider sizes, and heightened ongoing monitoring of high-risk accounts.
- General telecom/consumer-protection regulatory matter, not ticketing-specific — no direct connection found to the tracked research questions.

On July 27, 2026, a bipartisan coalition of 50 state and territorial attorneys general (AGs) submitted a [letter](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/letters/fcc-know-your-customer-kyc-rules-letter-2026_0.pdf) to the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) urging the FCC to adopt stronger “know your customer” (KYC) requirements for originating voice service providers. The coalition’s letter targets what the AGs view as inadequate due diligence on the part of providers, which may allow scammers to access U.S. telephone networks and flood consumers with illegal automated calls.

### The FCC’s Rulemaking and the Coalition’s Response

The FCC’s Further Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, issued on April 30, 2026, sought comment on proposals to expand and formalize KYC obligations. The coalition supports robust changes, noting that Americans received more than 29.6 billion scam automated calls and texts in 2025, which resulted in nearly $2 billion in consumer losses. Illegal automated calls require access to an originating provider’s network, and the AGs argue that current KYC requirements do not go far enough to prevent bad actors from gaining, and maintaining, that access.

The bipartisan coalition is led by the AGs of Illinois, Indiana, New Jersey, North Carolina, Ohio, and Pennsylvania.

### Key Recommendations

The coalition’s letter urges the FCC to adopt several enhanced KYC measures:

- *Enhance due diligence beyond identity verification.* The coalition contends that confirming a customer’s name and legal existence is insufficient. Providers should also be obligated to assess the nature of a prospective or existing customer’s operations, its track record, how it intends to use network access, and whether its activities comply with federal and state telecommunications and consumer protection laws.
- *Uniform standards regardless of provider size.* The AGs emphasize that bad actors may route more illegal traffic through smaller carriers if those entities face less regulatory scrutiny. Applying consistent onboarding obligations across the provider landscape would close this gap and eliminate an avenue for scammers to exploit.
- *Heightened ongoing monitoring for high-risk accounts.* While baseline vetting should apply to all customers, the coalition advocates for sustained, enhanced oversight of accounts exhibiting risk indicators to detect and address misuse after the initial onboarding stage.

### Why It Matters

The coalition’s push for enhanced KYC obligations signals continued pressure on the FCC to impose more rigorous gatekeeping requirements, which could have significant compliance implications for carriers of all sizes. Telecommunication companies and their compliance teams should monitor the FCC’s response to this letter and evaluate their existing customer vetting procedures in light of the coalition’s recommendations.
