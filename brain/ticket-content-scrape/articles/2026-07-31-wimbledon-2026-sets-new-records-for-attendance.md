# Wimbledon 2026 sets new records for attendance

## Summary

Wimbledon 2026 drew a record 550,151 attendees across its 139th staging, aided by the first rain-free Championships since 2019, plus a record 14,000 visitors (+42% YoY) at the pop-up "Wimbledon Court" activation in New York's Central Park.

### Key Points
- 550,151 total guests, with daily attendance records set on days 7, 12, and 14; strong walk-up/on-the-day Queue sales.
- New York pop-up "Wimbledon Court" in Central Park drew 14,000+ visitors over four days, up 42% from last year's equivalent event.
- The Wimbledon Foundation's charity ticket-resale scheme (with Barclays) raised £278,000 from 600+ guests representing 116 charity partners.
- On-court results: Jannik Sinner and Linda Noskova won the singles titles; full doubles/wheelchair results also reported.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**This year’s Wimbledon Championships has set a new attendance record.**

The 139th staging of The Championships welcomed 550,151 guests through the gates, with daily records set on days seven, 12 and 14. The popularity of the Queue was bolstered by the first rain-free Championships since 2019, with tens of thousands of guests purchasing tickets on the day.

The highest number of internationals guests for the British tennis Grand Slam were from the US, Ireland, Italy and Germany.

Additionally, international Wimbledon fans were able to experience a taste of the Championships in New York. [The Wimbledon Court in Central Park](https://www.thestadiumbusiness.com/2026/06/03/all-england-club-to-host-wimbledon-pop-up-court-in-central-park/) welcomed over 14,000 visitors, with the four-day event featuring a transformed Wollman Rink which became a grass tennis court. The experience also featured an exhibition match, The Wimbledon Court Invitational, with star players Andre Agassi, Caroline Wozniacki, James Blake and Eugenie Bouchard.

Events also included free coaching clinics and a wheelchair court session.

The 14,000 visitor mark was a record-breaking 42% increase vs The Hill experience in New York last year.

**Giving back**

The first Thursday of the Championships also welcomed a surprise visit from Her Royal Highness The Princess of Wales, who spent time meeting and scanning tickets of those in the Queue.

Meanwhile, the Wimbledon Foundation, which is the official charity of the All England Club and The Championships, welcomed more than 600 guests from 116 charity partners and received £278,000 (€325,000/$373,000) through the proceeds of the ticket resale scheme, supported by Barclays.

Elsewhere, the Wimbledon Lawn Tennis Museum welcomed more than 35,000 guests during the fortnight the Grand Slam was held.

Linda Noskova beat fellow Czech player Karolina Muchova to secure the women’s singles final, while Jannik Sinner overpowered Alexander Zverev to win back-to-back Wimbledon men’s singles titles.

The ladies’ wheelchair singles final was won by Japan’s Yui Kamiji, and the men’s was secured by fellow Japanese player Tokito Oda.

Great Britain’s Alfie Hewett and Gordon Reid won the Wimbledon men’s wheelchair doubles title for the seventh time, and Japan’s Kamiji and China’s Zhu Zhenzhen secured the wheelchair women’s doubles title.

China’s Hanyu Guo and France’s Kristina Mladenovic won the women’s doubles, and Great Britain’s Henry Patten and Finland’s Harri Heliovaara secured the men’s doubles.

The mixed doubles event was won by Latvia’s Jelena Ostapenko and El Salvador’s Marcelo Arevalo, while the wheelchair quad doubles title was taken by Israel’s Guy Sasson and The Netherlands’ Niels Vink.
