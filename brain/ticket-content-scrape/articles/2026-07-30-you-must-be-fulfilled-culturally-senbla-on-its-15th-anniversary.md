# ‘You must be fulfilled culturally’: Senbla on its 15th anniversary

## Summary

UK promoter Senbla marks its 15th anniversary, with CEO Ollie Rosenblatt reflecting on the company's evolution from bebop jazz promotion to orchestral programming, film-in-concert franchises (Harry Potter, The Godfather), and experiential IP work. He credits Senbla's 2019 acquisition by Sony Masterworks with broadening its access to performing-arts IP, and points to the Mundo-Pixar Experience at London's Wembley Park as a sign of continued diversification.

### Key Points
- Senbla began in bebop jazz promotion before expanding into orchestral and film-in-concert programming.
- A chance New York meeting led to Senbla securing rights to stage Harry Potter and The Godfather in concert.
- Sony Masterworks acquired a stake in Senbla in 2019, giving it access to PlayStation and Sony Pictures IP.
- Senbla is co-promoting The Mundo-Pixar Experience at London's Wembley Park.
- Rosenblatt attributes the company's longevity to prioritizing quality, culturally "fulfilling" work over chasing trends amid increasingly experimental audiences.

## ‘You must be fulfilled culturally’: Senbla on its 15th anniversary

The UK promoter's CEO Ollie Rosenblatt tells IQ that despite the company traversing a range of art forms and IPs, quality work 'always prevails'

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/07/Robbie-Williams-Bath-Royal-Crescent-drone-by-Ben-Foster-03-scaled.jpg.webp)

Robbie Williams Bath Royal Crescent © Ben Foster

As UK promoter Senbla marks its 15th anniversary, its CEO Ollie Rosenblatt says the role of the “producer-promoter” has grown in importance, with increasingly “experimental” audiences requiring promoters to leverage their passion for live IP more than ever.

Despite a wide range of genres and art forms under his belt, from beginnings promoting bebop jazz to becoming a major promoter in orchestral and experiential spaces, among others, Rosenblatt says at a time of unmatched variety in live entertainment, promoters must be “fulfilled culturally about what you are doing”.

Looking back at the company’s youth, the CEO says the jazz genre was “one of the hardest art forms you could promote in at the time”, and he credits this fact with teaching him to strive for a “critical understandings” of genre that has informed a decade and a half of promoting.

“The challenge at the time was, how do I compete in a business which feels so set in its ways with people doing business with specific people, with companies, historic, promoters involved with certain artists and shows,” he recalls.

After a gig at radio station Jazz FM linked Rosenblatt into promoting small shows, a progression in to promoting classical artists soon put orchestral events on Senbla’s radar, paving the way for this to become a specialty.

> “I always believe quality work prevails”

This progression s brought some standout moments, as Rosenblatt recalls: “We did a show with Don Black called *A Life in Song*, which we created with a concept, and it was about a basic story of him or another songwriter on stage, talking about their life and their career, and all the stories behind the songs, and then orchestra and guest artists would sing all those songs, and the narrative of the interview would help link all the songs together.

“It was, filmed by the BBC, and we did it with Tim Rice, with Burt Bacharach, with Quincy Jones as well.”

He recalls this came “at the early stages of orchestras doing a lot of film work with different composers” in a live setting, and when “there was no orchestras really touring the UK”. As a result, ticket prices were typically high for orchestral shows, and he admits the tour “did not do as well as we had hoped”.

But a chance encounter in New York a month later led to Rosenblatt to be offered the chance to stage Harry Potter and The Godfather film concerts.

“I was in New York, on one of my trips to go and see agents and one said to me, ‘you should go and see and meet our new agent who is working on some symphony stuff. \[…\] I said we know about touring orchestras, and we know about film concerts and was asked, ‘do you want to do *The Godfather* and *Harry Potter* in concert?'”, he recalls, saying the deal was struck then and there in a New York office.

“In relative terms, at the time it was a big investment, but in terms of small investments into our business after losing the money on the tour previously, it then turned into moving this world of films and concerts, which has now become as very big part of our business,” Rosenblatt says.

Asked how audience trends have evolved in recent years, Rosenblatt reflects that fans are “more experimental than they’ve ever been in terms of what they’ll buy a ticket for”.

> “You’ve got to be enriched and fulfilled culturally about what you are doing”

But with a wider range of art also more accessible than ever, the key, he says, is “being able to explain things in a much more easier way about what something might be.”

“There was always the bigger picture, but the trickiest part is if you’ve got a new concept in the show, how do  
you explain it in a sentence? How do you tell an audience what this is, in a sentence, to get them to buy?,” he ponders.

But challenges presented by novelty and variety in larger genres and venues come full circle for Rosenblatt, resonating with those early lessons promoting somewhat niche genres in the jazz landscape, he says, only on a larger scale.

“When I was trying to sell bebop jazz shows, what I was saying to people is what was going to try and get them interested and excite them from a marketing point of view. What am I going to try? How am I going to position your work?,” he says.

In 2019 Senbla was [acquired by Sony Masterworks](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2019/08/sony-music-expands-live-offering-senbla-stake/), which brought close collaboration with other arms of the corporation, including Playstation and Sony Pictures.

While he says “being a part of a broader corporate environment is obviously it can can be a shock to the system for some people”, he celebrates that the move brought Senbla “a much broader playing field, in terms of performing arts IP”.

“In the age of the catalogue acquisition and focus on on that. I think there’s huge opportunity in that part of the business, in looking at how we’re looking at IP and catalogue,” he adds.

This brings Rosenblatt to look ahead, naming Senbla’s ongoing work co-promoting the experiential IP *The Mundo-Pixar Experience* at London’s Wembley Park as an expedient reminder for Senbla “not to be shut off to looking at new areas to try and develop and grow”.

This leads the CEO to predict more strong years for the promoter, saying: “If it’s done sensibly in the right way, and we keep delivering a high service, to our clients, our artists, our IP holders, and keep producing and promoting quality work, I always believe quality prevails.”
