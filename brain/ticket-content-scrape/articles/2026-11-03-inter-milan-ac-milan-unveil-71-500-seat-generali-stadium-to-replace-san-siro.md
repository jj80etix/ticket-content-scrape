# Inter Milan, AC Milan Unveil 71,500-Seat Generali Stadium to Replace San Siro

## Summary

Inter Milan and AC Milan have unveiled design plans for the 71,500-seat Generali Stadium, a Foster + Partners/MANICA-designed venue that will replace their shared San Siro home by the 2031-32 season, pending regulatory approval. Insurer Generali has secured long-term naming rights in one of the largest stadium sponsorship deals in global sports.

### Key Points
- Design features a continuous 360-degree bowl, steep "Curva" ends up to 37 degrees, and premium hospitality amenities meant to boost matchday and non-matchday revenue.
- Project is backed by Inter owner Oaktree (Brookfield credit platform) and AC Milan owner RedBird Capital; the clubs acquired San Siro and surrounding land for ~€197M in 2025.
- Construction is targeted to begin in 2028; both clubs continue playing at San Siro in the interim.
- The surrounding 281,000-sq-m district is planned to host concerts, cultural programming and other non-matchday events.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Generali-Stadium-.webp)

Inter Milan and AC Milan have unveiled plans for Generali Stadium, a 71,500-seat venue that will replace their shared San Siro home and serve as the centerpiece of a new sports and entertainment district in Milan. The clubs are targeting the 2031-32 season for its opening, subject to regulatory approvals.

The two Serie A teams [introduced the stadium design](https://www.inter.it/en/news/inter-milan-generali-stadium-new-milano-stadium?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) at Castello Sforzesco, with architecture firms Foster + Partners and MANICA leading the project. Their plans feature a distinctive X-shaped structural frame, double-curved roof and diamond-shaped entrances that open onto concourses overlooking the city.

Inside, two tiers of seating will form a continuous, 360-degree bowl designed to improve sightlines and bring spectators closer to the field. The steep stands behind each goal, known as the Curvas, will rise at angles of up to 37 degrees, with acoustic features intended to amplify the atmosphere associated with San Siro.

The plans also include enhanced accessibility, premium hospitality and other amenities intended to generate more revenue on matchdays. Those upgrades are a significant part of the clubs’ business case for moving into a new facility after years of pursuing alternatives.

The stadium’s name is already secured. Italian insurer Generali has signed a long-term naming-rights agreement and will become the official insurance partner of both clubs. The companies describe the sponsorship as one of the largest stadium naming deals in global sports, although they have not disclosed its financial terms.

Generali Chairman Andrea Sironi and Group CEO Philippe Donnet said the partnership reflects ambitions beyond stadium signage.

“Our role goes far beyond simply lending our name to the stadium,” they said, adding that the company hopes to be part of the events and experiences it hosts.

The project is backed by Inter owner Oaktree, part of Brookfield’s credit platform, and AC Milan owner RedBird Capital Partners. The clubs jointly acquired San Siro and surrounding property for approximately €197 million in 2025, clearing a significant step toward building a replacement on adjacent land.

Beyond soccer, Generali Stadium is intended to anchor a 281,000-square-meter development incorporating public spaces, landscaped areas, shops and leisure destinations. The clubs say the surrounding district will be active throughout the year, opening opportunities for concerts, cultural programming and other non-matchday events. The design also incorporates natural ventilation and photovoltaic technology.

For the clubs, those activities could provide revenue well beyond ticket sales for their home games, including hospitality, visitor experiences and event rentals. The venue could also help Milan compete for major international events as Italy prepares to co-host the 2032 European Championship with Turkey, although no tournament designation for the facility has been confirmed.

Both clubs will continue playing at San Siro during construction. Work on Generali Stadium is currently scheduled to begin in 2028, and the existing venue would be largely demolished after the replacement opens. The timetable remains dependent on completion of the planning and approval process.
