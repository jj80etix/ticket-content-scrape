# Seven Pennsylvania DAs Now Pursuing Vivid Seats Hidden-Fee Lawsuit

## Summary

Six additional Pennsylvania district attorneys have joined the consumer-protection lawsuit against Vivid Seats over allegedly deceptive "drip pricing," expanding the prosecutorial coalition to seven eastern Pennsylvania counties. The amended complaint alleges Vivid Seats used misleading fee descriptions and dark-pattern website design to obscure final ticket costs.

### Key Points
- Bucks, Montgomery, Lackawanna, Pike, Wyoming, and Luzerne counties joined Monroe County's original suit.
- Amended complaint filed under a stipulation signed Aug. 24, publicly announced by Bucks County Sept. 9.
- Allegations center on drip pricing, misleading fee descriptions, and "dark pattern" checkout design under PA's Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law.
- Vivid Seats has not admitted to the allegations under the stipulation.

### Highlights
- Prosecutorial coalition now spans seven Pennsylvania counties, escalating from a single-county case.
- Core legal theory: drip pricing and dark-pattern UI as deceptive/unfair trade practices under state consumer-protection law.
- Filed in Monroe County Court of Common Pleas; pursued in the Commonwealth's name.

### Industry Problem
State-level "junk fee" enforcement against resale/ticketing platforms is broadening beyond single-jurisdiction actions into multi-county prosecutorial coalitions, raising the compliance stakes for any platform whose fee presentation or checkout flow could be characterized as obscuring total cost.

### Proposed Solution
No remedy specified in the article beyond the litigation itself; for a primary platform, this reinforces the value of transparent, all-in pricing and clear fee disclosure at checkout as both a compliance hedge and a differentiator against resale platforms facing this scrutiny.

Six additional Pennsylvania district attorneys have joined a consumer protection lawsuit accusing Vivid Seats of deceptive ticket pricing practices, substantially expanding the prosecutorial coalition behind a case first brought by Monroe County last year.

District attorneys from Bucks, Montgomery, Lackawanna, Pike, Wyoming and Luzerne counties have joined Monroe County District Attorney Michael Mancuso in the case, according to an amended complaint filed in the Monroe County Court of Common Pleas (embedded a end of article). The lawsuit is now being pursued in the name of the Commonwealth by district attorneys representing seven eastern Pennsylvania counties.

The expansion was formally agreed to Aug. 24, when attorneys for both the Commonwealth and Vivid Seats signed a stipulation allowing the amended complaint to be docketed and become the operative complaint in the case. The stipulation itself does not contain an admission by Vivid Seats to the allegations. Bucks County publicly announced the expanded coalition on Sept. 9.

The underlying lawsuit alleges that Vivid Seats violated Pennsylvania’s Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law through “drip pricing,” misleading descriptions of its fees and website design that prosecutors characterize as digital “dark patterns” intended to obscure the final cost of tickets.

“When hardworking Pennsylvanians budget their money to take their families to a concert, game, or theater performance, affordability and honest pricing matter,” Bucks County District Attorney [Joe Khan said in announcing his office’s participation](https://buckscounty.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/1624). “Hiding massive fees until the final second isn’t just bad business. It is deceptive, it is illegal under Pennsylvania law.”

The case was originally announced by Mancuso’s office in May 2025. Prosecutors allege that Vivid Seats would show consumers a ticket price that excluded mandatory fees, require them to proceed through several checkout screens while displaying a countdown timer, and reveal the substantially higher total only near the end of the transaction.

One example preserved in the amended complaint shows two general-admission tickets to the 2025 Elements Music and Arts Festival advertised for $332 each. Prosecutors allege that Vivid later added a $133.20-per-ticket service charge at the final payment stage, increasing the ticket cost by more than 40%. The complaint also challenges how Vivid described its service and electronic-transfer fees and alleges that consumers were not adequately told how those charges were calculated.

## Vivid Seats Has Since Switched to All-In Pricing

There is an important timing distinction between the conduct described in the lawsuit and the way Vivid Seats currently displays ticket prices.

Vivid Seats announced on May 12, 2025 that it had switched its website and app to all-in pricing nationwide, displaying mandatory fees in the ticket price from the beginning of the shopping process. The change came the same day the [Federal Trade Commission’s Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/05/ftc-clarifies-impending-all-in-pricing-rules-with-faq/) took effect, requiring live-event ticket sellers and marketplaces to prominently display the total price including mandatory fees upfront.

Vivid’s current customer-support materials likewise state that the marketplace uses all-in pricing and that service and delivery charges are included in prices displayed to shoppers.

The Monroe County DA announced the original lawsuit one day after that change. While the amended complaint continues to describe Vivid’s alleged drip-pricing practices in the present tense, a TicketNews review did not identify a transaction example alleging that Vivid continued the challenged practice after its May 2025 switch to all-in pricing. The core pricing allegations in the new complaint largely mirror those Monroe County made when it originally announced the case.

That does not eliminate the potential stakes of the lawsuit. The Commonwealth is seeking restitution for Pennsylvania residents who paid Vivid Seats service or transfer fees, as well as consumers from elsewhere who paid those fees when purchasing tickets to Pennsylvania events. Prosecutors are also seeking civil penalties, attorneys’ fees and a permanent injunction against the practices alleged in the complaint.

Vivid Seats’ prior pricing model had already generated legal scrutiny elsewhere. [TicketNews covered a 2024 class action](https://www.ticketnews.com/2024/02/28/vivid-seats-faces-ny-lawsuit-over-all-in-ticket-pricing-law/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) accusing the marketplace of violating New York’s all-in pricing law, as well as an earlier California lawsuit alleging that Vivid used hidden fees and “bait-and-switch” ticket pricing.

TicketNews contacted Vivid Seats Thursday afternoon seeking comment on the expanded Pennsylvania case, the underlying allegations and whether the company believes its adoption of all-in pricing affects the prosecutors’ request for injunctive relief. The company had not responded as of publication.

The case is *Commonwealth of Pennsylvania v. Vivid Seats, Inc. and Vivid Seats LLC*, No. 3090 CV 2025, in the Court of Common Pleas of Monroe County.
