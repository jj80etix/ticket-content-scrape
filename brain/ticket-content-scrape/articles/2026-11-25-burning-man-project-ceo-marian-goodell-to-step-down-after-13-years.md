# Burning Man Project CEO Marian Goodell To Step Down After 13 Years

## Summary

Burning Man Project CEO Marian Goodell will step down once a successor is found, expected early next year, though she'll remain on the board and assist onboarding. Goodell, CEO since 2013 and a co-founder of the nonprofit, says the organization is now financially stable with roughly 64,000 attendees this year, and plans to cap future attendance at 60,000 in 2027 amid rising fuel costs and falling tourism.

### Key Points
- Goodell will step down as CEO once a successor is hired, targeted for early next year; she stays on the board.
- A search firm has been engaged; candidates from the global Burning Man community are welcomed.
- 2026 attendance was roughly 64,000; the organization plans to cap 2027 attendance at 60,000.
- Goodell cites financial stability as a factor in the timing of her exit, following a multi-year succession-planning process started around 2021-2022.

Burning Man Project, the nonprofit organization that organizes the annual gathering in Nevada’s Black Rock Desert, is searching for a new CEO. Marion Goodell told *[Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/burning-man-ceo-marian-goodell-exit-interview/)* she will step down as Burning Man Project CEO as soon as they find a successor but will remain on the board. She expects someone to be hired “at the beginning of the year” and will assist with the onboarding process.

The organization hired a search firm to help find its new leader and expects candidates of all kinds, including those from the global Burning Man community. Anyone interested in the role can throw their hat in the ring at [executivesearch@burningman.org](mailto:executivesearch@burningman.org).

Goodell attended her first Burning Man, an annual event where thousands of participants build a temporary city centered around art, in 1995 and helped found the nonprofit two years later. She became CEO in 2013 and has guided the organization through various challenges, including the COVID-19 pandemic and financial struggles.

She said that Burning Man Project is now financially stable and that an estimated 64,000 people attended this year’s event at Black Rock City. Goodell considers that attendance a success, but with rising fuel prices and drops in tourism, she said the organization plans to cap the event at 60,000 in 2027.

When *Billboard* asked why she was leaving her post, Goodell said it was time. “It’s really easy for me to see that the work I’ve done is taking hold. I’ve been working on this \[exit\] since about 2022, 2021, right at the tail end of the pandemic when we had time on our hands, and I really looked at what we were doing inside the organization to actually prepare us for it,” she said. “… For me, it was more that during the pandemic we had the time to reflect and to create the organization in a way that makes it feel like we’re on the road to success. That the mission is actually not just Black Rock City. That people are getting it. And that’s what we’re seeing.”

Goodell added that she feels good about her work with Burning Man Project: “I don’t feel anything is left undone. I’m going to continue leading, inspiring the staff and listening to the community and building foundations for the future… to continue operating in a way that helps us all keep asking questions and listening to ideas from the community, particularly in some of the operations for the event. How do you run the gate better? How do you have less of a wait? Do you design the city differently? Do you change the date of the event? All of those are setting foundations of the future.
