# Gasometer Stockholm Announces Opening Performance

## Summary

Composer Jacob Mühlrad and creative director Alexander Wessely will create the opening performances for Gasometer, a new landmark venue opening inside Stockholm's historic 1899 gas holder in March 2027. The venue features two performance spaces — a 1,750-2,300 capacity "Theatre" and a 1,000-capacity "Dome" — and will host concerts, opera and other large-format cultural events, with a four-night opening program running March 17-20.

### Key Points
- Gasometer opens March 17, 2027, in Stockholm's historic Gasworks district (1899 gas holder building).
- Two spaces: the Theatre (1,750 seated/2,300 standing) and the Dome (up to 1,000, beneath the gasholder's dome).
- Opening program: four nights, four distinct acts composed/directed by Jacob Mühlrad and Alexander Wessely.
- Operated by Miramis; artist lineup beyond the opening nights not yet announced.

Composer Jacob Mühlrad and artist and creative director Alexander Wessely will create and direct the opening performances for Gasometer, a new landmark venue scheduled to open in the Swedish capital of Stockholm in March next year.

Wessely has created visual worlds for artists including The Weeknd, Rihanna, FKA twigs and Arca, with work spanning Coachella, Madison Square Garden, Sphere in Las Vegas and the 2025 Nobel Prize Banquet.

Mühlrad’s previous work includes commissions by BBC Proms and Radio France as well as the 2025 Nobel Prize Banquet. His music has been performed at the Royal Albert Hall in London as well as Stockholm Concert Hall, with recordings on Deutsche Grammophon and Warner Classics. Outside classical music, Mühlrad has collaborated with artists such as Swedish House Mafia and 070 Shake.

Stockholm’s Gasometer aims to bring together architecture, live entertainment and the latest visual and audio technology, once it opens March 17, 2027. The opening program runs across four consecutive nights until March 20, and is built around four separate acts, composed and designed by Mühlrad and Wessely, and featuring other performers.

The lineup of artists after that date has yet to be announced.

The venue is being built inside the historic 1899 gas holder designed by Swedish architect Ferdinand Boberg, located in the Gasworks district of Stockholm, and features two distinct spaces for concerts, opera, performing arts, live productions, galas, conferences, exhibitions and other large cultural and event formats.

The so-called Theatre is Gasometer’s main performance space, pillar-free, and able to accommodate 1,750 guests in a theater format, and up to 2,300 on a standing floor. Audiences are spread across four levels, with unobstructed sightlines.

The so-called Dome is at the top of the building, directly beneath the gasholder’s dome. It has a ceiling height of 17 meters and can accommodate up to 1,000 guests, depending on the configuration.
