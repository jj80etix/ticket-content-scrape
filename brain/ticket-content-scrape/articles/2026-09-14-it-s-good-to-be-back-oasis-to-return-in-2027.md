## Transcript

## It’s good to be back: Oasis to return in 2027

The band will hit the road two years after their Live '25 reunion, with 38 dates across the UK, Ireland, Europe, and the US

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/09/bb8d0535-70c7-4a16-b1c1-f1ff13c86eed-scaled.jpeg.webp)

Oasis Live '27 © Joshua Halling

Oasis will return to live performance with a 38 date world stadium tour sure to be one of the highest grossing events of the year, returning two years after the band broke their 16-year hiatus with their blockbuster *Live ’25*, which became biggest concert launch in British and Irish history.

The Live Nation/SJM-promoted run begins in late May in Glasgow, before a blockbuster homecoming Manchester residency. The band will then hit mainland Europe as well as six US dates, before returning to the UK and Ireland.

The UK and Ireland leg includes five shows at Glasgow’s Celtic Park, followed by a mammoth eleven night stand at Manchester’s Etihad Stadium between 4-26 June, and four nights between 11-19 September at Knebworth Park in Hertfordshire, where the band recorded a famous live album and documentary in 1996.

Ireland’s Slane Castle in Meath is also pegged for two performances on 4 and 5 September.

In between, European stadiums include the Allianz Arena in Munich in early July (75,000-cap), quickly followed by two nights at Estadio Olimpic Lluis Companys in Barcelona (up to 68,000), two nights at the Stadio Olimpico in Rome (up to 80,000), two nights at the Johan Cruyff Arena in Amsterdam (71,000), and two nights at the Stade de France between 22-24 July.

In North America, the band will visit Gillette Stadium near Boston and Las Vegas’ Allegiant Stadium for a combined six concerts in August.

The announcement comes after weeks of teasing from the Gallagher brothers, including a rare joint public appearance in Venice for the premier of a documentary about their 2025 world tour. Late last week, a drone display in their home city of Manchester teased today’s launch date.

The tour will look to capitalise on the phenomenal level of demand for tickets seen across last year’s blockbuster *Live ’25* reunion tour. The 2025 run was the biggest-ever concert launch in UK and Irish history, as more than 10 million fans from 158 countries competed to buy the 1.4m available tickets. No venues from 2025 are repeated for the new tour.

The on-sale for the run met with controversy after the use of [dynamic pricing](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/09/dynamic-pricing-beyond-the-controversy/) at its on-sale sparked the UK and Irish regulators to launch an investigation into the practice amid a wave of criticism from fans.

In an effort to avoid the issues of the previous on-sale, fans are being required to pre-register for a chance to buy a ticket for the 2027 tour. Prices for the tour are also outlined in advance, ranging between £71.50 (€83.50) and £266.60 (€311), including fees.

The sold-out, 41-date *Live ’25* tour began in the UK on 4 July and went on to visit stadiums in North America, Australia, Asia, and South America.

The group played to a combined audience of 2.6 million over the course of the run, which saw the Gallagher brothers perform on stage together for the first time in 16 years, wrapping up on 23 November at Estadio MorumBIS São Paulo, Brazil.

During the 2025 dates, Liam Gallagher appeared to tease a further run of shows, telling fans at Wembley Stadium: “See you next year.”

The commercial success of last year’s blockbuster comeback also extended across merchandise and licensing avenues, with Warner Music Group (WMG)’s artist services division reporting a 67.7% revenue rise to $327 million (€284m) on the back of the tour, which it said was “primarily due to higher merchandising revenue from the company’s partnership with Oasis and higher concert promotion revenue”.
