# See Tickets Oversells Glastonbury 2027 Coach Packages, Forces Booking Changes

## Summary

See Tickets oversold Glastonbury 2027 coach-and-ticket packages due to an "administrative error," forcing some customers to either accept conversion to general-admission-only (with a refund of the coach fare) or request to keep their coach seat by an Oct. 31 deadline — an unusual exception to Glastonbury's normally strict no-conversion coach policy. The error follows record-breaking, minutes-long sellouts for 2027 admission (record £408 price) and coach packages.

### Key Points
- Error traced to an administrative mistake on certain coach departures; See Tickets is converting affected bookings to GA and refunding the coach fare (£26–£185) unless the customer opts to keep their coach seat by Oct. 31.
- This conversion directly contradicts Glastonbury's published coach-ticket conditions, which normally prohibit converting coach packages to GA under any circumstances — a policy exception created by the vendor's own error.
- Neither the number of affected customers nor which departures were oversold has been disclosed; unclear how See Tickets will resolve capacity if more customers opt to keep coach seats than there is room for.
- Comes weeks after Glastonbury's 2027 coach packages sold out in under 30 minutes (Oct. 1) and GA sold out in ~42 minutes (Oct. 4), at a record £408 admission price (up £29.50 from 2025).
- See Tickets remains Glastonbury's sole authorized ticket seller; admission tickets are personalized/non-transferable and unaffected by this coach-specific issue.

### Highlights
- A primary-ticketing vendor's own administrative error forced it to break its client's stated no-conversion coach policy, with affected customers given until Oct. 31 to choose between a refund or holding their original booking — a visible operational failure for a Tier-1 competitor during a high-demand onsale cycle.

### Industry Problem
High-demand onsales with layered add-on inventory (coach + ticket bundles) create operational risk: an inventory-management error on a secondary product (coach seats) forced the vendor to override its own client's ticketing policy and absorb reputational and refund costs, illustrating the patron-facing cost of onsale/inventory errors at scale.

### Proposed Solution
The item itself describes only a reactive fix (refund + opt-in retention); for Etix, the signal is to harden inventory reconciliation for bundled/add-on products (transport, parking, merchandise) during high-demand onsales so oversell errors on secondary inventory don't force policy exceptions or erode client trust — directly relevant to Etix's own bundled-product and onsale-scalability handling.

See Tickets has oversold coach transportation packages for Glastonbury Festival 2027, leaving some customers facing changes to their travel arrangements after the ticketing company sold more seats on certain departures than were available.

Affected customers will retain their festival admission, but See Tickets is offering to convert their coach-and-ticket packages into standard general admission bookings and refund the transportation portion of their purchases. The arrangement represents an unusual exception to Glastonbury’s normally strict ticketing policies, which prohibit converting coach packages into general admission.

The problem resulted from an administrative error involving certain coach departures, according to an email See Tickets sent to customers and [reported by the BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cm5ynqkgwr69o?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) on Wednesday, Oct. 21.

Customers affected by the overselling have until Oct. 31 to indicate that they wish to retain their coach transportation. Those who do not opt in will automatically have their reservations converted to general admission tickets, with the coach fare refunded to the payment card originally used for the booking.

The refunds cover the transportation component rather than the festival admission itself. Coach fares for the 2027 festival range from £26 to £185, depending on the departure location and whether customers booked one-way or round-trip travel.

See Tickets has assured customers that their £100 festival ticket deposits remain valid and that they will be able to complete payment of the remaining admission balance during the usual April 2027 payment window.

The company apologized to affected customers but declined to comment further when contacted by the BBC. The broadcaster reported that Glastonbury Festival organizers were not responsible for the error.

Neither the number of affected customers nor the specific oversold departure locations has been publicly disclosed. It also remains unclear how See Tickets will accommodate customers who request to retain coach travel if their combined requests exceed the available seats.

The situation is particularly notable because Glastonbury’s published ticketing policies explicitly prohibit the type of conversion See Tickets is now offering.

Under the festival’s [official coach ticket conditions](https://www.glastonburyfestivals.co.uk/info/?direct=true&utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), customers who purchase combined admission and coach transportation are required to travel on their designated coach. The rules state that coach packages cannot be converted to general admission tickets under any circumstances.

The festival also ordinarily distributes admission tickets during the coach journey, rather than allowing customers to collect them at the festival grounds. Buyers unable to travel on their assigned coach would normally have to cancel their entire ticket booking.

Those restrictions support Glastonbury’s environmental initiatives, which encourage attendees to use public transportation to reduce emissions associated with travel to Worthy Farm.

The overselling error has now created an exception, allowing affected purchasers to retain festival admission while making their own transportation arrangements.

For some customers, the unexpected flexibility may actually be welcome.

Laura Brooks, a Birmingham resident interviewed by the BBC, explained that she and her friends had purchased a coach package after repeatedly failing to secure standard admission tickets. They had planned to meet in London for the journey to Somerset but can now arrange more convenient travel from their respective homes.

“I’m just glad we’ve not lost our tickets altogether,” Brooks told the BBC.

For customers who depended on the transportation included in their purchases, however, the resolution raises different questions. Receiving a refund for the original coach fare does not necessarily mean comparable alternative travel will be available at the same price.

And while See Tickets is allowing customers to request that their coach bookings remain intact, the company has not publicly explained how it intends to resolve any remaining capacity shortfall.

The error comes just weeks after Glastonbury’s highly competitive initial ticket sales.

As [TicketNews reported following the Oct. 1 coach package sale](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/glastonbury-2027-coach-tickets-sell-out/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the first allocation sold out in less than 30 minutes despite admission prices reaching a record £408 for the 2027 festival.

The subsequent general admission sale on Oct. 4 sold out in approximately 42 minutes, leaving unsuccessful buyers waiting until the festival’s planned spring resale for another opportunity to secure admission.

The [record admission price](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/glastonbury-2027-ticket-prices-record-408/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), which includes a £5 booking fee but excludes postage and transportation, represents an increase of £29.50 from the 2025 festival.

Glastonbury’s ticketing system is tightly controlled, with See Tickets serving as the exclusive authorized seller. Admission tickets are personalized to registered customers, include their photographs and cannot be transferred or resold to other individuals.

The current accommodation does not change those broader restrictions. Instead, it allows affected customers to retain their existing admission rights without the coach transportation originally attached to their purchases.

Glastonbury 2027 is scheduled for June 23-27 at Worthy Farm in Somerset, following the festival’s planned fallow year in 2026. Ticket holders must pay their outstanding festival balances April 1-7, 2027.

For affected coach package customers, the more immediate deadline is Oct. 31, when they must decide whether to request retention of their original transportation arrangements or accept conversion to general admission with a refund of the coach fare.
