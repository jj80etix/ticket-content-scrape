# Musicians Group Urges DOJ to Reject Live Nation Settlement, Seeks Breakup and Artist Grants

## Summary

United Musicians and Allied Workers filed a Sept. 4 Tunney Act comment urging the Justice Department to withdraw its proposed antitrust settlement with Live Nation/Ticketmaster, arguing the deal's behavioral restrictions (exclusivity changes, a 15% Ticketmaster fee cap at Live Nation amphitheaters, divestiture of 13 venues) don't go far enough without a full Live Nation–Ticketmaster breakup. UMAW is unusual among commenters (SeatGeek, AEG/Messina, NIVA) in centering musicians specifically, proposing artist grant funds from any state financial recovery, forced separation of Live Nation from its artist-management business, venue-ownership caps, and limits on festival radius clauses. The same remedies were also sent directly to the state AGs who won a separate April jury verdict against Live Nation/Ticketmaster and are pursuing their own structural-remedy process.

### Key Points
- UMAW's Sept. 4 Tunney Act filing calls for DOJ to withdraw the settlement and for the court to reject it; comment period has closed and DOJ must respond before Judge Arun Subramanian rules on the public-interest standard.
- DOJ's proposed judgment does not force a Ticketmaster sale — it caps Ticketmaster amphitheater service fees at 15%, changes exclusivity/distribution terms, and requires divestiture of 13 named venues.
- UMAW wants a portion of any state financial recovery directed to artist grant funds (regional/genre-diverse, prioritizing emerging/lower-income musicians) — distinct from NIVA's venue-focused relief ask.
- UMAW also seeks: Live Nation forced out of artist management, per-market venue-ownership caps, and a ban (or venue/festival separation) on festival radius clauses.
- UMAW sent the same remedy package directly to state AGs, who declined to join the DOJ settlement and won the major monopoly claims (incl. primary ticketing) at trial on April 15; Live Nation says it will not be "materially different" from the federal outcome and disputes the underlying harm claims.

### Industry Problem
The core structural question UMAW raises is whether behavioral restrictions on a vertically integrated promoter-ticketer-venue-manager can meaningfully restore competitive leverage for artists and independent venues, or whether only structural separation (a Ticketmaster divestiture) closes that gap — a live question for how much market opening independents like Etix can expect regardless of which remedy prevails.

### Proposed Solution
No product action follows directly from this filing; it's a legal/regulatory process story. The trigger to watch is Judge Subramanian's ruling on the Tunney Act comments and the parallel state remedy proceeding — a structural breakup outcome would be the signal to revisit competitive positioning against a potentially separated Ticketmaster.

United Musicians and Allied Workers is urging the Justice Department to withdraw its proposed antitrust settlement with Live Nation and Ticketmaster, arguing that leaving the companies together fails to address harms the organization says the live entertainment giant has inflicted on working musicians.

In a Sept. 4 Tunney Act comment obtained by TicketNews, UMAW calls for Live Nation to be separated from Ticketmaster while proposing a broader collection of artist-focused remedies, including direct grants to local musicians, separation of Live Nation from its artist-management businesses, limits on venue ownership and restrictions on festival radius clauses.

“The Justice Department should withdraw this proposed judgement, and the court should decline to accept it,” the organization wrote.

The filing makes UMAW the latest industry organization to challenge the Justice Department agreement during the public-comment process, joining companies and groups including [SeatGeek](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/seatgeek-live-nation-settlement-ticketmaster/?utm_source=chatgpt.com), [AEG/Louis Messina](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/aeg-louis-messina-live-nation-settlement/), the [National Independent Venue Association](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/niva-live-nation-ticketmaster-breakup-tour-cap/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and others that have argued the behavioral restrictions negotiated by DOJ do not adequately address Live Nation and Ticketmaster’s market power.

What distinguishes UMAW’s submission is its focus on musicians themselves.

The Justice Department’s proposed final judgment does not require Live Nation to sell Ticketmaster. Instead, it would impose a series of ticketing, promotion and venue restrictions, including changes to Ticketmaster exclusivity agreements, third-party distribution opportunities at Live Nation amphitheaters, a 15% Ticketmaster service-fee cap at those amphitheaters and divestiture of Live Nation’s ownership or control of 13 specified venues.

UMAW argues that those measures do not go far enough.

The organization’s first proposed structural remedy is straightforward: Live Nation and Ticketmaster should be separated.

But UMAW then goes beyond many of the other Tunney Act commenters by arguing that any financial recovery flowing to states should also reach working musicians.

The group proposes using part of the money paid to states to create grant funds for local artists, potentially administered through existing state arts councils or newly created programs. UMAW says funding should be distributed across geographic regions and musical genres, with particular attention to emerging and lower-income musicians.

UMAW said it also supports relief for ticket buyers and independent venues and promoters, but argued that musicians should not be left out of the remedy process.

That differs somewhat from the proposal advanced by NIVA, which also called for Ticketmaster divestiture and structural changes to Live Nation’s artist-management interests but focused its proposed financial relief on independent venues. NIVA additionally sought a restriction preventing Live Nation from promoting more than half of a major artist’s U.S. tour.

UMAW’s filing instead attempts to make compensation for individual working artists part of the antitrust remedy itself.

The group argues that Live Nation’s scale across promotion, venues, ticketing and artist services can reduce musicians’ negotiating leverage, particularly for artists without the commercial power of major touring acts.

UMAW consequently wants Live Nation forced to separate from its artist-management interests and prohibited from acquiring or creating additional artist-services businesses. The organization argues that having management, promotion and venue operations under the same corporate umbrella creates conflicts when artists or their representatives negotiate with other Live Nation businesses.

Those assertions reflect UMAW’s position in the proceeding; Live Nation has repeatedly disputed broader allegations that its integrated business model harms artists or competition.

## Venue Ownership, Merchandise and Radius Clauses

UMAW is also asking for restrictions that extend well beyond Ticketmaster.

The group wants limits placed on how many venues Live Nation can own or control within an individual market and calls for the company to be blocked from purchasing or constructing additional venues.

UMAW argues that markets where Live Nation controls venues across several capacity levels can leave touring artists with fewer practical alternatives when negotiating performance fees and other contract terms.

It also proposes prohibiting Live Nation from using festival radius clauses in markets where the company operates venues — or, alternatively, preventing Live Nation from operating both festivals and venues in the same market.

Radius clauses commonly restrict an artist from performing within a defined geographic area and time period around a festival appearance. UMAW argues that when the same company also controls local venues, those restrictions can limit both musicians’ ability to book competing shows and independent venues’ ability to hire them.

Another proposal targets merchandise commissions.

UMAW claims Live Nation previously required venues across its network to collect a share of artist merchandise sales, briefly stopped taking those cuts in 2023 and has since “resumed the practice.” It wants individual venues given greater ability to negotiate such terms directly with artists.

That claim requires some qualification. Live Nation currently says its [On the Road Again program](https://roadagain.live/) allows developing artists playing participating Live Nation clubs to keep 100% of their merchandise revenue, and the company repeated that claim in its 2026 proxy materials. The program is expressly focused on participating club venues, however, and does not establish what merchandise policies may apply throughout the rest of Live Nation’s venue portfolio. UMAW’s filing does not specify which venues or venue categories it is referring to when it says merchandise cuts have resumed.

## UMAW Is Pressing the Same Remedies With the States

The Tunney Act filing is only one part of UMAW’s intervention in the antitrust case.

The organization also sent a separate Sept. 4 letter to the state attorneys general that continued litigating against Live Nation and Ticketmaster after DOJ settled. [UMAW published that letter on its website Sept. 22](https://weareumaw.org/news/umaws-letter-on-the-live-nationticketmaster-antitrust-verdict), urging the states to pursue essentially the same package of artist-focused remedies after their courtroom victory.

That distinction matters because the case is now proceeding on two related tracks.

DOJ reached its settlement with Live Nation during trial, while a coalition of states declined to join the agreement and continued litigating. On April 15, a [Manhattan federal jury found for the states on the major monopoly theories submitted to it](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/04/live-nation-and-ticketmaster-operate-as-an-illegal-monopoly-jury-finds-in-landmark-antitrust-trial/), including primary ticketing and Live Nation’s large-amphitheater business. Live Nation has said the verdict remains subject to post-trial motions and potential appeals.

The states have separately indicated that they intend to pursue structural remedies, including a potential breakup, placing their remedy proceeding well beyond the terms DOJ accepted.

UMAW is therefore making essentially the same argument to both sets of government enforcers: DOJ should not settle for behavioral restrictions that leave Live Nation and Ticketmaster together, while the states that prevailed at trial should use their remedy process to address what UMAW views as harms suffered specifically by musicians.

Live Nation has defended the DOJ agreement and has challenged the jury verdict, saying in April that it remained confident the ultimate result of the states’ case would not be “materially different” from the federal settlement.

The Sept. 4 Tunney Act comment deadline has now passed. DOJ must consider the submitted comments and respond before U.S. District Judge Arun Subramanian determines whether the proposed final judgment satisfies the Tunney Act’s public-interest standard.
