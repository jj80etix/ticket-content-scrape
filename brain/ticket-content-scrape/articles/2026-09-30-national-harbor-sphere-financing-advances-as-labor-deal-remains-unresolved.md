# National Harbor Sphere Financing Advances as Labor Deal Remains Unresolved

## Summary

Prince George's County, MD lawmakers advanced two financing measures (CB-111-2026, CR-090-2026) supporting Sphere Entertainment's planned $1.4B, 6,000-seat National Harbor venue, without a committee recommendation, while a dispute over whether construction will be covered by a project labor agreement remains unresolved. CB-111 authorizes up to $215M in special-obligation bonds (covering both new Sphere-related support and refinancing of existing National Harbor bonds), with the Sphere-specific portion described as roughly $130M in tax-increment financing; CR-090 would expand allowed uses of TIF-backed bonds to include an immersive entertainment venue.

### Key Points
- $1.4B Sphere Entertainment venue (6,000-seat) planned for National Harbor, Maryland.
- CB-111-2026 authorizes up to $215M in special-obligation bonds; ~$130M of county incentive is Sphere-specific TIF financing, rest covers refinancing existing 2022 bonds.
- CR-090-2026 would expand TIF bond use to cover an immersive entertainment venue and dissolve the older National Harbor Special Taxing District.
- Both measures advanced to "Second Reading" without committee recommendation, preserving time for labor negotiations.
- Sphere executives have so far declined to commit to a project labor agreement despite the public financing ask.

### Highlights
- $1.4B venue backed by up to $215M in county bonds (~$130M Sphere-specific TIF) — a major new entrant building physical ticketing/venue capacity in the DC market.
- Labor-agreement dispute is the key open contingency that could still delay or reshape the financing package.
- Measures advanced without recommendation, signaling County Council is not yet fully aligned behind the deal.

### Industry Problem
Sphere Entertainment's expansion represents a well-capitalized new entrant building a next-generation immersive venue, which could reset market and patron expectations (production/experience quality) in a market a primary ticketing platform may also serve — while the public financing and labor fight shows how entangled new venue capacity is with local political risk.

### Proposed Solution
No product fix is described in the item itself; the posture for an independent primary platform is to monitor the project's financing/labor resolution and timeline as a trigger for whether and when to pursue a ticketing/technology relationship with the venue once it is operational.

## Construction Labor Fight Remains Open

The central dispute involves a proposed project labor agreement, or PLA, covering construction of the Sphere. Such agreements establish employment terms between contractors and labor organizations for a particular construction project. Sphere representatives have resisted entering into a PLA, while unions and several council members have argued that a project receiving substantial public support should include stronger guarantees for organized labor and local workers.

County Executive Aisha Braveboy said Tuesday that the county remains focused on ensuring residents receive jobs, local and minority-owned companies receive contracts and workers receive protections. She also pointed to a labor peace agreement included in the project agreement for Sphere’s eventual operational workforce.

That agreement does not resolve the construction dispute. A labor peace agreement covering workers after the venue opens and a PLA covering the contractors and trades that build the venue address different workforces, leaving the construction issue at the center of the ongoing negotiations.

The Maryland Sphere was announced in January as the first smaller-scale version of the company’s Las Vegas venue and would seat approximately 6,000 people. Sphere Entertainment said the venue would include an exterior Exosphere LED display, a 16K-by-16K interior display, immersive audio, haptic seating and 4D effects. The company said at the time that development remained contingent on definitive agreements and the required state and county incentives and approvals.

The broader incentive package has been described by Prince George’s County as including roughly $130 million in county TIF financing, $40 million for a publicly financed parking garage and $13.5 million in state funding. Sphere and its government partners originally described approximately $200 million in combined state, local and private incentives supporting the project.

Tuesday’s committee action keeps that financing process moving, but does not constitute final approval of the package. The next significant step will come as the measures move back before the full County Council, with negotiations over construction labor terms continuing in the meantime.
