# Richard Sterban, Vocalist For The Oak Ridge Boys, Dies At 83

## Summary

Richard Sterban, the deep bass vocalist of the Oak Ridge Boys, died Nov. 14 in Henderson, Tennessee, at 83, following complications from bronchitis and pneumonia after a 2025 pancreatic cancer diagnosis. He joined the group in 1972 after backing Elvis Presley, anchoring hits like "Elvira" and helping the band win four Grammys and induction into the Country Music and Vocal Group Halls of Fame. He is survived by his wife, children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren.

### Key Points
- Died Nov. 14, 2026 in Henderson, Tennessee, age 83, from bronchitis/pneumonia complications following a March 2025 pancreatic cancer diagnosis.
- Sang bass with the Oak Ridge Boys from 1972, after previously backing Elvis Presley with J.D. Summer and the Stamps Quartet.
- Anchored the band's biggest hit "Elvira" and helped them win four Grammys plus CMA/ACM honors.
- Inducted into the Gospel Music Hall of Fame (2000) and Vocal Group Hall of Fame (2001).
- Briefly rejoined the group at the Grand Ole Opry in January 2026 to perform "Elvira" after stepping back from touring in 2025.

## Transcript

![GettyImages 994418892](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/GettyImages-994418892-scaled.jpg)

Richard Sterban, deep bass vocalist for the Oak Ridge Boys, died Saturday, Nov. 14, in Henderson, Tennessee, his family and the band announced. He was 83.

Sterban, who announced he was diagnosed with pancreatic cancer in March 2025, died due to complications from bronchitis and pneumonia.

“We appreciate all of your love, support, and prayers,” the Sterban family said in a statement. “Please continue to lift us up in this difficult time, we find comfort knowing that he is currently face to face with Jesus.”

The Country Music Hall of Famer previously sang for Elvis Presley and joined the Oak Ridge Boys in 1972. “I wanted to be in the best vocal group in the world,” he said.

He, along with lead singer Duane Allen, baritone Willie Lee Golden and tenor Joe Bonsall, became among the most successful Southern gospel and country acts in the 1970s, producing 34 singles in the country Top 10 and winning Grammy, Country Music Association and Academy of Country Music awards.

Sterban stood out in the Oak Ridge Boys’ four-part harmony in the band’s biggest hit “Elvira” with his iconic low bass voice anchoring the chorus.

Born on April 24, 1943, in Camden, New Jersey, Sterban grew up near Collingswood and started singing at 6 years old as a soprano in Sunday school. He sang tenor in seventh grade glee club and dropped to bass by eighth grade. Sterban then studied music at Trenton State College, which is now The College of New Jersey, and had a drive to perform live.

He helped found the Keystones, a gospel quartet that also featured Bonsall, and he sang with the group while working at a clothing store, where he developed his sense of style. “I developed a real interest in fashion, and it’s something that has carried through,” Sterban once said, according to a release announcing his death.

His friendship with Bonsall grew into a decades-long partnership, and in 1994, the two were honored together on the Philadelphia Music Alliance Walk of Fame.

Sterban’s journey led him to J.D. Summer and the Stamps Quartet, who were hired to sing behind Elvis. Sterban backed up the legendary singer for about a year and a half and even appeared in the 1972 concert film “Elvis on Tour.”

Then came the Oak Ridge Boys, a gospel quartet that needed a bass singer following the departure of Noel Fox. Sterban took a chance to leave his gig with Elvis and chase his own dream, joining the group in 1972. Bonsall joined him the following year, and together they helped carry the Oak Ridge Boys through the next five decades. Sterban detailed his journey in his memoir, “From Elvis to Elvira: My Life on Stage,” which was written with Steven Robinson and published in 2012.

Johnny Cash featured the quartet on his tour and his single “Praise the Lord and Pass the Soup.” He helped the Oak Ridge Boys sign a deal with Columbia Records in 1973, but their Southern gospel songs didn’t sell many records.

When they opened for Roy Clark in 1975, Jim Halsey, Clark’s manager, encouraged the Oak Ridge Boys to sing country. They signed with Halsey and toured the Soviet Union in 1976 with Clark, becoming the first country group to perform there. The group then moved to ABC/Dot Records, and their song “Y’all Come Back Saloon” reached 3 on the country chart in 1977. They went on to produce more hits, including their first No. 1, “I’ll Be True to You,” “Leaving Louisiana in the Broad Daylight,” and “Trying to Love Two Women.” They were named the Vocal Group of the Year in 1978 by CMA.

The Oak Ridge Boys won four of their five Grammy Awards with Sterban, and the group was inducted into the Gospel Music Hall of Fame in 2000 and the Vocal Group Hall of Fame in 2001.

Bonsall retired from touring in 2023 and died the following year from complications of ALS.

After appearing with the Oak Ridge Boys at the Academy of Country Music Awards in May 2025, Sterban stepped away from touring because of his health. He rejoined the group in January at the Grand Ole Opry to sing “Elvira.”

“Having Richard on stage with us again was one of God’s greatest blessings,” Duane Allen said at the time.

Sterban is survived by his wife, Donna; his daughters, Lauren (Tyler) and Tori (Michael); his sons, Rich (Chele), Doug, and Chris; his grandchildren, Mali (Zachary), Nikolas, Tyler (Ryann), Madelyn, Alexandra, Rex, Owen, and Charli Grace; and his great-grandchildren, Salem, Asher, and Theo.
