# Burgas selected to host 2027 Eurovision Song Contest

## Summary

Burgas, Bulgaria's fourth-largest city, was selected to host the 71st Eurovision Song Contest in May 2027 at the 2023-built Arena Burgas, following Bulgaria's first-ever win (Dara, "Bangaranga") in Vienna this year. Organizers cite prior hosts' economic impact (Basel generated a reported €272m boost for Switzerland in 2025) as a benchmark.

### Key Points
- Eurovision 2027 will be held over three live shows (May 11, 13, 15) at Arena Burgas.
- Burgas was chosen over Sofia in a multi-city bid process that also considered Varna and Plovdiv.
- Cited comparables: Basel 2025 generated a reported €272m national economic boost; Vienna 2026 drew 88,000 extra visitors and an estimated €57m boost.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**Bulgaria’s fourth-largest city Burgas has been chosen to host the 71** **<sup>st</sup>** **Eurovision Song Contest in May next year.**

The event will be held over three live shows on May 11, 13, and 15 at Arena Burgas, which opened in 2023.

Bulgaria was awarded hosting rights following Dara’s victory at this year’s edition in Vienna with Bangaranga, securing the country’s first-ever Eurovision win.

“We are delighted that Burgas will be the Host City for the Eurovision Song Contest 2027,” said Eurovision Song Contest director Martin Green.

“Burgas brings something really special to the Eurovision Song Contest. It is a vibrant, welcoming city on the Black Sea with a strong musical and cultural identity, and it has the ambition, infrastructure and passion needed to welcome the Eurovision family.

“For the first Eurovision Song Contest ever to be held in Bulgaria, we want to create something that feels truly Bulgarian while also being unmistakably Eurovision – a celebration that brings people together through music.”

Burgas was selected following a host city process involving four Bulgarian cities.

Burgas and Sofia progressed to the final stage of the selection process following the initial bids, which included those from Varna and Plovdiv.

The assessment examined the proposed venues, technical and production requirements, local infrastructure, accommodation, transport and the ability of each city to welcome the thousands of artists, delegations, production staff, journalists and fans who will travel to Bulgaria for the contest.

Organisers are hoping for the event to have a similar economic impact on Burgas as it has for previous hosts.

Liverpool saw a €66m (£56m/$76m) economic boost in 2023, Malmö €40m in 2024 and Basel €58m in 2025, with Basel also generating a reported €272m boost for Switzerland nationally.

Vienna is also seeing returns already from the 2026 contest, with 88,000 additional visitors to the city, 160,000 hotel rooms sold and an estimated €57m economic boost.
