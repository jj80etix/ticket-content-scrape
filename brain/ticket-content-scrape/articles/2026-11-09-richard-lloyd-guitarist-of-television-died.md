# Richard Lloyd, Guitarist Of Television, Died

## Summary

Richard Lloyd, co-founder and guitarist of the influential punk band Television, died Nov. 7 at his Chattanooga, Tennessee home at age 75, of cholangiocarcinoma diagnosed in March. Lloyd was a pioneering figure in the 1970s New York punk scene, known for his guitar work alongside the late Tom Verlaine.

### Key Points
- Died Nov. 7, 2026; diagnosed with bile duct cancer in March.
- Co-founded Television and performed at CBGB during the birth of NYC punk.
- Earlier career included playing with John Lee Hooker and crossing paths with Jimi Hendrix and Led Zeppelin members.

## Transcript

![GettyImages 85063686](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/GettyImages-85063686-scaled.jpg)

Richard Lloyd performing at Glastonbury Festival. (Photo by Hayley Madden/Redferns)

Richard Lloyd, co-founder and guitarist of the influential band Television, died on Saturday, Nov. 7, in his home in Chattanooga, Tennessee. He was 75.

The musician’s family sent a statement to the [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/07/arts/music/richard-lloyd-dead.html) that said he died of cholangiocarcinoma. He was diagnosed with the bile duct cancer in March.

Lloyd was a pioneer of the 1970s New York punk scene, performing at iconic clubs like CBGB. He and his bandmate, the late Tom Verlaine, were guitar virtuosos who stood out, drawing from their jazz influences and 1960s rock idols to create a clean yet crunchy sound.

Lloyd was born in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, on Oct. 25, 1951, and grew up in New York. He learned from musician Velvet Turner, who was working with Jimi Hendrix. Lloyd then played with blues legend John Lee Hooker and traveled to various cities before he and his friend, Television manager Terry Ork, saw Verlaine perform at a New York club.

As an aspiring musician, Lloyd often interacted with legends like Jimi Hendrix, members of Led Zeppelin and Buddy Guy. In his journey through venues across the country, Lloyd saw a performance from Verlaine and soon began working with him to form a band.

![GettyImages 592276437](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/GettyImages-592276437-scaled.jpg)

Guitarist Richard Lloyd performing with American rock group, Television, on one of their two nights at the Hammersmith Odeon, London in May 1977. (Photo by Michael Putland/Getty Images)

Television, comprising drummer Billy Ficca, bassist Richard Hell, Lloyd and Verlaine, was one of the most innovative bands and paved the way for the new wave punk sound that influenced bands like U2, The Smiths and R.E.M. Hell departed from the band and was replaced by Fred Smith before the band’s debut album *Marquee Moon*, which is widely considered to be one of the greatest albums ever made.

“R.I.P Richard Lloyd. A seeker and finder and a true inspiration with and without the guitar. It was an honour to know him and a blessing to have been a fan. Thank you Richard. Carry on my friend,” Johnny Marr, guitarist for The Smiths, wrote on [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/officialjohnnymarr/posts/pfbid02Zs38gsuyEZYdQf3Cn6uiHtWhCfbaWHvYVGk8fRyJcNbKBkxzTc4ZTAvqVg7mtt6nl).

Geese honored Lloyd during a concert in Philadelphia on Nov. 7, performing a cover of Television’s “Aint That Nothin’.”

Rock band Garbage also [posted a tribute](https://www.instagram.com/p/Dfc3qBcPkGY/): “The musical community has lost a giant. Rest in your own brilliance dear Richard Lloyd. Thank you for your extraordinary excellence. Your flair. Your style. Your taste. Your touch. Sincere condolences to your beautiful wife and to everyone who ever loved you.”

Nels Cline of Wilco also praised Lloyd’s legacy and work with the guitar.

“Your soaring melodic guitar playing contains so much beauty, often mixed with a palpable kind of anguish,” he wrote on [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/nelscline/posts/rest-in-peace-restless-soul-richard-lloyd-your-soaring-melodic-guitar-playing-co/1643409077247910/). “Certainly those classic solos with Television stick in my head in all their stunning, impeccable form. I have been reflecting on a gig I heard on what might have been Richard’s first National tour post-Television (the first post-Television time – there were 2 or 3!). … But Richard blew my mind that night with his fiery, strangulated soloing, almost entirely using wah-wah, which is not used anywhere on that record and which I never heard him use again. His solos were amazing but also sounded phenomenally tormented – much like his accompanying facial expressions – and I just stood there slack-jawed. It was like fire arrows to the heart. He was a complicated guy for sure. And he was a masterful musician. Thank you Richard.”
