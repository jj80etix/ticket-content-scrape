# FTC and Eight States Sue Toast Over Hidden Fees and Deceptive Subscription Practices

## Summary

The FTC and attorneys general from eight states/localities sued restaurant point-of-sale/fintech provider Toast on Oct. 29, alleging hidden fees (an undisclosed 99-cent fee on online orders), misleading "$0 cost" and "24/7 support" marketing claims, punitive early-termination/hardware charges, and deliberately difficult subscription cancellation. The federal claims rest on FTC Act Section 5 and ROSCA; Toast disputes that ROSCA's consumer protections extend to negotiated B2B contracts, a question that could matter to any company selling recurring services to commercial customers.

### Key Points
- Complaint alleges a hidden 99-cent fee on online orders >$10 (2021-2023), $150/month early-termination charges, and mandatory hardware return/buyout after 120 days of non-use.
- Toast allegedly advertised "$0" cost and "24/7 support" while support fell short and true costs were undisclosed.
- Federal claims: FTC Act §5(a) and ROSCA §4; eight states plus LA County and San Francisco joined under their own consumer-protection statutes.
- Toast argues ROSCA doesn't cover negotiated B2B agreements — an open legal question for any B2B subscription provider.
- Coordinated federal-state-local action signals regulators are extending deceptive-pricing/junk-fee theories beyond consumer transactions into B2B SaaS/fintech contracts.

### Highlights
- Filed Oct. 29, 2026 in N.D. Cal.; FTC plus AGs of CO, FL, IL, IA, NE, OK, UT, plus LA County and San Francisco.
- Toast's ROSCA-doesn't-apply-to-B2B defense is an unresolved legal question that could affect any recurring-revenue B2B platform.

### Industry Problem
Regulators are applying the same hidden-fee/deceptive-subscription theories used against consumer ticketing platforms to B2B vendor relationships (venue/organizer contracts, cancellation terms, early-termination fees) — exposing primary platforms to junk-fee-style liability on the client side, not just the patron side.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor the ROSCA-B2B litigation outcome and proactively audit Etix's own client-facing contract disclosures (early-termination fees, support claims, cancellation process) against the same "clear and conspicuous disclosure" standard the FTC is now applying to B2B fintech.

On October 29, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and the attorneys general (AGs) of Colorado, Florida, Illinois, Iowa, Nebraska, Oklahoma, and Utah filed a [lawsuit](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Toast-Redacted-Complaint-Filed.pdf) against Toast Inc. and several affiliated entities in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California. Los Angeles County and the City of San Francisco also joined on behalf of California. The complaint alleges that Toast, a restaurant-focused point-of-sale and fintech provider, imposed hidden and unauthorized fees, pushed unwanted add-ons, and made its services difficult to cancel.

### Background and the Alleged Conduct

According to the complaint, Toast allegedly required restaurants to enroll in auto-renewing subscriptions averaging roughly $900 per month, without clear disclosure of several associated charges. The allegations include:

- **Hidden and unauthorized fees.** From September 2021 through late July 2023, Toast allegedly added a 99-cent fee on certain online orders over $10 without adequate disclosure or consent. The fee reportedly went to Toast, not the restaurants, on top of the fees restaurants already paid.
- **Early termination and hardware charges.** Customers who cancel early allegedly owe $150 for each month remaining on their contract. Customers who do not begin processing payments within 120 days must reportedly return their hardware or pay its full retail value.
- **Misleading cost and support claims.** The complaint alleges that Toast told customers their cost was “$0” and promised 24/7 customer support, while restaurants struggled to get timely help.
- **Difficult subscription cancellation practices.** Toast failed to provide customers with a simple cancellation process, instituting a counterintuitive, multistep, complicated cancellation process, and charged high, unexpected early termination fees.

The federal claims rest on Section 5(a) of the FTC Act and Section 4 of the Restore Online Shoppers’ Confidence Act (ROSCA). The states add claims under their own consumer protection statutes.

Toast has denied the allegations and says it will vigorously defend itself. It argues that ROSCA, which is aimed at consumer protection, does not apply to contracts negotiated between two businesses.

### Why It Matters

This lawsuit extends well beyond Toast and the restaurant industry, particularly for companies that sell to other businesses:

- **Hidden fees are a risk in B2B contexts, too.** Regulators are applying deceptive pricing theories to sales made to small businesses, not just to individual consumers. All fees and charges should be clearly and conspicuously disclosed.
- **Subscription and auto-renewal terms face close scrutiny.** Businesses offering recurring services must ensure that charges, renewal terms, and early termination fees are fully disclosed, that consent is informed, and that cancellation is straightforward.
- **ROSCA’s reach into B2B contracts may be tested.** Toast’s argument that ROSCA does not cover negotiated B2B agreements sets up a question that could matter to any company selling subscriptions to commercial customers. Until a court addresses it, B2B providers may wish to consider whether negotiated contracts could fall within these requirements.
- **Marketing and support promises must match reality.** Claims like “$0 cost” or “24/7 support” can potentially become the basis of deception allegations if the actual terms or service differ.
- **Coordinated state-federal actions raise the stakes.** When the FTC and multiple state and local authorities proceed together, the scope of potential liability and the complexity of any resolution both increase.
