# IQ 149 out now: Indie Champions revealed in third Indie edition

## Summary

IQ Magazine's final 2026 issue (IQ 149) unveils its third annual Indie Champions list of 20 independent live-entertainment companies, alongside career profiles of agents Dennis Arfa and Martje Kremers and a 2027 touring preview led by Shakira's record-breaking Las Mujeres No Lloran World Tour.

### Key Points
- 20 Indie Champions named, including Another Planet Entertainment, EXIT Festival, Tickets For Good and Untitled Group.
- Profiles cover IAG's Dennis Arfa and Primary Talent's Martje Kremers.
- Shakira's tour has sold 4m+ tickets and grossed $530m+, the highest-grossing tour by a Spanish-speaking artist.
- The issue also examines challenges in the mid-tier touring sector.

Also in this issue, Adam Woods reports on the profound effect Dennis Arfa has had on the live music business we know today in the latest Architects profile, and Lisa Henderson recounts stalwart agent Martje Kremers’s 25 years in the business.

Henderson also charts the year at large and previews what’s to come in 2027 in our annual Big (P)review. Coming out on top of this year’s tours is none other than Shakira’s *Las Mujeres No Lloran World Tour*.

> “There’s so much to celebrate about the role independent companies play in our industry”

Josefina Armendariz & Florencia Mauro go behind the scenes of the record-breaking run, which has sold over 4m tickets, generated more than $530m at the box office, and already secured its place as the highest-grossing tour ever by a Spanish-speaking artist.

Elsewhere, Gordon Masson examines creative solutions to the sometimes tricky mid-tier touring sector.

Plus, Derek Robertson discovers a live entertainment business that is sharply divided with out Swedish market report, while Manila becomes the latest city focus, with tips on how to avoid the pitfalls of touring in the Philippine capital.

For comments and columns, Tysers Live MD Tim Thornhill looks back on the issues shaping insurance premiums, In Place of War’s Ruth Daniel explains how expanding the global touring map elevates new voices, and Aviva Arena’s new head of programming Dave Gaydon unpacks his vision for the UK’s forthcoming independent venue.

As always, readers will also find the industry’s latest movements, new signings and new music, and key stories and analysis from across the global industry.

“There’s so much to celebrate about the role independent companies play in our industry. I hope you enjoy discovering all the incredible work they’ve been doing in this edition of IQ,” says editor James Drury.
