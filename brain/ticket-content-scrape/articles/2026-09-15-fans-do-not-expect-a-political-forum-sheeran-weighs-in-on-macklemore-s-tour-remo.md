# ‘Fans do not expect a political forum’: Sheeran weighs in on Macklemore’s tour removal

## Summary

Ed Sheeran addressed Macklemore's removal from his "Loop Tour" after the rapper's on-stage pro-Palestinian remarks, saying the decision was the promoter's, not his, and that his own shows are meant to be apolitical spaces rather than a "political forum." Gillette Stadium owner Robert Kraft confirmed he pushed for Macklemore's removal, citing concerns about antisemitic rhetoric, while Macklemore maintains a group of stadium owners issued a collective ultimatum to Sheeran's promoter.

### Key Points
- Macklemore was dropped from the tour after his Sept. 4 MetLife Stadium set, which included a "Free Palestine" statement and his protest song "Hind's Hall."
- Promoter Messina Touring Group said venues would not allow the shows to proceed with Macklemore on the bill.
- Robert Kraft (Gillette Stadium/Patriots owner) publicly confirmed pressuring for the removal, citing a "history of activity" he called antisemitic; no other venue owners have confirmed involvement.
- Sheeran says he held direct talks with promoters and activists on both sides but the venues' decision was final.
- Remaining tour dates span Philadelphia, Foxborough, Atlanta, Indianapolis, Charlotte, Arlington, and Tampa, wrapping Nov. 7 before Sheeran heads to South America.

## ‘Fans do not expect a political forum’: Sheeran weighs in on Macklemore’s tour removal

The rapper was dropped from the Loop Tour after a pro-Palestinian speech on stage, with one US stadium owner confirming he pushed to remove him from the run

![Macklemore](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Macklemore-©-Lucie-Gertsch-.png)

Macklemore © Lucie Gertsch

Ed Sheeran has spoken out about the removal of Macklemore from his *Loop Tour*, saying “those who come to my shows do not expect a political forum”, after the rapper was dropped from his support slot following on-stage comments in support of Palestine.

The decision to remove the rapper was confirmed yesterday (14 September) by the tour’s promoter, Messina Touring Group, which said that Macklemore had been relieved of his slot at the behest of the venues. Macklemore followed with his own statement, alleging that a group of US stadium owners pushed for his removal from the remainder of the tour.

Today (15 September), Sheeran addressed the ongoing discourse with a [statement](https://www.instagram.com/p/DdT-6KGAtuI/?img_index=4) that said: “Macklemore coming off tour was the promoter’s decision,” and while he “respect\[s\] Macklemore’s strength of purpose to stand up and shout for what he believes”, fans who attend “do not expect a political forum”.

“Those who have been to one of my shows will know that they are about unity, joy, escapism, love, and a place for everyone to feel welcome,” Sheeran said, and added that he would not “let down” the fans, touring crew, or other support acts by risking the shows going ahead.

He continued: “There is room for multiple approaches to the same end: peace. I choose to use my fame and platform to be a place of safety and sanctuary, to maintain diplomacy and keep conversations open, not closed. If we only focus on shouting the loudest, nothing will ever change. There are different approaches to being an advocate for change.”

Yesterday, following Messina’s statement to [*Rolling Stone*](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/macklemore-dropped-ed-sheeran-tour-pro-palestine-statements-1235625967/) that said the venues “will not allow a concert to take place with Macklemore on the lineup, Macklemore alleged that a group of owners had given Sheeran an ultimatum: “If Macklemore stays on the tour, you will not be allowed to play in our venues,” he [said](https://www.instagram.com/p/DdRdsy0m2HK/?hl=en&img_index=1).

> “The venue’s and promoter’s decision was final”

“Taking a side can cost you. Money, brand deals, sponsorships, festivals, private shows, relationships and access. I’ve lost all of those things. But there is no neutral position between the oppressor and the oppressed,” he added.

In his statement today, Sheeran confirmed venues had threatened to “pull the shows if he was still supporting” because of the way “Macklemore conveyed his message” rather than “everything that was said.”

“I was also involved in direct talks with promoters along with activists on both sides to try and find a mutual resolution for all, but the venue’s and promoter’s decision was final,” Sheeran said.

Amid the fallout from yesterday’s announcement, Robert Kraft, who owns the New England Patriots team and Gillette Stadium through The Kraft Group, confirmed that he pushed to bar the rapper from performing in his venue.

“Gillette Stadium has a longstanding commitment to providing a welcoming environment for all guests and ensuring that events held at our venue do not provide a platform for hate speech,” he said in a statement verified by [*Variety*](https://variety.com/2026/music/news/robert-kraft-macklemore-ed-sheeran-tour-palestine-1236861621/), citing the rapper’s recent actions and a history of activity Kraft deems “deeply offensive and hurtful to the Jewish community” and that his performances at Gillette “would cross that line”.

> “Ultimately our goal is the same”

Kraft, who is Jewish and has donated millions in support of Israel and Jewish-related causes, added that he has “tremendous respect for Ed and his music” and his ability to “bring people together and spread joy”, and said he would welcome the chance to “discuss the facts” with Macklemore, “because ultimately our goal is the same – peace for all people, an end to suffering, and an end to all hate.”

In his statement today, Sheeran confirmed he had spoken with Kraft as one of the venue leaders pushing for Macklemore’s removal.

The removal stemmed from a public debate over statements Macklemore made on-stage earlier this month. During a concert on 4 September at New Jersey’s MetLife Stadium, the rapper delivered a [two-minute speech](https://x.com/macklemore/status/2097033800811507990?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw%7Ctwcamp%5Etweetembed%7Ctwterm%5E2097033800811507990%7Ctwgr%5E88ee3b0a5c42961d46790ce70b66cd48d927d528%7Ctwcon%5Es1_c10&ref_url=https%3A%2F%2Fvariety.com%2F2026%2Fmusic%2Fnews%2Fmacklemore-defends-free-palestine-speech-fired-ed-sheeran-tour-1236861158%2F) in the lead-up to his protest song *Hind’s Hall*, in which he said “Free Palestine” and “To all my Jewish brothers and sisters, criticism of Israel, criticism of apartheid \[and\] being against genocide, in no way is a criticism against you.”

No other venue owners have publicly issued statements in response to Macklemore’s accusations of a collective effort. The remaining venues on the stadium route span: Philadelphia’s Lincoln Financial Field; Foxborough’s Gillette Stadium; Atlanta’s Mercedes-Benz Stadium; Indianapolis’ Lucas Oil Stadium; Charlotte’s Bank of America Stadium; Arlington’s AT&T Stadium; and Tampa’s Raymond James Stadium, the latter of which recently made headlines for hosting controversial rapper [Kanye West (Ye)](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/inside-the-decision-to-promote-kanye-west/) for two nights. AT&T Stadium is also scheduled to host Ye in November.

Of the remaining venues, only two are privately owned: Kraft’s Gillette Stadium and Tepper Sports & Entertainment’s Bank of America Stadium. The rest are owned and/or operated by public institutions, potentially raising questions around censorship and applicability of the country’s First Amendment freedom of speech protections.

The forthcoming dates wrap up on 7 November in Tampa, with Sheeran then traversing South America until the end of the year.
