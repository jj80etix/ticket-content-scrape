# Live Nation Sets Rates on $1.4 Billion Refinancing, Extending Debt to 2032

## Summary

Live Nation priced a roughly $1.4 billion private debt offering ($730M in dollar notes at 7.125%, €600M in euro notes at 6.125%) due 2032, primarily to redeem its $1.2 billion of 6.5% pandemic-era notes maturing in 2027. The refinancing extends that debt maturity by about five years and modestly increases annual coupon costs relative to the retired notes.

### Key Points
- Offering: $730M USD notes (7.125%) + €600M EUR notes (6.125%), both priced at 100% of face value, due 2032.
- Proceeds primarily redeem $1.2B of 6.5% senior secured notes issued in May 2020 and due 2027.
- Estimated annual coupon cost of the new notes (~$93M combined, at the Oct. 7 ECB reference rate) is roughly $15M more than the ~$78M on the retired notes.
- Final mix shifted from the Oct. 6 plan ($840M USD/€500M EUR) toward a larger euro tranche.
- Notes offered privately to institutional investors, guaranteed by Live Nation and certain domestic subsidiaries; closing expected Oct. 15.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/live-nation-priced-offering-senior-notes-2023.webp)

Live Nation Entertainment has priced a roughly $1.4 billion debt offering that would push a major pandemic-era borrowing into 2032, setting interest rates of 7.125% on its new dollar-denominated notes and 6.125% on a euro-denominated tranche.

According to the company’s [Oct. 7 pricing announcement](https://news.livenationentertainment.com/news/live-nation-entertainment-announces-pricing-of-private-senior-notes-offering?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the offering consists of $730 million in U.S. dollar notes and €600 million in euro notes. Both tranches were priced at 100% of face value and are due in 2032.

The final mix differs from the [initial refinancing plan reported by TicketNews](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/live-nation-refinance-pandemic-debt-2/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) on Oct. 6, which called for $840 million in dollar notes and €500 million in euro notes. The company increased the euro portion while reducing the dollar portion as it finalized the transaction.

Live Nation intends to use the proceeds primarily to redeem its entire outstanding $1.2 billion issue of 6.5% senior secured notes due in 2027. That debt was raised in May 2020 as the COVID-19 shutdown brought much of the concert business to a halt. The new financing would extend that substantial debt maturity by roughly five years.

The new 7.125% dollar notes carry a coupon 0.625 percentage points above the rate on the debt being retired. The 6.125% euro notes carry a coupon 0.375 percentage points below the old rate. Because the new offering is larger and split between currencies, those rate differences do not by themselves show the full change in annual interest payments.

On the announced principal amounts, the new notes would require about $52 million in annual coupon payments on the dollar tranche and €36.75 million on the euro tranche. Using the [European Central Bank's Oct. 7 reference exchange rate](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/policy_and_exchange_rates/euro_reference_exchange_rates/html/eurofxref-graph-usd.en.html?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) of $1.1177 per euro, that works out to approximately $93 million annually, compared with $78 million on the $1.2 billion of 6.5% notes targeted for redemption. That is roughly $15 million more in annual coupon payments at the referenced exchange rate.

The comparison is illustrative, not a forecast of Live Nation’s net interest expense. The new notes amount to about $200 million more in principal than the debt they are intended to replace at that exchange rate. Live Nation said proceeds not needed for redemption may cover transaction expenses and general corporate purposes, including paying down or repurchasing other debt. The dollar value of future euro payments may also change with exchange rates.

The new notes are being offered privately to eligible institutional investors rather than through a registered public offering. Live Nation said the notes will be guaranteed by the company and certain domestic subsidiaries that guarantee its senior secured credit facilities.
