# MLB Attendance Grows for Fourth Straight Year to Highest Level Since 2017

## Summary

MLB attendance rose for a fourth consecutive season in 2026, reaching 71.75 million (highest since 2017), a 0.4% year-over-year gain and the league's first four-year growth streak since 2004-2007. Gains were uneven across the league: only 13 of 30 teams increased attendance, led by Tampa Bay's return to Tropicana Field, while the Mets, Astros, and several other clubs posted the steepest declines.

### Key Points
- 2026 total: 71,750,000 across 2,425 dates, averaging 29,589 fans/game.
- Tampa Bay's return to Tropicana Field drove the single largest team gain (+628,255 fans).
- The Dodgers set a new franchise attendance record (4,034,219) for a second straight year.
- Five clubs topped 3 million in attendance, down from six in 2025 and eight in 2023.

Major League Baseball attendance increased for a fourth consecutive season in 2026, reaching 71.75 million during the regular season and marking the league’s highest total since 2017.

MLB teams averaged 29,589 fans across 2,425 dates, up 0.4% from an average of 29,471 in 2025, when total attendance reached 71.4 million. The four-year stretch of attendance growth is MLB’s first since 2004 through 2007.

The latest increase continues a run that began with a much larger post-pandemic rebound. In 2023, MLB attendance jumped 9.6% to 70.75 million, the league’s largest percentage increase in nearly three decades outside of COVID-affected seasons. Attendance has continued to rise since then, although the gains have become considerably more modest.

MLB reported 71,409,421 attendees in 2025, meaning this year’s increase amounts to roughly 340,000 additional admissions across the league.

The leaguewide increase was not evenly distributed, however. Only 13 of MLB’s 30 teams increased attendance this season, while 17 recorded declines.

The Tampa Bay Rays posted the largest gain, adding 628,255 fans to finish at 1,414,975 after returning to Tropicana Field. Tampa Bay played the 2025 season at the much smaller George M. Steinbrenner Field while Tropicana Field was repaired following damage from Hurricane Milton. Steinbrenner Field had a capacity of just 10,046 for Rays games, while Tampa Bay drew crowds as large as 28,055 at the Trop this season.

The Rays’ increase by itself exceeded MLB’s overall year-over-year attendance gain, underscoring the effect of the club’s return to its regular ballpark on the leaguewide figure.

At the other end of the attendance table, the Los Angeles Dodgers again led Major League Baseball and established another franchise record. The defending back-to-back World Series champions drew 4,034,219 fans, an average of 49,805 per game. The Dodgers had topped 4 million for the first time in franchise history in 2025.

Five clubs finished above 3 million in attendance this season, down from six in 2025 and eight in 2023.

Several clubs posted substantial gains alongside Tampa Bay. The Chicago White Sox increased attendance by 540,687 to 1,986,425 after earning an American League wild-card berth with an 84-78 record following three consecutive 100-loss seasons. The Toronto Blue Jays added 488,943 fans to reach 3,338,878, while the Seattle Mariners increased attendance by 345,745 to 2,883,798.

The New York Mets recorded the largest decline in baseball, falling by 472,450 to 2,712,120. Houston’s attendance dropped 255,759 to 2,472,118 despite winning the AL West, while Kansas City declined by 221,703 to 1,527,098. Arizona, Atlanta, Cincinnati, Colorado and Philadelphia each posted decreases between 100,000 and 200,000.

The Athletics remained at the bottom of MLB’s attendance rankings while playing their second temporary season in the Sacramento area ahead of a planned move to Las Vegas. The club drew 799,240 fans, averaging 9,867 across games played primarily at Sutter Health Park in West Sacramento, along with six games at Las Vegas Ballpark.
