# LA28 provides affordable ticket purchase opportunities

## Summary

LA28 Olympic Games organizers made 12,000 tickets available at affordable, below-market prices to local community groups, schools, and youth sports organizations ahead of the 2028 Games. Roughly 5,000 of those tickets carry the lowest $28 price point, with an average price around $70 across the community allocation. The initiative builds on a July 2026 partnership with the LA Rams that gifted 1,000 tickets to local nonprofits, funded by a $5m Rams donation, and comes as LA28 ticket registration has surged past 12 million with two years still to go before the Games open.

### Key Points

- 12,000 tickets made available in bulk to chambers of commerce, business associations, community organizations, schools, and youth sports groups.
- 5,000 of those tickets priced at the $28 (~£20/€25) entry tier; average price across the allocation about $70.
- Recipients included 28 youth sports orgs, 26 local schools, and 29 community groups from the LA region.
- Builds on a July 2026 partnership with the LA Rams, which gifted 1,000 tickets to 10 community organizations as part of a $5m Community Ticket Programme donation.
- LA28 ticket registration has surged past 12 million with two years still to go before the Games (July 14-30).
- Games mark Los Angeles's third time hosting, after 1932 and 1984.

### Highlights

- $28 price point (5,000 tickets) versus a roughly $70 average — a concrete affordability benchmark for a marquee mega-event.
- 12,000-ticket bulk allocation channeled through community intermediaries (schools, youth sports orgs, nonprofits) rather than sold direct-to-consumer.
- $5m LA Rams donation underwriting part of the community ticket program, showing a corporate-sponsorship funding model for affordability initiatives.
- 12M+ registrations two years out signals strong demand pressure that makes affordability/access messaging strategically important for the Games.

### Industry Problem

Ticket affordability and perceived access for ordinary fans, schools, and community groups is a persistent patron pain point, especially at marquee mega-events where dynamic and premium pricing tiers can otherwise price out exactly this audience.

### Proposed Solution

LA28's approach — a dedicated low-price tier plus bulk allocation through community/education intermediaries, subsidized by sponsor-donation programs — is a model an independent platform like Etix could package as a configurable "affordable-tier" or community-allocation feature for high-demand client events, paired with sponsor/donor funding tools to underwrite it.

**Organisers of the Los Angeles 2028 Olympic Games have made 12,000 tickets affordably priced for local chambers of commerce, business associations, community organisations, schools, and youth sports groups.**

Through the initiative, these groups were able to purchase tickets in bulk for their communities, 5,000 of which were priced at $28 (£20/€25), and an average ticket price of around $70.

“Among the groups who purchased tickets through LA28 were 28 youth sports orgs, 26 local schools and 29 community groups from the Los Angeles region,” read an LA28 statement.

“By ensuring nearly half of these community-allocated group tickets are secured at our most affordable price point of $28, LA28 has opened the doors for local students, young athletes and Angelenos alike to witness history firsthand.”

Last month, the Games’ organising committee partnered with the [Los Angeles Rams](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/07/la-rams-gift-1000-la28-tickets-to-community-organisations/) to help provide 1,000 tickets to 10 local community organisations.

The organisations mark the first recipients of the NFL team’s $5m (£3.8m/€4.3m) donation to the LA28 Community Ticket Programme.

The Games are scheduled to take place from July 14-30 and will mark the third time that Los Angeles has played host, following the 1932 and 1984 editions.

Organisers also recently revealed that ticket registration for the LA Olympic and Paralympic Games had climbed to [12 million](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/07/la28-ticket-registrations-surge-to-over-12-million-with-two-years-to-go/).
