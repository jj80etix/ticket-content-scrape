# Hundred attendance figures down by 18%

## Summary

Attendance for cricket's The Hundred fell 18.4% in the opening week of its 2026 season versus the same fixtures in 2025, with higher ticket prices (now £35-£55) cited as a possible factor. This is the first season under new franchise ownership following a £520M stake sale.

### Key Points
- Combined opening attendance of 89,413 vs. 109,519 the prior year across the first six men's matches.
- Declines at most venues; Cardiff's Sophia Gardens was the exception, posting the tournament's best opening attendance.
- Women's matches also saw attendance declines at five of six venues.
- New franchise owners (including IPL and Major League Cricket investors) now control ticket sales, with the ECB providing operational support only.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**Attendance figures for The Hundred dropped by 18.4% during the opening week of the current season compared to corresponding fixtures from 2025.**

The first six men’s matches drew a combined attendance of 89,413 in contrast to the 109,519 from the same six matches last year.

The Oval, Utilita Bowl, Lord’s, Edgbaston and Headingley all witnessed year-on-year declines, while Cardiff’s Sophia Gardens bucked the trend by recording a tournament-best opening attendance of 11,796.

The decline was not limited to the men’s competition, as attendances for the opening women’s matches also fell at five of the six venues.

Higher prices could be one factor behind the decline, with standard ticket prices said to have been raised to between £35 ($46/€41) and £55.

This season also marks the first under The Hundred’s new ownership model following the sale of franchise stakes earlier this year.

The sale generated £520m and valued the eight franchises at £975m, bringing in owners from the Indian Premier League and Major League Cricket while increasing player salary bands and attracting higher-profile overseas signings.

Responsibility for ticket sales has moved from the England and Wales Cricket Board (ECB) to the new franchise owners in partnership with host county clubs, although the ECB continues to provide operational support.

“We need to get the crowds in,” said SunRisers Leeds co-owner Kaviya Maran, as reported by [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/sport/cricket/article/hundred-attendances-20-percent-down-cricket-franchise-v9g0hqscj), calling the opening day at Headingley “a good beginning”.

“I see a lot of orange and that’s a great start for us. Obviously it’s a new brand for them. We just grow from here,” she added.

This year’s edition of The Hundred is set to run until August 16.
