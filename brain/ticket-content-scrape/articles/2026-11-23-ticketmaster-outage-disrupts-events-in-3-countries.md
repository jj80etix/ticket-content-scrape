# Ticketmaster Outage Disrupts Events In 3 Countries

## Summary

A Friday-night Ticketmaster service disruption locked attendees out of mobile ticket access at major events across three countries — New Orleans' Caesars Superdome (Chris Brown/Usher), Mexico City's Corona Capital Festival, and AFLW playoff games in Australia — causing delayed entry before Ticketmaster restored service and confirmed tickets were displaying normally the next day, without disclosing a cause.

### Highlights
- The outage affected fans retrieving tickets via the Ticketmaster app (not those who'd pre-downloaded tickets) at events in the US, Mexico and Australia.
- Superdome doors scheduled for 6pm saw fans stuck outside; the Chris Brown/Usher show's on-stage start was delayed to 9pm.
- Ticketmaster confirmed services "restored" the following day (Saturday) via its X support account, with no cause disclosed.

### Industry Problem
Mobile-ticket-display outages at the point of entry strand paying patrons outside venues with no fallback, directly damaging the day-of experience that primary ticketing platforms are responsible for — a reliability gap independent of fraud or pricing issues.

### Proposed Solution
No fix was detailed by Ticketmaster (cause undisclosed); for a primary platform like Etix, the takeaway is to validate offline/cached-ticket fallback paths so entry doesn't depend on a live app session — a concrete reliability improvement.

Fans were stuck waiting outside the Caesars Superdome in New Orleans as the outage prevented the display of tickets to the Chris Brown and Usher concert. Similar problems were reported at Mexico City’s Corona Capital Festival and another concert there. In Australia, there were issues with tickets to the Australian Football League Women’s playoff games.

Ticketmaster said Saturday that services had been restored but did not disclose what caused the outage.

“Tickets are now displaying properly, and other services that some fans experienced intermittent issues with earlier have returned to normal. We thank fans for their patience and thank the box office teams who worked with us to help get fans into their events,” the ticketing giant posted to its support account on X.

Doors for the Superdome show were scheduled for 6 p.m., but local TV station WWL said fans were stacking up, unable to retrieve their tickets. The concert was briefly delayed, though the Superdome posted footage of the two stars on stage at 9 p.m.. The typical on-stage time for the tour has been 2 hours and 45 minutes after doors.

The problems were confined to people who were trying to access their tickets through the app, rather than those who had previously downloaded their tickets.
