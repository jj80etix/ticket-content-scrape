# Manchester United mascot hit by ticket tout crackdown

## Summary

Manchester United's anti-touting crackdown has restricted ticketing accounts belonging to genuine supporters, including a season-ticket holder's six-year-old son, after the club's fraud-detection system flagged shared-device/shared-connection usage among a 19-person friend-and-family group as signs of centralized, tout-like control. The club says its detection has recovered over 14,000 tickets worth £1m+ this past season but acknowledges some innocent fans have been caught by the same heuristics.

### Key Points
- Detection heuristics flagged: repeated access to one account, plus an "extremely high" number of associated accounts sharing devices/connections.
- Affected family has 6 restricted accounts and is pursuing a second appeal after an initial one was denied.
- Club claims prior season's anti-touting operation recovered 14,000+ tickets worth over £1m through official channels.
- Club acknowledges the "vast majority" of flagged accounts were correctly identified but concedes some legitimate supporters were caught.

### Highlights
- Real-world false-positive rate from bot/tout-detection heuristics based on shared-device and multi-account signals.
- Appeals process exists but is described by affected fans as slow and initially unsuccessful.

### Industry Problem
Anti-bot/anti-touting detection built on account-clustering and shared-device signals inevitably catches legitimate group-buying behavior (families/friend groups coordinating seating), creating real patron friction and reputational risk even when the underlying fraud-detection goal is sound.

### Proposed Solution
The club's response (heuristic flagging + manual appeals) shows the operational cost of aggressive anti-touting: appeals volume and fan trust erosion. For a primary platform, the improvement opportunity is building higher-precision detection (e.g., verified group-seating features that don't trigger fraud flags) and a fast, transparent appeals path, rather than relying on blunt multi-account heuristics.

**A six-year-old Manchester United supporter has had restrictions placed on his season ticket as part of the club’s anti-touting crackdown.**

His mother, Katie, said she had been left unable to apply for an away game ticket, after the club claimed that repeated access to the account as well as an “extremely high” number of other ticketing accounts indicated that it was centrally controlled.

He is among fans who have had their accounts restricted by the club in a wider operation aimed at identifying [potential ticket touts](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/07/man-united-criticised-by-fan-group-as-supporters-made-to-feel-like-suspects/).

Manchester United previously said it was confident that the “vast majority” of accounts identified for misuse had been correctly flagged but acknowledged some innocent supporters might also have been affected.

Katie said 19 relatives and friends had formed a group so they could sit together in a singing section at Old Trafford.

She said she handled much of their ticket administration, including away applications, while family members helped one another manage accounts using shared devices and internet connections.

An initial appeal had been turned down but she has lodged another to challenge restrictions on six family accounts.

The club claims that similar anti-touting work last season recovered more than 14,000 tickets worth more than £1m ($1.3m/€1.1m) and returned them to supporters through official channels.
