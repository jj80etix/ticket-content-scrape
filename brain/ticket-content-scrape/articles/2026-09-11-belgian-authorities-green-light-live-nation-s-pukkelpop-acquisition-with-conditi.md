# Belgian Authorities Green Light Live Nation’s Pukkelpop Acquisition, With Conditions

## Summary

Belgium's Competition Authority closed its investigation into Live Nation's 2025 acquisition of Belgian festival Pukkelpop after Live Nation agreed to binding commitments barring it from integrating the festival into its broader promotion, booking, venue and ticketing operations for at least 10 (up to 15) years. Control over programming, bookings and — notably — ticket pricing stays with the Pukkelpop founder-controlled operating entity, monitored by an independent trustee; the deal was reviewed under EU competition law covering abuse of dominance rather than conventional merger thresholds.

### Key Points
- Commitments run at least 10 years, extendable to 15, with independent monitoring-trustee oversight.
- Pukkelpop retains control of bookings, programming, ticket pricing, suppliers, staffing and site/capacity decisions.
- Live Nation may still sell Pukkelpop market-rate, non-exclusive services, but cannot require exclusivity.
- Review relied on the EU Court of Justice's 2023 *Towercast* ruling, letting regulators scrutinize a dominant firm's acquisitions below normal merger thresholds.
- No finding of wrongdoing; the BCA's preliminary concerns (ticket pricing, programming independence, access for competing promoters/agencies) are resolved via the commitments, not litigated.

### Highlights
- Belgian Competition Authority closed its Pukkelpop review with a 10-15 year structural-separation commitment rather than blocking or unwinding the deal.
- Ticket pricing authority for Pukkelpop stays with the independent operator, not Live Nation, under the settlement.
- Relies on the *Towercast* precedent — a tool other EU regulators could use against Live Nation's below-threshold acquisitions elsewhere.

### Industry Problem
Vertical integration by a dominant promoter/ticketer (booking + venues + ticketing) raises competition concerns even in deals too small for standard merger review, creating a precedent that regulators can impose long-term structural firewalls instead of blocking acquisitions outright.

### Proposed Solution
The item describes a regulatory remedy, not a vendor product response. For an independent platform, the signal to monitor is whether other EU/national regulators replicate this *Towercast*-based below-threshold review against Live Nation, which could reshape competitive dynamics in affected markets over the coming decade.

Live Nation will retain ownership of Belgian music festival Pukkelpop, but it will be barred from folding the event into its broader live-entertainment operation for at least 10 years under binding commitments [accepted by the Belgian Competition Authority](https://www.belgiancompetition.be/sites/default/files/content/download/files/20260911_Press_release_37_BCA.pdf).

The regulator closed its investigation into the acquisition after Live Nation agreed to a sweeping operational separation that leaves control of the festival with an entity controlled by longtime Pukkelpop founder Chokri Mahassine. The arrangement can remain in place for as long as 15 years if the initial 10-year period is extended.

The result stops short of forcing Live Nation to sell Pukkelpop. Instead, it directly limits the company’s ability to integrate one of Belgium’s largest festivals with its other businesses in promotion, artist booking, venues and ticketing.

Under the commitments, the Mahassine-controlled festival operation will retain authority over artist bookings and programming, ticket prices, suppliers, staffing, the festival site, dates, capacity and staging, among other major operating decisions.

Prosecutor General Damien Gérard said the commitments were intended to preserve the “diversity and independence” of the festival sector while keeping Pukkelpop a distinct event with its own artistic identity and organizational autonomy.

## Investigation Followed Live Nation’s 2025 Takeover

Pukkelpop and Live Nation announced the ownership change in October 2025, presenting it as a continuation of a relationship stretching back more than 40 years. The festival said at the time that Live Nation would become its ultimate parent company while Mahassine, the other founders and the existing Pukkelpop team would continue running day-to-day operations.

The at the direction of Belgian Economy Minister David Clarinval.

At the time, the authority described Live Nation as operating across much of the country’s live-entertainment chain. Its activities included artist booking, major music festivals, concert venues and ticket sales through Ticketmaster. The BCA identified Pukkelpop as one of Belgium’s five largest festivals, with capacity of roughly 250,000 attendees.

The acquisition was especially notable because it did not meet the normal financial thresholds requiring merger review in Belgium or at the European Union level.

Instead, regulators examined the deal under competition laws covering restrictive agreements and abuse of a dominant position, relying in part on the European Court of Justice’s 2023 *Towercast* ruling. That decision opened a path for national regulators to examine certain acquisitions that fall below conventional merger-review thresholds when an already dominant company’s expansion may itself raise competition concerns.

The latest action therefore is not a conventional merger clearance. The BCA investigated the completed transaction and agreed to close that investigation after Live Nation offered commitments designed to resolve the regulator’s preliminary concerns.

Nor did the settlement produce a final finding that Live Nation violated competition law. The commitments resolve the investigation without an admission of wrongdoing.

## Ticket Pricing, Programming Had Emerged as Key Concerns

By this spring, the BCA’s concerns had become more explicit.

In April, Gérard said investigators were examining whether the takeover could interfere with Pukkelpop’s independent operation, including decisions involving programming and ticket pricing. The authority was also considering temporary measures that would keep the festival separate from Live Nation while the broader investigation continued.

The final commitments effectively turn that short-term concern into a much longer firewall.

For the ticketing market, the price provision is particularly notable. Control over Pukkelpop’s ticket pricing will remain with the independent festival operation rather than shifting to Live Nation as a consequence of its ownership.

Similarly, services purchased from Live Nation must remain non-exclusive, preventing ownership alone from requiring the festival to rely exclusively on other parts of the Live Nation ecosystem.

Those requirements target the same vertical-integration issues that prompted the investigation in the first place: whether ownership across several pieces of the live-event business can give a large company additional leverage over artists, competing promoters, service providers and ultimately consumers.

The commitments will remain binding for at least a decade, with independent oversight throughout that period and the possibility of another five-year extension. Live Nation gets to keep its ownership of Pukkelpop, but for the foreseeable future, Belgian regulators are requiring that ownership and operational control remain two very different things.
