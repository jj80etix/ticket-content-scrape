# Oasis Unveils 2027 Tour as Ticketmaster Faces New Rules From Live ’25 Onsale

## Summary

Oasis announced 38 stadium dates for "Live '27," replacing the chaotic 2024 queue-based onsale that triggered a UK CMA investigation with a registration-only system: fans must register by Sept. 17 for a chance at a unique, non-transferable sale-window code, with standard UK prices fixed in advance at £71.50-£266.60. The article ties the new process directly to the CMA's formal undertakings from the Live '25 pricing controversy and notes an active Irish consumer-law case against Ticketmaster over the same tour.

### Key Points
- No general onsale or public presale; registration-only, with unique non-transferable codes and assigned sale windows (UK sales split across Sept. 25-27).
- CMA found 2024 buyers weren't adequately told about two-tier "standing" pricing, and objected to Platinum tickets priced ~2.5x standard with no extra benefits.
- Ticketmaster's resulting undertakings: 24 hours' notice of tiered pricing, visible price ranges in queue, prompt sold-out notices for cheaper inventory, no misleading "Platinum" labeling — compliance reporting to the CMA runs two years.
- Ireland's CCPC has separately filed consumer-law charges against Ticketmaster's Irish arm over Platinum-ticket presentation for the Oasis Croke Park dates; Ticketmaster says it will contest.
- Standard prices are now fixed pre-queue, but VIP/hospitality tiers beyond the £71.50-£266.60 band remain unclarified.

### Highlights
- Registration deadline: 4 p.m. BST, Sept. 17, 2026; UK onsale windows Sept. 25-27.
- UK standard pricing fixed at £71.50-£266.60 including fees — a direct reaction to 2024's dynamic/tiered pricing backlash.
- The CMA's two-year compliance-reporting undertaking is currently active and will be tested live by this onsale.
- Ireland's CCPC has brought formal consumer-law charges tied to the same tour's prior Croke Park sale.

### Industry Problem
Opaque tiered/dynamic pricing and queue chaos at scale erode fan trust and invite regulatory intervention — Oasis's 2024 sale became the reference case for both UK and Irish authorities.

### Proposed Solution
Ticketmaster's fix bundles pre-registration, fixed advance pricing, and mandated queue transparency (visible price ranges, sold-out alerts). Etix's onsale tooling for high-demand events could adopt the same pattern — fixed, disclosed pricing before queue entry and real-time inventory-tier visibility — as a proactive compliance and trust move rather than a reactive one.

![Oasis announced UK, EU and U.S. dates for 2027 Monday morning. (Graphic via Oasis website).](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Oasis-27-graphic.webp)

Oasis announced UK, EU and U.S. dates for 2027 Monday morning. (Graphic via Oasis website).

Oasis will return to the road in 2027 for 38 stadium shows across the UK, Ireland, mainland Europe and the United States — but fans hoping to attend will face a substantially different ticket-buying process from the chaotic onsale that accompanied the band’s 2025 reunion.

There will be no general onsale and no other public presales for Oasis Live ’27. Instead, anyone who wants a chance to buy tickets must [register by 4 p.m. BST Thursday, Sept. 17](https://oasismusic.lnk.to/L27Registration). Successful registrants will receive a unique, non-transferable code giving them access to a designated ticket-sale window. Even members of the Oasis mailing list must register.

For the UK dates, ticket sales will be spread across Friday, Sept. 25; Saturday, Sept. 26; and Sunday, Sept. 27, with three separate purchase windows each day. Fans selected through the registration process will be told which window they can access and at what time. Organizers say standard ticket prices will be fixed between £71.50 and £266.60, including fees.

That last detail is particularly notable two years after the original Oasis reunion sale became one of the highest-profile ticket-pricing controversies in recent memory.

Oasis Live ’27 will begin May 21 with the first of five shows at Celtic Park in Glasgow. The band will then play 11 hometown concerts at Manchester’s Etihad Stadium in June before heading through Munich, Barcelona, Amsterdam, Paris and Rome. The U.S. portion consists of three shows at Gillette Stadium outside Boston and three at Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas. Oasis will finish with two September shows at Slane Castle in Ireland and four at Knebworth, marking a return to the site of the band’s landmark 1996 concerts.

Richard Ashcroft, who was among the opening acts on the Live ’25 UK run, will appear as special guest at all 38 shows. Manchester is the only market on the newly announced itinerary that Oasis also played during Live ’25.

The new registration-only system is being pitched as an effort to make the process smoother and keep tickets away from organized resellers. The tour announcement says the system was introduced to reduce queue times and give “genuine fans” a better chance to purchase tickets. Receiving a registration code, however, only provides access to a sale window; it does not itself guarantee that tickets will remain available.

It is a strikingly different approach from the August 2024 sale for Live ’25, when millions of fans entered lengthy online queues and many discovered only after waiting that less-expensive standing tickets had sold out.

The UK Competition and Markets Authority [subsequently investigated Ticketmaster’s handling of the sale](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/09/cma-secures-ticketmaster-pricing-changes-after-oasis-debacle/). The regulator found that buyers had not been adequately informed that standing tickets were being sold at two predetermined price levels, with the higher-priced inventory appearing after the cheaper tier sold out. It also objected to “Platinum” tickets being priced at nearly 2.5 times some standard tickets despite offering no additional benefits over seats in comparable areas.

Importantly, the CMA said it did not find evidence that Ticketmaster used an algorithm to raise Oasis ticket prices in real time based on demand, despite the sale frequently being described as an example of “dynamic pricing.” Instead, the regulator focused on how the predetermined pricing tiers and ticket descriptions were communicated to fans.

Ticketmaster ultimately agreed to a series of formal undertakings. The company must give fans at least 24 hours’ notice when tiered pricing will be used, display available price ranges to customers entering ticket queues, quickly tell buyers when cheaper inventory sells out and avoid labels that suggest a more expensive ticket offers additional benefits when it does not. Ticketmaster also agreed to report its compliance to the CMA for two years. The commitments were made without an admission of wrongdoing or liability.

Those requirements remain in place as Oasis prepares for its next UK ticket sale. If Ticketmaster is among the platforms handling Live ’27 inventory, the Oasis tour would provide an unusually direct test of reforms created in response to the band’s own previous onsale.

The band has already removed one of the biggest sources of uncertainty from the previous sale by announcing that standard prices will be fixed before buyers enter the queue. Still worth clarifying is exactly what Oasis means by “standard” tickets: the announcement does not by itself establish whether VIP, hospitality or other premium packages will be offered separately from the £71.50-to-£266.60 range.

The Live ’25 controversy also remains active in Ireland. Earlier this month, Ireland’s Competition and Consumer Protection Commission [brought two alleged consumer-law offenses against Ticketmaster’s Irish operation over the sale of Oasis tickets for Croke Park](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/ticketmaster-oasis-ireland-consumer-law-charges/). The charges concern the presentation of Platinum tickets and ticket-price information. Ticketmaster has said it will contest the allegations.

Monday’s announcement also sheds new light on the considerable speculation that preceded Live ’27.

A purported 42-date Oasis tour schedule circulated ahead of the announcement. With the official itinerary now available, the document appears to have been extraordinarily accurate: all 38 concerts Oasis announced Monday were included on the sheet with the same dates.

Only four dates from the leaked schedule were left off the official Live ’27 announcement: July 6 at Allianz Arena in Munich, July 20 at Stade de France in Paris, and Sept. 25 and 26 at Knebworth.

That does not establish that any of the four shows will ultimately happen. Tour planners routinely hold additional dates that never become concerts. But given the accuracy of the remainder of the document — and [Oasis’ history of expanding Live ’25 after what the band called “unprecedented demand”](https://www.ticketnews.com/2024/08/oasis-adds-more-tour-dates-amid-unprecedented-demand/) — those four dates are worth watching if the initial Live ’27 allocation sells quickly.
