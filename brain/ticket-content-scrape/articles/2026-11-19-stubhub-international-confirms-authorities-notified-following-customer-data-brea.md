# StubHub International Confirms Authorities Notified Following Customer Data Breach

## Summary

StubHub International (the Spain-based Ticketbis entity, unrelated to the North American StubHub business) confirmed it notified authorities and affected customers after unauthorized access to its systems between late October and early November 2026 exposed names, emails, phone numbers and country/region settings. The company says it found no evidence passwords, payment details, or exposed data were misused, and that an investigation with outside cybersecurity specialists is ongoing. The disclosure follows a separate October 2026 Ticketmaster Brazil breach, adding to a string of ticketing-sector data incidents this year.

### Key Points
- Breach window: late October–early November 2026; exposed data limited to names, emails, phone numbers, country/region (no confirmed password/payment exposure).
- StubHub International is operated by Ticketbis, S.L. and is a fully separate company from StubHub Inc. (North America) and from viagogo, following the UK CMA-mandated 2021 divestiture.
- Authorities have been notified (unspecified which) and affected customers directly contacted; scope (account count, markets, buyer vs. seller impact) remains undisclosed.
- StubHub International itself warned customers of phishing risk from the exposed contact details.
- Follows an October 2026 Ticketmaster Brazil breach via a third-party analytics system — no confirmed link between the two incidents.

### Highlights
- Exposed data: names, emails, phone numbers, country/region settings — no passwords or payment data confirmed accessed.
- Breach window late Oct–early Nov 2026; customer notifications began Nov. 10; public confirmation Nov. 19.
- Second notable ticketing-sector breach disclosure in two months (after Ticketmaster Brazil, Oct. 2026).

### Industry Problem
Patron trust in ticketing platforms is repeatedly tested by data-security incidents; even "contact info only" breaches create real phishing/identity-theft exposure for buyers and sellers, and the ambiguity around scope and notification timing (GDPR's 72-hour window is referenced but not confirmed met) compounds the trust problem.

### Proposed Solution
The article describes containment and customer notification but no new security tooling. For Etix, the actionable response is proactive: audit third-party analytics/vendor integrations (the vector in the Ticketmaster Brazil case) for excess PII exposure, and have a pre-drafted customer phishing-warning communication ready so any future incident response is faster and clearer than what's described here.

StubHub International has confirmed that it notified authorities and contacted affected customers following a security breach that may have exposed personal information belonging to users of the international ticket resale marketplace.

In a statement provided directly to TicketNews on Thursday, November 19, the company acknowledged unauthorized access to its systems but said it had found no evidence that passwords or payment details were compromised or that exposed information had been used fraudulently.

“We recently detected unauthorised access to certain systems on our platform. We acted quickly to contain it, have notified the relevant authorities and have contacted affected customers directly. To date, we have found no evidence that passwords or payment details were accessed, or that any information has been used fraudulently. We sincerely apologise for any concern this may cause to any of our customers,” a StubHub International spokesperson told TicketNews.

The company did not disclose how many accounts were affected, identify the authorities it notified or specify when the intrusion was discovered.

The notices do not specify how attackers gained access to the systems or whether the affected information was copied or removed. It also remains unclear whether the incident involved ticket buyers, professional sellers or both.

StubHub International’s confirmation that authorities have been notified adds a new detail to the public record, although the company did not identify the agencies involved or the timing of those notifications.

Under the European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation, companies generally must report qualifying personal data breaches to the relevant supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of them.

However, the available information does not establish when StubHub International first became aware of the breach or when any regulatory notifications were submitted.

## StubHub International Operates Separately From U.S. StubHub

Despite sharing the StubHub name, StubHub International is a separate business from the publicly traded StubHub company operating in North America.

The international operation was sold to investment firm Digital Fuel Capital in September 2021 after the United Kingdom’s Competition and Markets Authority required viagogo to divest StubHub’s business outside the United States and Canada following their merger.

The companies have different owners, management teams and operating platforms.

On November 17, the North American StubHub business [issued its own public statement](https://newsroom.stubhub.com/2026/11/17/stubhub-international-data-breach/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) emphasizing that distinction after reports of the security incident circulated.

The company said its teams had found no indication that StubHub Inc. or viagogo systems or customer data were affected by the intrusion.

The corporate distinction is particularly relevant because the companies continue to operate competing ticket resale services under different brands in international markets.

As [TicketNews reported in June 2025](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/06/cma-clears-viagogo-buy-stubhub-brand-rights-in-22-non-uk-markets/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), British regulators subsequently allowed viagogo to reacquire certain StubHub trademarks and domain names in 22 markets outside the U.K. and Europe. That transaction did not transfer StubHub International’s customer data or operating systems.

## Potential Phishing Risks for Ticket Buyers and Sellers

Although investigators have not identified evidence that payment information or account passwords were accessed, the potential exposure of customer contact details creates other security concerns.

Names, email addresses and telephone numbers can be used to construct convincing fraudulent communications impersonating legitimate businesses.

In the ticketing industry, such messages could involve fake ticket delivery notices, event cancellations, refunds, account verification requests or payment disputes.

Professional ticket sellers could face additional risks involving fraudulent payout notices, listing restrictions or requests to update account information.

StubHub International specifically warned customers in its notification that compromised contact information could potentially be used in phishing attempts, fraud or identity theft through emails, messages or telephone calls.

There is currently no confirmed evidence that such activity has resulted from this incident.

The company has advised customers to remain cautious about unexpected communications requesting personal information, passwords or banking details, and to avoid suspicious links or requests for verification codes.

Users who receive questionable communications can verify their account status directly through the marketplace’s official website rather than following links in unsolicited messages.

The disclosure follows other cybersecurity incidents involving major ticketing companies. In October, [TicketNews reported that Brazil’s data protection authority confirmed receiving a breach notification from Ticketmaster](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/ticketmaster-brazil-data-breach-2026/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), involving customer information potentially exposed through a third-party analytics system.

That incident involved different systems and potentially more extensive personal information, including identification records and ticket-purchase details. No connection has been established between the incidents.

For StubHub International, several important questions remain unresolved, including how many customers were affected, which geographic markets were involved and whether information associated with professional seller accounts was compromised.

The company’s ongoing investigation has also not publicly established the method of intrusion or whether additional customer information was accessed beyond the contact details identified in its notifications.
