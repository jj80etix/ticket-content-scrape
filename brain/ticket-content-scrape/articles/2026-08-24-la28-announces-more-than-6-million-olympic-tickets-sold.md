# LA28 Announces More Than 6 Million Olympic Tickets Sold

## Summary

LA28 has sold more than 6 million tickets for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics after its Visa presale and second ticket drop, with most available inventory now purchased. The remaining 2028 Games and Paralympic inventory will be released in 2027 through a first-come, first-served sale that drops the ticket-draw registration and per-fan purchase caps used in the first two drops.

### Highlights
- 6M+ tickets sold across two presale drops; organizers call it a demand signal ahead of 2027's final release.
- 2027 sale removes purchase limits (including for prior buyers) and drops the pre-registration/draw step in favor of straight first-come, first-served access.
- An LA28 ID account will still be required to purchase; 2027 also marks the start of Paralympic ticket sales.
- LA28 confirmed plans to launch a verified ticket resale program in 2027.

### Industry Problem
At Olympic scale, a lottery/draw-based access model creates friction and uncertainty for fans; LA28's shift to first-come-first-served for the final tranche signals a bet that direct-access selling can handle Olympic-level demand better than registration gating — a live test case for onsale scalability at the largest possible scale.

### Proposed Solution
LA28 itself is the "solution" here — moving to FCFS with no purchase caps and pairing it with a verified resale channel launching in 2027. Worth monitoring for Etix: how LA28's verified-resale program is structured (a primary platform running its own controlled resale) is a template worth watching for how a primary ticketer can own the secondary transaction rather than cede it to third parties.

More than six million tickets have been sold for the 2028 Olympic Games in Los Angeles, with organizers saying most of the available ticket inventory has now been purchased.

The latest sales figure was announced after the conclusion of LA28’s Visa presale and second ticket drop. The Games are set to bring thousands of athletes to Los Angeles and surrounding areas in 2028.

LA28 chairperson and president Casey Wasserman shared, “The demand we’ve seen across both drops is a powerful signal that the world is energised for LA28. We’re grateful to every fan who participated, and we’re just getting started—2027 will bring more inventory, more access and more ways to be part of the greatest sporting event the world has seen.”

Fans who have not yet purchased tickets will have another opportunity next year. LA28 plans to release the remaining tickets for every Olympic sport in 2027 through a first-come, first-served sale.
