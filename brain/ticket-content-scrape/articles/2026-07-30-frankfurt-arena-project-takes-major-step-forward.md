# Frankfurt arena project takes major step forward

## Summary

Frankfurt's proposed 15,200-capacity P9 multi-purpose arena advanced with the appointment of Populous as lead designer, following a Europe-wide tender. The venue, to be built next to Deutsche Bank Park as part of a larger stadium-campus expansion, targets a build-contract procurement process in Q3 2027 and joins several other German arena projects currently in development.

### Key Points
- Populous selected as lead designer for Frankfurt's 15,200-capacity P9 arena, beating four other shortlisted bidders in a Europe-wide tender run by developer Sportpark Stadion Frankfurt (SSF).
- The arena will sit adjacent to the 60,000-capacity Deutsche Bank Park within a larger campus that will also include a swimming pool, tennis and volleyball facilities, a winter sports hall, and a new railway station.
- Populous's current scope covers architectural planning, engineering, and regulatory/building-code work; detailed façade design will be developed later.
- The build-contract procurement process is expected to begin in Q3 2027.
- The project follows a stalled 2019 Katz Group arena proposal near Frankfurt Airport; the city council approved the current P9 project in principle in 2025.
- Other German arena projects are also underway, including a Live Nation-backed Munich arena targeting a 2029 opening and an EU-funded €52M arena in Würzburg.

Frankfurt’s P9 multi-purpose arena project has taken a notable step forward with the appointment of venue leader Populous as the 15,200-capacity space’s lead designer.

The company behind the proposed arena, Sportpark Stadion Frankfurt (SSF), launched a Europe-wide tender for the facility’s general planning services back in February, with Populous beating four other shortlisted bidders.

The venue, which would host concerts, events, and be home to sports teams, is set to be located adjacent to the 60,000-capacity Deutsche Bank Park as part of SSF’s push to expand the development of the stadium campus. The greater precinct is also set to include a swimming pool, a tennis complex, a volleyball court, a winter sports hall, and its own railway station.

“Frankfurt is an international city for sport and live entertainment,” says Frankfurt Mayor Mike Josef. “This multi-purpose arena will reinforce this position and strengthen our city as a destination for major events in the long term. With Populous, we have gained a partner with extensive global experience in designing world-class arenas, and we look forward to benefiting from that expertise throughout this project.”

Populous will deliver the venue’s architectural planning, engineering, and building services, alongside developing the planning, regulatory, and building code matters around the project. The detailed architectural design and arena’s façade are not part of the current scope and will be developed later.

Declan Sharkey, global director and senior principal at Populous, says: “We are proud to have been appointed to provide lead design services for the exciting P-9 Arena Project in Frankfurt. This will be a state-of-the-art, multi-purpose venue that will bring the best of sport and entertainment to the city. We look forward to bringing our global design expertise to create a world-class sustainable venue that enhances the fan and user experience for every single visitor.”

> “Our ambition is to deliver an arena that performs exceptionally throughout its operational life”

From here, the various stakeholders will enter the next stage of planning, developing the conceptual and technical foundations for the multi-purpose arena. The procurement process for the build contract is expected in Q3 2027.

“Our ambition is to deliver an arena that performs exceptionally throughout its operational life,” says SSF MD Moritz Schneider. “Achieving this requires a planning approach that prioritises quality, functionality and efficiency from the very beginning. Populous brings outstanding international arena expertise, and we are confident this experience will be a major asset in the successful delivery of the project.”

Frankfurt’s biggest indoor live music venue is currently the 13,500-cap Festhalle. In 2019, one of Canada’s largest private firms, The Katz Group of Companies, [revealed its ambition](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2019/09/europe-second-largest-arena-dome-frankfurt/) to build the largest indoor arena in Germany next to Frankfurt Airport, but the project stalled. City council approved this project back in [2025](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/01/frankfurt-arena-plans-approved-in-principle/).

Separate German arena projects are also underway in [Munich](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/01/live-nation-backed-munich-arena-project-takes-step-forward/), backed by Live Nation and on track for a 2029 opening, while the EU recently cleared [€52 million](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2231686/eu-clears-e52m-german-funding-for-wurzburg-arena-project-limits-impact) of public funding to support a [7,000-cap multipurpose complex](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2024/10/plans-for-new-german-arena-receive-funding-boost/) in Würzburg.

At the arena level, Populous’s portfolio includes Manchester’s Co-op Live, Seattle’s Climate Pledge Arena, Dubai’s Coca-Cola Arena, and France’s LDLC Arena, to name a few.
