# Music Icon Dolly Parton Passes Away at 80

## Summary

Dolly Parton died August 25, 2026 in Nashville at age 80, her family announced. TicketNews's obituary covers her seven-decade career from the Grand Ole Opry to sold-out arenas worldwide and her growth into one of entertainment's most recognizable brands.

### Key Points
- Parton died peacefully in Nashville on Aug. 25, 2026; cause of death not disclosed.
- Death announced by her family, including nephew Bryan Seaver.
- Obituary spans her career from Grand Ole Opry beginnings to global arena tours and broader entertainment/business ventures.

## Transcript

Dolly Parton, the country music icon whose seven-decade career stretched from the Grand Ole Opry to sold-out arenas around the world and ultimately into one of the entertainment industry’s most recognizable brands, died Tuesday in Nashville. She was 80.

Parton’s family announced her death on August 25, saying she died peacefully in Nashville. Her nephew Bryan Seaver also announced the news in a video shared through Parton’s social media accounts. No cause of death was disclosed.  
At Parton’s request, a small private service will be held for immediate family, according to the announcement. The family asked fans wishing to honor her to support Dolly Parton’s Imagination Library in lieu of sending flowers.

Parton’s death closes one of the longest and most consequential careers in American entertainment, spanning country music, film, television, live performance, theater, publishing and a collection of businesses centered around the celebrity and cultural identity she built over generations.

Born January 19, 1946, in Sevier County, Tennessee, Parton grew up as one of 12 children in the Great Smoky Mountains. She began performing on local radio and television as a child and appeared on the Grand Ole Opry stage at 13 before moving to Nashville after high school.

She signed with Monument Records in 1965 and joined *The Porter Wagoner Show* in 1967, a partnership that helped establish Parton as a national country music star. She became a member of the Grand Ole Opry in 1969.

By the 1970s, Parton had established a solo career built around songs including “Joshua,” “Coat of Many Colors,” “Jolene” and “I Will Always Love You.” The latter was written as Parton prepared to leave Wagoner’s television show and became a No. 1 country hit for Parton in both 1974 and 1982. Whitney Houston’s 1992 recording for *The Bodyguard* later turned the song into one of the world’s most recognizable pop ballads.

Parton’s reach increasingly extended beyond country music during the late 1970s and 1980s. “Here You Come Again” crossed into pop, while her starring role alongside Jane Fonda and Lily Tomlin in the 1980 film *9 to 5* produced another signature song and established her as a movie star.

She subsequently appeared in films including *The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas*, *Steel Magnolias* and *Straight Talk*, while continuing a recording and touring career that eventually carried her through arenas and major venues across North America, Europe and Australia.

Parton remained a major live draw well into the later decades of her career. Her 2014 performance at England’s Glastonbury Festival attracted an audience estimated at 140,000, one of the largest crowds of her career. Between 2006 and 2016, she mounted a series of major international tours.

That live-entertainment legacy extended well beyond her own concerts.

In 1986, Parton partnered in the transformation of the Silver Dollar City amusement park in Pigeon Forge, Tennessee, into Dollywood. The attraction grew into the centerpiece of a collection of tourism businesses that now draw millions of visitors annually to East Tennessee.

Dollywood became inseparable from both Parton’s public identity and the tourism economy of the region where she grew up, expanding over the decades with hotels, dinner attractions and increasingly ambitious rides and entertainment offerings.

Parton had continued working on new entertainment projects through the final year of her life.

A stage musical based on her story, *Dolly: A True Original Musical*, was [scheduled to arrive on Broadway in December 2026](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/dolly-partons-dolly-a-true-original-musical-sets-broadway-run/) after a developmental production in Nashville. Parton wrote the show’s music and co-wrote its book. Her official site had previously described the production as an autobiographical musical built around both her existing songs and new material.

She had also remained connected to live music through *Threads: My Songs in Symphony*, a multimedia orchestral production featuring her catalog that expanded into additional U.S. cities in 2026.

Parton had experienced health problems during the final portion of her career and had reduced some public appearances, [including calling off a planned residency stretch in Las Vegas earlier this year](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/dolly-parton-calls-off-vegas-residency-cites-health-issues/). Just days before her death, she discussed recent health issues while speaking about several projects still in development. She had also recently appeared virtually to accept the ACM Honors Poet’s Award.

Her family did not connect those health issues to her death, and Tuesday’s announcement did not disclose a cause.

Beyond entertainment, Parton’s most enduring institution may be Dolly Parton’s Imagination Library, the literacy program she established in 1995 in honor of her father, who could not read or write. The program has distributed more than 300 million books to children, according to her estate.

Parton’s recording career ultimately produced 25 No. 1 songs on Billboard’s country charts and more than 100 million albums sold worldwide, according to her official biography. She received 11 Grammy Awards, including a Lifetime Achievement Award, and was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1999 and Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2022.

Her husband of nearly 60 years, Carl Dean, died in 2025.

Parton’s family encouraged fans and friends to leave tributes through her official social media accounts and website.

For an artist whose career began singing as a child in East Tennessee and eventually encompassed recordings, movies, international tours, theme parks, Broadway and one of the country’s largest children’s literacy programs, the scale of Parton’s legacy reached far beyond the country music business that first made her famous.
