# NASCAR Bringing Sold-Out Coronado Street Race Back in 2027, Plans Military Base Rotation

## Summary

NASCAR will return to Naval Base Coronado in San Diego on Aug. 1, 2027 after its inaugural 2026 street-racing weekend sold out general-admission tickets, and the sanctioning body plans to rotate the military-base race concept to a different installation starting 2028. The announcement came alongside NASCAR's full 2027 national-series schedule, which also brings the Clash back to Daytona, restores a points-paying Dover Cup race, and shifts the All-Star Race to North Wilkesboro.

### Key Points
- 2027 Coronado weekend: O'Reilly Series race July 31, Cup Series race Aug. 1; will serve as the championship round of the five-race In-Season Challenge.
- 2026 inaugural event sold out GA tickets for both race days; NASCAR is focusing 2027 improvements on ingress/egress, concessions, and sightlines.
- NASCAR plans a rotating military-base race starting 2028 at a different installation/branch, aimed at markets without existing series presence.
- Craftsman Truck Series will not return to Coronado, moving instead to Canadian Tire Motorsport Park (Ontario) in Sept. 2027.
- Other 2027 schedule notes: Clash returns to Daytona (first time since 2021), Dover regains a points race, All-Star Race moves to North Wilkesboro, Bowman Gray Stadium gets its first national-series points race since 1971.

NASCAR will return to Naval Base Coronado in 2027 after the inaugural San Diego street-racing weekend generated sold-out general admission crowds, while the sanctioning body is already planning to take the military-base concept to other installations beginning in 2028.

The NASCAR Cup Series [will race at the active Navy base on Aug. 1, 2027](https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2026/08/25/nascar-will-return-to-naval-base-coronado-during-2027-season/), with the NASCAR O’Reilly Auto Parts Series joining the weekend for a July 31 event. The return was confirmed as NASCAR finalized schedules for all three of its national series this week.

The decision follows strong ticket demand for NASCAR’s first event at Coronado in June. General admission tickets for both Saturday’s O’Reilly Auto Parts Series race and Sunday’s Cup Series race sold out ahead of the weekend, with NASCAR saying shortly before the event that only a limited amount of premium hospitality inventory remained available.

That response appears to have helped turn what was initially conceived as a special event connected to America’s 250th anniversary into a repeat stop on the NASCAR calendar.

The 2026 event marked the first NASCAR race held on an active U.S. military base and featured all three national series racing on the temporary Qualcomm Circuit. The 3.4-mile, 16-turn street course wound through Naval Base Coronado with views of San Diego and military infrastructure providing a backdrop unlike NASCAR’s traditional speedways.

NASCAR Chief Operating Officer Ben Kennedy called the inaugural weekend “a tremendously successful event” immediately following the race and said this week that officials subsequently conducted an extensive review of the event before committing to another year.

While NASCAR expects the circuit itself to remain largely unchanged in 2027, Kennedy said organizers are concentrating on improving ingress and egress, concessions and sightlines for spectators. NASCAR views lessons from Coronado as potentially applicable to future temporary-course events elsewhere.

The 2027 weekend will begin July 30 with a Navy Community Night featuring O’Reilly Series practice and qualifying. Access that day will be restricted to Navy personnel and members of the Coronado community, followed by the O’Reilly race Saturday and the Cup Series event Sunday.

The Craftsman Truck Series, which competed at Coronado in 2026, will not return. Instead, the series will head to Canadian Tire Motorsport Park in Ontario in September 2027, marking its first Canadian race since 2019.

## NASCAR Eyes Other Military Bases

More significant than the second Coronado date may be NASCAR’s longer-term plan for the concept.

Kennedy said NASCAR has agreed to stage a race at another military installation in 2028, with the organization evaluating potential bases, branches of the military and geographic markets. NASCAR is particularly interested in locations where the series does not currently have a substantial presence.

The plan could effectively turn NASCAR’s military-base race into a rotating temporary event rather than establishing Coronado as a permanent annual stop.

Kennedy said the organization wants to develop a blueprint that can be applied at other installations and temporary circuits, building on NASCAR’s recent experiments with nontraditional venues including the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum, Chicago street racing and Mexico City.

That strategy also gives NASCAR another mechanism for bringing major live events into markets without the permanent racing infrastructure required for a traditional speedway. Temporary circuits carry their own logistical and cost challenges, but Coronado’s first-year ticket demand gives NASCAR evidence that unusual venues can create sufficient consumer interest to support the model.

The 2027 Coronado Cup race will also carry additional competitive significance. NASCAR has positioned the Aug. 1 event as the championship round of its five-race In-Season Challenge.

NASCAR is currently directing fans to register for presale ticket opportunities for the 2027 San Diego weekend, with additional ticketing and event details expected to be announced later.

TicketNews previously reported on NASCAR’s move to Ticketmaster as its official primary ticketing provider beginning with the 2025 season. The deal brought NASCAR-owned tracks onto Ticketmaster’s platform and integrated the NASCAR Tracks App with Ticketmaster’s technology.

## 2027 NASCAR Schedule Includes Other Major Changes

Coronado is one of several notable changes and returning events on NASCAR’s newly completed 2027 national-series schedules.

The preseason Clash will return to Daytona International Speedway on Feb. 13 for the first time since 2021, leading into the Feb. 21 Daytona 500. Dover Motor Speedway will return to hosting a points-paying Cup Series race after staging the All-Star Race in 2026, while the All-Star event shifts back to North Wilkesboro Speedway on May 23.

Richmond Raceway will open the Cup Series Chase on Sept. 11, while Watkins Glen moves into the postseason the following weekend. Homestead-Miami Speedway will again host championship weekend, with the Cup Series finale scheduled for Nov. 14.

The Craftsman Truck Series will also make a noteworthy Aug. 20 visit to Bowman Gray Stadium, giving the historic quarter-mile track its first NASCAR national-series points race since 1971.

But among the schedule changes, NASCAR’s decision to return to Coronado — and then take the military-base concept elsewhere — represents one of the more unusual expansions of the sport’s live-event footprint.

After selling out general admission inventory for its first weekend at an active military installation, NASCAR appears prepared to see whether the formula can travel.
