# Rams’ $5M Donation Sends ‘Swim Up Hill’ Students, Families To LA28 Games

## Summary

The Los Angeles Rams' $5 million donation to LA28's Community Ticket Program will fund Olympic tickets for local nonprofit Swim Up Hill, sending swimming-access students and families to the 2028 Games as part of a broader community-outreach ticket distribution effort.

### Key Points
- Swim Up Hill, an LA swim-access/water-safety nonprofit founded 2018, is among groups receiving Olympic tickets via the Rams' $5M donation to LA28's Community Ticket Program.
- Program aims to connect underserved local communities, many near Olympic venues, with in-person access to the 2028 Games.
- LA28 frames the effort as part of ensuring LA communities "see themselves reflected in the Games."​

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/LA2028olympics-1-jpg.webp)

Swim Up Hill, the Los Angeles-based organization working to expand swimming access and water safety education, will send students and families to the 2028 Olympic Games through LA28’s Community Ticket Program.

The nonprofit is among the local organizations selected to receive Olympic tickets funded by the **Los Angeles Rams’ $5 million donation** to the program, giving members of the communities it serves a chance to see the world’s top athletes compete in person.

Swim Up Hill founder and CEO Jamal Hill said the opportunity connects directly with the organization’s mission of making swimming more accessible to communities that have historically faced barriers to the sport.

“So many of the kids and families we work with have never had the chance to watch a swim meet in person, let alone the Olympics. Now some of them are going to sit in the stands for the biggest stage our sport has,” Hill said in a press release. “That’s what this work has always been about: not just teaching people to swim, but showing them they belong in these spaces, as athletes, as fans as part of the story.”

Founded in 2018, Swim Up Hill focuses on teaching swimming and water safety skills, particularly among low-to-middle-income communities of color that face disproportionate drowning risks. The organization has set a goal of teaching one million people per year to swim by the time the Olympic and Paralympic Games return to Los Angeles in 2028.

Tickets received through LA28’s program will be distributed directly to Swim Up Hill students and families, many of whom live close to venues scheduled to host Olympic competition.

“Swim Up Hill is exactly the kind of organization this program was built for,” said Erikk Aldridge, LA28 vice president of Impact & Legacy. “Jamal and his team are teaching kids and families in Los Angeles a skill that can save their lives, and now some of them will get to watch the Games that inspired that mission in the first place.”

Aldridge added that making sure Los Angeles communities see themselves reflected in the Games is central to the program’s goals.

Hill’s own competitive swimming career helped shape Swim Up Hill’s mission. After representing Team USA at the 2018 Para Swimming National Championships and later winning bronze in the 50-meter freestyle at the Tokyo 2020 Paralympic Games, he built the organization around the belief that “a true champion stands for more than just themselves.”

Swim Up Hill now reaches Southern California communities through efforts including the SoCal Swim Project, which brings swimming education into elementary schools, along with partnerships and educational initiatives such as Hill’s children’s book, *Sammy Swims*.

LA28 launched its Community Ticket Program in November 2025 with the Rams’ inaugural $5 million donation. The initiative encourages professional sports teams, philanthropists and other supporters to fund Olympic and Paralympic ticket donations for local nonprofit and community organizations.

For Swim Up Hill’s students, the program will provide more than a seat at the Games. It will give young swimmers and their families a firsthand look at the highest level of a sport the organization is working to make available to more people across Los Angeles.
