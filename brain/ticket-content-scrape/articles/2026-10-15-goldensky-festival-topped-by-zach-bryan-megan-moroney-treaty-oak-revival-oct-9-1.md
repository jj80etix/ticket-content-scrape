# GoldenSky Festival Topped by Zach Bryan, Megan Moroney, Treaty Oak Revival Oct. 9-10 In Sacramento

## Summary

Recap of the GoldenSky Festival in Sacramento (Oct. 9-10), topped by Zach Bryan, Megan Moroney, and Treaty Oak Revival.

### Key Points
- Festival recap covering a two-day Sacramento country-music event.
- Standard event-recap coverage; no ticketing/pricing/policy angle.

The country-flavored GoldenSky festival returns to Discovery Park in Sacramento Oct. 9-10 after a two-year hiatus, announcing a music lineup topped by Zach Bryan, Megan Moroney, Treaty Oak Revival, Ty Myers, and Whiskey Myers.

The event, produced by Danny Wimmer Presents, looks to build on the the event that debuted in 2022, and the news comes immediately after a record-breaking Aftershock Oct. 1-4 at the same location.

“We’re very excited for GoldenSky to come back,” says Del Williams, global head of talent at Danny Wimmer Presents. “Fans have really been hoping for it and there’s a lot of passion for it in that region, but we wanted to make sure that we had the right lineup and take a step back and reevaluate how we wanted to bring the brand back. We want it to be a true perennial, like Aftershock is, and be doing it 15, 20 years from now.”

Williams says it’s the event’s biggest lineup yet, and surely global stadium headliner Zach Bryan, who played DWP’s Bourbon & Beyond in 2024, is a huge draw. But, while decidedly leaning country, GoldenSky aims to be distinct from the traditional country music touring circuit, with a focus on up and comers and younger fanbases who consume media different from traditional country music fans.

“That’s, to me, the most exciting part about the lineup we put together is top to bottom,” said Williams, mentioning artists like Hudson Westbrook, Max Mcnown, Braxton Keith, Vincent Mason and Blake Whiten. “There’s just a lot of passion, and I’m excited about the future for each one of these artists. Artists like Noah Rinker and Mack Geiger, they’re already selling out wherever they play and only going to bigger, so I really love them in the mix. Then you have somebody like Carter Faith that was nominated for ACM Album of the Year and New Artist of the Year, and I think the time has come where she’s getting the acclaim she deserves.”

The event will have two main stages, a dance area and experiential attractions, immersive spaces, sports, and local Sacramento flavor. Passes go on sale tomorrow, Oct. 16, with a 10 a.m. SMS presale, 11am PT email subscriber presale and general on sale at 12pm PT. For the first time, offical RV, car and tent camping will be offered at the nearby Cal Expo with festival re-entry included and shuttle passes available, as well as hotel packages.

The festival is produced by DWP in partnership with Visit Sacramento, Sacramento County, as well as the City of Sacramento, where the festivals generate an approximate $54 million in local economic impact annually. The return of GoldenSky is part of a three-year commitment that will bring the event back to Discovery Park in 2027, 2028 and 2029.

[The event follows the successful Aftershock festival Oct. 1-4, which announced 180,000 fans in attendance](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/10/07/aftershock-sells-out-capping-record-breaking-festival-year-for-danny-wimmer-presents-with-more-than-1-million-attendees/), with a lineup topped by My Chemical Romance, Limp Bizkit, Pierce The Veil and Tool and more than 140 total bands performing on five main stages.

Williams says DWP is actively planning more country-centric events, as well as two new rock festivals to be announced soon.

“We will be doing more in the country space,” says Williams, mentioning momentum in the rock space as well as its talent buying division adding venue clients. “Obviously, there’s a lot of growth across the board, which can be a little overwhelming at times, but it’s exciting.”
