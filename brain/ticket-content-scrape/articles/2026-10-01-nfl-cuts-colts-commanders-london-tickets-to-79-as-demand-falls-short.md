# NFL Cuts Colts-Commanders London Tickets to £79 as Demand Falls Short

## Summary

Ticket prices for Sunday's NFL London game (Colts-Commanders) have been slashed from an initial £245 high down to £79, with resale prices as low as $62 — an 88% drop from the $500+ resale get-in price seen in a May snapshot of the same game. Early buyers who paid surge prices are reportedly frustrated, but the piece is a market/pricing-volatility recap for a single NFL game rather than a ticketing-platform, policy, or structural industry development.

### Key Points
- NFL cut primary prices from £245 (upper tier) to £115 then £79 as demand fell short of initial projections.
- Resale get-in price fell ~88%, from ~$500 in May to ~$62 by early October.
- More than 2,000 £79 tickets reportedly remained unsold roughly 10 days out.
- Early buyers at surge prices have publicly complained about the later discounts.

Ticket prices for Sunday’s Indianapolis Colts-Washington Commanders game at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium have been sharply cut, drawing complaints from fans who purchased at the initial surged prices only to see later sales at major discounts. Remaining primary tickets are reportedly available for £79, while resale prices are even lower in the days leading up to the London matchup.

The league is currently advertising lower-tier seats for the Oct. 4 game from £79.13, or about $105, while an Oct. 1 Ticket Club snapshot [showed resale member prices beginning at just $62](https://www.ticketclub.com/teams/nfl-international-series-tickets). Dozens of listings were available for $100 or less, giving late buyers options below even the NFL’s newly discounted primary-market price.

The market represents a dramatic reversal from the spring, when the same game appeared to be one of the stronger early international matchups.

In a May snapshot previously covered by TicketNews, [Colts-Commanders tickets carried a $500 resale get-in price](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/nfl-sets-record-2026-international-slate-ahead-of-full-schedule-release/) and $590 median listing price. Those early figures represented asking prices in a still-developing resale market rather than a settled measure of demand, but the current $62 entry point is nearly 88% below that initial get-in.

The NFL itself has also moved aggressively on price as kickoff approaches. The [Associated Pres](https://apnews.com/article/nfl-london-colts-commanders-ticket-price-reduced-b4fdf19231401e762bc99670f03d69e2) [s](https://apnews.com/article/nfl-london-colts-commanders-ticket-price-reduced-b4fdf19231401e762bc99670f03d69e2) [reported](https://apnews.com/article/nfl-london-colts-commanders-ticket-price-reduced-b4fdf19231401e762bc99670f03d69e2) that tickets initially sold for as much as £245, with some upper-tier seats that opened at £185 later reduced to £115 in August and ultimately £79. Ten days before the game, more than 2,000 £79 tickets appeared to remain available through Ticketmaster, the league’s official primary seller.

The league has also promoted the discounted inventory directly to fans and made tickets available through UniDays, where university students were offered 50% discounts, according to AP. The NFL did not respond to the outlet’s request for comment about the price reductions.

For consumers who waited, the late market now looks very different from both the initial onsale and the early resale market. Ticket Club’s Oct. 1 listings included a $62 member price in Section 324, tickets from $66 in several 500-level sections and multiple 200-level listings below $90. A substantial amount of inventory remained below $100.

That puts at least some resale inventory below the NFL’s own reduced price floor, illustrating how sharply ticket values can move when supply remains available close to an event.

The markdowns have been less welcome for fans who bought early. AP reported that Commanders supporter Andy Burrows paid £235 for a lower-bowl seat near the 15-yard line during a summer presale, only to see a nearby seat later sell for £79.

Another London NFL fan, Matt Cullen, told AP the late reductions showed that the game “wasn’t priced correctly in the first place.”

The NFL’s official London ticket site now explicitly promotes lower-tier Colts-Commanders seats from £79.13. The league also introduced all-in pricing for its 2026 international games, meaning its displayed ticket prices include mandatory fees from the beginning of the shopping process.

## London Demand Is Not Uniformly Weak

The softer Colts-Commanders market does not necessarily signal a broader collapse in demand for NFL games in London.

The Philadelphia Eagles-Jacksonville Jaguars game at Tottenham on Oct. 11 sold out quickly despite being priced above Colts-Commanders, according to AP. That contrast suggests the league is dealing at least partly with matchup-specific demand rather than a wholesale retreat by British NFL fans.

Still, this weekend’s game is testing a market that has been remarkably dependable for the NFL. The league has staged 12 games at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium since the building opened in 2019, and reported attendance has never fallen below 60,000. AP reported that the unsold inventory had raised the possibility that Colts-Commanders could become the first to break that streak.

The softer pricing is also arriving during the NFL’s largest international expansion yet. The league scheduled a record nine international regular-season games for 2026 across Australia, Brazil, London, Paris, Madrid, Munich and Mexico City. London remains the league’s most established overseas market, with three games scheduled in the city this month.

The increased European supply could also be giving traveling and local fans more choices. In addition to the three London games, Paris, Madrid and Munich each host an NFL regular-season game this fall.

Even within London, demand is diverging. Eagles-Jaguars at Tottenham sold quickly, while AP reported thousands of seats were also still available for the Oct. 18 Texans-Jaguars game at Wembley several weeks before kickoff.

For Colts-Commanders, however, the market has already delivered a clear advantage to fans willing to wait. A matchup that carried a $500 resale get-in during the spring can now be entered for $62 on Ticket Club, while the NFL itself is moving primary inventory at its lowest ticket category in an effort to fill Tottenham before Sunday.
