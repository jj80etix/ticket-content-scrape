# Macklemore Challenges Robert Kraft With $1M Palestine Donation as Loop Tour Fallout Continues

## Summary

Macklemore said he will donate the full $1 million he earned from Ed Sheeran's Loop Tour to organizations supporting Palestinians and challenged Patriots/Gillette Stadium owner Robert Kraft to match it. Kraft revealed Sheeran had already asked him to commit $2 million matching Sheeran's own humanitarian donation, and Sheeran now plans to approach other venue owners about joining the effort.

### Key Points
- Macklemore pledges the full $1M in Loop Tour earnings to Palestinian aid groups
- He publicly challenges Robert Kraft to match the donation
- Kraft says Sheeran already asked him to commit $2M matching Sheeran's own gift
- Sheeran plans to approach other venue owners for similar contributions
- Follows Macklemore's removal from the tour and the resulting opener walkouts

Macklemore says he will donate the entirety of the $1 million in net earnings he received from Ed Sheeran’s Loop Tour to organizations supporting Palestinians, while challenging New England Patriots and Gillette Stadium owner Robert Kraft to match the contribution.

“Whatever we disagree about, perhaps we can agree on this: Palestinian lives are worth protecting,” wrote Macklemore. “A Palestinian life is no less valuable than any other life on this earth.”

Hours later, Kraft revealed that Sheeran had already contacted him before Macklemore issued that challenge, asking Kraft to commit $2 million to match what Kraft described as Sheeran’s own donation for humanitarian aid in the region. Kraft said Sheeran now plans to approach other venue owners about joining the effort.

The development adds another turn to a controversy that has [already cost Sheeran every announced opening act on his remaining tour](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/ed-sheeran-tour-fallout-macklemore-aaron-rowe/) and prompted intense criticism of the stadium owners whose objections resulted in Macklemore’s removal. It also shows Sheeran taking a substantial humanitarian step behind the scenes even as he has defended his preference to keep explicit political advocacy out of his concerts.

At the same time, a counterreaction to the criticism surrounding Macklemore’s removal is becoming visible inside the live entertainment business — including from one of Ticketmaster’s most influential music executives.

David Marcus, Ticketmaster’s executive vice president and global head of music, reposted an X post Wednesday praising a sharply critical column from *Spiked* writer Brendan O’Neill about the Macklemore controversy. The post, from commentator Ben Green, said O’Neill “absolutely nails it again” and excerpted an argument that the more troubling element of the episode was not Macklemore being removed but the penetration of pro-Palestinian activism into spaces such as Sheeran concerts.

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Screenshot-2026-09-16-175118-470x600.png)

Marcus did not add commentary of his own, meaning the repost should not be read as a statement that Ticketmaster participated in or directed Macklemore’s removal. There remains no evidence that Ticketmaster or Marcus played any role in the promoter or stadium decisions that forced Macklemore off the tour. But the decision by Ticketmaster’s top music executive to amplify the argument is notable amid a live-industry controversy that has largely produced the opposite reaction from the other artists involved.

Macklemore announced the donation Wednesday, saying he wanted to redirect attention away from the controversy surrounding his removal and toward Palestinians affected by the conflict.

“I will be donating the entire $1 million of my net earnings from Ed Sheeran’s Loop Tour,” Macklemore wrote, saying the money would go to six organizations working to support Palestinians. He separately invited Kraft to match the $1 million contribution.

The size of that payment introduces a potentially significant business detail that remains unexplained.

Macklemore only completed two Loop Tour performances, opening Sheeran’s Sept. 4 and 5 concerts at MetLife Stadium before being removed from his remaining dates. Sheeran said Tuesday that Macklemore’s touring agreement was with promoter Messina Touring Group rather than with Sheeran personally.

It is therefore unclear whether Macklemore’s reference to $1 million in “net earnings” represents compensation for those two completed performances, guaranteed compensation under his larger support agreement, money still owed despite his removal, or some combination of payments under the contract.

The donation announcement comes just two days after [Macklemore accused Kraft of setting in motion the series of venue objections that ultimately led Messina Touring Group to remove him](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/macklemore-ed-sheeran-stadiums-cancel-shows/).

Kraft has acknowledged personally asking Sheeran to consider removing Macklemore from the Gillette Stadium shows and contacting leadership at another stadium about his concerns, while denying Macklemore’s allegation that he organized venue owners behind a coordinated ultimatum. Messina Touring Group independently confirmed that multiple venues told the promoter they would not host Sheeran concerts if Macklemore remained on the lineup.

Kraft has said his objection was not to advocacy for Palestinians itself but to what he described as Macklemore’s “broader history of antisemitic rhetoric and imagery” and material presented during the MetLife performances that Kraft believed crossed Gillette Stadium’s line against hate speech. Macklemore has rejected the characterization that his advocacy is antisemitic and has said his criticism is directed at the Israeli government rather than Jewish people.

## Kraft Says Sheeran Had Already Asked Him for $2 Million

> Kraft responded later Wednesday in a statement distributed by Gillette Stadium, saying Macklemore’s challenge came after he had already spoken with Sheeran about humanitarian aid.
> 
> “Earlier today, before Macklemore challenged me to match his donation, Ed called me and asked me to commit $2 million to match his donation to aid in the region to fight this humanitarian crisis,” Kraft said.
> 
> Kraft added that Sheeran planned to contact other venue owners and said their goal was to “continue to work together to build bridges.”
> 
> The wording of Kraft’s statement indicates that Sheeran is himself committing $2 million and asked Kraft for an equivalent contribution, an interpretation also reported by Boston media Wednesday evening. Sheeran had not separately detailed the donation publicly as of publication.
> 
> Kraft also used the statement to push back against suggestions that his objection to Macklemore reflected opposition to Palestinian humanitarian concerns. He said he has supported programs creating economic opportunities for Palestinians and initiatives bringing young Palestinians and Israelis together.

## Ticketmaster Music Chief Amplifies Counterargument

Until Wednesday, most of the highly visible response from musicians connected to the Loop Tour had gone decidedly in Macklemore’s direction.

Aaron Rowe, Lukas Graham and Finneas all withdrew from scheduled opening slots after his removal, while Beoga — the Irish band performing a portion of Sheeran’s own set — also left the tour. Their statements variously framed Macklemore’s removal as the silencing of artists advocating for Palestinians and objected to wealthy venue owners having the power to determine which political views artists could express.

Marcus’s repost surfaces a very different view from a particularly powerful corner of the concert business.

O’Neill’s *Spiked* column argues that Sheeran’s audience should be able to attend a pop concert without being drawn into an Israel-Palestine political campaign and attacks the subsequent pressure on Sheeran for declining to make similar political statements himself. The column describes Macklemore’s removal as less troubling than what O’Neill characterizes as the growing expectation that public spaces and entertainment events participate in pro-Palestinian activism.

Marcus’s amplification is meaningful because his influence at Ticketmaster extends well beyond ordinary corporate communications.

When Ticketmaster brought Marcus back to the company in 2017, it placed him in charge of a newly consolidated music division encompassing artist services, venue and promoter ticketing and TicketWeb. Ticketmaster said Marcus had previously built its artist-services division and helped develop early versions of products that became Platinum and VIP ticketing. His portfolio later expanded globally.

Marcus also became one of Ticketmaster’s principal public advocates for Verified Fan and registration-based ticket distribution, technologies intended in part to prevent professional resellers from acquiring tickets during major onsales. TicketNews has previously traced the company’s broader move toward so-called “slow ticketing” — including staggered distribution, registration systems and premium pricing intended to capture more of the market value of high-demand tickets — in significant part to strategies Marcus advocated as Ticketmaster’s music operation evolved.

His company also has a direct commercial relationship with nearly the entire remaining stadium itinerary at issue. Sheeran’s official tour information identifies Ticketmaster as the primary ticketing company for seven of the eight remaining stadium concerts from which Macklemore was removed. The lone exception is the Oct. 24 show at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas, where SeatGeek is the official ticketing provider.

None of those relationships establish any Ticketmaster involvement in the decision to remove Macklemore. The available record continues to point to stadium operators and Messina Touring Group as the relevant decision-makers. But Marcus occupying such a central position in how major concert tours are ticketed and distributed makes his decision to publicly amplify one side of the dispute considerably more consequential than an ordinary social-media repost.

## Loop Tour Fallout Continues

Marcus’s repost is also one of the clearer examples so far of a counterargument emerging after two days in which the public artist response has overwhelmingly centered on Macklemore’s removal.

That does not establish broader public opinion about the dispute. Social-media reaction is not representative polling, and the controversy has produced vocal reactions in both directions. But the artist response surrounding the tour itself has been unusually one-sided: every announced remaining support act has now chosen to leave rather than continue following Macklemore’s removal.

Macklemore’s $1 million pledge now takes the dispute back directly to Kraft. The rapper said that whatever disagreements remain between the two men, protecting Palestinian lives should be common ground.

Sheeran, meanwhile, has said Macklemore’s removal was the promoter’s decision and that he unsuccessfully attempted to broker a compromise with the affected venues. He has defended his preference to keep his concerts politically neutral, saying audiences include people from widely different backgrounds and that he wants his shows to function as a place where they can come together.

Kraft’s statement Wednesday adds an important qualification to that posture: Sheeran’s unwillingness to make the Israel-Palestine conflict part of his concerts apparently has not prevented him from committing substantial money to humanitarian relief. If Kraft’s account is accurate, Sheeran has pledged $2 million himself and is now seeking additional contributions from the same venue-owner network drawn into the controversy over Macklemore.

The Loop Tour remains scheduled to resume Saturday at Lincoln Financial Field in Philadelphia. As of Wednesday evening, Sheeran and Messina Touring Group had not announced replacement support acts following this week’s departures.
