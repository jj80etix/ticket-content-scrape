# US events axed as ‘dangerous’ storm batters East Coast

## Summary

A powerful nor'easter storm forced cancellation of a slate of major US East Coast events over the weekend, including Maryland's Oceans Calling festival, New York's Global Citizen Festival (a first in its 14-year history) and All Things Go, plus headline stadium shows by Ed Sheeran and AC/DC. Organizers cited safety concerns over flooding and high winds.

### Key Points
- Oceans Calling (Maryland, C3 Presents) cancelled after site conditions deteriorated.
- Global Citizen Festival in Central Park cancelled for the first time in 14 years.
- Ed Sheeran's two Gillette Stadium shows and AC/DC's MetLife Stadium show cancelled.
- AC/DC's cancellation is the tour's second weather-related one, after storm damage to Toronto's Rogers Centre.
- Follows a similar washout of Goldenvoice's inaugural Ocean Way Festival weeks earlier.

## US events axed as ‘dangerous’ storm batters East Coast

Maryland's Oceans Calling, New York's Global Citizens Festival and All Things Go, and headline shows from Ed Sheeran and AC/DC were cancelled

![AC/DC](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ACDC.png)

AC/DC

A slate of festivals and events on the US East Coast was cancelled over the weekend as a powerful nor’easter storm battered the region, including Maryland’s Oceans Calling and New York’s Global Citizen Festival and All Things Go.

Among the shelved events were Ed Sheeran’s two nights at Massachusetts’ [Gillette Stadium](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/09/fans-political-forum-sheeran-macklemore-tour/) and AC/DC’s show at New York’s MetLife Stadium, the band’s second  weather-related cancellation on this leg, after a ‘hundred-year storm’ damaged Toronto’s [Rogers Centre](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/09/ac-dc-toronto-show-cancelled-after-hundred-year-storm-damages-rogers-stadium/).

In Maryland, the C3 Presents-backed festival Oceans Calling initially pushed back the start of the three-day beachfront event due to the deterioration of the site, but ultimately cancelled it as “tides, floods, and high winds have prevented us from safely continuing the build”, organisers said. Headliners included Dave Matthews Band, Hootie & The Blowfish, Twenty One Pilots, Gwen Stefani, Mumford & Sons, and Matchbox Twenty.

It was the second US beachfront event to be cancelled in recent weeks, after Goldenvoice’s inaugural [Ocean Way Festival](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/09/goldenvoices-ocean-way-festival-washed-out-ahead-of-launch/) was axed for similar reasons on the other side of the country following a hurricane.

Up in New York, a handful of major events were called off, including Global Citizen Festival in NYC’s Central Park, the first cancellation in the event’s 14-year history. The one-day event was due to include Ms. Lauryn Hill & Wyclef Jean, Lenny Kravitz, John Legend, Lainey Wilson, Shaboozey, Tomorrow x Together, and Alicia Keys & Friends, among others.

“Forecasts predict dangerous conditions and circumstances in which we cannot safely bring tens of thousands of Global Citizens, artists, crew, vendors, partners, and staff onto the Great Lawn, let alone all of the equipment required. Safety has to come first,” organisers said.

> “We don’t really know how to put into words how heartbroken we are”

The NYC edition of the three-day All Things Go festival, staged at Forest Hill Stadium, was also called off ahead of launch, though the inland Maryland edition went ahead. Zara Larsson, Lola Young, Brandi Carlile, and Muna were among the headliners due in New York and still performed at the sister event.

“We don’t really know how to put into words how heartbroken we are. Thank you for understanding, and thank you to the fans, artists, and crews who make this festival what it is,” organisers said.

And Brooklyn’s Under the ‘K’ Bridge Park venue also saw the cancellation of two events, a headline show from The Prodigy and the single-day CBGB Festival, which was due to feature Morrissey, Patti Smith, Interpol, Sex Pistols, Bikini Kill, and more.

“The safety of our fans, the artists, and local crews is our top priority,” organisers said. “Thank you to the fans and all those involved who have supported the shows.”

Across the board, fans are expected to receive full refunds from their points of sale.

The powerful cyclone delivered a weekend of high winds, heavy rain, flash flooding, and strong waves on the eastern seaboard, also resulting in the cancellation of 500 flights and 4,000 delayed flights, per [*CNN*](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/09/26/weather/live-news/noreaster-weekend-storm).

Extreme weather is becoming an increasingly standard hurdle for event organisers. After a summer hit by festival cancellations across Europe, *IQ* spoke with several legal and insurance experts, who warned that events face “ [very little manoeuvrability](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/09/what-legal-risks-face-festivals-in-a-future-of-extreme-weather/) ” against cancellation orders from authorities, as well as growing reluctance from insurers in the sector.
