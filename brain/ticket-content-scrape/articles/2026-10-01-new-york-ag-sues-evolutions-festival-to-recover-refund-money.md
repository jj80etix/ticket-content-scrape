# New York AG sues Evolutions Festival to recover refund money

## Summary

New York AG Letitia James sued Evolutions Festival and promoter Vibrations LLC to recover over $243,000 in ticket/vendor-fee money after the 2025 festival was cancelled 10 days out with zero refunds issued to the 521 affected ticketholders. The AG is seeking full restitution, civil penalties, and a $500,000 bond requirement before the promoter can hold future NY events — an unusually direct case of a state regulator stepping in on refund enforcement where a promoter simply never paid anyone back.

### Key Points
- $243,158 in ticket sales plus $21,000+ in vendor fees collected and never refunded after the festival's Aug. 2025 cancellation.
- AG seeking restitution for 521 ticketholders and vendors, civil penalties, and a future $500K performance bond on the promoter.
- Promoter blamed cancellation on permitting issues; the venue operator disputes this, citing missing contracts/insurance docs from the promoter.
- A rare example of a state AG directly litigating for consumer refund recovery rather than acting on pricing/disclosure rules.

### Highlights
- $243K+ in unrefunded ticket sales to 521 buyers since the 2025 cancellation.
- Remedies sought: full restitution, civil penalties, $500K bond on future NY events.

### Industry Problem
When an independent promoter collapses or cancels without reserves, ticketholders often have no practical recourse beyond regulatory intervention — refunds depend entirely on the promoter's solvency and willingness to pay, with no structural backstop.

### Proposed Solution
The lawsuit itself proposes a bonding requirement as a forward-looking safeguard. For a primary ticketing platform, this is a monitor-and-differentiate signal: processes that hold/escrow funds or require promoter solvency checks before large advance sales reduce this exact failure mode and are a trust differentiator worth highlighting to venue/promoter clients.

Evolutions Festival

New York attorney general Letitia James has filed a lawsuit against Evolutions Festival in a bid to recover over $240,000 in refund money allegedly owed to ticketholders since 2025.

The suit relates to the 2025 edition of Evolutions Festival, which was cancelled 10 days prior to its outing. The three-day electronic event has been scheduled for 5-7 September 2025 at the Sugar Mountain Performing Arts Center in Caneadea, Allegany County, and had sold more than $243,000 in advance tickets to 521 people, also collecting more than $21,000 in advance vendor fees, the suit alleges.

“Attorney General James is seeking restitution for all ticketholders and vendors, civil penalties, and an order preventing the promoters from holding future events in New York state unless they provide a $500,000 bond,” the attorney general’s office said.

James later added in a social media post: “I won’t allow unsavoury promoters to take advantage of consumers or small business owners.”

It is a rare example of a public official intervening in refund redress, with the festival alleged not to have issued a single refund. The lawsuit says that starting on 20 March 2025, the festival and promoter Vibrations LLC “sold tickets to the Evolutions Festival 2025 but then abruptly cancelled the event on August 26, 2025, without returning any money to ticket purchasers”.

The promoter has been approached for comment.

> “Attorney General James is seeking restitution \[…\] and an order preventing the promoters from holding future events in New York”

Respondents collected $243,158.04 in advance ticket sales through the sale of 1,157 tickets (740 festival attendance tickets, 352 camping passes, and 65 transportation and parking passes, the lawsuit alleges.

The cancelled 2025 edition had been set to debut several upgrades for the annual festival, including new glamping facilities and three new “custom stages”. Organisers blamed the cancellation of the event on the festival, saying it had not “secured the necessary permitting for the festival”.

However, the venue disputed these claims on 27 August 2025, with its owner-operator Jeremy Lane saying in a statement: “Our event requires involvement of over 150 individuals, including security, medical, and sanitation personnel, working throughout the weekend. None of these individuals received signed contracts, funding, or communication from \[Vibration’s\] Michael Brown or the Evolutions team within the last two weeks.”

“With only 24 hours to load in and seven days until the gates opened, essential elements such as proof of insurance and hold harmless agreements were not provided. Despite my repeated requests for documentation, the response from Evolutions was that they were forced to cancel due to our alleged negligence,” he added.
