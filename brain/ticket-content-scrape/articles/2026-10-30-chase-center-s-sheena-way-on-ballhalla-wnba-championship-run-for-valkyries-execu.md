# Chase Center’s Sheena Way on ‘Ballhalla,’ WNBA Championship Run For Valkyries (Executive Profile)

## Summary

Pollstar profiles Chase Center VP of content and programming Sheena Way, covering the arena's packed summer of sold-out concerts (Doja Cat, Phoebe Bridgers, Diljit Dosanjh, A$AP Rocky, Florence + The Machine, Weezer, Rod Wave) alongside the Golden State Valkyries' WNBA championship run, as the venue embraces the "Ballhalla" nickname.

### Key Points
- Chase Center hosts both the WNBA's Valkyries and NBA's Warriors (same ownership group, Joe Lacob/Peter Guber) plus a full concert calendar
- Venue opened in 2019 ($1B, waterfront); surrounding 11-acre Thrive City campus (restaurants, green space, 200+ free annual events) added in 2024
- Way joined Golden State in 2020 after a career based in Vancouver and Edmonton

## Transcript

![GettyImages 2291354872](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/GettyImages-2291354872-scaled.jpg)

Exterior of Chase Center with a digital billboard promoting Journey’s Final Frontier Tour 2026 concert scheduled for November 28, Mission Bay, San Francisco, California, August 20, 2026. (Photo by Smith Collection/Gado/Getty Images)

Chase Center is the place to be this week as the second-year WNBA Golden State Valkyries valiantly take on the Atlanta Dream toward the goal of bringing the championship to the Bay. Game 7, taking place Halloween night back in San Francisco, will determine the winner of all the marbles, and win or lose, Chase Center, now nicknamed “Ballhalla” (a play on “Valhalla” a part of Valkyries’ Norse mythological narrative) will still be buzzing, much like it has all summer with sold-out concerts on top of the hoops action.

“We had Doja Cat last night, so a late-night wild crowd going into game five of the WNBA finals, and then we’re just getting ready for a couple nights of Phoebe Bridgers tomorrow,” says Sheena Way, vice president of content and programming at Chase Center.

On top of hosting top talent and championship caliber home teams in the Valkyries and the NBA’s Golden State Warriors – owned and operated by the same group led by Joe Lacob and Peter Guber – Chase Center this summer saw sellouts with concerts including doubles with Punjabi superstar Diljit Dosanjh, Daniel Caesar and Olivia Dean, along with other shows by artists including A$AP Rocky, Florence + The Machine, Weezer, Rod Wave and more.

Adding to the destination on top of being a modern, $1 billion waterfront arena that opened in 2019, the surrounding [11-acre Thrive City campus arrived in 2024,](https://news.pollstar.com/2024/04/25/thrive-city-arrives-in-fifth-year/) bringing restaurants, green space and more than 200 annual free community events right outside the arena, making for a buzzing environment as the Valkyries look to bring home the hardware.

Way, a Canadian who has spent most of her career in Vancouver and Edmonton before joining Golden State in 2020, takes her job seriously in making Chase Center the place to be for tours, special events and – most importantly – fans.

**Pollstar: You have two basketball teams and you’re bringing in additional special content and major concerts, but you’re all on the same team as part of the Golden State organization.**  
**Sheena Way:** I think it helps that Joe (Lacob) and Peter (Guber) own the Warriors, the Valkyries and the building, so we all work for one common good. Watching and working with working together to, to build the team, we all want a championship and we’re all on the same side. So even though my job is to bring in all, you know, all different types of great events and lots of multi-day events like the Laver Cup, which runs the same time as the playoffs, the goal is to help do everything we can to help the Valkyries win a championship.

  
**How do you make sure to fit in the right dates for the concerts? The playoffs for both teams run quite a while.**  
It’s looking ahead, it’s working with promoters, it’s explaining where we are and when you might need a contingency date, explaining, “If you move it back two dates, you won’t need a contingency. Can that fit into the tour?” It’s letting them know a year in advance, “This is what the playoff dates are going to look like. I don’t know what the free dates are yet, but if you could be outside of this will really help without needing a contingency date.” It’s just sharing the information, working with the promoters and just trying to do our very best together to accommodate everyone.

If we can all do this well, we might end up with a really great busy schedule, a lot of new fans, a lot of return fans and maybe there’s a championship at the end of that, you know?

**Summer can be a quieter season for arenas, but you’ve seemed to help keep things busy and successful. What stands out this summer?**  
In markets like this, where it’s warm, it’s typically amphitheater season. There’s wineries, the Greek Theatre, it’s a heavy festival market and they do super well. We do a summer league series, the California Classic, and then sometimes you get a little bit walloped with great shows coming in that are doubles, last summer it was Lady Gaga with a triple.

This year, we knew Diljit Dosanjh would do well, but we were still a little surprised, it being our first Indian-music concert, preparing for that show in a different way, knowing the audience will be 90% to 100% South Asian fans that haven’t been to Chase Center before. They have free turban wrappers – he wears a fan on his turban, a unique style, and everybody wants to wear it, but you need to know how to do it. So they find 60 or 80 (volunteer) turban tiers and we had to find an area and get all these chairs set up. When people came in, they all know about it — the kids, men, even women, everyone wants to look like him and get their turbans done. It was very serious. I hadn’t seen anything like that. It was very popular and really fun, and you look at the audience and there’s hundreds of people with this exact same white turban with the fan on it.

Then we went into Olivia Dean, who started her first North American arena tour here. She came into the market with us did a little rehearsal, and then she did two sold out nights that were just beautiful, magical. She’s quite something.

Daniel Caesar, that one was like wildfire. When it went on sale, everyone was madly like, “Yep, going into a second show.” You’re not always prepared for that, especially when it’s not someone that you’ve seen five times and you’ve seen them come up through the club level, from The Independent to the Bill Graham to the Greek. It doesn’t happen like that all the time anymore.

A$AP Rocky turned out well for everybody. That was a big win across the board. We had a fun Weezer show, Rod Wave, those ones all were really fun. A surprise sellout in that it was our second time with him was Joji. He did huge business here, and sold out really fast, so that was a really fun one for us too.

**There’s only so many dates on the calendar, and you want them all to be full, but have to be selective.**  
Everybody wants all the A shows and a lot of the A shows like to be here, so we do well with that. But, are all the A shows reaching all the fans, the demographic, and the price range? I need to be looking at everything and make sure there’s something for everyone. “Hot Wheels” is a great one and such a fun event for little kids. The target audience is 5-year-old boys, so I’m like, “Great, we’re going to corner the market on that.” They’re gonna grow up with Thrive City, they’re growing with our retailers, they’re growing with our staff at Chase Center and they’ll come back for the one that’s targeted for 10-year-olds. I want to keep making new events and working on new events for different fans who might not be a Warriors or Valkyries fan and find a way that we can welcome them into Chase Center, so they can have a relationship and an experience with us, and that fits into their culture, their ethnicity and their price. Something for everyone is what we want to create here.

**How do you keep it exciting for the artists as well as fans?**  
We also like to do fun things when they do get here. For Wu-Tang Clan, we had enough conversations with the promoter that the promoter took a chance and said, “Let’s, let’s try Wu-Tang at Chase Center.” So, so I was like, “Wow, this is a really big opportunity. We just need to do something really great that no one else is gonna do for Wu-Tang, because this is a big deal for us.”

So, you know that Cuban link necklace with Wu-Tang logo, we made a giant inflatable necklace, giant, each chain link was 20 feet high and we hung it up in front of Chase Center. It was so much fun.

The artists get a lot of things, and we like that. We support that. But we really try to do some fun fan things. We had Doja Cat last night that I was talking about, and I was with her agent last night and two years ago, Doja started her tour here, and it was Halloween, and she had that single “Paint The Town Red,” so we painted Thrive City red. All the fans are dressed up, as they do for a Doja Cat show, plus it’s Halloween and the whole Thrive City entrance to the arena is red. It was wild.

**I’m sure the response is always great.**  
We’ve gotten really positive feedback because everything is shared like lightning, and we’re pretty smart in the way that we vet these things through the tour, like through their manager or their agent ahead of time, just to make sure there’s no sort of negative surprise.

**There are some other competitive arenas in the market, with similar-sized venues in former Warriors home in Oakland as well as SAP Center in San Jose. Is there enough content for everyone?  
**The really important thing is that the Bay Area does have so much music and so many music fans that are coming out constantly. I was talking to Another Planet earlier this week and they were saying they have like 135 shows across their properties this month. Can you believe that? It’s just incredible that one promoter could have that many shows.

**What’s it like seeing the WNBA come to town and go gangbusters right out of the gate?**  
This is our second year. I believe the average sold tickets for a WNBA game and is around 6,000 and the Valkyries have been selling out 18,000 tickets the entire last season and this season. It started out with us thinking there would be one type of crowd but it’s really turned into older people, younger people, kids, families, male, female, everybody seems to be a Valkyries fan. They did a good job with the merchandising and really inviting everyone to come along with it.

All these cats out of Nashville from Messina Touring are here going to be at the game tonight and I’ve been talking to them already, saving, “I have earplugs for you. You will need them.” It is the loudest. It is so crazy how loud these games are. I cannot be in the bowl. I go to all my concerts without earplugs, but I cannot be at a Valkyries game without earplugs (laughs). The fans are just are so loud.

**You have some big shows coming up for the rest of the year, including Teddy Swims, Jonas Brothers and Journey. How is the rest of the year and next year shaping up for concerts?**  
It looks like a lot of good tours are going out. Some of these dates I’m looking at are like, “Ah, I think that could be a double.” Last year was such a big stadium year and just talking to the stadiums, I think they’re doing good business next year as well. I’m really happy with the way the fall’s looking. You need everything to go on sale. It used to be that as soon as you, talk about a date, you get a first hold, it’s happening. But things seem to move and zig and zag, now. Since COVID, all of a sudden things can change, “Oh, that’s not quite confirmed.” But I think it is looking fun, there’s lots of really good pop out there next year, some great female shows.
