# Movers and shakers: AEG names new VP of partnership sales

## Summary

A roundup of industry personnel moves: AEG Global Partnerships named Stephanie Streeter as VP of partnership sales, a new role expanding brand partnerships across AXS and the wider AEG portfolio as AXS expects to process nearly $5 billion in ticketing transactions in 2026. AEG Presents also named Chris Proctor programming director for its UK mid-sized venue portfolio, and talent agencies ITG and Roam Artists made additional appointments.

### Key Points
- Stephanie Streeter named VP of partnership sales at AEG Global Partnerships, focused on AXS and AEG-wide brand partnerships; AXS expects ~$5B in 2026 ticketing transactions.
- Chris Proctor appointed programming director for AEG Presents' UK mid-sized venue portfolio, covering British Airways ARC, University of Wolverhampton at The Halls, Watford Colosseum, and Indigo at The O2.
- Independent Talent Group and Roam Artists also announced new agency appointments.

In the latest round of industry appointments, **AEG Global Partnerships** has appointed Stephanie Streeter as vice president, partnership sales, a newly created role the firm says is “focused on expanding brand partnerships across AXS and the broader AEG portfolio”.

Streeter previously led residencies and theatres at AEG’s ticket platform AXS, with her new responsibilities covering “connecting brands with live entertainment audiences through AXS’s ticketing technology, digital platforms and extensive network of venue and event partners,” AEG said.

The appointment comes as AXS expects to process nearly $5 billion in ticketing transactions in 2026. Streeter will be based in San Francisco.

In **AEG Presents** news, the company has appointed Chris Proctor as the new programming director of the UK mid-sizes venue portfolio.

The newly created position will make Proctor responsible for the booking and programming strategy for AEG’s newest UK venue, the 3,800-cap British Airways ARC in London, as well as University of Wolverhampton at The Halls, Watford Colosseum, and Indigo at The O2.

In the agency sector, **Independent Talent Group** has a new head of music and entertainment partnerships with the appointment of Khadeen O’Donnell.

In the role, she will “dovetail with Independent ’s agents and its established commercial and branding department, using their extensive brand and rights holder relationships to leverage new opportunities for the agency ’s existing talent,” the company said.

O’Donnell is also leading a recently announced commercial partnership with Outernet London. Her appointment comes as the agency also names Giulia Zecchini as head of sports partnerships.

Elsewhere, former UTA staffer Oliver Ward has joined **Roam** **Artists** as a senior agent. Ward leaves UTA after a 13 year stint.

“A massive thank you to the incredible colleagues and dear friends who were part of a whirlwind journey at UTA. There was never a boring day and I achieved more than I could have thought possible from when I started as an intern there back in 2013,” Ward said. “But now I am ready for an exciting next step, as I learn, grow and expand my horizons with a bold and ambitious team.”

Meanwhile, Ward’s former agency **UTA** has also announced it is moving its London office, in a move to “bring all of its London businesses together”.

The new headquarters is located at Rathbone Square in Central London, with the move coinciding with UTA uniting its business with literary and talent agency Curtis Brown Group (led by CEO Jonny Geller) and international football agency ROOF.

Darnell Strom, head of UTA’s UK Office, said, “This move represents our commitment to our UK presence and the role it will play in UTA’s future globally. Bringing UTA, Curtis Brown and ROOF together in one place will allow our teams to collaborate in new ways and deliver even more opportunities for our clients, as we connect that talent with the full reach of UTA’s global network.”

Finally, in festival news, **Burning Man** CEO Marian Goodell is stepping down after 13 years in the position.

“I am ready and excited to move out of the CEO role, and to pass these responsibilities on to the new executive director we find. I will stay on the board, support the new leader however they need, and take on a different role that is still being designed,” Goodell said.

During her tenure at the Black Rock City-based desert festival, Goodell oversaw [fundraising rounds](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/01/burning-man-14m-short-of-fundraising-goal/) to save the event from financial difficulty.
