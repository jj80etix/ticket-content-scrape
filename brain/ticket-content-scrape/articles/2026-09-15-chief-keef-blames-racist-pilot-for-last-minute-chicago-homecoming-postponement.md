# Chief Keef Blames ‘Racist’ Pilot for Last-Minute Chicago Homecoming Postponement

## Summary

Chief Keef's planned Chicago homecoming show at Huntington Bank Pavilion was postponed roughly 30 minutes before its scheduled start after travel problems kept him from reaching the venue in time; he blamed a pilot he described as "very racist" for canceling his plane. The show was rescheduled for Sept. 23 with previously purchased tickets honored, and the rest of the tour continued on schedule with stops in Minneapolis and Detroit.

### Key Points
- Sept. 12 Chicago show postponed ~30 minutes before doors after Keef couldn't arrange alternate transportation in time.
- Live Nation said travel delays would have prevented an adequate set before the venue's curfew; Keef blamed a "racist" pilot for canceling his flight, an allegation not independently substantiated.
- Rescheduled for Sept. 23 at the same venue; previously purchased tickets remain valid.
- The date was meant to be Keef's first proper headlining Chicago show in over a decade, capping years of being informally blacklisted from local venues.
- Tour continued uninterrupted in Minneapolis (Sept. 13) and Detroit (Sept. 14), with Indianapolis next.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Untitled-design-2024-05-14T170204.134.webp)

Chief Keef’s long-awaited headlining return to Chicago was postponed just minutes before showtime Saturday after travel problems kept the rapper from reaching the venue, with Keef blaming the disruption on a pilot he said “got very racist.”

The Sept. 12 show at Huntington Bank Pavilion at Northerly Island had been scheduled to begin at 7:30 p.m. and serve as the opening night of the Chief Keef Live 2026 tour. The venue announced the postponement around 7 p.m., roughly 30 minutes before the scheduled start, after fans had already arrived for the anticipated hometown show.

The concert has since been rescheduled for Wednesday, Sept. 23 at 7:30 p.m., with Live Nation and Huntington Bank Pavilion both currently listing the new date. Previously purchased tickets will be honored for the rescheduled performance.

The decision came particularly late for ticket holders. According to the Chicago Sun-Times, the venue first announced around 6 p.m. that the show was delayed before telling fans about an hour later that Keef would not perform that night. Some attendees had traveled significant distances for a concert that was supposed to mark a major hometown milestone for the Chicago-born rapper.

Live Nation told the Sun-Times that travel problems would have prevented Keef from arriving with enough time to provide an adequate performance before the venue’s curfew. The promoter said the venue ultimately made the decision to postpone.

Keef subsequently offered a considerably more pointed account of what caused the delay.

In an Instagram Story, Keef blamed a “pilot who got very racist,” alleging that the pilot canceled his plane shortly before the planned trip to Chicago. Keef said his team searched for another way to reach the city but could not arrange transportation that would get him there in time for the show. He did not provide further details about the alleged incident, and the accusation against the pilot has not been independently substantiated.

“No I ain’t happy, yes I am upset,” Keef wrote while apologizing to fans who had been waiting for the hometown performance.

The postponement did not derail the broader tour. Keef went on to perform at the Armory in Minneapolis on Sept. 13 and at the Fillmore Detroit on Sept. 14, according to concert setlist records. Live Nation lists his next performance for Tuesday, Sept. 15 at Old National Centre in Indianapolis.

That makes Chicago an unusually significant postponed date rather than a stalled tour launch. Saturday’s show was intended to be Keef’s first proper headlining concert within Chicago city limits in more than a decade and the centerpiece of a gradual hometown return for one of the most influential artists to emerge from the city’s drill scene.

Keef had already begun appearing around Chicago again. He returned to the area for Lyrical Lemonade’s Summer Smash in Bridgeview in 2024 and again this year, and earlier this month appeared as a surprise guest during Ye’s Soldier Field show. The latter marked Keef’s first concert appearance inside Chicago city limits in more than a decade, but the Huntington Bank Pavilion date was supposed to put him at the center of his own hometown production.

Notably, Keef spent much of his career effectively shut out of Chicago venues. He was unofficially blacklisted locally for years, and in 2015 an attempted hologram performance at a benefit in Indiana became a national story after opposition from Chicago officials. The office of then-Mayor Rahm Emanuel called Keef an “unacceptable role model” and said the proposed appearance represented a public-safety risk.

His relationship with Chicago has thawed considerably since. Keef’s longtime manager, Idris “Peeda Pan” Abdul-Wahid, told the Sun-Times ahead of the planned tour opener that Mayor Brandon Johnson’s administration had provided an opportunity for Keef’s team to work with city officials and help clear a path toward his hometown return.

The postponement therefore delayed a show with considerably more history attached to it than an ordinary tour stop. Keef had specifically chosen Chicago to launch the Chief Keef Live 2026 run after years of returning to the area for other artists’ shows and festival appearances.

Instead, the tour moved forward elsewhere while the hometown headlining return was pushed back 11 days.

Keef has since played Minneapolis and Detroit, with Indianapolis next on the schedule, while Live Nation continues to list the rescheduled Chicago performance for Sept. 23 at Huntington Bank Pavilion. Previously purchased tickets remain valid for the new date.
