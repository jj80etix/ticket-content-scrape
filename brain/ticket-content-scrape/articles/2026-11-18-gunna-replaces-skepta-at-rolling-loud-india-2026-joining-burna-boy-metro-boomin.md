# Gunna Replaces Skepta at Rolling Loud India 2026, Joining Burna Boy, Metro Boomin

## Summary

Gunna has joined Rolling Loud India 2026 as a headliner, replacing Skepta (who withdrew Nov. 11 for personal reasons) and making his India debut on December 13 alongside Karan Aujla. The two-day hip-hop festival returns to Loud Park in Navi Mumbai Dec. 12–13, with Burna Boy and Metro Boomin headlining the opening day across a roughly 30-act lineup.

### Key Points
- Gunna replaces Skepta as a Dec. 13 headliner, debuting in India alongside Karan Aujla.
- Skepta withdrew Nov. 11 from his India and Australia dates, citing family/personal reasons.
- Rolling Loud India 2026 runs Dec. 12–13 at Loud Park, Navi Mumbai.
- Burna Boy and Metro Boomin headline the opening day, both making their first India appearances.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Rolling-Loud.webp)

Gunna has joined the lineup for Rolling Loud India 2026, replacing British rapper Skepta as a headliner for the festival’s second edition in December. The Atlanta rapper will make his India debut on December 13, sharing top billing with Punjabi superstar Karan Aujla.

The two-day hip-hop festival returns to Loud Park in Kharghar, Navi Mumbai, on December 12-13, with Burna Boy and Metro Boomin headlining the opening day. The event will feature approximately 30 international and Indian performers, bringing together some of the biggest names in global hip-hop alongside established and emerging artists from India’s growing rap scene.

Gunna’s addition follows Skepta’s November 11 announcement that he was withdrawing from scheduled performances in India and Australia, citing personal reasons. The British grime star, who had been expected to make his Indian debut at Rolling Loud, apologized to fans and said he needed to prioritize his family.

The December 12 lineup will be led by Nigerian superstar Burna Boy and producer Metro Boomin, both making their first appearances in India. Burna Boy, whose *Twice as Tall* won the Grammy Award for Best Global Music Album, has become one of Africa’s most successful international touring artists.

Big Sean is another notable addition to the opening day, also making his first appearance in India. Other December 12 performers include GoldLink, Dhanda Nyoliwala, Paal Dabba, Steel Banglez, Slayr, SoSoCamo, Snappy Kaal and Jorjiana.

The December 13 lineup features Gunna and Karan Aujla alongside ScHoolboy Q, Seedhe Maut, BabyChiefDoIt, Wazir Patar, Raja Kumari, NAVZ-47 and other performers. Aujla returns to headline for the second consecutive year after becoming the first global Punjabi artist to headline Rolling Loud India during its inaugural 2025 event.

The festival’s programming also reflects the diversity of India’s domestic hip-hop scene. More than 10 artists with Indian roots are featured across the two days, representing regional styles ranging from Punjabi and Haryanvi rap to Tamil, Marathi and Gujarati hip-hop.

Rolling Loud co-founder and CEO Matt Zingler said the lineup was shaped by the audience response to the festival’s first edition.

“One thing that really stood out to us in India was how open the fans were to everything. If the music connects, they show up for it,” Zingler said. “That’s influenced how we’ve approached this year’s lineup – bringing international and Indian artists together across both days and making it feel like one festival, not two different worlds.”

The festival debuted in India in November 2025, attracting more than 65,000 attendees across two days, according to organizers. Its inaugural lineup featured international artists including Central Cee, Wiz Khalifa and Don Toliver, alongside Indian performers including Aujla, DIVINE and Hanumankind.

The 2026 edition is again being produced in partnership with District, which handles ticketing and local event production.

District CEO Rahul Ganjoo said the decision to feature artists from India’s regional hip-hop communities alongside international performers was informed by the reception to last year’s festival.

“At the first edition, the crowd didn’t thin out between the international sets and the Indian ones, and that told us the audience was already a step ahead of the industry,” Ganjoo said. “This line up is built on what we saw.”
