# AEG, Japan’s Creativeman Enter Strategic Partnership

## Summary

AEG Presents announced a strategic partnership with Japan's Creativeman Productions, the country's largest independent concert promoter, to expand joint festival and touring opportunities across Japan and Asia — a deal that will also boost AEG's ticketing arm, AXS, in the region. The tie-up links Creativeman's Summer Sonic festival (celebrating its 25th anniversary) into AEG's global festival portfolio alongside Coachella and BST Hyde Park.

### Key Points
- Creativeman organizes 12+ annual festivals in Japan (Summer Sonic, Punkspring, Loud Park, Greenroom) and has expanded Summer Sonic to Bangkok.
- The partnership is explicitly framed to grow AXS's footprint in Japan/Asia.
- Creativeman previously had a Live Nation Japan joint venture (2012); Live Nation now partners with a different Japanese promoter, H.I.P.
- Announced Aug. 11, coinciding with Summer Sonic's 25th-anniversary weekend.

### Highlights
- Deal explicitly extends AEG's ticketing platform AXS into the Japanese/Asian market via Creativeman's promoter relationships.
- Creativeman previously partnered with rival Live Nation (2012 JV), underscoring how promoters realign ticketing allegiances as competitors court them.

### Industry Problem
Regional touring expansion increasingly hinges on promoter partnerships that come bundled with a primary ticketing platform, meaning market share in ticketing is won or lost through promoter alliances rather than direct-to-consumer competition.

### Proposed Solution
No new product is proposed here — AEG is extending its existing AXS platform through Creativeman's promoter relationships. Worth monitoring whether Tier 2 ticketing incumbents (AXS, Ticketmaster) continue to expand share via promoter M&A/partnerships in growth markets, since that consolidation pattern could squeeze out independent platforms from adjacent regional deals.

On August 11, AEG Presents announced that it had entered into a strategic partnership with Japan’s Creativeman Productions, a leading concert promoter, thus increasing AEG Present’s involvement in Japan’s lucrative live entertainment business. According to an AEG press release, the two companies “will work together to create new opportunities for artists to connect with audiences throughout Japan, while continuing to elevate the fan experience and support the country’s vibrant live music market.” The partnership will also give a considerable boost to AXS, AEG Presents’ ticketing company.

Naoki Shimizu, the CEO of Creativeman, said in a statement, “Over the past 37 years, Creativeman has grown from a company with just two staff members to become the largest independent music promoter in Asia. This partnership with AEG Presents will propel our growth as we expand our festivals and artists from Japan to Asia and the rest of the world.” Shimizu specifically pointed out that the new arrangement would “connect Summer Sonic,” Creativeman’s signature summer music festival, which is celebrating its 25th anniversary this weekend, “with a portfolio of world-class festivals and create a network that joins Coachella in the U.S. and BST Hyde Park in the U.K., serving as a cornerstone for the music industry’s growth.”

Shawn Trell, executive vice president and COO of AEG Presents, added, “Creativeman is an important presence in Japan and across the region. \[Its\] vision and business strategy align perfectly with ours. Beyond Creativeman’s thriving concert and festival business, Summer Sonic has become a globally recognized brand on its own.”

Creativeman, in fact, organizes more than a dozen music festivals throughout the year in Japan, including Punkspring, Loud Park and the Greenroom Festival, and has begun expanding the Summer Sonic brand to other Asian cities like Bangkok. It has also been instrumental in exposing Japanese audiences to major artists from other parts of Asia.

In 2012, Creativeman entered into a joint venture with Live Nation Entertainment Inc. to form Live Nation Japan. Currently, Live Nation is working with a different Japanese promoter, H.I.P.
