# Santa Monica Cancels Ocean Way Festival After Hurricane Damage; Fans Question Soft Ticket Sales

## Summary

Santa Monica and Goldenvoice canceled the inaugural two-day Ocean Way Festival (Sept. 26-27, headlined by The Killers, Olivia Dean and Jack White) two weeks before its debut, citing beach erosion and storm-drain damage from Hurricane Marie that shrank the safe event footprint below what the planned infrastructure and crowd required. Refunds are automatic and organizers plan to return in 2027. The announcement immediately drew public speculation that soft sales at Ocean Way's premium price points played a role, fueled by contrast with the nearby, storm-affected Ohana Festival proceeding the same weekend at lower prices and reportedly near sell-out.

### Key Points
- City said the safe shoreline setback would have needed to move 96 feet inland, with the storm drain forcing the southern boundary 500 feet north.
- Two-day GA pricing ran $399-$459; single-day from $229; premium pit packages started at $749.
- Goldenvoice says it will bring the festival back in 2027; all 2026 tickets refund automatically at point of purchase.
- Nearby Ohana Festival, also hit by Hurricane Marie, is proceeding the same weekend and was largely sold out/waitlisted at lower price points ($199 one-day GA).
- TicketNews found no independent confirmation that sales volume factored into the cancellation; city and promoter attribute it solely to storm damage.

### Highlights
- Hurricane Marie forced a 96-foot inland shoreline setback and a 500-foot northward site-boundary shift, per Santa Monica officials.
- Two-day GA pricing of $399-$459 (premium pit from $749) drew criticism from the announcement onward.
- A comparable nearby festival at lower price points is nearly sold out the same weekend, intensifying public doubt about the official explanation.

### Industry Problem
Premium/dynamic festival pricing draws immediate public scrutiny, and when an event cancels for any reason, patrons default to assuming soft sales over force-majeure explanations — especially when a similarly timed, lower-priced event proceeds nearby.

### Proposed Solution
The item describes no vendor-side fix. For an independent primary platform, the signal is to pair premium pricing tiers with transparent sell-through/communication practices so that legitimate event-safety cancellations aren't read as disguised demand failures.

Santa Monica has canceled the inaugural Ocean Way Festival two weeks before its planned debut, with city officials and promoter Goldenvoice offering an unusually detailed explanation of how Hurricane Marie damaged the beachfront site and made the event unsafe to stage.

That explanation has not stopped a wave of speculation among would-be attendees that sluggish ticket sales may also have contributed to the decision.

The two-day festival [was scheduled for Sept. 26 and 27 on the beach south of the Santa Monica Pier](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/inaugural-ocean-way-festival-announces-headliners-the-killers-jack-white-olivia-dean/), with The Killers, Olivia Dean, Jack White, Khruangbin, Sublime and Alvvays among the acts booked.

In a [joint announcement](https://www.santamonica.gov/press/2026/09/11/beach-damage-from-hurricane-marie-forces-cancellation-of-inaugural-ocean-way-festival-to-2027) Friday, the City of Santa Monica and Goldenvoice said storm surf generated by Hurricane Marie and runoff from the Pico-Kenter Storm Drain had significantly reduced the amount of usable beach. City officials said the safe shoreline setback would have needed to move 96 feet inland, while damage around the storm drain forced the proposed festival’s southern boundary roughly 500 feet north.

## Fans Quickly Question Whether Ticket Sales Played a Role

The city’s explanation is explicit, and there is independent evidence that Hurricane Marie caused serious coastal damage throughout Southern California.

But within hours of the announcement, social media discussions about Ocean Way became dominated by another theory: that the storm gave organizers a way out of an event that had struggled to sell tickets.

There is currently no evidence that ticket sales played any role in the cancellation, and neither Santa Monica nor Goldenvoice has said that they did.

Still, multiple threads among Los Angeles-area festival fans include commenters claiming Ocean Way had remained at its initial ticket-price tier, describing the event as undersold or suggesting the hurricane damage provided a convenient explanation for calling it off. [One heavily discussed Santa Monica thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/SantaMonica/comments/1wdsinl/ocean_way_fest_cancelled/) began with a commenter asking what erosion officials were referring to and suggesting the event had simply failed to sell well. Another commenter explicitly pointed to Ohana Festival continuing the same weekend despite storm damage farther south.

The pricing had drawn criticism from some fans from the moment Ocean Way was announced. Two-day general admission began at $399, with later tiers advertised at $429 and $459. Single-day tickets began at $229, while two-day premium pit packages started at $749.

Several commenters Friday claimed tickets had never progressed beyond the initial pricing tier. TicketNews has not independently confirmed the festival’s sales volume or how much inventory remained at the time of cancellation.

## Ohana Festival Adds Fuel to the Speculation

One comparison in particular has gained traction.

Eddie Vedder’s established Ohana Festival is scheduled for Sept. 25-27 — the same weekend Ocean Way was supposed to take place — at Doheny State Beach in Dana Point, roughly 70 miles down the Southern California coast.

Ohana’s 2026 lineup includes Eddie Vedder, Tyler Childers and Pearl Jam as headliners, alongside Maná, Alabama Shakes, Jon Batiste, Billy Idol, Pixies and Fontaines D.C.

As of Friday, Ohana’s official website was advertising that “very few tickets remain.” Three-day GA passes and Sunday single-day GA tickets were on waitlist, while limited Friday and Saturday tickets remained available. One-day GA tickets start at $199, while a three-day GA pass was priced at $535.

That comparison became even more striking because Dana Point was itself hit by Hurricane Marie.

The City of Dana Point declared a local emergency after coastal flooding damaged several homes, while California State Parks warned Thursday that storm debris had washed onto Doheny State Beach itself, where Ohana will be staged. The park notice warned visitors about debris, coastal erosion and impacts to park infrastructure but did not announce a closure of the beach or the upcoming festival.

That does not mean the two sites suffered comparable damage. Santa Monica says its festival footprint was specifically compromised by both shoreline erosion and a storm-drain channel cutting into the event site, and the infrastructure and capacity requirements for Ocean Way are unique to that location.

But the fact that another large beach festival is proceeding nearby on exactly the same weekend — at a site also affected by the same hurricane — has plainly contributed to the skepticism surrounding Ocean Way’s cancellation.

For some fans, the contrast has reinforced a belief that Ocean Way’s premium pricing and competition from an established festival in the same regional market may have made its inaugural year a difficult sell.

For now, that remains speculation rather than an established explanation.

The official account is much less ambiguous: Santa Monica says it evaluated the damaged site itself and determined Ocean Way could not safely be built there. Goldenvoice publicly backed that decision, and both parties say they intend to try again in 2027.

That leaves the biggest unanswered ticketing question — how well Ocean Way had actually sold before the storm arrived — without a public answer.
