# ‘Guitar music that isn’t superficial’: Amplifire on its debut booking strategy

## Summary

Germany's debut Amplifire Festival (Hamburg, Aug. 7-8) drew 10,000 fans by deliberately narrowing its booking focus to sophisticated punk, alternative rock, and post-hardcore rather than broad guitar-music branding, anchored by Biffy Clyro and Bad Religion. Promoter KJ Projects credits a below-€50 "social ticket" tier and free ticket insurance for helping win over cost-sensitive fans amid a broader climate of sluggish ticket sales and disappearing festivals; a 2027 return (Aug. 6-7) is confirmed.

### Key Points
- Debut edition drew 10,000 attendees to Hamburg's harbour, Aug. 7-8; lineup led by Biffy Clyro, Bad Religion, and Frank Turner alongside German punk/hardcore acts.
- Head of booking Thomas Harsman credits a hyper-specific genre vision — punk, post-hardcore, alt-rock — over broad "guitar music" branding for establishing a clear festival identity in year one.
- Offered a social ticket tier priced below €50 for fans unable to pay full price, plus free ticket insurance, as deliberate affordability/access measures.
- Organizers cite general industry headwinds — sluggish ticket sales, festivals disappearing, reduced cultural spending — as the backdrop the debut had to overcome without prior lineups or buzz to point to.
- 2027 return confirmed for Aug. 6-7.

### Highlights
- Below-€50 social ticket tier plus free ticket insurance were named as key levers for building a first-year audience amid sluggish broader ticket demand.
- Festival launched into an acknowledged down cycle for ticket sales industry-wide, yet still hit 10,000 attendees.

### Industry Problem
As overall ticket-buying demand softens and production costs rise, festivals are experimenting with built-in affordability tiers (reduced-price tickets, free cancellation/insurance) to remove financial-risk barriers for price-sensitive fans rather than relying on discounting after the fact.

### Proposed Solution
Track tiered/social pricing and bundled ticket insurance as a demand-side response pattern; for Etix, this is a signal to evaluate whether affordability tiering or included insurance/flex options could reduce buyer hesitation for cost-sensitive events on the platform.

## ‘Guitar music that isn’t superficial’: Amplifire on its debut booking strategy

As the new festival set out its stall in the punk and hardcore space, the event tells IQ how a hyper-specific genre vision made for a successful first year

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/08/KJ-Pressematerial-Amplifire_Festival-Bad_Religion___Amplifire_2026__credits_-_Peter_Detje_-7389_26419-scaled.jpg.webp)

Bad Religion at Amplifire Festival © Peter Detje

Germany’s Amplifire Festival brought in 10,000 fans to its debut edition at Hamburg’s harbour on 7-8 August, saying it capitalised on growing demand for a subset of German domestic punk acts that are increasingly filling larger and international venues.

Promoted by KJ Projects, the alternative rock, punk, and hardcore festival emphasised to *IQ* that the stylistic specificity of its booking strategy was a key pillar of a successful premier, explaining that focussing down to the sub-genre level and discriminately booking within its genre remit helped forge an identity in year one.

At the same time, though, established acts from both within and outside the punk genre anchored the lineup, including Biffy Clyro and Bad Religion.

“The combination of a carefully curated and diverse programme – put together by fans for fans – the venue’s unrivalled atmosphere, and the festival’s commitment to taking a clear political stance proved a resounding success and was very well received,” KJ Projects said following the event.

Describing how the team approached putting together a brand new genre festival, Amplifire’s head of booking Thomas Harsman told *IQ* it had to begin with an idea of style that went beyond simple genre labels to a more specific aesthetic, political, and emotional vision.

“We did have a clear plan for what the lineup should look like. The cornerstones were always sophisticated alternative rock, punk rock, and post-hardcore—in other words, the kind of guitar-driven music that isn’t superficial and conveys a certain emotional depth. In addition, diversity in the lineup was also a key consideration,” he said.

He adds that “during the booking process, our goals naturally shifted here and there due to availability and financial constraints,” but says that thanks to the specificity of the original vision, “we managed to bring our vision to life exactly as we’d envisioned it”.

> “The cornerstones were always sophisticated alternative rock, punk rock, and post-hardcore”

The clarity of this mission statement was enabled by the historical strength of these musical styles in Germany, with a newer generation of domestic acts in the style solidifying future demand in the market.

“There is definitely a demand for punk and hardcore in Germany. This is evident, for one thing, in the fact that the two biggest German punk bands, Die Toten Hosen and Die Ärzte, sell out their shows at will,” Harsman said.

However, he also noted that the “designated successors” of these band are also graduating to larger venue types on recent or ongoing tours, which provided the festival with an opportunity to capitalise. “Broilers, Feine Sahne Fischfilet, and Donots, are also playing the biggest tours of their careers,” he notes.

Broilers’ current tour, for example, will be capped off by two successive nights at the 7,500-cap Mitsubishi Electric Halle in Düsseldorf, while Feine Sahne Fischfilet’s ongoing run will take them to South America in October, where they will play the 15,000-cap Movistar Arena in Buenos Aires, significant steps up for both bands for headline shows.

Meanwhile, in the parallel hardcore landscape in Germany, Harsman notes that “it’s primarily international bands that are gradually building a larger following in Germany”. But it is a progressive form of the genre which is finding Germany to be fertile ground for touring, as Harsman notes the booking team were confident in anglophone post-punk being an attractive option to German audiences.

“Of particular interest here are post-punk and post-hardcore bands from the UK and Ireland, such as Idles, Fontaines DC, and High Vis, whose stage presence and modern approach are opening up these genres to the under-30 crowd as well,” he added.

The other side of the coin for Amplifire, however, with such a specific artistic mission, was the increased difficulty of pitching the event’s identity to backers.

> “Of particular interest here are post-punk and post-hardcore bands from the UK and Ireland”

Reflecting on the challenges of staging the debut edition, Harsman told *IQ:* “The biggest challenge will always be financing a festival of this kind. In our specific case, it was of course also a challenge to explain our vision to the promoters, agents, and bands and get them on board. We didn’t have lineups from previous years, word-of-mouth buzz, or great photos and videos from past events to show.”

With the leap of faith involved in launching a new genre festival, Harsman credits “the trust placed in KJ” for ensuring the festival was able to put together a remarkable lineup”.

“After that, however, we had to do the same work to win over our audience—and we had to do it at a time when ticket sales are generally sluggish, festivals are disappearing, and more and more people are spending less money on culture,” he noted.

This made it all the more important that the festival “removed barriers for people who either have limited financial resources or are reluctant to plan far in advance,” he said, naming the festival’s social ticket tier which offered reduced prices below €50 for fans unable to pay full price, as well its free ticket insurance as key parts of its audience cultivation.

Sonic diversity was still an important consideration for the booking team, however, as the festival welcomed Biffy Clyro and the acoustic sounds of Frank Turner to the top of its bill.

“For us, the highlights undoubtedly included the performances by Biffy Clyro, Frank Turner & The Sleeping Souls, and Bad Religion’s exclusive concert in Germany in 2026—all of which ensured that we received overwhelmingly positive feedback on the first edition of the Amplifire Festival,” Harsman said.

As the inaugural edition came to a close, organisers confirmed the festival’s 2027 return for 6-7 August.
