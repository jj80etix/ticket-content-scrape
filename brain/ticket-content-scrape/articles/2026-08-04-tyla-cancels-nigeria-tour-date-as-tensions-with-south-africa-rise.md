# Tyla cancels Nigeria tour date as tensions with South Africa rise

## Summary

South African star Tyla quietly removed the Lagos stop from her newly announced A*POP World Tour as diplomatic tensions rise between Nigeria and South Africa over anti-migrant demonstrations and repatriation efforts. Neither Tyla nor her management has publicly explained the change, which follows mounting pressure from Nigerian fans and commentators.

### Key Points
- Lagos date disappeared from Tyla's A*POP World Tour schedule days after its initial announcement, with no official statement linking it to the tensions.
- Diplomatic friction stems from anti-migrant demonstrations and repatriation efforts targeting Nigerians in South Africa.
- The episode echoes 2019, when Burna Boy and Tiwa Savage withdrew from South African shows amid similar xenophobia-related tensions.
- Highlights how cross-border diplomatic and social tensions can create reputational and commercial risk for touring artists between Africa's two biggest music markets.

The singer quietly removed a Lagos performance from her just-announced A\*POP World Tour as tensions flare over anti-migrant demonstrations in her native South Africa

**South African star Tyla has removed the Lagos stop from the itinerary of her forthcoming A\*POP World Tour as relations between Nigeria and the singer’s native country sour over anti-migrant demonstrations and repatriation efforts in the southern nation.**

Neither Tyla nor her management has publicly explained the decision after the Lagos disappeared from the official schedule just days after it was first announced, and no official statement has linked the change to the recent diplomatic tensions between Nigeria and South Africa. However, the move follows mounting calls from Nigerian fans and commentators urging the Grammy-winning artist to cancel the performance amid renewed allegations of xenophobic attacks against foreign nationals in South Africa.

It is not the first time relations between Africa’s two largest music markets have deteriorated following anti-migrant protests in South Africa. Nigeria has criticised South Africa’s response to attacks targeting foreign nationals, while reports indicate that thousands of migrants, including Nigerians, have departed the country through repatriation programmes.

The incident recalls a similar period in 2019, when xenophobic violence also spilled into the live entertainment business. At the time, Burna Boy withdrew from the Africa Unite concerts in Cape Town and Pretoria amid mounting security concerns and controversy surrounding comments he made during an earlier wave of alleged xenophobic attacks, where he said he would not return to South Africa until the government addressed the issue.

As a result, promoters issued a statement saying: “Following extensive engagement with Burna Boy’s management team, Phambili Media & Play Network have decided to withdraw Burna Boy from the Africans Unite concert.”

At the time, Tiwa Savage also pulled out of Johannesburg’s DStv Delicious Festival, saying she could not perform while Nigerians were being targeted.

These incidents have demonstrated how larger diplomatic and social tensions between Nigeria and South Africa can directly affect live activity between the continent’s two biggest music markets, even as commercial relationships between artists, promoters and audiences remain strong.

While cross-border touring between the two countries has continued to grow in recent years, Tyla’s removed Lagos date suggests that political developments can still create reputational and commercial risks for artists operating across both markets.

Whether the current situation proves temporary or develops into a longer-term consideration for promoters remains unclear. However, the episode highlights the extent to which events outside the live industry can very much influence tourin g decisions, particularly when artists become focal points for wider public sentiment.
