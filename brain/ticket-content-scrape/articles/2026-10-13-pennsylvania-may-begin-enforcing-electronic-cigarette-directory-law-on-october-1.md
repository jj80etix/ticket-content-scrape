# Pennsylvania May Begin Enforcing Electronic Cigarette Directory Law on October 19

## Summary

Pennsylvania's Attorney General announced that enforcement of the state's new electronic-cigarette directory law (Act 57) may begin October 19, targeting importers, wholesalers, and retailers selling ENDS products not listed in the state's approved directory. Non-compliant products face civil penalties, seizure, and destruction, with manufacturers required to post bonds and pay listing fees.

### Key Points
- PA AG enforcement of ENDS directory law (Act 57) begins as early as Oct. 19.
- Applies to importers, wholesalers, and retailers of unlisted vape/e-cigarette products.
- Manufacturers must post a $50,000+ surety bond and pay per-brand listing fees.
- Offshore manufacturers must name a U.S. importer who shares liability.
- Penalties escalate for repeat violations; inspectors will also offer compliance guidance.

On October 8, Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday [announced](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/taking-action/enforcement-of-act-57-which-prohibits-pa-sales-of-unapproved-vape-and-e-cigarette-products-can-begin-october-19/) that his office may begin enforcing the Commonwealth’s new electronic nicotine delivery system (ENDS) directory starting October 19. Pursuant to [Pennsylvania’s Act 57](https://www.palegis.us/statutes/unconsolidated/law-information?SessYr=2025&ActNum=0057.&plPageNum=0348&SessInd=0) (72 P.S. § 206-1), importers, wholesalers, and retailers may face civil penalties for selling electronic cigarettes not listed in the state’s ENDS directory, and unlisted products may be seized, forfeited, and destroyed.

The Attorney General plans targeted enforcement across Pennsylvania. Inspectors will provide compliance guidance, and penalties will increase for repeat violations.

### What Does Act 57 Say?

Act 57 requires the Attorney General to maintain a public directory of nicotine-containing electronic cigarettes approved for sale in Pennsylvania. Manufacturers must certify each year that their products meet the law’s requirements, including criteria tied to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration’s premarket review process.

Eligibility alone does not secure a listing. Manufacturers must post a surety bond of at least $50,000 and pay initial fees of $2,000 per brand family and $200 per brand style. Annual fees are $1,000 per brand family and $100 per brand style. Manufacturers outside Pennsylvania also must appoint an in-state agent for process service. Offshore manufacturers must identify their U.S. importers, which are jointly liable for penalties.

### Which ENDS Can Be Sold in Pennsylvania?

An ENDS product may be sold in Pennsylvania only if it has FDA marketing authorization or meets Act 57’s criteria for products in the federal premarket tobacco product application (PMTA) process.

Act 57 also addresses non-nicotine electronic cigarettes sold under the same brand name as an ENDS product. Unless the product is a part or component included in the related federal PMTA, the manufacturer must certify that it contains no nicotine and submit packaging images and a signed test from a certified domestic laboratory.

### Is Pennsylvania an Outlier or Part of a Movement?

Pennsylvania is part of a broader state movement to regulate electronic cigarettes through product directories, licensing, and sales restrictions. To date, approximately [18 states have adopted ENDS directory laws or similar provisions](https://www.publichealthlawcenter.org/resources/state-e-cigarette-registry-bill-map) and many more are considering similar legislation, and [as our team has discussed](https://www.tobaccolawblog.com/2026/08/appellate-vape-rulings-may-expand-state-regulation-powers/), most of these laws have so far survived legal challenges.

Many directory laws follow a similar pattern. Manufacturers certify each year that a product either (1) has an FDA marketing granted order, or (2) was on the market as of August 8, 2016, and has a PMTA filed by September 9, 2020, that is still pending or whose denial was stayed or vacated in order to be listed on the directory and sold in the state.

That second option tracks [FDA’s original 2020 enforcement discretion policy](https://www.fda.gov/media/133880/download). In other words, many states wrote FDA’s enforcement priorities into state law as a condition of sale. For example, Wisconsin’s directory law requires the manufacturer to certify either (1) that the product has received a marketing granted order or (2) was on the U.S. market as of August 8, 2016, the manufacturer submitted a premarket tobacco product application to FDA by September 9, 2020, and the application remains under FDA review or a final decision on the application has not otherwise taken effect. Wis. Stat. § 995.15.

Notably, the FDA’s 2020 enforcement discretion policy has now been superseded by a [new May 2026 policy](https://www.fda.gov/media/192403/download), which allows certain ENDS products that have reached the filing review stage to be sold pending FDA’s review.

Some directory laws have been updated to account for this change. North Carolina, for example, amended its directory law to cover products with pending premarket tobacco product applications that are marketed under a published FDA enforcement policy. This broader standard accounts for the May 2026 policy and future changes. N.C. Gen. Stat. § 143-245.11.

That means a product may be permitted to remain on the market under FDA policy but still be ineligible for sale in states with narrower directory laws.

In addition, some states combine directory requirements with flavor bans or nicotine caps. California bans flavored tobacco products, including ENDS, and limits its Unflavored Tobacco List to products certified as unflavored. Cal. Health & Safety Code §104559.1. In Utah, the definition of a “premarket authorized or pending electronic cigarette product” eligible for listing on the state registry does not include products that exceed 4.0% nicotine by weight per container or a nicotine concentration of 40 mg/mL. Utah Code § 76-9-1101(18). And Alabama prohibits the addition of ENDS products that are not made, packaged, labeled, and manufactured in the United States, unless they have a marketing granted order. Ala. Code § 28-11-17.2(b).

State regimes can also interact. Pennsylvania may deny certification to a manufacturer finally denied listing in, or involuntarily removed from, another state’s comparable directory. 72 P.S. § 206-I(j)(2).

Manufacturers will need to closely analyze the relevant state laws to determine whether their ENDS products are eligible for listing.

### What Happens if a Product Is Not Listed?

Beginning October 19, nicotine-containing electronic cigarettes intended for retail sale in Pennsylvania are contraband if they are not listed in the directory. They may be seized, forfeited, and destroyed.

Businesses whose products are confiscated must pay the destruction costs, which may exceed the civil penalty.

Products purchased in good faith by consumers are not subject to seizure.

The Attorney General and Department of Revenue may conduct unannounced inspections of businesses that store or sell covered products. Inspectors may review inventory, invoices, books, and other records.

Selling or offering an unlisted product carries a civil penalty of up to $500 for each electronic cigarette.

The Attorney General must maintain a public noncompliance database of manufacturers, importers, wholesalers, and retailers with at least three violations in the preceding 12 months. Buying from or selling to a listed business carries an additional penalty of up to $500 per product.

Violations also constitute unfair or deceptive practices under Pennsylvania’s Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law.

### What Should Businesses Do Before October 19?

Before October 19, manufacturers, distributors, and retailers selling in the Commonwealth should confirm that every covered product they sell appears in Pennsylvania’s ENDS directory. They should check regularly because listings may change.

Manufacturers must report material changes to their certifications, such as changes to a product’s FDA premarket review status. The Attorney General must update the directory at least monthly. Businesses should have procedures to remove, sell through, or return products that lose their listings.

### Why It Matters

FDA status alone does not establish compliance in Pennsylvania or other states with directory laws. Manufacturers must meet each state’s certification and listing requirements, and sellers must confirm that covered products remain listed. Beginning October 19, unlisted products in Pennsylvania may become contraband and expose businesses to seizure, destruction costs, civil penalties, and greater sanctions for repeat violations.
