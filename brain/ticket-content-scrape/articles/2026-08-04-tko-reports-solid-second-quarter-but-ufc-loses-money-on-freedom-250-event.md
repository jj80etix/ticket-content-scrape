# TKO reports solid second quarter, but UFC loses money on Freedom 250 event

## Summary

TKO Group Holdings, the parent of UFC, WWE and IMG, reported Q2 2026 revenue of $1.5 billion and net income of $303.9 million, up from $273.1 million a year earlier. UFC revenue rose to $535.7 million despite losing roughly $30 million on its invite-only Freedom 250 event at the White House, which the company had expected to run at a loss.

### Key Points
- Q2 revenue: UFC $535.7M, WWE $620.9M, IMG $354.7M; adjusted EBITDA $649.9M (+23% y/y).
- UFC's Freedom 250 White House event lost roughly $30M, an expected outcome tied to the 250th-anniversary celebration rather than a live-ticketing event.
- WWE revenue breakdown: $359.7M media rights/production, $152M live events/hospitality, $63.2M partnerships/marketing.
- IMG (including On Location) revenue skewed heavily to live events/hospitality ($198.5M) on FIFA World Cup demand.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**TKO Group Holdings, the sports and entertainment company behind UFC, WWE, sports marketing agency IMG, hospitality provider On Location and more, has reported a revenue of $1.5bn (£1.1bn/€1.3bn) for the second quarter of 2026.**

The company also posted a net income of $303.9m, and adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) of $649.9m. The net income total is an improvement of $30.8m from $273.1m in the prior year period, while adjusted EBITDA’s total is an increase of 23%.

Revenue for UFC amounted to $535.7m, compared to $415.9m for the second quarter last year, while the first six months in 2026 saw revenue reach $936.9m compared to $775.6m the year before.

Breaking down revenue for UFC, media rights, production and content brought in $325.2m, while $47.8m can be attributed to live events and hospitality. Partnerships and marketing brought in $144.8m, and consumer products, licensing and other amounted to $17.9m.

While UFC’s revenue increased, it lost around $30m on the Freedom 250 event, an [invite-only event on the South Lawn of the White House](https://www.thestadiumbusiness.com/2026/06/15/ufc-makes-history-with-white-house-event/) forming part of the celebrations around 250 years of American independence. However, the company expected to make a loss on the event.

WWE’s revenue for the second quarter of 2026 totalled $620.9, compared to $556.2m, with revenue for the first six months ended June 30 amounting to $1.096bn up from $947.7m. Meanwhile, IMG’s revenue for the second quarter equalled $354.7m compared to $306.6m. The six-month revenue mark totalled $1.01bn compared to $782.8m for the same period in 2025.

Breaking down revenue for WWE, $359.7m can be attributed to media rights, production and content, and $152m can be linked to live events and hospitality. Partnerships and marketing amounted to $63.2m and consumer products licensing and other totalled $46m.

IMG, which also includes On Location, saw most of its revenue attributed to live events and hospitality at $198.5m, followed by media rights, production and content at $147.2m. Partnerships and marketing brought in $3.5m, and consumer products licensing and other brought in $5.5m.

**‘Solid results in Q2’**

“Despite a challenging global environment, TKO delivered solid results in Q2, with strong momentum heading into the back half of the year,” said Ariel Emanuel, executive chair and chief executive of TKO.

“Premium live content and experiences are heating up in an increasingly AI-driven world, and our businesses are well positioned to fully capitalise on societal secular tailwinds.”

Mark Shapiro, president and chief operating officer of TKO, added: “From UFC Freedom 250 to the FIFA World Cup, TKO continues to deliver on the biggest stages and this quarter reinforced our 2026 execution story. Our decision to raise full-year guidance reflects both our performance to date and our confidence in TKO’s multi-year trajectory.

“Our global fan base is expanding, and we are capitalising on the commercial promise across ticketing, premium hospitality, marketing partnerships, and financial incentive packages. The demand in the experience economy is undeniable and positions us well for multi-year growth, margin expansion, and overall value creation.”
