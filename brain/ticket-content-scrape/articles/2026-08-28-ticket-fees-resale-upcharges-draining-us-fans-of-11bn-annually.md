# Ticket fees, resale upcharges ‘draining US fans of $11bn annually’

## Summary

A Vanderbilt University white paper by researcher Brian Shearer estimates US ticket buyers lose more than $11 billion (€9.5bn) annually to fees and resale upcharges, and proposes capping resale prices at face value and online processing fees at 10% as federal and state lawmakers continue to struggle to regulate the market.

### Key Points
- Estimated annual patron loss: over $11 billion (€9.5bn) in the US from fees and resale markups combined.
- Paper's proposed remedies: cap resale prices at face value; cap online processing fees at 10%.
- Frames large corporate ticket brokers and online ticketing platforms as jointly exploiting consumers' limited options.

### Industry Problem
This is a direct, dollar-quantified articulation of patron pain around fees and resale upcharges — the core driver behind ongoing state/federal legislative pushes (price caps, fee caps) — and academic research like this tends to get cited directly in the legislative debates the harvest already tracks (e.g., California's AB 1349/1720).

### Proposed Solution
The paper's own proposal is regulatory (price and fee caps) rather than product-driven, so the platform-side lever is transparency and pricing structure: leading with all-in pricing and keeping processing fees modest and clearly itemized would put a primary platform ahead of the exact complaint this research is fueling. Worth citing as grounding evidence for Etix Assistant's patron-facing fee/pricing context, and watching whether $11bn-style figures get cited directly in the current legislative session's floor debates.
