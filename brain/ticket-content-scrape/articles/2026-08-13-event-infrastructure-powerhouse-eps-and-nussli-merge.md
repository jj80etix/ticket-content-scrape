# Event Infrastructure Powerhouse: eps and NUSSLI Merge

## Summary

German event-infrastructure firm eps and Swiss firm NUSSLI have merged into a combined 820-employee, 50-location "globally active" temporary-event-infrastructure group led by former NUSSLI CEO Andy Böckli, spanning turf/crowd-control systems (eps) and temporary stadiums/grandstands (NUSSLI). Their combined client base includes Live Nation, AEG Live, the IOC, and major festivals like Coachella and Tomorrowland.

### Key Points
- Combined company: 820 employees, 50 locations; CEO is Andy Böckli (former NUSSLI Group CEO).
- eps (turf/ground protection, crowd control, 30 years old) and NUSSLI (temporary structures/stadiums, 85 years old) will keep operating under both brand names.
- NUSSLI's recent notable project: Adele's temporary stadium in Munich.
- Combined customer base includes Live Nation, WEF, AEG Live, IOC, FKP Scorpio, Formula 1, and festivals including Coachella, Tomorrowland, and EDC.
- Leadership frames the merger as a response to clients wanting fewer, broader-capability suppliers amid tighter event schedules.

### Highlights
- Merger creates a single-source supplier for turf/crowd control plus temporary stadiums/grandstands, serving Live Nation, AEG Live, and major festival organizers directly.
- Explicitly motivated by clients consolidating toward fewer infrastructure vendors under tighter production schedules.

### Industry Problem
Event organizers and promoters are pushing infrastructure suppliers to consolidate so they can coordinate fewer vendor relationships under increasingly compressed production timelines — a supply-side consolidation pattern mirroring the demand-side consolidation among promoters and ticketing platforms.

### Proposed Solution
No direct ticketing-platform action; useful as market-structure context for how infrastructure consolidation is reshaping who promoters and venues depend on — worth tracking if similar single-source consolidation pressure extends toward ticketing/technology vendor relationships.

Gemany-based eps and Switzerland-based NUSSLI have merged, creating a Europe-based, globally active provider of temporary event infrastructure.

The range of services both companies can now deliver from one source include turf and ground protection systems to crowd control, stages, and grandstands, all the way to temporary stadiums.

The new group employs 820 people at 50 locations and carries out projects around the world. The Group CEO is Andy Böckli, previously CEO of the NUSSLI Group. Both companies will continue to operate in the market as eps and NUSSLI, with their headquarters in Munich and Hüttwilen.

Eps, a leader in temporary turf and ground protection systems as well as crowd control, was founded 30 years ago and specializes in turf and ground protection systems, crowd control, and visitor management for events. The owner-managed family business, headquartered in Munich, employs 350 people at 29 locations and supports approximately 5,000 events annually, with a focus on Europe, North America, Brazil, and Australia.

For 85 years, NUSSLI has been constructing temporary grandstands, stadiums, stages, halls, expo pavilions, exhibitions, and special structures. The Swiss group employs 470 people at 21 locations and carries out approximately 1,500 projects annually, with a strong presence in its home market of Switzerland, as well as in Central and Southern Europe and the Americas.

The most notable recent project realized with the help of NUSSLI was Adele’s temporary stadium in Munich, Germany.

In 2017, an investor consortium led by Helvetica Capital invested in the NUSSLI Group during a special situation. Helvetica and the entrepreneurial investors brought a combination of operational experience, strategic clarity, and patient capital. This has enabled the management team, following the successful restructuring of the business, to lay the foundation for sustainable, profitable growth.

“It had been clear for some time that, following the successful turnaround and strong growth, the shareholders wished to arrange succession from a position of strength. Helvetica and NUSSLI’s management deliberately chose a strategic owner from within the industry who brings market knowledge and growth capital to the table. As a result, NUSSLI is transitioning into an owner-managed European family-owned company with a long-term perspective,” the press release announcing the merger with eps states.

The merger resulted is “a global market leader in temporary event infrastructure with 820 employees at 50 locations and projects around the world. The combined customer base includes, among others, Live Nation, the WEF, AEG Live, the IOC, FKP Scorpio, Formula 1, the ATP Tour, the Ski World Cup, the  
Ryder Cup, Red Bull, and organizers of international tours, as well as participants in world’s fairs and the world’s most renowned festivals such as Coachella, Tomorrowland, and EDC.

Both eps and NUSSLI are celebrating an anniversary this year: eps has been in business for 30 years, and NUSSLI for 85. “Together, that’s 115 years of experience that will be channeled into joint projects in the future,” the press announcement concludes.

Comments:

Tom Bilsen, CEO of eps, “We know from our clients that they prefer to work with a smaller number of suppliers in order to optimize coordination and meet ever-tighter schedules as well as stricter technical and organizational requirements. Starting today, that’s exactly what we can do. In addition, we will now also have a presence in markets where previously only one of the two companies was active.”

Andy Böckli, Group CEO, “Both companies are in a strong operational and financial position. That’s exactly why the timing is right: We’re acting from a position of strength. The merger is geared toward growth: Additional markets and a broader range of services mean increased expansion, with a corresponding need for additional investments and employees. The professional and consistent service our customers have come to expect will not change, but the range of services behind it will.”
