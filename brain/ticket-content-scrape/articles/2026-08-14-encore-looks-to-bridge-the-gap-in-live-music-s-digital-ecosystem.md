# Encore looks to bridge the gap in live music’s digital ecosystem

## Summary

Encore, a new social platform built by ex-Spotify employee Adam Offitzer and his brothers, aims to give concertgoers a single place to log shows, share memories and photos, follow tastemakers, and discover upcoming events — consolidating a live-music digital experience currently scattered across Reddit, Instagram, TikTok, Songkick, and Bandsintown. The platform is in beta with 1,000 sign-ups in its first week and an app launch targeted by year-end; founder Offitzer says the goal is not to build a ticketing platform but to potentially partner with existing ticketing/resale platforms.

### Key Points
- Founded by brothers Adam, Derek, and Jonah Offitzer; currently in beta with an app launch targeted by end of 2026.
- Reached 1,000 sign-ups in its first week, drawing music writers, artists, and industry figures alongside general concertgoers.
- Explicitly not pursuing a ticketing-platform model — founder says he wants to partner with existing platforms on tickets/resale rather than compete.
- Positions itself as "AI-proof" by emphasizing verified human posts over algorithmic content, differentiating from AI-driven discovery tools like Suno-owned Songkick.

## Transcript

## Encore looks to bridge the gap in live music’s digital ecosystem

![Adam Offitzer](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Adam-Offitzer-1.png)

Adam Offitzer

From discovery to purchase to post-show discourse, the live industry is fragmented across the digital realm – something ex-Spotify employee and writer Adam Offitzer is looking to build a new platform to piece the journey together into one central place.

Built by brothers Adam, Derek, and Jonah Offitzer, [Encore](https://encoreconcerts.fm/) is designed to give concertgoers a place to log shows, share memories and photos, follow friends and tastemakers, discover upcoming shows, and potentially connect with their favourite artists. The platform is currently in beta, with an app launch targeted by the end of the year.

It arrives at a pivotal time for the industry, with no shortage of concert clips and online discourse bringing live experiences online. But the disconnect across those platforms is at the core of Encore, which has a tagline of ‘where live music lives’.

“I always point to where all this stuff lives currently after a show, in places like Reddit, Instagram, and TikTok, or before shows on Songkick or Bandsintown,” Offitzer tells *IQ*. “None of this is a cohesive place where I can go and follow the coolest music writers, artists, friends, and family. There’s got to be something.”

The concept is less about creating another music discovery destination, [a key facet of social networks today](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/social-media-ban-live-impact/), and more about consolidating a fragmented experience around a live show.

Within its first week of beta testing, Encore reached 1,000 sign-ups, an early indicator of demand for the concept. With an initial audience of verified music writers and industry personnel, the online platform has quickly become a place to share personal memories, turning concert tracking into a social archive.

> “There’s no other platform where you can do that right now”

“I started seeing music critics, artists, and strangers direct messaging me, starting to log shows, and then writing very personal memories about their first shows, and it’s just been so fun to see those stories,” he shares. “There’s no other platform where you can do that right now, unless a TikTok or Instagram post happens to go viral. But this is dedicated to people who love this very specific thing, which is live music and concerts, so it’s been fun to watch.”.

From the beginning, Offitzer says he wanted to establish a layer of credibility by recruiting music writers and established industry figures, mixing a verified network of tastemakers with an expanding audience of concertgoers. According to a recent [Live Nation study](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/04/music-fandom-is-central-to-gen-zs-identity/), more than half of Gen Z rely on curated cultural gateways – things like playlists, creators, and live events – to navigate an oversaturated content ecosystem.

“I think it’s valuable to flag them as verified, and I’m trying to balance that with not wanting to gatekeep. I want everyone to feel like Encore is a space for them, and anyone can write about their shows and follow their friends and family,” Offitzer says. “But there is also a layer of trusted, reputable folks who are not tastemakers because I’ve deemed them to be, but are tastemakers because they’re reputable in the scene already. I am trying to have Encore be a place where it has weight to be verified or a curator in some way.”

Launching into a sector increasingly impacted by artificial intelligence and algorithms – with concert discovery leader [Songkick](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/11/ai-music-company-suno-acquires-songkick-from-wmg/) acquired last November by AI company Suno – Offitzer sees Encore’s human-led approach as a differentiator.

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/08/03-248x310.png.webp)

“The beauty of it, especially again in like the age of AI slop, is if someone goes to a concert one night and then posts about it on Encore, it would be very strange to create a spammy version of that,” he says. “You’ll only gain clout on this platform if you’re going to shows and if you have good taste, and if you share real memories you had there. I do view it as a little bit AI-proof in that way, and a little future-proof because live music is just going to be important forever.”

While in its early stages, the model could also extend beyond social engagement, with the potential to add a discovery-to-purchase pipeline that better connects fans with offline experiences. That could position Encore at the centre of fans, artists, venues, and tickets, rather than being a competitor to any.

“I have the benefit of being unbound from VCs \[venture capitalists\], investors, or a parent company, so I’m going to start by reaching out to the platforms that inspire me with how they handle tickets and resale. My goal is not to create a ticketing platform or get in the ticketing business, but to help people go to and explore great concerts in their areas, and engage with those shows.

“If I can team up with great platforms to make that a click away, I would love to do that,” he says, adding that independent and grassroots venues are at the forefront of his mind to support the spaces underpinning the ecosystem.

> “You’ll have the potential for a much more focused conversation between artists and fans”

Artists represent another growth vertical for Encore. The platform could give them channels to interact with fans or share memories from their shows, creating a concentrated way to connect the lifeblood of the live experience, in a model Offitzer compares to film-logging platform Letterboxd.

“On Encore, you’ll have the potential for a much more focused conversation between artists and fans that I find really exciting,” he says. “I’m curious to see how artists want to use this. There will be some who want to log every show with little stories and reflections. Some I think will just lurk, and some might not even use it. I’m excited to see how that progresses, too.”

Longer term, Offitzer sees Encore extending into the physical realm, too, with potential avenues of working with venues on promotion and engagement. Beyond a digital diary, the ambition with Encore is to strengthen the relationship between fans, venues, artists, and communities behind live music today.

In the meantime, the mission remains simple: create a singular digital home for in-person experiences.

He boils the mission down to saying: “Finally, there’s a dedicated place that’s just for people who love concerts.”
