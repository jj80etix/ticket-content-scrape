# Non-English language shows now 30% of Live Nation’s top 50 tours

## Summary

Live Nation CFO Joe Berchtold told a Goldman Sachs investor conference on Sept. 9 that non-English-language shows now make up 30% of the company's top 50 global tours, up from 8% pre-pandemic, and predicted that more than half of its top artists will soon be non-US/UK English-speaking acts. He said this growth is straining venue capacity, creating an arena infrastructure gap across roughly two-thirds of Live Nation's top 75 touring markets.

### Key Points
- Non-English shows: 30% of Live Nation's top 50 tours today vs. 8% pre-pandemic.
- Berchtold expects non-US/UK-English artists to exceed half of top-tier acts "before long."
- Arena infrastructure is described as a constraint in roughly two-thirds of Live Nation's top 75 city touring markets.
- Remarks made at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia and Technology Conference, Sept. 9, 2026.

### Highlights
- Non-English tours: 30% of Live Nation's top 50 (vs. 8% pre-pandemic) — a structural shift in global touring demand.
- Arena capacity/infrastructure is cited as a binding constraint in ~two-thirds of Live Nation's top 75 touring markets.

### Industry Problem
Rapid internationalization of top-tier touring demand is outpacing venue/arena infrastructure in many of the largest touring markets — a capacity pain point for promoters and, by extension, ticketing platforms serving those markets.

### Proposed Solution
The item names no specific fix beyond flagging the gap. Worth monitoring as a long-horizon signal: venue capacity constraints in high-growth international markets could shape where new primary-ticketing demand emerges over the next several years.
