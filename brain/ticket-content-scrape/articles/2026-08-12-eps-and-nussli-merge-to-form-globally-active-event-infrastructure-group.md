# EPS and NUSSLI merge to form ‘globally active’ event infrastructure group

## Summary

German firm eps and Swiss firm NUSSLI have merged into a combined 820-employee, 50-location temporary-event-infrastructure group led by former NUSSLI CEO Andy Böckli, pairing eps's turf/crowd-control services with NUSSLI's temporary stadiums and grandstands. Leadership frames the deal as a response to clients wanting fewer, broader-capability suppliers, with a combined customer base including Live Nation, AEG Live, the IOC, and major festivals like Coachella and Tomorrowland.

### Key Points
- Combined group: 820 staff, 50 locations; led by group CEO Andy Böckli (former NUSSLI CEO).
- eps (turf/ground protection, crowd control) and NUSSLI (grandstands, temporary stadiums) will continue operating under both existing brand names.
- Merger follows EPS's December 2025 hire of CEO Tom Bilsen, brought in with a mandate for international expansion.
- Combined client roster includes Live Nation, WEF, AEG Live, IOC, FKP Scorpio, and Formula 1.
- Leadership explicitly cites client demand for a "single source" of services amid tighter production schedules and stricter technical requirements as the deal rationale.

### Highlights
- Merger creates one supplier spanning both turf/crowd-control and temporary-stadium construction, serving the same major promoters/rights-holders (Live Nation, AEG Live, IOC) as ticketing platforms.
- Explicitly motivated by clients wanting fewer vendor relationships to coordinate amid tighter production timelines.

### Industry Problem
Vendor consolidation pressure — organizers wanting fewer, broader-capability suppliers — is reshaping event infrastructure the same way it's reshaping promoter and ticketing-platform relationships.

### Proposed Solution
No direct action for Etix; useful market-structure context showing consolidation pressure extending across the live-events supply chain, not just ticketing/promoter M&A.

## EPS and NUSSLI merge to form ‘globally active’ event infrastructure group

The new company combines EPS' turf and crowd control solutions with NUSSLI's temporary structure offerings based across 50 locations worldwide

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/08/eps_Nussli_Okan-Tombulca_li_Andy-Bockli_re_01_high-scaled.jpg.webp)

(Left) Okan Tombulca, owner, eps Nüssli (Right) Andy Böckli, group CEO, eps Nüssli

Event infrastructure firms EPS and NUSSLI have announced a merger, which the companies say will create “a Europe-based, globally active provider of temporary event infrastructure”.

EPS is a provider of turf and ground protection, as well as crowd control solutions, while NUSSLI supplies grandstands, event structures, and special-purpose structures, including temporary stadiums. With the merger, the company said its customers will receive “the full range of services from a single source”.

The new group employs 820 staff at 50 locations, and is led by former NUSSLI CEO Andy Böckli. Its combined customer base includes Live Nation, the WEF, AEG Live, the IOC, FKP Scorpio, and Formula 1. It noted its customers include additional firms that partner with festivals including Coachella, Tomorrowland, and EDC.

“The two companies also complement each other geographically through a strong market presence in regions that are, in some cases, distinct, and by serving different customer segments,” the companies said.

The merger comes as EPS has in recent months been strengthening its leadership team, with CEO Tom Bilsen [appointed in December 2025](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/12/eps-installs-tom-bilsen-as-ceo-targets-further-expansion/) with a remit to lead international expansion at the firm.

> “Both companies are in a strong operational and financial position. That’s exactly why the timing is right”

The merger is intended to foster global coverage through combining both companies’ existing market presences. The group also noted the new combined scale also provides advantages in terms of service availability and response times.

EPS CEO Bilsen said the move reflects that event clients “prefer to work with a smaller number of suppliers in order to optimise coordination and meet ever-tighter schedules as well as stricter technical and organisational requirements”.

Meanwhile, group CEO Böckli said: “Both companies are in a strong operational and financial position. That’s exactly why the timing is right: we’re acting from a position of strength. The merger is geared toward growth; additional markets and a broader range of services mean increased expansion, with a corresponding need for additional investments and employees.

“The professional and consistent service our customers have come to expect will not change, but the range of services behind it will.”
