# US gov’t sues California ticket broker over ‘depriving’ fans with $120m resale scheme

## Summary

The U.S. Department of Justice is suing San Diego-based ticket broker REPS & Company and three employees for allegedly violating the BOTS Act and FTC Act by circumventing security controls on Ticketmaster and AXS to acquire 18.5 million tickets -- including for Beyoncé and Post Malone shows -- generating more than $120 million in resale revenue since January 2022.

### Key Points
- DOJ alleges REPS & Co. and three employees bypassed "security measures, access control systems, or other technological controls" on Ticketmaster and AXS.
- Scheme allegedly ran from January 2022, acquiring 18.5 million tickets and generating over $120 million (~€107m) in revenue.
- Targeted high-demand acts including Beyoncé and Post Malone.
- Follows an August 2025 FTC action against Key Investment Group over bypassing security to resell Taylor Swift tickets at a markup -- signaling continued, active federal BOTS Act enforcement against ticket brokers.

### Industry Problem
Large-scale automated circumvention of primary-platform purchase controls continues at significant scale (18.5M tickets, $120M+) despite the BOTS Act, showing that bot-driven inventory diversion from fans to brokers remains a major unresolved structural problem for high-demand onsales.

### Proposed Solution
This is a DOJ enforcement action, not a new vendor technology -- the read-through for a primary platform is to keep investing in and monitoring bot-mitigation/access-control technology (queue integrity, device/behavioral fingerprinting), since federal enforcement acts as a backstop, not a substitute, for technical controls at the point of sale.
