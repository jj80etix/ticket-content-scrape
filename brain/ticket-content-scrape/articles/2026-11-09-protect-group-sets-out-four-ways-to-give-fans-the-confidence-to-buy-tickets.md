# Protect Group sets out four ways to give fans the confidence to buy tickets

## Summary

Protect Group, a ticket-protection/insurance vendor, published sponsored content via IQ Magazine arguing that fan "booking anxiety" — hesitation to complete a ticket purchase — is a measurable, buildable problem rather than something to merely manage at checkout. The piece cites Protect Group's "Global Booking Anxiety Study 2026," run with Phocuswright and Web in Travel, pointing to rising ticket prices and tighter household budgets as drivers of buyer hesitation. The article promises "four ways" to build booking confidence, but the full list sits behind IQ Magazine's paywall after the opening paragraph, so specifics aren't captured in this harvest.

### Key Points
- Protect Group frames fan "booking anxiety" (checkout hesitation) as a solvable, measurable problem for ticketing/live-event platforms, not just something to manage reactively.
- Evidence base is Protect Group's "Global Booking Anxiety Study 2026," conducted with Phocuswright and Web in Travel.
- Rising ticket prices and tighter budgets are cited as the main drivers of buyer hesitation.
- The promised "four ways to give fans confidence" are behind IQ Magazine's paywall — not visible in this harvest.
- This is sponsored/promotional content from Protect Group (a ticket insurance/protection vendor), published via IQ Magazine, not independent reporting.

### Highlights
- Headline claim: booking confidence can be "built, measured, and grown" rather than left to checkout-moment damage control.
- Named source: Protect Group's Global Booking Anxiety Study 2026 (Phocuswright + Web in Travel).
- Drivers cited: higher ticket costs and tighter buyer budgets.
- Content is paywalled after one paragraph — no hard figures from the study are available in this harvest.

### Industry Problem
Buyers increasingly hesitate before completing ticket purchases as prices rise and budgets tighten — a patron-side checkout-friction and conversion problem. Protect Group frames ticket protection/insurance as a candidate lever for reducing that hesitation, which is adjacent to broader refund/cancellation-flexibility expectations at checkout.

### Proposed Solution
The article's own pitch (undetailed behind the paywall) is that platforms should actively measure and build "booking confidence," implicitly through protection/refund-flexibility offerings at checkout. For Etix, the near-term response is to watch for the study's actual figures (or a follow-up with real numbers): if protection/flexible-refund messaging is shown to meaningfully reduce checkout abandonment, that would justify evaluating an add-on ticket-protection product or clearer refund-flexibility messaging at checkout as a concrete improvement.
