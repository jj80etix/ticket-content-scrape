# Jonathan Groff Sets Broadway Return in ‘The Amish Project’

## Summary

Jonathan Groff will return to Broadway on Feb. 10, 2027, as the sole performer in "The Amish Project" at the Broadhurst Theatre, a play inspired by a real shooting in an Amish community near Groff's hometown.

### Key Points
- Broadway run begins Feb. 10, 2027, at the Broadhurst Theatre.
- Groff is the sole performer; Jessica Dickey's Broadway playwriting debut, directed by Sarna Lapine.
- Play originally premiered Off-Broadway in 2009 at Rattlestick Playwrights Theater.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Broadways-The-Amish-Project-Jonathan-Groff.webp)

Jonathan Groff will return to Broadway on February 10, 2027, when performances of “The Amish Project” begin at the Broadhurst Theatre.

The Tony-winning actor will be the sole performer in Jessica Dickey’s play, directed by Sarna Lapine. The production marks Dickey’s Broadway debut as a playwright.

The Broadway engagement comes nearly two decades after the play’s Off-Broadway premiere. “The Amish Project” first appeared at the New York International Fringe Festival, continued its development at the Cherry Lane Theatre and opened at Rattlestick Playwrights Theater in 2009.

The play draws inspiration from a tragic shooting in an Amish community near where Groff grew up, examining its aftermath through questions of compassion, grief and forgiveness. Written for one performer, the play considers how that response affects those touched by the tragedy and challenges assumptions about what forgiveness requires.

In a statement announcing the production, he recalled the event that prompted Dickey to write the play. “20 years ago this month, an unimaginable tragedy happened in a one room Amish schoolhouse in Lancaster, Pennsylvania and Jessica Dickey wrote an astounding work of art in response,” Groff said.

Lapine introduced him to the script last year, setting the stage for their collaboration on the Broadway production.

“Sarna Lapine sent me ‘The Amish Project’ last year and I was floored by the urgency and potent poetry of Jessica’s writing as she dives headfirst into the many aftershocks of this horrific event and explores the idea that sometimes the most radical human act is forgiveness,” he said.

“I am profoundly grateful to be bringing Jessica’s play to Broadway alongside Sarna and this incredible team.”
