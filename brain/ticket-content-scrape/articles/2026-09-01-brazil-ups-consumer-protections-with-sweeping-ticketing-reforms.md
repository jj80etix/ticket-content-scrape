# Brazil ups consumer protections with sweeping ticketing reforms

## Summary

Brazil's government issued a sweeping regulatory decree overhauling ticket sales across both primary and secondary markets, mandating price and resale transparency, cracking down on deceptive and speculative resale practices, and outlawing mass ticket acquisition by bots. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva signed the decree on August 31, 2026, with full effect expected by the end of September 2026, covering concerts, festivals, and other ticketed events.

### Key Points
- Decree covers concerts, festivals, and other ticketed events nationwide.
- Signed Aug. 31, 2026; slated to fully take effect by end of September 2026.
- Explicitly bans mass/bot ticket acquisition.
- Mandates price and resale transparency; targets deceptive and speculative practices in both primary and secondary markets.
- Source article is paywalled after the opening paragraphs — implementation/enforcement mechanics are not yet detailed.

### Highlights
- Effective date: full effect by end of September 2026 (decree signed Aug. 31, 2026).
- Covers both primary and secondary ticket markets in one decree.
- Explicit bot-purchase ban paired with transparency mandates, echoing the U.S. federal/state junk-fee and bot-enforcement pattern.

### Industry Problem
Brazil joins a growing list of jurisdictions moving to regulate speculative resale and bot-driven ticket acquisition, signaling that this kind of enforcement pressure is becoming a global rather than purely North American compliance concern for ticketing platforms with any international exposure.

### Proposed Solution
Track the decree's implementing rules as they publish and benchmark current bot-mitigation and price-transparency practices against them now, in case future expansion into Latin American markets requires day-one compliance; the source gives no specifics on enforcement mechanics or vendor requirements yet.
