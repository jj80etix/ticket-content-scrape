# Germany confirms federal soundproofing funding support for club venues

## Summary

Germany's federal government has confirmed its noise-reduction soundproofing funding scheme for live clubs and festivals will continue in full after an oversubscribed pilot year, setting aside €1.5m. LiveKomm, the German clubs' industry body, welcomed the move as a "positive sign" but flagged that funding still falls well short of demonstrated demand.

### Key Points
- Federal ministry for housing and urban development has committed €1.5m to the national noise-reduction programme for venues and festivals.
- Noise pollution complaints are among the most common causes of music venue closures in Germany, per LiveKomm.
- The 2025 pilot phase drew 136 applications totalling over €16m in requests against just €3m available, showing acute undersupply.
- LiveKomm managing director Christian Ordon called the continuation a "sign of appreciation for club culture" but said funding should have increased to meet demand.
- Comes the same week LiveKomm elected a new board at its general meeting in Leipzig.

## Transcript

© LiveKomm

LiveKomm, Germany’s industry body for live clubs, has called the rubber-stamping of a federal soundproofing scheme for venues a “positive sign for the cultural landscape”, despite reduced funding compared to an initial pilot phase.

The federal ministry for housing and urban development has set aside €1.5m for the national ‘noise reduction programme’, which it has confirmed will continue in full after a vastly oversubscribed year-long pilot phase.

The programme aims to assist venues and festival to improve their soundproofing equipment, which LiveKomm said is “often a costly measure”.

Noise pollution conflicts are among the most frequent reasons for the closure of music venues in Germany, LiveKomm said. “Accordingly, noise abatement measures create the conditions necessary for housing and often already existing cultural offerings to be permanently reconciled in densely populated areas,” it added.

The [pilot phase](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2025/12/e3m-soundproofing-scheme-unveiled-for-german-venues/) was designed to support music clubs with a capacity of up to 2,000 in addition to festivals under threat due to significant noise complaints.

> “ Vibrant, mixed-use cities can only exist if a coexistence of culture and housing is guaranteed”

The organisation’s managing director, Christian Ordon called the news “a sign of appreciation for club culture”, while caveating that “an increase in funding would have been necessary to meet the demand” seen since the beginning of the pilot.

“Vibrant, mixed-use cities can only exist if a coexistence of culture and housing is guaranteed. With the construction accelerator, the federal government is increasing the pace of urban densification,” Ordon added.

“This entails the responsibility to effectively protect the existing cultural landscape. The Federal Noise Protection Program is an important component in this context. We are very pleased that we succeeded in continuing this program.”

However, LiveKomm said there is room to grow the scheme after applications for a share of the original €3m sum made available in 2025 far exceeded the grant.

“136 expressions of interest totalling over €16 million, with only €3 million in funding available: The need for better soundproofing among clubs and festivals is enormous, as demonstrated by the high number of applications during the pilot phase of the Federal Noise Protection Program,” the organisation said.

The news comes after LiveKomm [elected a new board](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/11/germanys-livekomm-elects-new-board-to-tackle-enormous-challenges/) during its general meeting in Leipzig this week.
