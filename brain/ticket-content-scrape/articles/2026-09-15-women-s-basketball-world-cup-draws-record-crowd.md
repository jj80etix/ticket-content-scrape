# Women’s Basketball World Cup Draws Record Crowd

## Summary

The 2026 FIBA Women's Basketball World Cup in Berlin drew a record 236,015 total spectators across 36 games, well above the previous record of 145,519 set in Sydney in 2022 despite two fewer games played. The U.S. won its 12th title and fifth straight championship, defeating France in the final after overcoming a 14-point halftime deficit.

### Key Points
- Average attendance of 6,556 per game, with multiple sold-out sessions at the 12,650-capacity Berlin Arena.
- New record exceeds the 2022 Sydney mark by more than 90,000 total attendees.
- U.S. beat France 97-79 in the final; Breanna Stewart named tournament MVP for the second time.
- Spain took bronze, defeating host Germany 81-58.

The 2026 FIBA Women’s Basketball World Cup drew a record crowd during its run in Berlin, with more than 236,000 spectators attending games across the tournament.

The competition, held from September 4 through September 13, attracted 236,015 attendees, significantly exceeding the previous Women’s Basketball World Cup attendance record of 145,519 set in Sydney in 2022.

The new mark was achieved despite the 2026 tournament featuring fewer games than the previous edition. Berlin hosted 36 matches, compared with 38 games played in Sydney.

Attendance averaged 6,556 spectators per game throughout the tournament, establishing another notable figure for the competition. The tournament also featured multiple sold-out sessions at Berlin Arena, which had a maximum capacity of 12,650 spectators for the event.

The previous record for attendance at a single Women’s Basketball World Cup game in the modern era remains the 2022 final between China and the United States. That matchup attracted 15,895 fans in Sydney.

On the court, the United States finished the tournament as champions after defeating France 97-79 in the final at Berlin Arena. The victory gave the U.S. its 12th Women’s Basketball World Cup title.

With the victory, the United States extended its winning streak at the Women’s Basketball World Cup to 36 consecutive games.
