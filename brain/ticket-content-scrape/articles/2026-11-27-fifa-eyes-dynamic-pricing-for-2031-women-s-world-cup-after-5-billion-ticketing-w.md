# FIFA Eyes Dynamic Pricing for 2031 Women’s World Cup After $5 Billion Ticketing Windfall

## Summary

Sources involved in the 2031 Women's World Cup bid (US/Mexico/Costa Rica/Jamaica) say dynamic/demand-based ticket pricing is now viewed as "virtually inevitable," following the 2026 men's World Cup's reported $5 billion in ticketing and hospitality revenue — despite the pricing drawing consumer backlash and NY/NJ attorney general subpoenas over prices, inventory releases, and seat-location disclosures. Bid-book projections ($35-$120 group stage, $120-$600 for the final) are explicitly non-binding; the 2026 tournament's top final-ticket price reached $10,990 versus an original ~$1,550 projection. Brazil's 2027 Women's World Cup will take the opposite approach, with no dynamic pricing and match packages as low as ~$5-$18.

### Key Points
- 2031 Women's World Cup bid projects $550M in ticket sales, $300M hospitality, $400M other matchday revenue — nearly 13x the $43M generated by the 2023 tournament; 4.5M tickets targeted.
- FIFA insists its 2026 approach was "variable," not "dynamic," pricing, but consumers saw prices escalate sharply between sales phases regardless of label.
- New York and New Jersey attorneys general subpoenaed FIFA in May over 2026 World Cup ticket prices, inventory releases, and allegedly misleading seat locations — an active state-AG inquiry into a major event organizer's pricing practices.
- FIFA also ran its own resale marketplace for the 2026 tournament, collecting fees from both buyers and sellers on top of primary-market revenue.

### Industry Problem
Demand-based primary pricing is proving highly profitable for organizers but widens the gap between advertised/projected prices and what fans actually pay, fueling affordability backlash and direct regulatory scrutiny (state AG subpoenas) of pricing transparency and fairness — a patron-trust and compliance risk that applies to any platform using dynamic pricing for high-demand events.

### Proposed Solution
The article describes no fix; it frames the Brazil 2027 approach (fixed, low-cost multi-match packages) as a live counter-example to dynamic pricing. For Etix: monitor how the NY/NJ AG inquiry resolves and whether it produces new disclosure norms for demand-based pricing (trigger for a posture change), while treating affordability/transparency complaints as grounding material for how dynamic-pricing UX should surface price-change reasoning to patrons.

## FIFA’s $5 Billion Precedent

As [TicketNews reported in October](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/fifa-world-cup-ticket-revenue-fan-backlash/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), FIFA’s reported $5 billion in ticketing and hospitality revenue from the 2026 World Cup came despite widespread criticism from supporters, consumer advocacy organizations and reported opposition from within FIFA itself.

The figure substantially exceeded FIFA’s original financial expectations, though audited results have yet to be released and the precise contribution of demand-based pricing to that revenue remains unclear.

FIFA maintained throughout the tournament that it was using variable pricing rather than dynamic pricing. In its [official ticketing guidance](https://gpcustomersupportfwc2026.tickets.fifa.com/hc/en-gb/articles/30201776314397-10-How-does-FIFA-determine-ticket-prices-for-the-FIFA-World-Cup-2026?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the organization distinguished its approach from automated pricing systems, explaining that prices could be adjusted following assessments of demand and available inventory.

For consumers, the practical consequence was that the price of a ticket could increase considerably between sales phases.

FIFA also operated its own resale marketplace, collecting fees from both buyers and sellers and giving the governing body another means of generating revenue from tickets after their initial purchase.

The organization’s ticketing practices attracted regulatory attention, with the attorneys general of New York and New Jersey [issuing subpoenas in May](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-and-attorney-general-davenport-subpoena-fifa-over-world?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) as part of an investigation into concerns about ticket prices, inventory releases and potentially misleading seat locations.

Sources cited in the latest Guardian report pointed to the prevalence of uncapped ticket resale in the United States as part of the rationale for expecting demand-based primary-market pricing in 2031.

That justification raises a familiar question for the ticketing industry: whether efforts by event organizers to capture more of the market value of their tickets ultimately improve access for consumers or simply redirect revenue that might otherwise be generated on the secondary market.

The commercial success of FIFA’s 2026 tournament does not necessarily answer that question. Strong attendance and record revenue demonstrate the willingness and ability of some consumers to pay elevated prices, but reveal considerably less about the supporters priced out of attending.

## A Different Approach in Brazil

The next Women’s World Cup, taking place in Brazil in 2027, will reportedly not feature dynamic pricing.

FIFA is scheduled to [begin ticket sales for that tournament on Dec. 1](https://www.fifa.com/en/articles/ticket-sales-begin?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), offering multi-match packages that include tickets priced as low as 30 Brazilian reais, or approximately $5 per match.

Some three-match packages will cost less than the equivalent of $18, with additional discounts available to eligible spectators under Brazilian law.

Different economic conditions, stadium capacities and levels of demand make direct comparisons between Brazil and the United States imperfect. Nevertheless, the two tournaments could offer sharply contrasting approaches to building audiences for women’s soccer.

In [announcing the successful 2031 bid](https://www.ussoccer.com/stories/2026/11/federation/united-states-mexico-costa-rica-jamaica-awarded-fifa-womens-world-cup-2031?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), U.S. Soccer emphasized its ambitions to expand participation in the women’s game and create opportunities for future generations of players.

Whether the ticketing strategy adopted for 2031 will make the tournament accessible to those same future generations of players and supporters remains one of the more consequential decisions facing FIFA and the tournament’s organizers.
