# Pennsylvania AG Reaches Settlement With Mavis Tire Over Alleged Deceptive Auto Repair Practices

## Summary

Pennsylvania's Attorney General reached a $215,000 settlement with Mavis Tire Supply over allegations of deceptive auto-repair sales practices, including charging for unnecessary or unperformed services. Mavis denied wrongdoing but agreed to consumer restitution, compliance reporting and a suspended $100,000 penalty; affected consumers have until January 26, 2027, to file complaints.

### Key Points
- $215,000 settlement ($190K restitution, $25K investigative costs) plus a suspended $100K civil penalty.
- Allegations: unnecessary repairs recommended/charged, and billing for services not actually performed.
- Mavis denies all allegations; settlement filed as an Assurance of Voluntary Compliance, not an admission of liability.
- Two years of mandatory compliance assessments, employee training and AG reporting required.
- Consumer complaint window open through Jan. 26, 2027.

## Transcript

Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday [announced a settlement](https://cdn.attorneygeneral.gov/wp-content/uploads/Mavis-Tires-AVC-Packet.pdf) on September 28, 2026, with Mavis Tire Supply to resolve allegations that the company engaged in deceptive sales and repair practices. Under the settlement, filed in the form of an Assurance of Voluntary Compliance (AVC), Mavis agrees to pay $215,000 — $190,000 for consumer restitution and $25,000 for investigative costs. The agreement also provides for a suspended $100,000 civil penalty that could become due if Mavis is found to have violated the agreement. Mavis agreed to the settlement without admitting liability or any wrongdoing.

Mavis, a provider of complete tire services, oil changes, brakes, alignments, and general automotive repair, was allegedly recommending and charging consumers for unnecessary vehicle repairs and maintenance services. Mavis was also allegedly claiming that repairs had been completed when they had not and charging for services not performed. The commonwealth alleged that the conduct violated provisions of Pennsylvania’s Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law as well as the state’s Automotive Industry Trade Practices regulations.

Mavis has denied all allegations and denied any violation of Pennsylvania law.

In addition to the monetary payment, the settlement contains several compliance-related obligations. For the next two calendar years, Mavis must conduct annual compliance assessments and provide reports to the commonwealth. The company also agreed to maintain oversight, employee training, and monitoring measures designed to ensure compliance with applicable consumer protection laws and automotive repair regulations. Moreover, the agreement requires cooperation with the attorney general’s office in identifying and compensating eligible consumers.

The attorney general’s office has encouraged potentially affected consumers to submit complaints before January 26, 2027, to determine whether they may be eligible for restitution.

### Why It Matters

The settlement demonstrates that state AGs remain focused on allegedly unnecessary or unperformed services as well as alleged inaccurate service representations, particularly in regulated consumer-service industries. It also underscores how state AGs will use industry-specific rules coupled with general consumer protection laws to pursue restitution for consumers. More broadly, the settlement shows that enforcement resolutions may extend beyond monetary payments to require employee training, internal oversight, compliance monitoring, reporting, and other operational changes. Companies in regulated service sectors should therefore maintain strong controls over sales practices, employee recommendations, service documentation, and quality assurance.
