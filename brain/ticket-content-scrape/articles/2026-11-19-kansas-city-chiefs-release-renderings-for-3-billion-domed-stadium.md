# Kansas City Chiefs Release Renderings For $3 Billion Domed Stadium

## Summary

The Kansas City Chiefs unveiled renderings for their planned $3 billion domed stadium in Wyandotte County, Kansas, set to open in 2031, featuring a translucent dome, tailgating areas, an adjacent entertainment district and Final Four adaptability. MANICA is lead design architect, HNTB is architect of record, and CAA ICON served as owner's representative through the RFQ/RFP process.

### Key Points
- $3 billion domed stadium planned for Wyandotte County, Kansas; targeted 2031 opening.
- Renderings unveiled at the first Kansas Sports Facilities Authority meeting.
- Design includes a translucent dome (visible military flyovers), tailgating zones, and an entertainment district with restaurants and a team shop; adaptable for events like the NCAA Final Four.
- MANICA (lead design architect) and HNTB (architect of record) are leading design; CAA ICON served as owner's representative for the selection process.

## Transcript

The Kansas City Chiefs unveiled renderings for their planned $3 billion domed stadium in Wyandotte County, Kansas, which is set to open in 2031.

Released during the first Kansas Sports Facilities Authority meeting, Chief’s President Mark Donovan said, “Today is a historic milestone for the Chiefs and the state of Kansas, and one worth celebrating. The documents before you provide the framework for achieving our shared vision. An extraordinary amount of work brought us here. Now the real work begins: turning that vision into reality and delivering on our commitments.

“Success will require a strong partnership between the Chiefs and this Authority,” he continued. “We are committed to working together, addressing the challenges ahead and delivering a project that both the Chiefs and the state of Kansas will be proud of.”

![kcinterior](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/kcinterior.webp)

Translucent dome makes military flyovers visible to fans. Rendering courtesy Kansas City Chiefs/MANICA

Among the versions were images of tailgaters, exterior fireworks, gameday crowds and even an example of how the stadium could be adapted to host the NCAA Final Four. Another rendering offered a first look at the possible entertainment district, with a paved walking area surrounded by restaurants and a Chiefs shop.

Earlier this year the Chiefs named Kansas City-based MANICA as the lead design architect for the stadium. In addition, the Kansas City-based sports architecture firm HNTB was selected as the architect of record (AOR) for the project. CAA ICON is serving as owner’s representative on the stadium project, having led all elements of the RFQ, RFP, and lead design competition processes.

“It is the honor of a lifetime to be entrusted with designing the next home of the Kansas City Chiefs in our hometown,” stated David Manica, President and owner, MANICA, following the announcement. “My entire team is committed to creating a stadium that honors the legacy, tradition, and unmatched energy of Arrowhead while boldly reimagining its future. This stadium is being designed not just for today’s fans, but for the generations of Chiefs Kingdom to come – a place that reflects the passion, pride, and enduring strength of one of the greatest fan bases in all of sports.”

The state-of-the-art stadium will be fully enclosed with a translucent ETFE roof and is expected to have a total capacity of approximately 70,000. Parking lot infrastructure will be built specifically to enhance the tailgating experience. The stadium’s size, shape, and roof geometry have been studied by audio engineers to maximize crowd noise and help ensure that Chiefs fans can continue to provide an unrivaled homefield advantage.

The stadium’s seating bowl is reminiscent of Arrowhead Stadium and will include a Drum Deck for pregame and in-game ceremonies, as well as the prominent placement of the Chiefs Ring of Honor above the bowl. The roof will also include more than 400,000 square feet of translucent panels directly above the playing surface, maximizing visibility for military flyovers.

Outside the stadium, Chiefs fans will be able to visit the Lamar Hunt statue in a modern plaza, access a specially curated Chiefs Hall of Honor more than twice the size of its current space, and shop at the expanded Chiefs Team Store, which is approximately four times larger than the current store.

![KCChiefsBB1Manica](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/KCChiefsBB1Manica.webp)

Stadium design could potentially accommodate NCAA Final Four. Renderings courtesy of Kansas City Chiefs/MANICA

“The opportunity to help shape the next home of the Kansas City Chiefs is deeply meaningful to HNTB,” added Tom O’Grady, President of HNTB and Project Executive. “This project brings together our deep roots in Kansas City and our experience delivering transformative sports and entertainment venues that elevate the fan experience and strengthen community connections. Together with MANICA, we look forward to creating a destination that reflects the legacy of the Chiefs and becomes an enduring landmark for generations of fans.”

The project is being financed through a public-private partnership, with the public portion funded through Sales Tax and Revenue (STAR) bonds and the Attracting Professional Sports to Kansas Fund. Both funding mechanisms were established specifically to ensure that there will be no new state taxes and no negative impact on the state budget. The remaining portion will be privately funded. The project also includes $1 billion in additional development in the State of Kansas.
