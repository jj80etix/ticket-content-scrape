# Ticket Broker Elite Events Settles With FTC For $300K

## Summary

The FTC reached a settlement with Georgia-based ticket broker Elite Events (doing business as SmartScalpers) over alleged BOTS Act violations, requiring $300,000 in civil penalties out of a $10.7 million total that is largely suspended. The complaint alleges the broker used hundreds of fictitious and employee-named purchasing accounts, virtual credit card numbers, IP proxy services, and multi-session browsers to bypass ticket-purchase limits across more than 2,400 events, then resold the tickets at a markup on the secondary market.

### Key Points
- Settlement: $300,000 payable now; total civil penalty is $10.7M, with the remainder suspended unless principals Kevin W. McKerley and Aaron L. Vera are found to have misrepresented their finances.
- Alleged tactics: fictitious/employee-named purchasing accounts, virtual credit card numbers, IP proxy services, and multi-session browsers used to defeat purchase limits.
- Scope: circumvented purchase limits across 2,400+ events; bought tickets for $50–$270 and resold for $100–$400.
- Example cited: 277 Metallica tickets at a Virginia Tech concert (Sept. 2024–Mar. 2025) obtained via 75 accounts, against a seller-imposed 6-ticket limit.
- Proposed order bars Elite Events from the conduct alleged in the complaint going forward.

### Highlights
- $300K penalty (with $10.7M suspended) is one of the larger publicized BOTS Act enforcement actions to date.
- The complaint details specific circumvention tactics — fictitious accounts, virtual cards, IP proxies, multi-session browsers — effectively a checklist of what bot-mitigation controls must defend against.
- Scale (2,400+ events) shows bot-driven purchase-limit circumvention operates systematically, not as isolated incidents.

### Industry Problem
Even with active FTC enforcement, brokers continue to circumvent per-purchaser ticket limits at scale using automated account creation and identity/IP obfuscation, showing that purchase-limit rules alone are insufficient bot defense for primary ticketing platforms.

### Proposed Solution
The item documents after-the-fact enforcement rather than a product fix; the actionable response for a primary platform is hardening BOTS Act compliance and bot mitigation — stronger identity verification, device/IP fingerprinting, and anomaly detection tuned to the specific tactics named in this complaint — while monitoring whether rising FTC enforcement volume changes the cost-benefit calculus for scalping operations.

A Georgia-based ticket broker will pay $300,000 in civil penalties to settle allegations it violated the BOTS Act.

In a complaint, the Federal Trade Commission alleged that Elite Events — which also does business as SmartScalpers and via smartscalpers.com — “used a variety of unlawful tactics to bypass ticket purchasing limits for more than 2,400 different events and resold those tickets on the secondary marketplace at a significant profit,” purchasing millions of dollars worth of tickets to high-profile events.

“For example, Elite Events used 75 accounts to purchase 277 tickets to a Metallica concert at Virginia Tech University between September 2024-March 2025 even though the ticket seller limited purchasers from buying more than six tickets,” the FTC said. “After Elite Events spent between $50 to $270 per ticket, they resold the tickets in the secondary marketplace for $100 to $400 per ticket, according to the complaint.”

The FTC alleged Elite Events used hundreds of purchasing accounts, created under fictitious names or with names of the firm’s employees; created virtual credit card numbers; and operated using IP proxy services and multi-session browsers, which, the FTC says, allowed the company to bypass controls set up by the primary ticketing services.

The proposed order establishes civil penalties of $10.7 million with all but the $300,000 suspended. The full amount will be payable if the company’s principles — Kevin W. McKerley and Aaron L. Vera — are found to have lied about their finances. In addition, Elite Events is barred from the behavior alleged in the original complaint.
