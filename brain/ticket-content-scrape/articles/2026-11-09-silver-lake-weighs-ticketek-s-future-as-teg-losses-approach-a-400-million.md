# Silver Lake Weighs Ticketek’s Future as TEG Losses Approach A$400 Million

## Summary

Private equity owner Silver Lake is weighing a sale or restructuring of Ticketek parent TEG after the Australian ticketing/entertainment group posted roughly A$397 million in combined losses over three years and carries more than A$1 billion in debt at ~9.32% interest; adviser Moelis is reportedly gauging buyer interest at an ~A$1.5 billion valuation, well below the A$2.5B+ Silver Lake once sought. The financial pressure follows TEG losing major venue contracts to Ticketmaster (Venues NSW) and AXS (Melbourne Park) over the past year.

### Key Points
- Three-year losses: A$97M (FY24) + A$160M (FY25) + A$140M (FY26) ≈ A$397M combined; debt >A$1B at ~9.32% average rate.
- Silver Lake added two distressed-debt/restructuring specialists to TEG's board in September (AlixPartners' Matt Hinds, ex-Goldman's Steven Sher).
- Moelis approaching prospective buyers at ~A$1.5B valuation; no transaction announced, could be sale, refinancing, or restructuring.
- Lost Venues NSW ticketing contract (Accor Stadium, SCG, CommBank Stadium, Allianz Stadium) to Ticketmaster; lost Melbourne Park (Rod Laver Arena, John Cain Arena) to AXS.
- New CEO Cameron Hoy (since June) has cut ~44 positions and reorganized touring leadership since taking over from short-tenured Brad Banducci.
- Silver Lake bought TEG in 2019 at ~A$1.3B valuation; an earlier attempt to sell above A$2.5B did not close.

### Highlights
- A$397M in three-year losses and >A$1B in debt at ~9.32% interest are driving a potential sale/restructuring.
- TEG has lost two marquee Australian venue-ticketing contracts to Ticketmaster and AXS in the past year, eroding its venue-relationship moat.
- Reported shop-around valuation (~A$1.5B) is roughly 40% below Silver Lake's earlier >A$2.5B ask.

### Industry Problem
A heavily leveraged primary ticketing/venue-services incumbent is losing long-term venue contracts — the core recurring-revenue relationship in this business — to better-capitalized competitors, illustrating how balance-sheet weakness translates directly into competitive share loss.

### Proposed Solution
TEG's own response is cost-cutting, leadership restructuring and exploring a sale/refinancing to shore up its balance sheet. For Etix, the situation is a market-structure signal to monitor: a sale could introduce a new, better-capitalized Ticketek competitor in APAC, or further distress could open venue-relationship opportunities if a buyer deprioritizes assets — worth tracking the eventual buyer's identity and strategy.

Private equity firm Silver Lake is weighing the future of Ticketek parent company TEG as the Australian ticketing and entertainment giant faces mounting financial pressure, with nearly A$400 million in losses over three years, more than A$1 billion in reported debt and new restructuring expertise added to its board.

According to a November 8 [report from the Australian Financial Review](https://www.afr.com/companies/media-and-marketing/silver-lake-appoints-crisis-debt-specialists-to-ticketek-board-20261108-p61bgh?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), TEG recorded a loss of approximately A$140 million in its most recent financial year. The financial disclosures come as Silver Lake explores options for its investment, including a potential sale or restructuring of the business. No transaction has been announced, and it remains unclear whether Silver Lake will pursue a sale, refinancing or another restructuring arrangement.

Corporate accounts reviewed by the publication also showed that Silver Lake sought a US$52.5 million loan, equivalent to approximately A$75 million, for the business. Most of that facility had reportedly been drawn by early August.

## Major Ticketing Contract Losses Add to Challenges

TEG’s financial pressures have coincided with a series of competitive setbacks in the Australian ticketing market.

As [TicketNews previously reported](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/ticketek-parent-teg-reportedly-begins-business-restructure/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), Ticketek lost two major venue ticketing agreements earlier this year, with the Venues NSW contract moving to Live Nation-owned Ticketmaster and the Melbourne Park agreement awarded to AEG’s AXS.

The Venues NSW agreement covers high-profile facilities including Accor Stadium, the Sydney Cricket Ground, CommBank Stadium and Allianz Stadium.

Meanwhile, AXS secured the Melbourne Park ticketing contract, covering Rod Laver Arena, John Cain Arena, Margaret Court Arena and AAMI Park.

AXS also expanded its Australian presence through a ticketing agreement with Sydney’s Hordern Pavilion.

Those contracts are especially important in an industry where long-term venue relationships provide ticketing companies with recurring event inventory, access to promoters and opportunities to develop additional commercial partnerships.

Losing major venue agreements can therefore affect more than immediate ticketing revenue. It can also weaken a platform’s competitive position when pursuing future event and venue business.

For Ticketmaster and AXS, the contract changes have expanded their presence in a market historically dominated by Ticketek. For TEG, rebuilding market share may require continued spending on ticketing technology, commercial partnerships and competitive bids at a time when substantial debt limits financial flexibility.

The company’s challenges are not confined to ticketing. TEG also operates a major live entertainment portfolio encompassing concert promotion, festivals, touring businesses and venue operations, making the financing situation relevant to a broader range of live-event industry participants.

## Leadership Changes and a New Direction

The financial developments follow a turbulent period of leadership changes and restructuring at TEG.

Former Woolworths chief executive Brad Banducci, who joined TEG in 2025, departed after approximately 13 months in the role. [Cameron Hoy took over as group chief executive on June 1](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/ticketek-parent-teg-names-cameron-hoy-ceo-as-brad-banducci-exits-after-short-tenure/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), returning day-to-day leadership to a longtime Ticketek executive.

Within weeks, Hoy initiated a cost-cutting and organizational restructuring program reportedly affecting approximately 44 positions across Australia and the Philippines.

The company also began reconsidering elements of its touring strategy, including international expansion plans announced earlier in the year.

In September, TEG [reorganized its touring leadership](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/mark-dodds-teg-live-touring-leadership/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), appointing Mark Dodds to lead TEG Live and Handsome Tours while elevating Paul Dainty to president of touring and theatrical. The changes followed the departure of longtime touring executive Tim McGregor and came as the company emphasized a more focused Asia-Pacific strategy.

In its [September leadership announcement](https://www.teg.com.au/teg-unveils-powerhouse-leadership-lineup/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), TEG characterized the changes as part of a strategic reset intended to strengthen the business across ticketing, technology and live entertainment.

The company’s continuing investments and leadership appointments suggest it remains focused on competing for future business rather than simply reducing operations to service its debt.

Still, the financial disclosures introduce another dimension to the turnaround effort: whether TEG can rebuild its competitive position quickly enough to satisfy lenders and support the investment returns sought by Silver Lake.

## What a Potential Sale Could Mean for Ticketing Competition

A sale or restructuring of TEG could have significant implications for competition in Australia’s ticketing market.

Ticketek remains a major competitor to Ticketmaster and AXS, and its ownership of complementary entertainment businesses gives it relationships extending beyond ticketing technology alone.

A change in ownership could bring additional investment to the platform or a different strategy for its ticketing, touring and venue businesses. Conversely, a restructuring focused primarily on reducing debt could affect the resources available for expansion and competition.

The identity of any eventual buyer would also matter. An acquisition involving an existing ticketing competitor would raise different competitive considerations than a transaction with a financial investor or a restructuring that leaves the business operating independently.

There is currently no indication that Ticketek has suspended ordinary ticketing operations or that the financial reports themselves affect the validity of tickets purchased by consumers. Nor do the reported losses or board appointments establish that the company is insolvent.

The immediate questions concern Silver Lake’s ownership strategy, TEG’s financing arrangements and the ability of Hoy’s management team to restore growth after a series of important contract losses.

With prospective buyers reportedly being approached and restructuring expertise now represented on the board, the next steps could determine both TEG’s ownership future and the competitive landscape of Australian live-event ticketing.
