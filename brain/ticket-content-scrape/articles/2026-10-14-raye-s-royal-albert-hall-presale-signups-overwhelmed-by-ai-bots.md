# Raye’s Royal Albert Hall Presale Signups ‘Overwhelmed’ By AI Bots

## Summary

Raye scrapped the presale for her one-off Royal Albert Hall show after 1.8 million signups overwhelmed the registration process with AI bots, far beyond the 5,000-capacity venue. Rather than risk a presale scalpers could exploit, she and her team are manually reviewing the sign-up list to shortlist real fans — an "analog" workaround in place of automated bot screening. The venue said it already blocks tens of millions of bot requests per event and still regularly sees tickets resold at inflated prices.

### Key Points
- 1.8 million signups for a 5,000-capacity venue; Raye called the bot scale unprecedented ("I have never witnessed bots on this scale").
- Presale was dropped entirely in favor of manual review of the sign-up list to identify likely real fans.
- Royal Albert Hall says it has blocked up to 38 million bot requests in a single 24-hour period for past onsales.
- Venue reports continued secondary-market resale at "vastly inflated prices" despite existing bot-mitigation measures.

### Highlights
- 1.8M signups vs. 5,000 capacity — a ~360:1 ratio that broke automated screening.
- Manual, human-reviewed shortlisting replaced the presale entirely rather than being layered on top of it.
- Royal Albert Hall's own anti-bot tooling (blocking up to 38M requests/24h) was insufficient to prevent this signup flood.

### Industry Problem
Existing automated bot-mitigation (queue gating, CAPTCHA-style screening) is being overwhelmed at the registration/signup stage itself, before any ticket sale begins — forcing high-profile artists to abandon self-service presale mechanics altogether in favor of manual, non-scalable fan vetting. This is the same failure mode flagged in the 2026-10-13 note on this same show's earlier presale attempt, suggesting the problem persisted through subsequent mitigation attempts.

### Proposed Solution
Raye's team addressed it with manual list review — not a solution a platform can offer at scale. For a primary platform like Etix, this strengthens the case for identity-verification or proof-of-fandom gating upstream of ticket sale (e.g., verified fan programs, account-age/history signals, or CAPTCHA alternatives resistant to LLM-driven bots) rather than relying solely on post-signup bot filtering.

Presale plans for Raye’s one-off at the Royal Albert Hall have been dropped after signups were “overwhelmed” by AI bots.

There were 1.8 million signups for the show at the historic 5,000-cap venue, where Raye is scheduled to perform “This Symphony May Contain Hope” Oct. 23.

“It is a new world we are living in now,” the singer posted to [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/Dea0PM_m-nj/?hl=en&img_index=1). “I have never witnessed bots on this scale.”

Saying that she wants her fans to be able to purchase tickets at face value rather than relying on the secondary market, Raye said she and her team are opting for an analog solution.

“Therefore, rather than risk opening a presale that scalpers could exploit, we felt the best and safest solution for this specific show was to manually review the sign-up list to identify a shortlist of people we believe are real fans,” she said.

The Royal Albert Hall said it regularly takes measures to combat bots and scalpers, once blocking 38 million bot requests in a 24-hour period.

“Despite these measures, we regularly see tickets advertised at vastly inflated prices. Fans deserve to see the artists they love without being exploited by ticket touts,” the venue said.

Raye is currently opening dates for Bruno Mars. During a spring and summer headline tour, she sold out two nights at Los Angeles’ Greek Theatre May 12-13, grossing a combined $832,044 on 11,785 tickets, according to Pollstar Boxoffice reports.

She’s represented by THE·TEAM.
