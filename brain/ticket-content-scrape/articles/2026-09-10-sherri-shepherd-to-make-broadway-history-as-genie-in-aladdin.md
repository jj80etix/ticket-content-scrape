# Sherri Shepherd to Make Broadway History as Genie in ‘Aladdin’

## Summary

Sherri Shepherd will make Broadway history playing the Genie in "Aladdin."

### Key Points
- Casting announcement for an existing Broadway production.
- Notable for being a historic casting choice per the outlet.

## Transcript

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Aladdin-Broadway.webp)

Sherri Shepherd will return to Broadway this fall in Disney’s “Aladdin,” becoming the first woman to play the Genie in any Disney-produced stage version of the musical.

The Daytime Emmy Award-winning talk show host, actress and comedian will begin performances October 16 at the New Amsterdam Theatre. Her limited engagement is scheduled to continue through January 3, 2027. Current Genie Caleb A. Barnett will remain with the production as the standby during Shepherd’s run.

“Joining *Aladdin* on Broadway is an absolute dream come true—and an incredible honor,” Shepherd said in a statement. “The Genie is such an iconic character, and I’m beyond excited to bring my own energy, humor, heart, and a little bit of magic to this role.”

“I hope audiences see themselves in this Genie and leave the theatre feeling so much joy and inspired to believe in the impossible,” Shepherd continued. “To step on that stage as the first woman to ever play the Genie is something I don’t take lightly. I’m ready to rub the lamp, unleash some magic, and have the time of my life on Broadway.”

Disney Theatrical Group Managing Director Andrew Flatt and Executive Producer Anne Quart shared, ““Throughout her career, Sherri has represented delight, wit, and loving good humor, making her the perfect entertainer to play the life force that is the Genie.”

“We can’t wait to see what happens when Sherri’s authentic, brilliant comic energy meets the joyous escape of Aladdin. Audiences are going to adore her take on this iconic role,” the producers continued.

James Monroe Iglehart originated the Genie role when “Aladdin” opened on Broadway and won the 2014 Tony Award for best featured actor in a musical. Other Broadway performers who have played the character include Major Attaway, Michael James Scott and Barnett.
