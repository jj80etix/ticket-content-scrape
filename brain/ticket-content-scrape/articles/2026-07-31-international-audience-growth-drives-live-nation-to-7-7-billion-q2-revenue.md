# International audience growth drives Live Nation to  $7.7 billion Q2 revenue

## Summary

Live Nation reported Q2 2026 revenue of $7.7 billion (up 9% year-on-year), driven by 10% international audience growth and what executives called record consumer spending, dismissing "blue dot fever" concerns about tour cancellations. Ticketmaster grew AOI 14% on 90 million fee-bearing tickets (+8%), while Live Nation continued an aggressive venue-acquisition push and defended its Spotify "Reserved Feature" presale-ticket tie-up against criticism from artist agents and managers.

### Key Points
- Q2 revenue of $7.7B (+9% YoY); operating income $522M (+7%); adjusted operating income grew only 2% due to tour timing and continued venue/festival investment.
- International attendance grew 10%, adding 5M fans to a record 49M global total for the quarter; management guided to double-digit international fan growth through Q3/Q4.
- CEO Michael Rapino cited a 1.1% cancellation rate, below the 1.6% historical average, to rebut "blue dot fever" narratives around tour cancellations.
- Ticketmaster delivered 14% AOI growth on 90M fee-bearing tickets sold (+8% YoY); international markets (Latin America, APAC, Europe) drove 70% of that AOI growth.
- Live Nation completed three arena acquisitions in 2026 (Impact Arena in Bangkok, Forum in Milan, Movistar Arena in Buenos Aires) and expects four to five more by 2027, targeting capacity for 15M additional fans between 2026 and 2027.
- Rapino defended the Spotify Reserved Feature presale-ticket-allocation deal amid pushback from agents and managers over "who owns the fan relationship," framing it as a paid-inventory arrangement comparable to past Verizon and Citi presale deals.

### Highlights
- Three named 2026 arena acquisitions (Impact Arena Bangkok, Forum Milan, Movistar Arena Buenos Aires), with 4–5 more expected by 2027 and a stated target of +15M fan capacity by 2027.
- Ticketmaster: 90M fee-bearing tickets sold (+8% YoY), 14% AOI growth, with international markets contributing 70% of that AOI growth.
- Spotify "Reserved Feature" presale deal explicitly likened to prior Verizon/Citi inventory-purchase presale arrangements; Live Nation frames it as a paid-inventory deal, not a transfer of fan-relationship ownership.
- Cancellation rate of 1.1% vs. a 1.6% historical average, cited as evidence against "blue dot fever" concerns.

### Industry Problem
Live Nation/Ticketmaster's continued arena roll-up and DSP-linked presale partnerships (e.g., Spotify Reserved) illustrate accelerating vertical and geographic consolidation by the dominant primary-ticketing incumbent, while the "who owns the fan relationship" pushback from agents and managers signals unresolved industry unease about incumbents controlling presale/fan data as they expand.

### Proposed Solution
The item describes Live Nation's own expansion and data-partnership strategy rather than a third-party fix; for an independent primary platform like Etix, this is chiefly a monitor/evolve signal — track the pace and geography of Live Nation's arena acquisitions and DSP-style presale-data deals as indicators of where competitive openings (and closings) are emerging, and evaluate whether an independent presale-allocation or fan-data-sharing model could differentiate Etix with promoters and agents wary of ceding fan-relationship control to a vertically integrated incumbent.

Executives also dismissed the notion of 'blue dot fever', saying consumers are spending at "record levels"

![Unipol Forum](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Unipol-Forum-©-Live-Nation.png)

Unipol Forum

Live Nation has revealed that international markets drove 10% growth in its global audiences in Q2, as the company reported revenue of $7.7 billion for the quarter, up 9% year-on-year.

These numbers brought company-wide operating income to $522 million, a 7% rise, though executives said the timing of major tours and ongoing investment in the company’s expanding venue and festival portfolio meant that the adjusted operating income (AOI) figure showed only 2% growth.

Speaking to analysts on the firm’s earnings call, executives dismissed notions of so-called ‘ [blue dot fever](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/05/live-nation-dont-get-the-blues-over-blue-dot-fever/) ‘, saying instead that cancelations were running at historical lows and that fans are buying at “record levels”.

“The whole ‘blue dot’: we have fewer cancellations this year than ever. We’re running below historical lows at 1.1% cancellations versus 1.6% average. So every now and then the media blows up about a certain tour cancelling— it’s the 1%, not the 99%,” CEO Michael Rapino told analysts.

He added Live Nation is “seeing consumers buy at record levels,” and that “it’ll be another record year after multiple record years”.

Within concerts, revenue grew 8%, as CFO Joe Berchtold said Live Nation is guiding towards “double digit fan growth for the full year”. Executives noted that international markets were the standout performer, driving 10% attendance growth and adding five million fans to a record total 49 million globally for the quarter.

The CFO also said Live Nation expects this growth to continue with “double‑digit fan count growth through Q3 and Q4 internationally”.

“ The reason for confidence is that our shows have been booked and confirmed, and deferred revenue is at a level now that gives us confidence—tickets are in the bank; it’s a matter of playing off the shows,” Berchtold said.

> “ We’ve talked about international—we’re truly delivering now”

Ticketmaster also delivered 14% AOI growth on the back of selling 90 million fee‑bearing tickets, an 8% increase on the prior year. International markets contributed 70% of Ticketmaster’s AOI growth, with executives attributing this to the strength of territories like Latin America, Asia‑Pacific, and Europe.

The firm’s venue business is also set for one of its largest growth periods to date, executives said. Live Nation has already completed three arena acquisitions in 2026 — Impact Arena (Bangkok), Forum (Milan) and Movistar Arena (Buenos Aires) — and expects “four to five” more by 2027. These put the company on track to add capacity for 15 million additional fans between 2026 and 2027, Berchtold said.

Rapino concluded his comments on international markets by telling analysts: “ We’ve talked about international—we’re truly delivering now. You can see it in the numbers across every segment in the first half. The runway is tremendous for many years.”

Finally, addressing Live Nation’s recent tie-up with Spotify on its Reserved Feature, which has been a source of [consternation among some agents and managers](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/07/who-owns-the-fan-relationship-agents-raise-questions-over-spotify-reserved/) in the business, Rapino said: “The goal has always been: as long as we own the transaction, we’ll look for discovery signals that help drive data sales. We’ve done this with Facebook, Snapchat, Groupon, Citi, Verizon.”

He added the arrangement is “a traditional deal where someone pays us for inventory”, comparing it to previous presale deals Live Nation has struck with Verizon, and saying the scale of the Reserved project is “no different” in terms of inventory.

“We looked at this like a Verizon or Citi deal. If Spotify was willing to compensate us properly for access to some presale tickets, we think it’s a great win‑win. It’s a very small allocation globally—they’re not solving on‑sale problems on an Ariana Grande tour. But with a [Role Model tour](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/06/role-model-up-first-for-spotifys-reserved-tour-ticket-initiative/), selling an allocation to their dedicated fans makes sense,” he said.
