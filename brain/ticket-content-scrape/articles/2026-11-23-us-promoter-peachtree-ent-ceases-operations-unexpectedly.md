# US promoter Peachtree Ent. ceases operations ‘unexpectedly’

## Summary

Nashville-based promoter Peachtree Entertainment, the company behind the Kid Rock-affiliated Rock The Country touring series and Rock The South festival, has abruptly ceased operations, leaving the future of its festival brands and a planned Kentucky venue uncertain. The shutdown follows a troubled 2026 season of cancellations and reports that some vendors remain unpaid, plus the prior week's departure of the company's president and festival founder.

### Highlights
- The City of Decatur, AL confirmed Peachtree "decided to cease operations and close its business" (Nov. 20).
- Some Rock The South vendors reportedly remain unpaid; the 2026 touring series was marred by cancellations tied to political controversy.
- President/founder Shane Quick exited the week prior; Peachtree itself has issued no public statement.
- Open questions remain over Furnace Fest (Birmingham) and the planned 2,200-cap "The Rail" venue in Lexington, KY, due to open early 2027.

### Industry Problem
Abrupt promoter insolvency leaves festival brands, booked artists, ticket holders and venue partners in limbo with no clear transition plan — a consolidation/exit risk relevant to how primary platforms manage promoter-side counterparty risk.

### Proposed Solution
No solution is offered in the source; posture should be Monitor — watch how ticket refunds/transfers for affected shows are handled and whether Peachtree's venue/festival assets get acquired, which would be the trigger to revisit competitive implications.

## US promoter Peachtree Ent. ceases operations ‘unexpectedly’

The Nashville-based company is behind the Rock The Country touring series, which was marred by controversy and cancellations this year

![Kid Rock](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Kid-Rock.png)

Kid Rock

The promoter behind the Rock The Country touring series, Nashville-based Peachtree Entertainment, has reportedly shut down abruptly.

Alabama’s City of Decatur issued a statement to local media *[WAFF](https://www.waff.com/2026/11/20/rock-souths-future-uncertain-after-peachtree-entertainment-decides-close/)* on Friday (20 November) that it had been informed Peachtree “has decided to cease operations and close its business”, described as a “significant and unexpected development”.

The city has a five-year agreement with the promoter for its flagship Rock The South festival brand, which was extended to become a touring series in collaboration with Kid Rock in 2024.

This year’s edition of the touring event was marred by artist cancellations, forcing organisers to [call off the South Carolina stop](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/02/festival-news-rock-the-country-and-more/), due in part to controversy around its right-wing partisan affiliation.

City officials say they are “assessing what this decision means” for the festival’s future, and say that “no city funds have been exchanged for the 2027 contract year”.

While Peachtree has not issued a public statement, the company’s senior director of marketing, Gillian Smith, wrote [online](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7530036703273721857/) that “this isn’t how I imagined it ending, but I wouldn’t trade a second of it” and that “the company may be gone, but the best parts of it aren’t going anywhere”.

The news also follows quickly behind the exit of the company’s president of live events and founder of Rock The South, Shane Quick, who announced his departure last week, according to [*MusicRow*](https://musicrow.com/2026/11/shane-quick-exits-peachtree-entertainment-exclusive/).

> The team is also set to open a venue in Kentucky early next year

Rock The South’s first edition in Decatur was pushed back by four months to October due to heavy rain, with the four-day event led by Zach Top, Jason Aldean, Jessie Murph, and Riley Green. Some vendors are also reportedly still awaiting payment for this event, according to [*WAFF*](https://www.waff.com/2026/11/21/some-rock-south-workers-say-they-havent-been-paid-after-peachtree-entertainment-ceases-operations/).

The future of the team’s Furnace Fest in Birmingham is also in question, though organisers recently announced October 2027 dates for the next edition. Peachtree took over the brand this year and covered the event’s financial losses to do so.

Outside of the festival domain, the promoter is also behind a handful of tours and was recently awarded the contract for the 2,200-capacity [The Rail](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/10/venue-news-unmistakably-nashville-venue-the-truth-opens/) venue in Lexington, Kentucky.

Due to open early next year, the venue is to anchor a 43-acre mixed-use entertainment district called The Commons, with Lynyrd Skynyrd, Cole Swindell, and Billy Currington among the first confirmed names for the space.

As it stands, the opening slate of concerts for The Rail and other shows are all still active on the company’s website, though questions remain over how they will proceed. Peachtree’s social profiles have all been taken offline.

*IQ has reached out to Peachtree for comment*.
