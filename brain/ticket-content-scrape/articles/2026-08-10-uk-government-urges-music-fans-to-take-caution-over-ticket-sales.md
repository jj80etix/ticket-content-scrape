# UK government urges music fans to take caution over ticket sales

## Summary

The UK government and O2 launched a "Stop! Think Fraud" campaign warning festival-season concertgoers about last-minute ticket scams, after O2 research found £150m ($111m/€130m) lost to UK ticket fraud in the past year, with 14% of surveyed gig-goers reporting they'd bought a fake ticket.

### Key Points
- 77% of the fake tickets bought were purchased via online platforms.
- The government has committed £250m to a broader fraud crackdown and stood up a new online crime centre pairing police and industry.
- O2 sponsors 21 UK venues, sold 1.7 million-plus Priority Tickets last year, and has partnered with FanFair Alliance since 2017 on fair/transparent resale.
- The campaign urges fans to buy only from official/authorised sellers and to use capped fan-to-fan resale platforms when shows are sold out.

### Highlights
- £150m ($111m/€130m) lost to UK ticket fraud in the past year, per O2 research.
- 14% of surveyed gig-goers reported buying a fake ticket; 77% of fakes were bought via online platforms.
- The UK government's broader fraud strategy includes a £250m investment and a new online crime centre.

### Industry Problem
Ticket fraud remains a large, apparently growing patron pain point in the UK market: scammers exploit high-demand, last-minute buying moments, and official/authorised channels aren't yet the fans' default reflex even with capped resale alternatives available.

### Proposed Solution
The campaign's own remedy is consumer education (buy only from official/authorised sellers, use capped resale). For a primary platform like Etix, the read-through is to keep authenticated-ticket messaging and verified resale/transfer options highly visible at the point of purchase, and to treat this kind of fraud-education content as AI-context grounding for client- and patron-facing support.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**As the festival season continues, the UK government has warned fans to take caution over last-minute ticket sales.**

The government has partnered with telecommunications company O2 to urge the public to “Stop! Think Fraud” and to always use official ticketing websites.

It comes after research by O2 revealed the amount lost to ticket fraud in the last year reached £150m ($111m/€130m).

The data shows that 14% of gig-goers surveyed have bought a fake ticket with 77% of the tickets bought via online platforms.

“Fraud is an absolutely shameful crime, and this data shows that anyone can be a victim,” said Fraud Minister Lord David Hanson.

“Scammers exploit our excitement and desperation to see our favourite artists live. They post fake messages on social media seeking to catch fans when their guard is down in a last-minute rush for tickets.

“I urge everyone to Stop! Think Fraud before buying tickets – let’s not let scammers get in the way of enjoying concerts this summer.”

The government delivered £250m of investment to crackdown on fraud and protect the public earlier this year, as part of a new fraud strategy.

It established a new online crime centre to bring together police and industry to disrupt scams at source and boost support for victims.

As part of the initiative, the Stop! Think Fraud campaign aims to make it easier for the public to recognise fraud and take steps to protect themselves, their family and friends.

O2, which sponsors 21 venues across the UK and sold more than 1.7 million tickets via Priority Tickets last year, has campaigned alongside FanFair Alliance since 2017 for a fairer and more transparent ticketing market.

“Ticketing scams are a blight on the UK’s live music industry, and it’s heartbreaking to see so many fans being targeted,” said director of partnerships and sponsorship at Virgin Media O2, Gareth Griffiths.

“Seeing your favourite artist live should be an exciting experience, not one that leaves you out of pocket or turned away at the door.

“That’s why O2 is encouraging fans to stay vigilant, buy tickets only from official authorised services and use capped fan-to-fan resale platforms when a show is sold out.”
