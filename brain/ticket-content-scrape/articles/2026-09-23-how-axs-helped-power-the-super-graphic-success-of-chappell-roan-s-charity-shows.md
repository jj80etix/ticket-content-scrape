# How AXS helped power the Super Graphic success of Chappell Roan’s charity shows

## Summary

AXS' Fair AXS ballot system powered a sold-out registration-based onsale for Chappell Roan's two-night Midwest Princess Project charity shows at The Shrine, with only about 5% of tickets expected to reach the secondary market versus roughly 40% for some major pop tours on unrestricted onsales. AXS' Dean DeWulf says the platform blends fairness (equal-chance registration, measured invitation waves) with bot/scalper detection and face-value-only official resale for fans who can no longer attend.

### Key Points
- Fans register with verified details; duplicates and suspicious activity are removed before equal-chance sale-link invitations go out in waves.
- AXS applies behavioral and technical signals to separate real buyers from bots/scalpers, configured per artist/event.
- Prior Fair AXS deployment for Roan's *Visions* shows saw ~5% secondary-market leakage and ~97% average attendance.
- Official resale for the new shows is capped at face value through AXS's own resale channel.
- Fair AXS has also been used for Radiohead and Céline Dion; the model is spreading to other high-demand/charity onsales.

### Highlights
- AXS reports ~5% of tickets reaching the secondary market on its Fair AXS ballot system, versus an estimated ~40% for some unrestricted major-pop-tour onsales — a concrete before/after benchmark for registration-based anti-scalping.
- Official resale is being capped at face value through the platform's own channel rather than left open to third-party marketplaces.

### Industry Problem
High-demand onsales routinely produce chaotic queues and heavy bot/scalper capture, leaving genuine fans facing near-impossible odds and driving prices up on secondary markets.

### Proposed Solution
AXS' answer is a registration-and-ballot on-sale (equal-chance selection, waved invitations, bot/behavioral screening) paired with a face-value-only official resale channel that keeps the artist in control of who ultimately holds tickets. This is a concrete build/improve benchmark for Etix's own high-demand onsale and resale tooling — particularly the combination of ballot fairness plus a capped-price official resale option rather than leaving resale to open marketplaces.

## How AXS helped power the Super Graphic success of Chappell Roan’s charity shows

"Fans will accept a few extra steps when the outcome feels more fair," AXS' Dean DeWulf tells IQ of the ballot-led process for the fundraiser

As the industry increasingly looks to [ballot systems](https://www.iqmagazine.com/magazine/ballots-the-answer-to-high-demand-on-sales/) for high-demand on-sales, Chappell Roan’s fan-first ticketing win with Fair AXS demonstrates another sell-out success story for the ticketing trend, taking the pressure off fans while giving the artist control over the registration data.

Next month, a legion of glitter-adorned fans will descend on The Shrine in Los Angeles for a two-night fundraising event to mark the first anniversary and support her [Midwest Princess Project](https://www.iamchappellroan.com/midwestprincessproject/) non-profit initiative.

In addition to live entertainment, volunteer opportunities, and community engagement, Roan herself will also perform in the 6,300-seat theatre, with net proceeds to support the Project’s work uplifting trans youth and LGBTQ+ communities.

It will be an intimate opportunity to see the headliner, who most recently performed at Olivia Rodrigo’s Daisy Chain Fields but has largely rested from the touring circuit after headlining festivals in South America, Australia, and New Zealand at the beginning of the year.

“Demand for Chappell was always going to outweigh supply, particularly for two intimate charity shows,” Dean DeWulf, AXS’ head of North American venues, tells *IQ*. “The Super Graphic Spectacular is about community, so the ticketing needed to reflect that.”

Building on a previous alliance for Roan’s *Visions of Damsels & Other Dangerous Things* shows last year, AXS was tapped to help alleviate the pressure around the in-demand star while pooling data for the artist to expand on in the future.

For that set of shows, around 5% of tickets reached the secondary market, with average attendance nearly 97%. In comparison, DeWulf estimates roughly 40% of tickets for some major pop tours reach the secondary space, all while keeping the experience “calm and straightforward,” he says of audience feedback.

“Fair AXS has the power to turn one chaotic onsale into a more managed process designed to reduce the type of anxiety that can accompany this type of high-demand event,” he explains. “We can send invitations in measured waves, monitor conversion and avoid inviting thousands of fans to an empty purchase page.

> “Fans will accept a few extra steps when the outcome feels more fair”

“It also provides a qualified picture of genuine demand. Where fans opt in, Chappell’s team can add them to her own list and build a direct, consent-based relationship with that audience,” he adds.

Fans could register with details like their name, email, billing address, and valid payment details, with anyone who entered given equal opportunity to be selected. Successful applicants received a private, one-time-use ticket link connected to their account, with purchase windows running a week later. Once the registration window closes, duplicates and suspicious activity are removed, with verified fans given an “equal chance” to receive a sale link.

“We also look at behavioural and technical signals that help distinguish real customers from bot or scalper activity,” DeWulf says. “The fundamentals remain consistent, but each programme is configured around the artist, the event, and the expected demand.”

Despite the demand, DeWulf says the goal with the on-sale was not how fast they could sell out, but instead how they could give “invited fans a meaningful opportunity to buy”. For those who secured a ticket but could no longer attend, this event’s resale rules allowed face-value resale through AXS’ official resale channels.

While the event is about a month away, set for 20-21 October, its sell-out success and previous takeaways from *Visions* have informed how AXS and Roan approach whatever comes next.

“Fans will accept a few extra steps when the outcome feels more fair. The Super Graphic Spectacular allowed us to refine the model further and gives Chappell’s team a proven framework for future high-demand shows,” he says.

Outside of Roan’s shows, Fair AXS has also been deployed for high-profile tours and residencies including Radiohead and Céline Dion. The Midwest Princess Project launched in October 2025, raising nearly $600,000 in its [first three months](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/68c30adbd28b4c45612bd6ad/t/69c55e4e313a8f4bfdab58bb/1774542414695/TMPP_2025_Impact_Report.pdf), and has since supported dozens of organisations.
