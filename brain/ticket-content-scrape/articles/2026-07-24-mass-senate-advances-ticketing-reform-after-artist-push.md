# Mass. Senate Advances Ticketing Reform After Artist Push

## Summary

The Massachusetts Senate advanced Amendment 20 (the "Great Divide Act") to the state's economic development bill, delivering sweeping ticketing reform after a public push by Sen. Dylan Fernandes, Gov. Maura Healey, and a coalition of artists with New England ties. As passed by the Senate, the measure caps resale prices at 110% of face value, bans speculative ticketing, and prohibits various deceptive marketing practices, but a proposed 10% fee cap from the original bill was stripped out before passage. The bill now heads to a conference committee to reconcile it with the House's version of the economic development bill, which contained no ticketing provisions.

### Key Points
- Amendment 20 / "The Great Divide Act" passed the Massachusetts Senate late Thursday.
- Provisions: 110% resale price cap, speculative-ticketing ban, bans on deceptive marketing practices.
- The original bill's 10% fee cap was dropped from the Senate's final version.
- Bill now goes to a conference committee to reconcile with the House bill, which lacks ticketing reform.
- Momentum followed Vermont's passage of a similar law and a lobbying push by artist-organizer Randy Nichols (National Independent Talent Organization).
- Artist coalition backing the bill included Noah Kahan, Dropkick Murphys, Pixies, Goose, MGMT, and others with Massachusetts/New England ties.
- Gov. Healey personally engaged after attending Noah Kahan's Fenway Park shows.

### Highlights
- Resale price cap proposed: 110% of face value.
- Fee cap: originally proposed at 10%, removed from the Senate-passed version.
- Bill status: passed Senate, now in conference committee against a House bill with no ticketing reform.
- Precedent cited: Vermont's recently passed ticketing reform law.

### Industry Problem
This evidences the growing state-level regulatory patchwork on resale price caps, speculative ticketing, and deceptive marketing — exactly the fragmented, state-by-state compliance burden a primary ticketing platform must track and adapt to market by market.

### Proposed Solution
The bill itself is a legislative remedy, not a vendor solution; Etix should monitor whether the fee-cap language resurfaces in conference committee (a direct operational trigger, since it would affect fee display/collection in MA) and treat the speculative-ticketing ban and resale cap as a template likely to spread to other states following Vermont and Massachusetts.

The Massachusetts Senate advanced an amendment that would bring sweeping ticketing reform to the Bay State late Thursday.

Originally introduced by Sen. Dylan Fernandes and then [given a boost by Gov. Maura Healey](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/07/17/massachusetts-gov-to-tackle-ticketing-reform-with-the-great-divide-act/), the reforms — known variously as Amendment 20 to the economic development bill or “The Great Divide Act” — would cap resale prices at 110% of face value, ban speculative ticketing and prohibit various deceptive marketing practices.

As originally introduced, the reforms include a fee cap of 10%, but that provision was not included in the Senate’s final version.

The entire economic development bill now heads to a conference committee to reconcile differences in the Senate bill with the version previously passed by the House (which did not include ticketing reform).

Fernandes has been working on the bill for months and it looked to be in trouble as the General Court’s session wound down, but Healey’s late push came at an opportune time.

National Independent Talent Organization board member Randy Nichols tells *Pollstar* that Fernandes began his push after Vermont passed a similar law — with a little help from Noah Kahan. Healey, it turns out, is a fan of Kahan and attended one of his four Fenway Park concerts.

“She learned about the situation and the bill and there wasn’t a huge plan to bumrush her but everyone talked to her about it and that helped. It spun from being at the shows to a press conference,” he says.

Nichols then helped build a coalition of artists with Massachusetts and broader New England ties to sign a letter advocating for the reforms. Those artists included Kahan, Dropkick Murphys, Pixies, Goose, Aimee Mann, Guster, All That Remains, Belly, Dicky Barrett of Mighty Mighty Bosstones and The Defiant, Dispatch, Killswitch Engage, Lori McKenna, Mission of Burma, MGMT, Shadows Fall, and others.

“When you get to Massachusetts, those guys and women have pride they are from there, so we said let’s tap into that local, we-can-take-care-of-our-own mentality,” Nichols says. “We hit everyone up and i think 100% of the artists who replied said they would be part of the coalition.”
