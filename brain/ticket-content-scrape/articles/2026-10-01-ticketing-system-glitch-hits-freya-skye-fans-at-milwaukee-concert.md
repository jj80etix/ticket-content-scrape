# Ticketing-System Glitch Hits Freya Skye Fans at Milwaukee Concert

## Summary

Hundreds of fans were turned away or delayed entering a sold-out Freya Skye concert in Milwaukee after ticket barcodes failed at the door, affecting buyers who purchased through both Ticketmaster and resale marketplaces (StubHub, Vivid Seats) alike. The show used Ticketmaster's Face Value Exchange, a walled-garden resale-restriction program that withholds scannable barcodes until ~48 hours before showtime; StubHub says the failure originated in the event's ticketing system, not its marketplace, and is making affected customers whole. No technical cause has been confirmed, but the fact that Ticketmaster-purchased tickets failed alongside resale tickets undercuts early reporting that blamed third-party resale.

### Key Points
- Scanners showed red-light failures for buyers via Ticketmaster, StubHub, and Vivid Seats alike at a Sept. 30 Milwaukee show.
- Event ran under Ticketmaster's Face Value Exchange, which locks resale to Ticketmaster-only and delays barcode release to ~48 hrs pre-show.
- StubHub disputes blame placed on resale, says it's making customers whole and seeking answers from the venue/ticketing provider.
- Echoes a 2019 Black Keys/SafeTix incident where walled-garden resale restrictions caused similar door failures.
- Neither Live Nation nor Ticketmaster had issued a technical explanation as of the report.

### Highlights
- Sept. 30 Milwaukee show, doors resolved ~8:45 p.m. after hours of entry failures.
- Face Value Exchange withholds barcodes until ~48 hours pre-show, centralizing control with the ticket issuer.
- Prior precedent: 2019 Black Keys/Wiltern SafeTix lockout under similar non-transferable restrictions.

### Industry Problem
Walled-garden resale-control programs concentrate technical control of barcode issuance with the primary platform, so when something breaks, legitimate buyers across every purchase channel can be locked out at the door with no independent recourse — and public blame tends to fall on resale by default regardless of actual cause.

### Proposed Solution
No fix is proposed in the piece itself. For Etix, this argues for entry-day monitoring/alerting on barcode-validation failure rates (not just sales uptime) and a documented incident-communication protocol so a primary-platform-side failure isn't reflexively blamed on secondary markets — worth building as a readiness checklist for high-demand onsales.

Hundreds of Freya Skye fans encountered ticket-scanning problems outside her sold-out Milwaukee concert Wednesday night, with local reporting indicating that the trouble affected customers who bought through both the Ticketmaster platform that the box office uses as well as independent resale marketplaces.

The problems erupted as fans arrived at Landmark Credit Union Live for the Sept. 30 stop on Skye’s Stars Align Tour. The station said it had not independently determined the cause of the individual ticket problems or whether the affected tickets were fraudulent.

Live Nation told CBS 58 that everyone who purchased through Ticketmaster was ultimately admitted to the concert.

## Show Used Ticketmaster Face Value Exchange

The episode is particularly notable because Skye’s Milwaukee show was operating under Ticketmaster’s Face Value Exchange program, which is designed to tightly control ticket resale in locations where such walled garden practices are not explicitly banned by law.

[FPC Live’s official event page told customers](https://www.fpc-live.com/event/freya-skye-stars-align-tour/) that Skye was using Face Value Exchange “to help fans get tickets at the original price” and that tickets could only be resold through Ticketmaster at face value.

Ticketmaster’s tour-specific help page [similarly said tickets could only be resold on Ticketmaster](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/44010756960657-Freya-Skye-Stars-Align-Tour). It also told buyers that although their orders would appear in their accounts, the event organizer would not make the scannable barcode available until closer to the event, estimated at approximately 48 hours before showtime.

Neither policy proves what caused Wednesday night’s problems. TicketNews has not confirmed that Ticketmaster changed transfer settings, cancelled tickets or altered barcodes shortly before the Milwaukee concert.

But the structure does place the ticket issuer and event organizer in control of when usable barcodes become available and how officially sanctioned resale occurs, making a definitive attribution of the disruption to third-party marketplaces premature without additional evidence about what actually happened inside the ticketing system.

No widespread issues have been reported for other dates on the tour thus far, with social media posts indicating fans had been able to use resale tickets successfully in other locations.

## Ticket Restrictions Have Caused Previous Entry Problems

The Milwaukee incident also recalls previous cases in which restrictions imposed through primary ticketing technology have left legitimate resale buyers unable to enter an event.

In 2019, [hundreds of fans were locked outside a Black Keys concert at the Wiltern in Los Angeles](https://www.ticketnews.com/2019/09/safetix-hundreds-locked-black-keys-wiltern/) after resale tickets failed under a Ticketmaster SafeTix setup. Ticketmaster said tickets for that performance had been designated non-transferable, while fans complained that the restriction had either not been adequately disclosed or had rendered tickets they purchased elsewhere unusable.

TicketNews has also previously examined [how mobile ticketing technology and transfer restrictions can create problems](https://www.ticketnews.com/2024/05/luke-combs-fans-slam-stubhub-after-invalid-ticket-issues/) that look at the venue entrance like invalid-ticket or resale failures even when the underlying issue involves a ticket being revoked, rendered non-transferable or otherwise made unusable after its original issuance.

None of those earlier incidents establishes that the same thing happened Wednesday in Milwaukee. At this point, neither Live Nation nor Ticketmaster has publicly provided a technical explanation accounting for the failed scans reported by fans who purchased through multiple channels.

That makes the attempt to reduce the episode to a problem caused by ticket resale particularly questionable before the underlying failure has been identified.

For now, what is established is that fans experienced widespread trouble getting through the doors, local reporting identified affected customers who purchased from Ticketmaster as well as resale marketplaces, and StubHub says the failure originated with barcodes in the event’s ticketing system rather than its marketplace.

StubHub said it is making its affected customers whole while seeking a fuller explanation from the venue and ticketing provider.
