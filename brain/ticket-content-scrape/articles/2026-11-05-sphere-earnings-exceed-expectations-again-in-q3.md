# Sphere Earnings Exceed Expectations Again In Q3

## Summary

Sphere Entertainment again beat Wall Street expectations in Q3, posting $274.3m in revenue (vs. $269.2m consensus) and a smaller-than-expected adjusted per-share loss of 98 cents. Shares rose nearly 8% on the report, with The Wizard of Oz experience continuing to drive Sphere segment revenue.

### Key Points
- Q3 revenue: $274.3m, beating Zacks consensus of $269.2m.
- Adjusted per-share loss of $0.98 vs. consensus estimate of a $1.79 loss.
- The Wizard of Oz has surpassed $500m in sales and 4 million tickets sold since its August 2025 debut.
- Shares rose ~8% intraday following the report.

Yet again, Sphere Entertainment beat Wall Street expectations with its third quarter earnings report, both in revenue and on the bottom line.

The operator of the eponymous Vegas venue and the MSG family of networks posted revenues of $274.3 million in Q3, ahead of the Zacks consensus of $269.2 million. Sphere was also a beat on the bottom line, posting a per-share loss, adjusted for asset impairment, of 98 cents; Zacks’ consensus was a loss of $1.79.

The report, released ahead of the opening bell Thursday, pushed shares up nearly 8% by midday.

“With today’s results, our Sphere segment remains on track to deliver substantial growth this calendar year. Looking ahead, we continue to make progress on our long-term vision for Sphere, including a global network of venues and a growing slate of original content,” Executive Chairman and CEO James Dolan said.

*The Wizard of Oz* continues to be a reliable revenue driver. *Oz* has now surpassed $500 million in sales since its August 2025 debut with more than 4 million tickets sold. Revenues for the Sphere Experience vertical were up $13.1 million year-over-year due to both higher performance count and greater per-show revenues.

Revenue at Sphere itself was $198.9 million, $24.8 million more than the same quarter last year, booned by the aforementioned rise in Experiences revenue as well as increased in “events-related revenue” with higher concert count as compared to last year.

The other half of Sphere Entertainment’s business — the MSG family of regional sports networks — saw revenues decrease by 15% as subscribers dropped 15.5%.
