# Residencies lift MSG Ent. Q3 revenue 37% to $217m

## Summary

Residencies from Harry Styles, Bon Jovi, and Phish helped lift Madison Square Garden Entertainment's fiscal Q3 revenue 37% to $217.1m, with the Garden logging its highest concert count ever in the quarter. Sphere Entertainment also reported Q3 results the same day, with Sphere segment revenue up 14% to $198.9m, boosted by the long-running Wizard of Oz experience.

### Key Points
- MSGE Q3 revenue: $217.1m, +37% YoY; concert revenue up $30.1m on volume and higher per-concert take.
- Record number of concerts in a single quarter at Madison Square Garden.
- Food/beverage/merchandise revenue up 56% YoY to $35.7m.
- Net income $8.9m vs. a prior-year net loss of $21.7m.
- Sphere Entertainment Q3 revenue up 4% YoY to $274.3m; Sphere segment revenue up 14% to $198.9m.

## Transcript

Harry Styles, Bon Jovi, and Phish all delivered multi-night runs in the period, which also saw the arena capture a new record for concerts

![Harry Styles at MSG](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Harry-Styles-at-MSG-©-Anthony-Pham.png)

Harry Styles at MSG

Residencies from Harry Styles, Bon Jovi, and Phish helped lift Madison Square Garden Entertainment (MSGE)’s Q3 revenue by 37% to $217.1 million (€193m), according to the latest fiscal report.

The company says the period, which ended 30 September, saw the highest number of concerts ever for the famed Madison Square Garden arena. This included just over half of Harry Styles’ 30-night *Together, Together run*, a nine-night stand from Bon Jovi, and another five shows from Phish.

Quarterly revenue from concerts increased by $30.1m (€26.7m), attributed to the volume increase and higher per-concert venue, but offset by fewer concerts within the theatre portfolio.

Food, beverage, and merchandise revenues rose 56% YoY to $35.7m (€31.8m), primarily due to a $12.5m (€11.1m) increase in food and beverage sales at concerts.

Adjusted operating income rose to $34.3m (€30.5m) from $7.1m (€6.3m) a year earlier, attributed to the revenue increase and offset by higher direct operating and administrative expenses.

Net income was listed as $8.9m (€7.9m), including a $6.6m (€5.9m) income tax benefit, from a net loss of $21.7m (€19.3m) a year prior.

“Our business is off to a strong start in fiscal 2027,” says James Dolan, MSGE executive chairman/CEO. “We expect our live entertainment offerings to continue benefiting from healthy demand, and we remain well positioned to drive robust growth in both revenue and adjusted operating income this fiscal year.”

> “We continue to make progress on our long-term vision for Sphere”

Sphere Entertainment Co. also released its Q3 fiscal report today, with quarterly revenue up 4% YOY to $274.3m (€244.3m).

Operating loss is listed at $240.6m (€214.3m), an increase of 47% YOY, while adjusted operating income is $39.2m (+8%) compared to the year prior.

For the Sphere segment, quarterly revenue is up 14% to $198.9m (€177.1m), with $13.1m (€11.7m) of the $24.8m (€22.1m) lift coming from Sphere Experience programming, like that of the Wizard of Oz experience.

Event-related revenues increased by $6.1m (€5.4m), compared to the previous year’s quarter, attributed to three additional concerts held at the venue.

Also announced today is a forthcoming five-night residency from Rush, who will present the *Elevate from the Norm* stand on 21, 23, 26, 28 & 30 May.

Production is also underway on *The Rocky Horror Picture Show* Sphere Experience, expected to roll out at the end of March 2027.

“With today’s results, our Sphere segment remains on track to deliver substantial growth this calendar year,” Dolan says. “Looking ahead, we continue to make progress on our long-term vision for Sphere, including a global network of venues and a growing slate of original content.”

Last week saw the 6,000-capacity Maryland version [clear the final funding hurdle](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/10/second-us-sphere-venue-greenlit-with-215m-funding-approval/) with the approval of $215m (€191.5m) in bonds.
