# Industry pays tribute to ‘heartbeat’ of Australian music Molly Meldrum

The live-music industry is paying tribute to Molly Meldrum, the Australian broadcaster and music commentator described as the "heartbeat" of the country's music scene. Figures from across the Australian industry shared recollections of his decades-long influence on music media and artist careers.

## Industry pays tribute to ‘heartbeat’ of Australian music Molly Meldrum

The broadcaster and champion of domestic talent passed away yesterday at age 83, and will be honoured with a state funeral

![Ian ](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Ian-Molly-Meldrum-Michael-Gudinski-©-Mushroom-Group.png)

Ian "Molly" Meldrum & Michael Gudinski © Mushroom Group

One of the most “profound” leaders in Australia’s music industry, Ian “Molly” Meldrum AM, passed away yesterday (23 November) at age 83.

His family confirmed the passing of the promoter, record producer, and broadcaster, describing him as the “heartbeat, the champion, and the spirit of Australian music”. He will be honoured with a state funeral for his contributions to the industry, and will be remembered by his long-term partner, Yan Wongngam, and his family.

Meldrum is best known as the host of Australian television programme *Countdown* for over a decade, where he played a leading role in championing Australian talent and international acts within the country. One of the most recognisable figures within the entertainment market, he also had stints as a record producer, music journalist, and industry personality.

Mushroom Group’s chief executive Matt Gudinski said: “Molly had a profound impact in helping shape the Australian music industry into what it is today. At a time when our local industry was still in its infancy and homegrown artists were just finding a path, Molly was one of their greatest champions and instrumental in changing that.”

Gudinski adds that Meldrum will be “forever a part of the Gudinski family” having had a close relationship with his late father, Michael Gudinski, mother Sue, and sister Kate.

“Molly’s impact on Australian music is immeasurable, but for me he’ll always be more than just an industry icon,” Gudinski said.

> “We will always be grateful for his belief in us and the doors he helped open”

One of the artists highlighted on *Countdown*, Kylie Minogue, has also paid tribute to Meldrum, saying: “He championed so many Aussie artists and welcomed international artists to Australia in his own wonderful and inimitable way,” she wrote. “I’m so grateful for his unwavering love and support. Vale Molly. I’m going to turn the music up in your honour.”

AC/DC also paid homage to the figure, saying he has been “championing AC/DC since 1974,” in line with his 1974-87 run as *Countdown* host.

Blondie also offered condolences, saying he was “instrumental in Blondie’s success in Australia, championing our music and introducing us to a whole new audience.

“We will always be grateful for his belief in us and the doors he helped open. He had a true passion for music, a wonderful sense of humour, and was truly one of a kind. He will be greatly missed,” they said.

And Australian rocker Jimmy Barnes also said Meldrum “worked tirelessly to promote Australian music here and overseas and along the way, we shared a lot of laughs”.

“Molly and I went through a lot,” he continued. “At the start, he was the enemy. Running the best music TV show in the country but insisting that we had to mime. We fought, but eventually we agreed to go on the show. The impact was immediate. Our shows went from pubs to bigger pubs to big halls in a matter of months. Molly exposed us to a huge music audience we could never have reached, and we were always thankful to him for that.”

The Australian Recording Industry Association (ARIA) also put out a statement honouring Meldrum, saying he devoted six “exceptional” decades to promoting the country’s talent.

> “He became a central part of the Australian soundtrack”

Through his life, he was recognised with numerous honours, including appointment as a Member of the Order of Australia in 1986, induction into the Logies Hall of Fame in 2012, and his ARIA Hall of Fame recognition in 2014 – when he became the first non-recording artist to be inducted.

ARIA chief executive Annabelle Herd said, “he shaped the music taste of a generation of Australians and gave so much of himself for the good of Australian artists, for which our whole industry is eternally grateful”.

Australia’s Prime Minister Anthony Albanese also spoke in honour of Meldrum, saying: “On Countdown and beyond, he championed Australian artists and brought international acts to our shores. He gave new artists a leg up and established artists a welcoming embrace. And over the years, as he implored us to do ourselves a favour, he became a central part of the Australian soundtrack.

“We were lucky to have him. May Ian ‘Molly’ Meldrum rest in peace,” he concluded.
