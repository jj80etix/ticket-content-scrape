# Cheap Trick To Take Time Off, Cancels 2027 Tour Dates

## Summary

Cheap Trick canceled its 2027 tour dates to "take a much-needed break" for an undetermined period, per venue notices citing word from Creative Artists Agency, including a scheduled Thunder By The Bay festival slot in Sarasota and a Harrah's Cherokee date in North Carolina. The band's remaining fall 2026 "All Washed Up" tour dates, running through Nov. 22 in Detroit, remain on the schedule.

### Key Points
- 2027 tour dates canceled; band taking an "undetermined" break per CAA notice to venues.
- Affected events include the 29th Annual Thunder By The Bay Music & Motors Festival (Sarasota) and a Harrah's Cherokee Casino Resort show.
- Fall 2026 "All Washed Up" tour continues as scheduled through Nov. 22 in Detroit.

## Transcript

Cheap Trick has canceled their 2027 tour dates to “take a much-needed break,” according to venues who scheduled to host the rock band.

The band’s remaining shows for its “All Washed Up” tour this fall remain listed on its website, but the 2027 dates have been removed.

Social media pages for venues and events affected by the cancellations informed fans of the news. Suncoast Charities for Children, a Florida nonprofit that organizes its annual Thunder By The Bay Music Motors Festival in Sarasota, said it was notified by Creative Artists Agency that, “following many years of touring, Cheap Trick has made the decision to take a much-needed break from performing for an undetermined period.”

“As a result, the band will unfortunately no longer be performing at the 29 <sup>th</sup> Annual Thunder By The Bay Music & Motors Festival in February 2027,” the nonprofit added. “While we are disappointed by this unexpected news, we respect the band’s decision and wish them all the best.”

Harrah’s Cherokee Casino Resort posted a similar message, informing its followers that the band’s show at the North Carolina venue was canceled because the band “decided to take an unspecified amount of time off.”

Cheap Trick’s resumes performing Nov. 5 at Fort Wayne’s Clyde Theatre, followed by shows at The Sylvee in Madison and Kansas City’s Uptown Theater. Other stops include The Factory in St. Louis, Davenport’s Capitol Theatre, The Astro in La Vista, Milwaukee’s Landmark Credit Union Live, Cincinnati’s Taft Theatre and Northfield Park Racino in Ohio before wrapping up Nov. 22 at Detroit’s Sound Board Theater.
