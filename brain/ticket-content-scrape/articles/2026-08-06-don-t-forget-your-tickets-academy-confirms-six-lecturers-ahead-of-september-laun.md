# Don’t Forget Your Tickets Academy confirms six lecturers ahead of September launch

## Summary

TicketCo has confirmed six of ten lecturers for its free "Don't Forget Your Tickets Academy," a 10-week online training programme for the ticketing industry launching September 10, 2026. Built on TicketCo's existing podcast, the Academy aims to formalize professional education around ticketing as a commercial (not just operational) discipline, with weekly live 45-minute sessions covering topics like fan data/segmentation, event revenue strategy, sponsorship integration, crowd density management, and stadium economics. Confirmed lecturers include veterans from the London 2012 Olympics, Roskilde Festival Group, Octagon Worldwide, and WHU business school.

### Key Points
- Academy launches September 10, 2026, running weekly Thursday sessions at 13:00 CET for 10 weeks; free and open to all, on-demand replay available.
- Six of ten lecturers confirmed, spanning data/fan journeys, major-event revenue strategy, sponsorship, crowd safety, and the economics of empty seats.
- Notable lecturers: Paul Williamson (ex-director of ticketing, London 2012 Olympics; Two Circles), Morten Therkildsen (Roskilde Festival Group crowd safety lead for UEFA finals and Eurovision), Professor Dominik Schreyer (WHU).
- TicketCo CEO Carl-Erik Michalsen Moberg frames the initiative as closing a real, significant industry knowledge gap: professionals responsible for revenue, fan relationships, data, and crowd management have "had nowhere to learn the craft."
- Registration is live; remaining four lecturer slots and some session dates still to be confirmed.

### Highlights
- TicketCo (Academy operator) explicitly names revenue, fan relationships, data, and crowd management as board-level industry functions with no formal training pathway to date.
- Free, standalone 10-week programme launching September 10, 2026 — a concrete new "product" response to that gap, independent of TicketCo's core ticketing platform.
- Lecturer roster draws credibility from large-scale ticketing/operations experience (e.g., 11 million Olympic tickets sold, 135,000 festival-goers managed in a single day).

### Industry Problem
The article evidences a client-side (venue/promoter/organizer staff) pain point: there is no established formal education path for the commercial and operational skills — data, revenue strategy, crowd management — that ticketing and event professionals need, despite these being board-level concerns.

### Proposed Solution
TicketCo's response is a free, structured lecture series taught by recognized industry practitioners, positioning ticketing as a commercial competency rather than pure back-office operations. For Etix, this points to a potential content/education play (webinars, certification content, or an "Etix Assistant" knowledge base) as a client-relationship and thought-leadership lever, distinct from core product features.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**TicketCo’s Don’t Forget Your Tickets Academy has confirmed six of 10 lecturers who will teach on the free online education programme, designed to offer the ticketing industry formal training.**

The final four lecturers will be confirmed over the coming weeks, ahead of the first lecture on September 10.

Building on the ‘Don’t Forget Your Tickets’ podcast, the Academy aims to raise the level of knowledge across the industry, and to make the case that ticketing is a commercial function, not just an operational one. It is a standalone initiative and is free to all.

“Revenue, fan relationships, data, and crowd management are board-level issues at every serious club,” said Carl-Erik Michalsen Moberg, chief executive of TicketCo. “The people responsible for delivering them have had nowhere to learn the craft. That gap is real, it is significant, and closing it is exactly what the Academy is built to do.”

Powered by TicketCo and hosted by Moberg, the Academy will run for 10 weeks with one live lecture broadcast every Thursday at 13:00 CET from September 10. Each session will last 45 minutes, and will include a presentation from the week’s lecturer, a question and answer segment and a closing discussion. Every lecture will be available on demand afterwards, and registration for the education programme is now live.

**Six lecturers confirmed**

David Tavares Pansbo will open the Academy with a model on data, segmentation and fan journeys. He is a partner and senior advisor at Playmakers, helping sports organisations turn fan data into stronger relationships and commercial growth.

The following week will be led by Paul Williamson, director at Two Circles and non-executive director of the Tour de France Grand Départ 2027. Williamson was also the director of ticketing for the London 2012 Olympics, and held the same role with England Rugby in 2015. He will lead the module on revenue strategy in major sporting events.

Phil Carling, managing director of football consulting at Octagon Worldwide will teach the session on how ticketing fits inside the wider commercial and sponsorship model on September 24. The fourth module will be led by Morten Therkildsen, director of safety and guest relations at the Roskilde Festival Group. Through the festival’s commercial arm RFX, he has managed crowd safety at the UEFA Champions League and Europa League finals, the Eurovision Song Contest, World Pride, and the Formula 1 at Silverstone. Therkildsen will lead the module on crowd density management on October 1.

Daniel Cook is the head of ticketing at the Welsh Rugby Union and Principality Stadium and will lead a module, though his date is not yet confirmed. Rounding off the confirmed lecturers is Professor Dominik Schreyer of WHU (Otto Beisheim School of Management), who will lead the module on the economics of empty seats. The date will be confirmed in due course.

“Each of these six lecturers has done something in this industry almost nobody else has,” said Moberg. “Selling 11 million Olympic tickets. Keeping 135,000 festival-goers safe in a single day. Building a football revenue strategy from nothing to a world record. This profession has always had this level of expertise. The Academy is the first way to teach it.”
