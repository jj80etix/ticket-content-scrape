# Investigations Begin Over Red Hot Summer Gold Coast Grandstand Collapse

## Summary

Queensland Police and Workplace Health and Safety Queensland are investigating the collapse of a multi-tiered grandstand during the Red Hot Summer festival's Gold Coast stop on Nov 1, which injured 22 attendees (4 hospitalized) among roughly 700-1,000 people on the structure. The show resumed after an hour-long pause, and promoter Face to Face Touring says the grandstand had been engineer-approved the day before.

### Key Points
- Incident occurred during Boom Crash Opera's set at Coolangatta Beach; singer Dale Ryder halted the show to call for security and first aid.
- Full refunds promised to grandstand ticket holders; promoter says it will investigate with the engineering firm that signed off on the structure.
- Gold Coast mayor Tom Tate is demanding a safety review and has floated a possible ban on beach concerts, which could affect other major local events (Out2Lunch, Cooly Rocks On, SummerSalt).
- The Red Hot Summer Tour was due to continue to Victoria, Western Australia and Adelaide stops.

## Transcript

![GettyImages 2167956258](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/GettyImages-2167956258.jpg)

Brisbane, Queensland, Australia: façade of the Queensland Police Service headquarters, Queensland’s main law enforcement agency – Roma Street Police Station.

Queensland Police, and Workplace Health and Safety Queensland, are investigating the collapse of a grandstand at the Gold Coast stop of the Red Hot Summer festival tour.

Between 700 to 1,000 of the 10,000 who attended were on the multi-tiered structure at the Sunday Nov. 1 show at the iconic Coolangatta Beach.

Twenty-two people needed medical treatment and four were hospitalized. A woman’s ankle became trapped beneath the crumpled structure.

The mishap occurred at 2:50 pm during a set by Boom Crash Opera, second on the bill.

Singer Dale Ryder immediately stopped the show, calling out, “The grandstand has collapsed — we need security, we need first aid, we need assistance.”

Alicia Glohe, one of two off-duty nurses in the sold-out crowd who helped the injured, told the *Courier Mail* paper, “As we were listening to the stage, you could hear a commotion behind us.

“We didn’t realize what it actually was until you heard the crowd. I turned around and could see the grandstand look like a wave.”

After an hour, the show continued, with gospel sisters Vika & Linda, and rock bands Eskimo Joe, Birds of Tokyo, Men At Work and Australian Crawl who were advertised as its original lineup reuniting after 40 years.

Event promoter Face to Face Touring revealed, “Yesterday that grandstand was signed off by an engineer and we will be having a meeting with that particular company to thoroughly investigate why that just happened.”

Full refunds were promised to those in the grandstand.

Gold Coast mayor Tom Tate, himself a civil engineer, is also demanding answers.

He warned that depending on a review on safety procedures employed at the show, Red Hot Summer might not be allowed back on the beach.

A ban on all concerts on Coolangatta Beach could also be on the cards.

These could include TEG Live’s EDM festival Out2Lunch which draws 30,000 and injects A$54 million ($37.6 million) into the local economy,

Experience Gold Coast’s 1950s culture themed Cooly Rocks On gets 120,000 to 160,000 over a five-day run and generates A$8 million ($5.58 million) for the local economy.

Face To Face’s touring festival SummerSalt brought an estimated 8,000 to the sands.

Tate said, “I’d like to get the investigation completed and I will ask (for it) to be on my desk because I’ll understand the engineering part of it and then I’ll make a decision with the CEO,” he said.

The Red Hot Summer Tour was expected to resume its schedule to Victoria Park in Ballarat in regional Victoria (Nov. 7), Mornington Racecourse on the Victorian surf coast (Nov. 8), Sandalford Wines, Swan Valley in Western Australia (Nov. 14) and Glenelg Beach in Adelaide (Nov. 15).

It began its run Oct. 17 at Queens Park in Toowoomba, Queensland to 10,000 and played four other sites before the Gold Coast.
