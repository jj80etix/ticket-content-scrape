# Lagos arena reveals June 2028 target date as rival project emerges

## Summary

A 12,000-capacity Lagos arena under construction by MetroWave Sports/Elalan Construction has set a new June 2028 completion date (after missing its original December 2025 target), as a rival 20,000-capacity "Dream Theatre" concept from Landmark Africa also emerged, underscoring a broader wave of arena development across Nigeria and Africa.

### Key Points
- The original project, backed by a consortium including Live Nation, Oak View Group, Persianas Group, the Nigerian Sovereign Investment Authority and others, was first announced in 2024 and would be Nigeria's first IBF-standard indoor arena.
- Estimated project cost is €78m-€104m; the completion-date update came via broker Reverie Brokerage after a developer site visit, not from the developers directly.
- Estate Intel estimates more than 30,000 seats are in Nigeria's emerging arena pipeline targeted for completion by 2030.
- Lagos's renovated National Theatre (now the Wole Soyinka Centre) reopened in October 2025 and has since hosted major shows including a sold-out Adekunle Gold concert.

## Transcript

As an under-construction 12,000-cap space announced a new completion date, a rival arena project has released plans for a venue set for 2030

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/lagos-arena-main-photo.avif)

The under-construction Lagos arena (impression)

An under-construction Lagos arena has confirmed the new target completion date of June 2028, according to Nigerian real estate firm Reverie Brokerage, the first communication from developers since the project missed its original December 2025 target.

The announcements comes through Reverie Brokerage following the company being invited to a site visit where it said developers revealed the new target date. The venue, located in Oniru, Lekki, is being developed by MetroWave Sports and Infrastructure Limited, with Elalan Construction serving as the main contractor. It is set to be Nigeria’s first indoor arena built to the standards of the International Basketball Federation (IBF), and will have a seated capacity of up to 12,000.

Architectural design is being led by Turkish firm Yazgan Design Architecture. The arena is designed as a six-level structure, with the ground floor serving as the service floor and spectator seating beginning from the first floor. The project is estimated to cost between €78m and €104m.

“Construction works are in their early state with a completion date set for June 2028,” Reverie Brokerage said in an online post. It added the arena is “a huge investment but a valuable one”.

When completed, the arena is expected to address one of the biggest infrastructure gaps facing Nigeria’s live entertainment industry. Despite being one of Africa’s largest music markets and home to some of the biggest artists from the continent, Lagos currently lacks a purpose-built international-standard indoor arena capable of accommodating major touring productions year-round.

> “Construction works are in their early state with a completion date set for June 2028”

The project was first announced in 2024 by a consortium comprising Persianas Group, the Nigerian Sovereign Investment Authority (NSIA), Live Nation, Oak View Group, Yinka Folawiyo Group, Adino Capital and MBO Capital. At the time, the partners mentioned the venue as “a catalyst for attracting more international concerts, sporting events and family entertainment productions to Nigeria while supporting the continued growth of the country’s live entertainment sector”.

The venue is one of several arena developments currently underway across Africa. In June, IQ reported on plans for a [9,000-capacity multipurpose arena in Cotonou, Benin](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/06/benin-backs-exhibition-live-ambitions-with-9000-cap-arena/), while Nairobi is set to welcome a new arena also scheduled for completion in 2028.

It also comes as a rival arena development in Lagos unveiled concept plans for a 20,000-capacity venue known as the Dream Theatre. The proposed facility comes from Landmark Africa, a lifestyle and hospitality destination in Lagos, and would span more than 70,000 square metres. However, the proposal remains in its pre-development stage, with its exact location and project team yet to be disclosed.

The announcement adds to a [wider wave of arena development taking place across Nigeria](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/01/lagos-shows-signs-of-market-maturity/). Lagos-based property market publication Estate Intel estimates that more than 30,000 seats are currently in the country’s emerging pipeline of major arena developments targeted for completion by 2030.

The former National Theatre also reopened in October 2025 following a major renovation and was renamed the Wole Soyinka Centre for Culture and Creative Arts. The 4,000-capacity venue has since returned to hosting major live performances, including a sold-out [concert by Adekunle Gold](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/02/adekunle-gold-puts-lagos-rebuilt-national-theatre-to-the-test/) in December.

The development furthers a model that has long defined Lagos, where live entertainment infrastructure is integrated with real estate, hospitality and wider lifestyle offerings rather than developed as standalone venues. As seen with [Ilubirin](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/06/livespot-eyes-long-term-venue-use-for-lagos-residential-development/), a residential and commercial waterfront development on Lagos Island that hosted Livespot’s week-long December 2025 event series, the Dream Theatre would also sit within a mixed-use destination incorporating retail, conference and event facilities, family leisure and recreation, hospitality and tourism, and luxury corporate real estate.
