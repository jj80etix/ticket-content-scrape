# FTC’s Subscription Crackdown: What Payment Processors Need to Know

## Summary

A Troutman Pepper "Payments Pros" podcast episode examines a recent FTC enforcement action against a subscription scheme involving 15 corporations and eight individuals operating through Cyprus- and Ukraine-based entities with access to US payment rails. Host Keith Barnett walks through the FTC's allegations — undisclosed recurring charges, unauthorized billing, and obstructed cancellation mechanisms — and the broad temporary restraining order covering asset freezes and expedited discovery reaching third-party banks and payment processors. The episode frames this as evidence the FTC remains aggressive on deceptive billing and disclosure practices across the payments ecosystem.

### Key Points
- FTC action targets 15 corporations and 8 individuals in a subscription scheme run through Cyprus- and Ukraine-based entities with US payment-rail access.
- Alleged violations include undisclosed subscription terms, deceptive advertising, unauthorized/recurring billing, and deliberately obstructed cancellation.
- A temporary restraining order includes asset freezes, foreign asset repatriation, customer data protections, and expedited discovery extending to banks and payment processors.
- Businesses are advised to prioritize clear disclosures, simple cancellation options, and strong BSA compliance.
- Episode frames the FTC (under the Trump administration) as continuing an active, aggressive enforcement posture in payments/billing.

### Highlights
- FTC enforcement covers 15 corporations + 8 individuals; entities based in Cyprus and Ukraine with US payment-rail access.
- TRO scope includes asset freezes, foreign asset repatriation, and expedited discovery reaching third-party banks/payment processors.
- Guidance explicitly calls out compliance obligations for payment processors, not just merchants.

### Industry Problem
This evidences the FTC's continued aggressive posture on deceptive billing, non-disclosure, and cancellation-friction practices — the same enforcement climate underlying junk-fee and all-in-pricing scrutiny that bears on any platform (including a primary ticketing platform) handling recurring charges or billing disclosures.

### Proposed Solution
The item itself proposes no product fix, only compliance advice (clear disclosures, simple cancellation, BSA diligence). For Etix, the takeaway is to monitor FTC enforcement trends against payment/billing partners and ensure checkout and any subscription-like or auto-renew flows maintain unambiguous fee disclosure and easy cancellation, reducing exposure to the same enforcement lens.
