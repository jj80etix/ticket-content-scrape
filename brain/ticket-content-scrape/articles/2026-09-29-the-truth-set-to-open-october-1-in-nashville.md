# The Truth Set to Open October 1 in Nashville

## Summary

The Truth, a new 4,400-capacity Live Nation/AJ Capital venue in Nashville's Wedgewood-Houston neighborhood, opens October 1 with Miranda Lambert headlining and a songwriting-themed design (lyrics displayed throughout, printed setlists after shows). The three-tier room is designed so no fan is more than 150 feet from the stage.

### Key Points
- Opening-night headliner: Miranda Lambert; year-end schedule includes Jack White, The Chicks, Sting, Smashing Pumpkins, Beck, Tedeschi Trucks Band, and more.
- Designed by Live Nation's in-house Blueprint Studio; three audience tiers rise above the stage, max 150 feet from performer.
- Name references songwriter Harlan Howard's "three chords and the truth" line; Lyric Lobby features a chandelier projecting that night's artist's lyrics.
- Positioned to celebrate Nashville's songwriting history while hosting acts across genres.

The Truth, a new 4,400-capacity live music venue from Live Nation and AJ Capital, is set to open October 1 in Nashville’s Wedgewood-Houston neighborhood. The three-tier space was designed to celebrate the city’s songwriting history while hosting performers across a range of genres.

Miranda Lambert will headline the opening-night concert. The venue’s schedule through the end of the year also includes Malcolm Todd, Role Model, Freya Skye, Jack White, The Chicks, Sting, The Smashing Pumpkins, HARDY, Beck, GloRilla and Tedeschi Trucks Band.

The venue’s name is inspired by songwriter Harlan Howard’s observation that a good country song needs three chords and the truth. Live Nation has carried the songwriting theme into the building, with lyrics displayed in the lobby and printed setlists available after performances.

“We want fans to feel connected to the music,” said Sally Williams, Live Nation’s president of Nashville music and business strategy. Williams added that the company wants “artists to feel genuinely welcome and cared for.”

Blueprint Studio, Live Nation’s in-house design and development group, planned the concert room so its three audience tiers rise above the stage. According to Live Nation, no fan will be more than 150 feet from a performance. Exposed brick, concrete, steel and wood shape the interior, while the room’s acoustics were designed to provide clear sound throughout.

In the Lyric Lobby, a custom chandelier will project lyrics by the artist performing that night. Upstairs, the Set List Studio will use an antique 1950s letterpress to print that night’s setlist as a keepsake. Five local printmakers have also created works for backstage areas inspired by Nashville, Tennessee and the surrounding neighborhood.

The two-story Vinyl Room includes artifacts selected in collaboration with nearby United Record Pressing. Its food and beverage offerings will include an Asian-inspired menu and craft cocktails. Harlan’s, a whiskey bar on the mezzanine, will carry bottles from 34 Tennessee makers. Other menu items draw on local ingredients and Tennessee food traditions.

Live Nation has made multiyear commitments to Nashville Songwriters Association International and W.O. Smith Music School. Students from W.O. Smith will perform during a soft opening before October 1, becoming the first musicians to play The Truth’s stage.

Dr. Valerie Cordero, W.O. Smith’s executive director, said the students have worked hard on their repertoire. Being the “first to perform on this stage,” she said, will be an achievement they remember.

The venue is also partnering with Humphreys Street, a neighborhood social enterprise that creates jobs and mentorship opportunities for teenagers. It will provide coffee and soaps for artists and crews backstage.
