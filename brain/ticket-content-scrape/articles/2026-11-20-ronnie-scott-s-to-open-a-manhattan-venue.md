# Ronnie Scott’s to open a Manhattan venue

## Summary

London's legendary Ronnie Scott's jazz club is moving ahead with a Manhattan venue at 246 Mercer Street, after Manhattan's Landmarks Committee approved development plans on 16 November. The club, which already secured a liquor license in January, will seat up to 332 and cap at 400 occupancy, with ticketed-only access to live performances.

### Key Points
- New York venue will occupy a long-vacant corner building at 246 Mercer Street near Washington Square Park.
- Plans approved by the Landmarks Committee of Manhattan Community Board Two on 16 November; liquor license secured in January.
- Layout: ground floor and basement, 97 tables, 332 seats, two no-seating bars, 332 seated/400 max legal occupancy, basement members' lounge.
- Venue will be ticketed-access-only for scheduled live performances, with recorded/DJ music between sets; no barricade or velvet-rope setups permitted.
- Move mirrors Manhattan-native jazz brand Blue Note's earlier 2026 expansion into London; Ronnie Scott's previously ran a Birmingham outpost (1991-2002).

## Transcript

## Ronnie Scott’s to open a Manhattan venue

![](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Ronnie-Scotts-New-York.jpg)

Ronnie Scott's New York (rendering) © Kostow Greenwood Architects

Legendary London jazz club Ronnie Scott’s is preparing to open a New York venue.

The move will see London and New York trade new branches of famed jazz clubs, after the Manhattan-native Blue Note brand [opened a London space](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/08/a-younger-generation-embracing-jazz-blue-note-on-opening-its-first-uk-venue/) earlier this year.

Plans took their latest step forward on 16 November, when the Landmarks Committee of Manhattan Community Board Two approved plans from architects for the development the site.

Set to be located at 246 Mercer Street near Manhattan’s Washington Square Park, Ronnie Scott’s New York will take over a corner building that has been vacant since 2018. It follows the clubs securing a liquor license in January this year.

Details of the license confirm the venue will consist of ground floor and basement rooms. There will be 97 tables and 332 seats, as well as two customer bars with no seating, equalling a total seated occupancy of 332 persons and a proposed maximum legal occupancy of 400. According to plans approved this week, the basement will contain a member’s lounge.

London’s Ronnie Scott’s club, like with many other jazz clubs, runs Upstairs at Ronnie’s, a secondary room for smaller events in addition to its concert space.

The upstairs of the London club was upgraded and reopened earlier this year as a 130-cap performance space.

The New York club will be ticketed access only, with live music at hours of scheduled performances, and recorded and DJ music in-between events. The venue does not have permission for metal barricade or velvet rope ingress set-ups.

Ronnie Scott’s previously ventured outside of London with a Birmingham franchise outpost, which operated between 1991 and 2002. *IQ* has asked Ronnie Scott’s for comment on the plans.
