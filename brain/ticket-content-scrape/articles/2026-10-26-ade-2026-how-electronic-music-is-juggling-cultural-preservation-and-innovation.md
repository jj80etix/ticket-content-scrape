# ADE 2026: How electronic music is juggling cultural preservation and innovation

## Summary

Amsterdam Dance Event's 30th edition closed with conference discussions on preserving electronic music culture while keeping it forward-looking, alongside the launch of the Nighttime Culture Index and announcement of Insomniac's new Map Point Summit. Panelists also discussed grassroots venue funding, including the UK's proposed per-ticket levy on larger venues.

### Key Points
- ADE 2026 drew ~4,000 artists across 300+ Amsterdam venues for its 30th year.
- Insomniac founder Pasquale Rotella announced Map Point Summit, debuting May 2027 in Las Vegas alongside EDC.
- UK Music Venue Trust ambassador Tinie Tempah backed a proposed £1-per-ticket levy on venues above ~5,000 capacity to fund grassroots clubs.
- Panel discussion also touched on VAT/business-rate relief for small promoters and Berlin's cultural protections for its techno scene.

As the renowned festival and conference marks 30 years, conversations focussed on keeping the grassroots healthy

Fittingly, as Amsterdam Dance Event closed its 30th edition, the conference conversations focused on how to preserve electronic music culture without losing the genre’s forward-looking approach, amid a [“thriving”](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/10/nighttime-culture-index-reveals-nightlifes-global-cultural-impact/) global scene.

The event welcomed almost 4,000 artist, who played more than 300 venues across Amsterdam, organisers say, with conference highlights including ADE and VibeLab’s launch of the first [Nighttime Culture Index](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/10/nighttime-culture-index-reveals-nightlifes-global-cultural-impact/), which draws on more than 12,000 line-ups and 3,000 venues to measure cultural vitality of electronic music around the world.

The accompanying festival opened with the sold-out *Swan Lake Remixed* at Gashouder, and guest of honour Jean-Michel Jarre discussed AI and spatial sound at Felix Meritis before an audiovisual show at AFAS Live.

Among the many new announcements, one key initiative came from Insomniac founder Pasquale Rotella, who used his ADE keynote to announce Map Point Summit, a three-day conference created with ADE. It debuts in May 2027 at Virgin Hotels Las Vegas, between the two weekends of Electric Daisy Carnival.

The summit will bring together aspiring creators, independent promoters, artists and established professionals for sessions, workshops and networking. Its name comes from the early rave days, when partygoers gathered at a “map point” to get directions to a secret location.

“We need to support independent promoters, share knowledge, create opportunities for the next generation, and work together to build an industry that allows rave culture to thrive on its own terms,” Rotella said.

At Wednesday’s ‘Live Industry: How Is It Doing?’ Panel chaired by producer and DJ Dave Clarke, Mercedes Coco, head of brand and partnerships at newly-refurbished venue Gashouder, explained the 4,000-capacity space is aimed at mid-career artists, filling the gap between Amsterdam’s 1,500-capacity rooms and the 6,000-capacity AFAS Live (the next step up). It plans to host a mix of dance, art and performance to  
widen its audience.

Elsewhere, rapper Tinie Tempah, an ambassador for the UK’s Music Venue Trust, backed the organisation’s £1-per-ticket levy on venues above roughly 5,000-capacity, with the money redistributed to grassroots clubs. He also called for lower VAT and business rates for small promoters, and pointed to Berlin’s cultural protection for its techno scene as a model the UK could copy. He suggested community-owned venues and a heritage-style listing system. Grassroots venues, he said, are the industry’s “research and development” department.

> “\[We need\] an industry that allows rave culture to thrive on its own terms”

Other panellists described how they are navigating the financial pressures of being independent. Sharon Simonetta of Ibiza club Chinois said its recipe for success sees early-bird prices kept low and it builds its marketing around its dance floor, which gives DJs room to experiment. Tamás Kádár of Hungary’s Sziget festival also argued that festivals should spend less on main-stage headliners and more on the wider festival  
experience.

Superstruct COO Rebecca Kane Burton said curation matters more than ever, and that the group has renamed its booking teams “curators”. And she shared information about the company’s new [Artistic Code](https://www.iqmagazine.com/2026/09/superstruct-reveals-artistic-code-for-festival-portfolio/).

A separate panel on global touring offered practical advice for artists. Business manager Daniel Berrocal urged artists to spend about a fifth of their time on the business side, including company set-up, insurance and tax planning. Kathi Longinus, an artist manager, said artists should check their budgets regularly and plan US visas well ahead. Elise Milani of Toronto’s BLK Matter suggested building direct fan revenue through subscriptions, and several panellists backed merchandise, sample packs and add-on events such as after-parties.

Elsewhere across the conference, Scott Campbell, lead talent buyer for AEG Presents Rocky Mountains, said an artist’s lake-at-dawn video helped build their audience without climbing the venue ladder one rung at a time. He also advised artists to be canny about adding merch and after-party concepts.

Panellists also pointed to growth in South America and Southeast Asia, and urged artists to tour multiple regions rather than rely on repeatedly touring one home market.

Marking the close of the event, organisers said: “Thank you all for being part of the most memorable ADE ever. \[…\] It’s been magical, to share our love for electronic music with each other this intensely.”

The 2027 event will run from 20-24 October.
