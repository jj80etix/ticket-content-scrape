# Kansas City’s City Council Approves Plan To Help Fund $1.9B Stadium Project

## Summary

Kansas City's city council approved tax-increment financing and bond issuance to cover the city's $600 million commitment toward a new $1.9 billion Royals ballpark, part of a broader Crown Center sports-entertainment-district redevelopment set to open for the 2030 season. The bonds are projected to generate about $537 million toward construction, with total payments and interest estimated near $1.39 billion over the term.

### Key Points
- KC city council approved TIF/bond financing for its $600M share of a $1.9B Royals ballpark project.
- Bonds expected to generate ~$537M for construction; total cost with interest projected at ~$1.39B.
- New ballpark targeted to open for the 2030 season, replacing Kauffman Stadium.
- Project is part of a larger mixed-use Crown Center sports-entertainment-district redevelopment.

Kansas City Royals fans are another step closer to getting a new ballpark for their beloved MLB team. To support the $1.9 billion stadium project, the city council approved plans on Thursday to establish a tax increment financing plan and authorize issuing bonds for the city’s $600 million commitment, the [*Kansas City Star*](https://www.kansascity.com/news/local/article317545028.html) reported.

The publication notes that “the interest on the bonds over the decades will result in a price tag more than twice that amount.” The bond issuance is expected to generate about $537 million, which will go toward construction of the stadium and Royals team offices, with the payments and interest projected to cost about $1.39 billion.

The new Royals ballpark, which will see the team relocate from Kauffman Stadium, is set to open in time for the 2030 season. The venue is being planned as part of a multibillion-dollar development of Kansas City’s Crown Center area into a sports entertainment district with new housing and retail, according to the *Kansas City Star.*

“Today Kansas City voted to keep the Major League Baseball and Hallmark Cards in Kansas City, clearing the way for a transformational baseball-centered district that delivers for taxpayers, workers, and neighborhoods,” Mayor Quinton Lucas said in a statement.  
  
The Kansas City Royals and Hallmark Cards, which is based in Kansas City, [announced in April](https://www.mlb.com/press-release/royals-hallmark-cards-unveil-landmark-plan-to-bring-major-league-baseball-downtown) that they were teaming up to bring the new ballpark and mixed-use development to life.

New economic activity in the sports entertainment district is expected to bring in $1.4 billion over the next three decades, thanks to taxes including hotel and parks sales taxes, which will go toward helping cover the cost of bond payments and interest. The *Kansas City Star* adds that “a financial analysis report projects a new Royals ballpark in Crown Center will raise attendance at games by 20%, helping fuel an increase in tax revenue that could go toward paying off the city’s contribution toward the project.”  
  
Private investment in the project is expected to total an estimated $2 billion.
