# Secondary US sellers sue to halt ‘legally flawed’ D.C. resale laws

## Summary

The Ticket Policy Forum and four secondary-market members — SeatGeek, Vivid Seats, Gametime and TickPick — filed a federal suit in the U.S. District Court for D.C. seeking to block enforcement of Washington D.C.'s newly passed Resale Act, calling it "legally flawed" and arguing it does "nothing to prevent consumer deception" while raising constitutional objections. Most of the article's detail on the specific claims is behind IQ's paywall, but the filing signals resale platforms are moving to challenge resale-specific regulation directly in court rather than only lobbying against it.

### Key Points
- Plaintiffs: Ticket Policy Forum, SeatGeek, Vivid Seats, Gametime and TickPick.
- Suit filed in U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, challenging D.C.'s "Resale Act."
- Plaintiffs argue the law violates the Constitution on multiple grounds (specifics paywalled) and fails to address actual consumer deception.
- Part of a broader pattern of jurisdictions passing resale-specific rules that secondary platforms are now contesting directly in court.

### Highlights
- Four major secondary platforms (SeatGeek, Vivid Seats, Gametime, TickPick) jointly sued, via the Ticket Policy Forum trade group, to block a passed D.C. resale law.
- Core legal theory: the law is constitutionally flawed and doesn't actually address consumer deception, rather than being an anti-competitive burden argument alone.
- Signals a shift from resale platforms lobbying against resale legislation to litigating against it after passage.

### Industry Problem
State/local resale-specific legislation (price caps, transfer restrictions, disclosure rules) is proliferating faster than resale platforms can absorb compliance-wise, and this suit shows platforms now see litigation, not just lobbying, as a lever to slow or reshape that patchwork — a sign the regulatory landscape Etix must track (Q9/Q11) is becoming more contested and less predictable.

### Proposed Solution
No product fix is implied; this is a legal/regulatory posture question. For Etix, the right move is to monitor how this case resolves and what legal theory succeeds, since it will shape which resale/transferability rules are durable across jurisdictions Etix's clients operate in — a #monitor posture until the litigation clarifies the rules, with #improve readiness to adapt resale/transfer features once a jurisdiction's rule is settled rather than contested.
