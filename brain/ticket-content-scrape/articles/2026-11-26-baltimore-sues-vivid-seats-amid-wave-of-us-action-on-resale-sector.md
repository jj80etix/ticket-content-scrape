# Baltimore sues Vivid Seats amid wave of US action on resale sector

## Summary

Baltimore has sued resale platform Vivid Seats, part of a wider wave of US state- and city-level legal action against the secondary-ticketing sector. IQ's full detail on Baltimore's specific claims and relief sought is behind its paywall; the available text frames this as one of several resale-platform litigations currently being challenged in US courts.

### Key Points
- Baltimore filed suit against Vivid Seats.
- Described as part of a broader wave of US state/city legal actions targeting resale platforms.
- Multiple other resale-sector litigations are reportedly being challenged in parallel across US courts (not individually named in the accessible text).

### Highlights
- A US city (Baltimore) has taken direct legal action against a major resale platform (Vivid Seats), adding to a pattern of multi-jurisdiction US litigation against secondary-ticketing companies.
- Signals continued, broadening municipal/state appetite for resale-sector enforcement beyond the federal FTC/DOJ actions already tracked (Q9, Q10).

### Industry Problem
Resale platforms face mounting, fragmented legal exposure across US states and cities over pricing/transparency practices — a compliance-burden and reputational-risk pattern for the secondary market that could reshape how resale (and by extension primary-platform resale integrations) operate.

### Proposed Solution
No remedy is proposed in the available text. For Etix: monitor this as part of the broader resale/transferability regulatory trendline (Q11) — the trigger for a posture change would be a ruling or settlement that sets new disclosure/pricing norms resale integrations would need to match.
