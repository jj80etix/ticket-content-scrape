# The Biz: HYBE’s Record Quarter; UMG Earnings; Jury Demand In LN BOTS Trial

## Summary

Pollstar's "The Biz" roundup covers record quarters for HYBE and Universal Music Group, plus a significant ticketing-litigation development: the FTC and four states (Colorado, Florida, Illinois, Nebraska) have filed a motion demanding a jury trial in their BOTS Act case against Live Nation and Ticketmaster. The underlying suit alleges the companies knowingly let brokers evade posted ticket limits — including declining stronger identity verification in 2021 because it was "too effective" — to capture revenue from both the original sale and the resale.

### Key Points
- FTC + 4 states move for a jury in the BOTS Act prosecution of Live Nation/Ticketmaster (3 other plaintiff states did not join)
- Suit (filed Sept. 2025) alleges Ticketmaster "tacitly worked" with brokers who controlled 6,345 accounts holding 246,407 tickets to 2,594 events
- FTC cites an internal Ticketmaster email admitting the company "turn[s] a blind eye as a matter of policy" to limit violations
- Suit says Ticketmaster rejected third-party identity verification in 2021 because it was "too effective" at stopping broker evasion
- TradeDesk software reportedly lets Ticketmaster identify limit-violating brokers but the suit alleges it isn't used to stop them
- Suit also alleges bait-and-switch pricing predating Ticketmaster's 2023 voluntary move to all-in pricing

### Highlights
- A jury trial (rather than bench trial) raises the stakes and public visibility of the BOTS Act case against Live Nation/Ticketmaster
- Core allegation: Ticketmaster had the technical means (TradeDesk, ID verification) to stop broker limit-evasion but chose not to, for revenue reasons
- Case is distinct from, but parallel to, the DOJ antitrust litigation — both center on Ticketmaster's structural incentives around broker/resale activity

### Industry Problem
Primary ticketing platforms that also profit from resale face an inherent conflict of interest in enforcing anti-bot/purchase-limit rules against brokers, since vigorous enforcement would reduce resale-driven revenue — a dynamic now at the center of federal BOTS Act litigation.

### Proposed Solution
For an independent primary platform like Etix, the proposed solution is positioning: enforce purchase limits and offer real identity verification without a resale-revenue conflict, and use this litigation as grounding material (AI-context) for messaging around trustworthy anti-bot enforcement. Watch trial developments and any resulting remedies/consent-decree changes as triggers to revisit posture.

K-pop mega-giant HYBE surpassed $1 billion in revenue the third quarter, the first time it’s hit that mark.

The company also reported record concert revenues — $512 million — driven largely by the European and American legs of BTS’s “ARIRANG” tour.

In all of 2025, HYBE’s concert revenues were roughly $560 million.

The big revenue boost pushed operating profit to $133 million. In the same quarter last year, HYBE posted a loss.

Merch and licensing revenue also had a record quarter — $236 million. Merch purchases at BTS’ US dates alone hit $75 million.

Meanwhile, rival SM had a much tougher quarter. Revenues for the company behind aespa and others were off 9.5% year-over-year in Q3. SM pinned the losses on exchange rates as the won gained strength through the quarter; HYBE also noted that even with its record numbers, the strengthening won hurt overseas revenues.

**UMG, Sony Post Q3 Gains**

Third quarter revenues at Universal Music Group were €3.394 billion (USD $3.9B), up 11.7% year-over-year.

Notably, subscription revenue grew 19.2% on the back of both price increases and subscriber growth.

“Our results show the enduring value of backing extraordinary artists, bold ideas, and the teams and technology that help bring their work to the world. The industry is healthy and growing, and we will keep evolving from a position of strength. We are investing in human artistry, improving the economics of streaming, and expanding what’s possible for artists and fans. To support that strategy, we are making UMG faster and more focused so we can operate more efficiently and drive sustained growth and long-term value,” chairman and CEO Lucien Grange said.

**FTC, States Make Jury Demand In Live Nation BOTS Act Trial**

The Federal Trade Commission and four states are asking for a jury in their prosecution of Live Nation and Ticketmaster for violations of the BOTS Act.

Colorado, Florida, Illinois and Nebraska joined the feds’ motion. Other plaintiff states — Tennessee, Utah and Virginia — did not join the motion for reasons that were not disclosed.

The suit filed in September 2025 claims that Ticketmaster and Live Nation “tacitly worked” with secondary sellers, allowing them to buy tickets in the thousands and then list them for resale at marked-up prices, often on Ticketmaster’s own platform, thus giving Ticketmaster multiple bites at the revenue apple: from the initial purchase, the listing by the secondary seller and the consumer who ultimately buys the resold ticket.

The FTC says an unnamed senior Ticketmaster executive wrote in an internal email that the companies “turn a blind eye as a matter of policy” to brokers’ violations of posted ticket limits and that five brokers controlled 6,345 Ticketmaster accounts and possessed 246,407 concert tickets to 2,594 events.

The suit says that Ticketmaster’s TradeDesk software gives the company the ability to determine which brokers are violating purchase limits, but armed with that information, does nothing to stop it.

“The companies also have consistently declined to deploy additional technology that would more effectively prevent brokers from evading ticket limits because such tactics would decrease their revenue, according to internal company documents,” the FTC said in a statement. “For example, the company in 2021 opted against using third-party identity verification because it was ‘too effective.’”

The suit says Ticketmaster and Live Nation’s “illegal conduct frustrates artists’ desire to maintain affordable ticket prices that fit the needs of ordinary American families, costing ordinary fans millions of dollars every year.”

The lawsuit further alleges bait and switch by Ticketmaster by advertising list prices that didn’t include mandatory fees. Ticketmaster voluntarily switched to all-in upfront pricing in June 2023 for tickets at Live Nation venues ahead of an FTC rule change made in the waning days of the Biden Administration that mandated all-in pricing across the ticketing industry.

The suit also alleges BOTS Act violations, saying that Ticketmaster is complicit in the use of automated bots and deceptive practices by secondary sellers on its site. That echoes a defense by Key Investment Group — a conglomerate that controls numerous resale sites that is also being sued for BOTS Act violation — who said, in short, that it cannot violate the BOTS Act because Ticketmaster is aware of the “common” tactics used by secondary sellers.

“American live entertainment is the best in the world and should be accessible to all of us,” FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson said in a statement. “It should not cost an arm and a leg to take the family to a baseball game or attend your favorite musician’s show.”

The suit asks a judge to permanenly enjoin Ticketmaster and Live Nation from violations of the FTC Act, the BOTS Act and the consumer protection laws of the plaintiff states, as well as undetermined monetary relief and fines for BOTS Act violations.
