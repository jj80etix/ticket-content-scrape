# Live Nation CEO Rapino Leads $54 Million Stock Selling Spree Among Executives

## Summary

Three senior Live Nation executives — CEO Michael Rapino, President/CFO Joe Berchtold, and EVP John Hopmans — sold a combined $54.1 million in company stock on Nov. 12-13, per new SEC filings, bringing 2026 insider sales to roughly $81.1 million. Most of the Rapino/Berchtold selling reflects exercise of 2017 stock options (strike price $29.03) nearing their March 2027 expiration rather than new discretionary liquidation, and both retained the same share count they held before exercising; the filings don't establish any view on Live Nation's share-price outlook.

### Key Points
- Combined Nov. 12-13 sales: Rapino $35.4M, Berchtold $16.7M, Hopmans $2.0M (total $54.1M across 300,248 shares).
- Rapino and Berchtold exercised 2017-granted options (strike $29.03/share) set to expire March 16, 2027, then sold the same number of shares acquired — no net reduction in their stock position.
- 2026 year-to-date insider sales across Live Nation officers/directors total about $81.1 million.
- Comes after Live Nation's record Q3: revenue $10.1B (+19% YoY), adjusted operating income roughly $1.18B.

While the size of the transactions is substantial, SEC disclosures indicate that most of the selling was connected to the exercise of stock options awarded nearly a decade ago that were approaching expiration, rather than executives liquidating previously owned shares.

## Expiring Stock Options Generate Nearly $44 Million Before Taxes

Both Rapino and Berchtold exercised options granted in 2017 that allowed them to purchase Live Nation stock at $29.03 per share, dramatically below the prevailing market price.

In his [November 13 SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1335258/000133704126000023/xslF345X06/wk-form4_1794598746.xml?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), Rapino reported exercising options for 195,578 shares at a total cost of approximately $5.68 million. He subsequently sold those shares in three transactions at weighted-average prices of $180.19, $181.16 and $182.11, generating approximately $35.41 million in gross proceeds.

The difference between Rapino’s exercise costs and sale proceeds amounted to approximately $29.73 million before taxes and other expenses.

Berchtold conducted a similar transaction on November 13, exercising options for 93,537 shares at the same $29.03 price before selling them in three transactions at weighted-average prices between $178.21 and $180.03.

His [SEC disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1335258/000155228126000014/xslF345X06/wk-form4_1794620623.xml?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) shows approximately $16.73 million in gross proceeds against an exercise cost of approximately $2.72 million, producing a difference of roughly $14.01 million before taxes and expenses.

Combined, Rapino and Berchtold generated approximately $52.13 million in stock sales, with an estimated $43.74 million remaining after subtracting their option exercise costs, but before taxes and other expenses.

Both executives’ filings contain an explanation for the timing of their transactions. The stock options were granted in 2017 and scheduled to expire March 16, 2027, with the company indicating that the executives were exercising them during one of the last remaining open trading windows available to insiders before expiration.

The executives also retained considerable equity positions. Because Rapino and Berchtold sold the same number of shares they acquired through the option exercises, neither reduced his previously held common-stock position through these transactions.

Rapino reported retaining 4,188,167 shares following his sales, while Berchtold retained 901,617.

Hopmans’ November transaction was structured differently. His [Form 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1335258/000187054326000016/xslF345X06/wk-form4_1794620653.xml?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) reports the sale of 11,133 shares at $180.50 each, generating approximately $2.01 million. A separate Form 144 identifies the shares as previously awarded performance compensation acquired in November 2025 and January 2026. Following the sale, Hopmans retained 163,299 shares.

The reported gross proceeds do not represent the executives’ final after-tax earnings, which cannot be determined from the disclosures alone.

The November transactions are not isolated. A review of Live Nation’s SEC filings shows approximately $81.1 million in reported stock sales by company officers and directors since the beginning of 2026.

In May, Hopmans sold 93,078 shares for approximately $15.46 million, including shares acquired through exercises of 2017 stock options. Michael Rowles, then the company’s general counsel, conducted a similar option exercise and sale in May involving 63,776 shares and approximately $10.47 million in gross proceeds.

Additional transactions during the year included approximately $533,600 in shares sold by Chief Accounting Officer Brian Capo in August and two sales by director Jeffrey Hinson totaling approximately $526,700.

The transactions illustrate the substantial wealth accumulated through Live Nation’s executive compensation programs, particularly stock options awarded years earlier when the company’s shares traded at considerably lower prices.

In August, [SEC filings showed that four senior Live Nation executives collectively held nearly $1 billion in company stock](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/08/live-nation-executives-hold-nearly-1-billion-in-stock-as-new-equity-tranche-vests/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), even after portions of previously awarded restricted shares were withheld to cover tax obligations.

Those August transactions differed significantly from the latest sales. The earlier share withholding primarily reflected automatic tax payments associated with the vesting of equity awards rather than discretionary sales generating cash proceeds for executives.

Berchtold has also previously realized substantial proceeds from expiring equity compensation. In December 2023, he [exercised and sold more than 350,000 shares acquired through options awarded approximately a decade earlier](https://www.ticketnews.com/2023/12/live-nations-joe-berchtold-nets-36-million-from-lyv-stock-sales/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), generating more than $32 million in gross sales.

The latest transactions follow another strong quarter for Live Nation, which [reported third-quarter revenue of $10.1 billion](https://investors.livenationentertainment.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001335258-26-000052/lyv-2026q3xex991er.htm?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), an increase of 19% from the previous year. The company also reported operating income of $892 million and adjusted operating income of approximately $1.18 billion.

Although executive stock sales sometimes attract attention as potential indicators of management’s expectations for a company’s future performance, the documented expiration dates and exercise terms provide a specific explanation for much of Live Nation’s recent activity. The filings do not establish that the transactions were motivated by expectations of declining share prices.

What they do establish is that Live Nation’s senior leadership continues to convert extraordinarily valuable long-term equity awards into substantial personal proceeds, while retaining significant ownership stakes in the concert promotion and ticketing giant.
