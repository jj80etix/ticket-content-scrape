# Massive Attack barred from Singapore after onstage display

## Summary

English trip-hop duo Massive Attack have been banned from returning to Singapore after unfurling a Palestinian flag onstage during their July 29 show at the 5,000-capacity Star Theater. Singapore authorities detained and questioned the band following the performance, citing strict national rules against displaying foreign flags without a permit, and issued "stern warnings" alongside the entry ban. The band defended the act as spontaneous solidarity with fans who had organically begun "Free Palestine" chants during the set.

### Key Points
- Incident occurred July 29 at Star Theater (5,000-capacity) in Singapore during Massive Attack's only stop in the country.
- Singapore Police Force and the Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) issued a joint statement citing risk to "racial and religious harmony."
- Authorities banned the band, Robert "3D" Del Naja and Grant "Daddy G" Marshall, from re-entering Singapore; future performance applications will not be approved.
- Local promoter was Lushington Entertainments, which operates in Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, and Hong Kong.
- Authorities noted the organizers' "prior acknowledgement of the licence conditions" governing the show.
- The band continues its tour with three Australian arena dates (Brisbane, Sydney, Melbourne) from August 6-11.

## Transcript

The English duo will not be allowed back in the Southeast Asian country after being detained for unfurling a Palestinian flag mid-show

![Massive Attack at LIDO](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Massive-Attack-at-LIDO-©-Sophie-J-Carey.png)

Massive Attack in 2025 © Sophie J Carey

After breaching national law by displaying a Palestinian flag onstage, English outfit Massive Attack have been banned from returning to and performing in Singapore.

During their show at the 5,000-capacity Star Theater on 29 July, the trip-hop group – composed of Robert “3D” Del Naja and Grant “Daddy G” Marshall – unfurled the flag after hearing “large sections of the auditorium organically” lead ‘Free Palestine’ chants, the group said in a [statement](https://www.instagram.com/p/Dbihk0UDDwF/?hl=en&img_index=1) following the events.

Singapore has strict rules on displaying foreign flags without a permit. The choice to display the flag onstage led to the group’s detainment and questioning by local authorities following the show.

“The Police take a serious view of acts which could potentially harm the racial and religious harmony in Singapore and urge the public, including foreigners, to refrain from importing foreign politics as this can undermine our social cohesion and the rule of law,” reads a [statement](https://www.police.gov.sg/Media-Hub/News/2026/08/20260731_joint_spf_imda_statement_on_incident_relating_to_british_band_massive_attack) from the Singapore Police Force and the Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA).

Authorities highlighted event organisers’ “prior acknowledgement of the licence conditions” within their statement. The show was promoted by local company Lushington Entertainments, which primarily operates in Singapore in addition to Kuala Lumpur and Hong Kong.

> “We are proud to have made this impromptu expression with our fans in Singapore”

Following the investigation, authorities issued “stern warnings” to the duo and said they would be banned from entering the country, and further performance applications would not be approved.

In response, the band said: “For our part, we did not imagine that merely holding up the flag of a sovereign state recognised by 157 countries (Palestine) would violate any law.”

“On reflection, we are proud to have made this impromptu expression with our fans in Singapore who clearly felt a moral imperative to show solidarity with the people of Palestine in their ongoing reality of illegal occupation, apartheid and genocide, and we salute their courage in doing so,” they continue.

“We are grateful to the people of Singapore who made us feel so welcome, and hope that the Government of Singapore will choose to ratify the UN International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights and allow their citizens expressions of conscience without fear of state prosecution. As we were saying … Free Palestine,” they conclude.

The concert was the band’s only stop in Singapore on their current outing. They are due to play across a trio of Australian arenas in the next week, from 6-11 August, across Brisbane, Sydney, and Melbourne.
