# StubHub Data Shows Pop Culture Drawing New Ticket Buyers to Sports, Concerts and Broadway

## Summary

StubHub's 2026 "Screens to Stands" trend report finds that pop-culture crossover promotions — anime nights, bestseller-themed baseball games, viral casting in Broadway shows — are reliably pulling first-time buyers into live events, with some promotions driving demand 9-75x above baseline. The effect spans sports, theater, and concerts: Bad Bunny's Super Bowl halftime show lifted Latin music's share of US concert demand on StubHub by over 50% in the following month, and creator-led live events have grown fivefold since 2019. StubHub notes the data comes from its own marketplace only and doesn't address whether these new buyers convert into repeat customers or how the crossover affects transaction prices.

### Key Points
- MLB promotional-night demand spikes: Dodgers' "One Piece" night drew 75x typical demand (two-thirds first-time MLB buyers); Yankees' "Naruto" night drew 9x.
- Broadway casting of non-traditional stars (reality-TV, influencers) correlated with large single-day demand jumps: "Chicago" +911% after a "Mormon Wives" star joined, +760% after a former NFL cheerleader joined.
- Bad Bunny's Super Bowl halftime show lifted Latin-music demand share on StubHub 50%+ for 30 days; ~80% of buyers for Latin acts in that window were first-time Latin-music buyers on the platform.
- Creator-led live events (e.g., live podcasts) have grown 5x since 2019, with live podcasts showing the highest first-time-buyer rate of any segment (~50%).
- Post-promotion searches for events outside the host league/category ran 1.5-4.5x higher than in-category searches, suggesting cross-category discovery effects.
- StubHub does not report pricing impact or long-term retention of these newly acquired buyers.

### Highlights
- Demand multipliers as high as 75x (MLB) and 911% (Broadway) tied to specific pop-culture tie-ins/castings.
- Latin-music concert demand share up 50%+ for 30 days following Bad Bunny's Super Bowl halftime show.
- Creator-led live events up 5x since 2019; live podcasts have the highest first-time-buyer rate (~50%) of any segment measured.

### Industry Problem
Secondary-market platforms are demonstrating that fandom crossover — not just artist/venue/price — is a measurable driver of new-buyer acquisition, but StubHub's own data stops short of showing whether this converts to retained, repeat customers or affects pricing/margin.

### Proposed Solution
No concrete fix is proposed in the piece; StubHub frames this as an open question for 2027 programming. For Etix, this is a Monitor-tag item: watch whether competitor primary platforms start building crossover-aware marketing/discovery tools (e.g., surfacing non-obvious events to buyers based on outside-category interests) and treat a competitor shipping such a feature as the trigger to evaluate a similar capability.

## Can Crossover Events Create Lasting Customers?

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/SH-2026EOY-02-423x600.webp)

For teams and ticketing companies, the longer-term opportunity lies in converting those customers into repeat ticket buyers.

StubHub conducted additional research into the subsequent behavior of first-time MLB buyers who purchased tickets to three pop culture-themed games: the Dodgers’ *One Piece* promotion on July 2, the Giants’ *KPop Demon Hunters* game on Aug. 14 and the Yankees’ *Naruto* promotion on Sept. 8.

The marketplace told TicketNews that each game was followed by increased interest in future MLB games. Perhaps more significantly, searches outside MLB were approximately 1.5 to 4.5 times as high as MLB-specific searches.

That finding suggests the potential benefits of crossover programming could extend beyond the individual team or league hosting an event.

“Fandom is fluid. People aren’t necessarily defining themselves as only sports, music or theater fans,” Gonzalez said. “A cultural moment or community they already care about can be the entry point that gets them to experience something completely new.”

The additional research does not establish how many of those subsequent searches resulted in ticket purchases, however. Whether consumers attracted by a particular promotion become regular customers remains an important question for event organizers assessing the long-term value of these partnerships.

StubHub’s first-time buyer statistics also reflect purchasing histories on its own platform. A customer purchasing an MLB ticket through StubHub for the first time may have previously attended games using tickets acquired elsewhere.

## From Reality Television to Broadway

Broadway producers are seeing their own examples of how established fan communities can influence ticket demand.

StubHub recorded a 911% single-day increase in ticket demand for “ [Chicago](https://stubhub.prf.hn/click/camref:1100l9NoF/destination:https%3A%2F%2Fwww.stubhub.com%2Fchicago-the-musical-tickets%2Fperformer%2F9231) ” following the announcement that Whitney Leavitt, a star of Hulu’s “The Secret Lives of Mormon Wives,” would join the production. Once Leavitt began performing, interest in the show climbed 203% above typical levels.

Leavitt’s engagement as Roxie Hart was subsequently [extended with an encore run](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/02/whitney-leavitt-to-return-to-chicago-on-broadway-for-encore-engagement/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), and she is scheduled to return to Broadway in December in “Just in Time.”

The casting of former Dallas Cowboys Cheerleader Reece Weaver in “Chicago” coincided with a 760% increase in ticket demand, while the announcement that internet personality Dylan Mulvaney would [join the cast of “Six”](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/01/broadways-six-to-welcome-back-jasmine-forsberg/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) preceded a 333% increase.

Such casting decisions illustrate how performers with an established following outside traditional theater can bring heightened attention to existing productions, including long-running shows competing with newer offerings for audiences.

Meanwhile, some digital personalities are creating live entertainment experiences of their own. StubHub reported that ticket demand for creator-led events has grown fivefold since 2019. Live podcasts recorded the highest proportion of first-time buyers of any segment on the marketplace, at approximately 50%.

Film and television properties are also finding audiences in live entertainment. Demand for theatrical productions adapted from films increased 46% year over year, while more than half of buyers for *La La Land in Concert* were making their first purchase on StubHub.

## Cultural Moments Lift Concert Demand

![](https://www.ticketnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/SH-2026EOY-05-423x600.webp)

The ability of widely watched performances to generate demand for live events was particularly evident following Bad Bunny’s Super Bowl halftime appearance.

In the 30 days following the performance, Latin music’s share of U.S. concert demand on StubHub increased by more than 50%. During the comparable period in 2025, that share remained relatively unchanged.

The increase extended to artists other than Bad Bunny. StubHub reported that demand for [Carín León](https://stubhub.prf.hn/click/camref:1100l9NoF/destination:https%3A%2F%2Fwww.stubhub.com%2Fcarin-leon-tickets%2Fperformer%2F100326849) grew fivefold compared with the 30 days preceding the Super Bowl, while demand for Silvestre Dangond and Gloria Trevi tripled.

Approximately 80% of Latin music ticket buyers on StubHub during the month following the Super Bowl had not previously purchased tickets to a Latin music event through the platform.

Similar demand spikes followed major festival performances and awards-show appearances. StubHub reported increases in demand for upcoming performances by several artists following Lollapalooza, including:

- Charli XCX: 212%
- John Summit: 186%
- aespa: 117%
- YOASOBI: 80%

Emerging artist Sienna Spiro recorded a 180% overnight increase in ticket demand following her Best New Artist win at the MTV Video Music Awards.

StubHub also identified country singer [Ella Langley](https://stubhub.prf.hn/click/camref:1100l9NoF/destination:https%3A%2F%2Fwww.stubhub.com%2Fella-langley-tickets%2Fperformer%2F101880852) as its fastest-growing emerging artist in 2026. Demand for Langley’s tickets grew eightfold year over year as her single “Choosin’ Texas” gained popularity, moving her from outside the marketplace’s top 30 country artists to fourth.

## Audience Development Opportunities for 2027

The findings offer a potential roadmap for teams, promoters, venues and producers looking to expand their customer bases in 2027.

They also illustrate the value of examining customer behavior across multiple entertainment categories. A consumer who initially visits a ticket marketplace to purchase tickets to a themed baseball game may subsequently discover concerts, theater productions or other sporting events.

For event organizers, the figures raise questions about how programming decisions can incorporate communities that have historically existed outside their core audiences.

The data comes from a single marketplace rather than the broader ticketing industry, and StubHub declined to provide corresponding ticket pricing figures. The extent to which the reported demand increases affected transaction prices or event profitability therefore remains unclear.

Still, the ability to attract customers who are new to a marketplace or entertainment category provides an additional measure of the value of cultural partnerships beyond the immediate increase in ticket demand.

“For teams, promoters and producers, there’s an opportunity to think beyond traditional audiences,” Gonzalez said. “Creators, entertainment properties and online culture already have highly engaged fanbases, and live events give those fans another way to participate in something they love.”
