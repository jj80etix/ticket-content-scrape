# Record fan demand drives continued Live Nation growth in Q2

## Summary

Live Nation posted Q2 2026 revenue of $7.7 billion (up 9% year-over-year), driven by record fan demand and more artists touring than ever. Ticketmaster contributed $852 million in revenue (up 15%) on 90 million fee-bearing tickets sold (up 8%), with international markets — especially South America — leading growth; Live Nation also announced a joint venture with Cidade Center Norte to build what would become Brazil's largest indoor arena.

### Key Points
- Q2 revenue $7.7bn (+9% YoY); operating income $522m (+7%); adjusted operating income $817m (+2%).
- Concert revenue $6.4bn (+8%); 49 million fans attended (+10%); international stadium/arena/festival attendance up over 20%, while US stadium attendance dipped on show timing.
- Ticketmaster revenue $852m (+15%), AOI $331m (+14%), 90 million fee-bearing tickets sold (+8%); international ticket sales up 12% with GTV up 20%, led by South America.
- Full-year capex projected at $1.1bn, mostly ($800m) for venue expansion/enhancement.
- New joint venture with real estate developer Cidade Center Norte to build a purpose-built arena in São Paulo, positioned as South America's largest indoor live-entertainment venue.
- CEO Michael Rapino cited over 143 million tickets sold through mid-July, 14 million ahead of last year's pace, and record deferred revenue of $6.4bn signaling a strong second half.

![TheTicketingBusiness News](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/TBN-LOGO-reversed-white.png)

TheTicketingBusiness News

**Live Nation Entertainment has said that record fan demand and more artists touring than ever has contributed to Q2 growth.**

The live entertainment giant has posted a revenue of $7.7bn (£5.7bn/€6.6bn), up 9% compared to the second quarter in 2025. Operating income has increase to $522m, up 7%, while adjusted operating income (AOI) totalled $817m, up 2%.

Concert revenue reached $6.4bn, an increase of 8% compared to same period the year prior, with a fan count of 49 million – marking a 10% rise. Live Nation reported that international attendance at stadiums, arenas and festivals was up over 20%. However, in the US, stadium attendance declined due to show timing, but the country’s attendance at amphitheatres and arenas was up double digits.

AOI for concerts totalled $130m, down 14%, and full-year fan attendance is projected to grow 10%. For the full year, Live Nation said the concerts segment remains on track to deliver double-digit AOI growth, with the majority of year-over-year improvement occurring in the final quarter.

**Demand for concerts fuels highest Q2 for Ticketmaster**

Ticketmaster’s revenue reached $852m in the second quarter of 2026, an increase of 15% compared to the same period last year.

Meanwhile, AOI totalled $331m, a jump of 14%, with 90 million fee-bearing tickets sold, an increase of 8%.

Concerts remained the primary growth driver with tickets sold up 11%, accounting for 90% of the ticket volume growth. International markets sold 39 million tickets, up 12%, with Gross Transaction Value (GTV) up 20% led by strong growth in South America.

Elsewhere, Venue Nation has witnessed onsite food and beverage spending increase at large US amphitheatres, and across European arenas and theatres. Live Nation’s sponsorship segment has also recorded revenues of $338m, up 12%, led by international markets. AOI for this segment amounted to $257m, an increase of 13%.

“In a world of endless screens and AI-generated everything, the one thing that can’t be copied is being there. More artists are on the road than ever — and fans keep choosing to be in the room with them, driving the strongest concert ticket sales we’ve ever seen. More than 143 million tickets have sold through mid-July, over 14 million ahead of last year’s pace, with mid-teens ticket sales growth across all large venue types: stadiums, arenas, and amphitheaters,” said Michael Rapino, president and chief executive of Live Nation.

“None of this happens without the artists — they make these moments, and we’re grateful to every artist and crew who trust us with their tours. This was a quarter of milestones: nearly 49 million fans attended our shows, Ticketmaster grew adjusted operating income 14%, and all-time-high deferred revenue points to a strong second half. The first-quarter legal accrual will weigh on reported operating income, but we remain on track for double-digit adjusted operating income growth this year — and to compound at that level for years to come.”

**Venue expansion and long-term growth plans**

According to Live Nation, full year capital expenditure is now projected to be $1.1bn, towards the lower end of its initial range due to timing of projects. Some $800m of total capital expenditures is for venue expansion and enhancement projects, and approximately $200m will come from funding by joint-venture partners, sponsorship agreements and other sources.

[Live Nation also announced](https://www.thestadiumbusiness.com/2026/07/31/live-nation-backed-sao-paulo-arena-aims-to-be-south-america-market-leader/) it had linked up with real estate developer, Cidade Center Norte, to develop what is proposed to be Brazil’s first purpose-built, world-class arena.

It would become the largest indoor venue for live entrainment in South America.
