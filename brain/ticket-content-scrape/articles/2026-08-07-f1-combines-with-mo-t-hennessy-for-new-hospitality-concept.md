# F1 combines with Moët Hennessy for new hospitality concept

## Summary

Moët Hennessy and Formula 1 launched "The Out Lap," a fine-dining hospitality concept for 12 guests that pairs a chef-curated tasting menu with a race-history narrative told by a former driver, debuting at the Belgian Grand Prix and continuing at the Dutch, Italian and Spanish Grands Prix.

### Key Points
- Concept accommodates 12 guests per session; combines a "travelling dining room" experience with storytelling from a former racing driver.
- Confirmed dates: Dutch GP (Aug. 23, Zandvoort's final year on the calendar), then Italian GP (Monza) and Spanish GP (Madrid).
- Each edition's menu and route are unique to the host circuit.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**Moët Hennessy and Formula 1 have launched The Out Lap, a new hospitality concept that focuses on fine dining.**

Having initially launched at the Belgian Grand Prix, The Out Lap is set to feature at the [final Dutch Grand Prix](https://www.thestadiumbusiness.com/2024/12/04/circuit-zandvoort-set-to-make-f1-farewell/) on August 23, which is due to leave the F1 calendar after this year.

Accommodating 12 guests, the experience takes guests on an immersive journey aboard a travelling dining room that stops at the circuit’s most iconic locations.

“At every stop, the story of the track comes alive through the memories of a former racing driver, whose personal perspective offers a unique window into the defining moments of the sport,” read a Moët Hennessy statement.

“Each chapter is interpreted through an exclusive culinary creation imagined by one of the world’s leading chefs and paired with exceptional wines and spirits from the Moët Hennessy portfolio, creating an experience where gastronomy and racing history become inseparable.”

Following the Dutch Grand Prix at Circuit Zandvoort, The Out Lap is set to travel to the Italian and Spanish grands prix in Monza and Madrid, respectively.

“Every edition of The Out Lap is unique. Each circuit inspires its own route, its own stories and its own culinary identity, ensuring that no two experiences are ever the same,” the statement continued.
