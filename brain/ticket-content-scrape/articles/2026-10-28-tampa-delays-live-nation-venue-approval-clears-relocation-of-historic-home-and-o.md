# Tampa Delays Live Nation Venue Approval, Clears Relocation of Historic Home and Oak

## Summary

Tampa's Barrio Latino Commission postponed approval of Live Nation's proposed 4,300-capacity Ybor City concert venue until March 2027, while separately approving relocation of a historic house and protected oak tree on the site. The delay extends months of scrutiny over the Gasworx-district project, amid continued opposition from independent-venue advocates who tie the dispute to Live Nation's broader market power — invoking April's federal antitrust jury verdict against Live Nation/Ticketmaster and the recent closure of Ybor's independent Crowbar club.

### Highlights
- Venue construction vote delayed to March 23, 2027; only the historic-structure/tree relocation was approved.
- Former independent-venue owner Tom DeGeorge (Crowbar, closed July 2026) opposed the relocation, citing added traffic/parking impact from the planned 4,300-capacity hall.
- Opposition explicitly links the local fight to the April federal jury verdict finding Live Nation/Ticketmaster liable for anticompetitive conduct.
- Original April 2026 announcement projected a late-2028 opening; no new opening target has been set after the delay.

### Industry Problem
Independent-venue operators argue that a dominant promoter/ticketer building additional company-operated rooms compounds competitive pressure on independent venues already struggling with rising costs — a local instance of the market-concentration concerns central to the Live Nation/Ticketmaster antitrust litigation.

### Proposed Solution
No product fix is proposed in the article; for a primary ticketing platform the relevant posture is to monitor how local approval fights and the federal antitrust remedy process affect incumbent venue expansion and whether they create competitive openings for independent promoters/venues in affected markets.

Tampa officials have postponed a decision on Live Nation’s proposed 4,300-capacity concert venue in Ybor City until next year, even as they approved relocating a historic home and a protected grand oak tree occupying the planned development site.

The split decision by the Barrio Latino Commission on Tuesday, Oct. 27, advances a significant piece of site preparation for developer Kettler without granting approval for the three-story concert building itself. The venue is planned for the Gasworx mixed-use development near Ybor City’s entertainment district.

According to [Creative Loafing Tampa’s account of the hearing](https://www.cltampa.com/news/ybor-city-live-nation-venue-vote-delayed-but-developers-are-cleared-to-relocate-casita-and-grand-oak-tree/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), a representative of the developers asked to put off the venue vote until March 23, 2027. That is a date on the city’s [published 2027 commission calendar](https://www.tampa.gov/sites/default/files/document/2026/2027-blc-schedule.pdf?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). The commission agreed to the postponement.

The city’s [October 27 hearing agenda](https://www.tampa.gov/sites/default/files/document/2026/blc-ph-agenda-october-27-2026-ada.pdf?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) identifies two separate applications under property owner KS Gateway Property Owner LLC. One, BLC-26-0000115, concerns moving a contributing historic structure at 1402 N. 15th St. The other, BLC-26-0000117, seeks a certificate of appropriateness for new commercial construction at that address and 1419 E. Fourth Ave.

## Relocations Advance After Months of Scrutiny

The relocations have been central to the dispute since the spring. In May, commissioners resisted a proposal that would have involved removing the mature oak, with the commission chair questioning whether other options had been exhausted. By August, Kettler was presenting plans to move both the tree and the house, but commissioners complained that key documentation was missing and continued the project’s review until October, [the Tampa Bay Times reported](https://www.tampabay.com/culture/entertainment/music/music-news/2026/08/26/ybor-city-live-nation-music-venue-proposed-plan-barrio-latino-commission-tampa/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

The new vote removes one preservation obstacle while leaving the design and construction application unresolved. It is not yet clear from the publicly posted agenda what conditions, if any, accompany the relocation approval or what other permits would be required before the house and tree could actually be moved.

TicketNews [reported the venue announcement in April](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/04/live-nation-to-operate-new-4300-capacity-venue-in-tampas-gasworx-district/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), when Kettler and Live Nation described a mid-sized room intended to attract touring acts that might otherwise skip Tampa. The companies projected a late-2028 opening and positioned the venue near the Centro Ybor parking garage and a planned streetcar stop. ## Independent Venue Opposition Remains

Opposition from neighborhood residents and independent music operators has continued through multiple hearings. Critics have questioned whether a large, corporate-operated concert hall fits the historic district and whether the expected crowds would create additional traffic, parking and pedestrian-safety problems.

Tom DeGeorge, the former owner of Ybor City’s Crowbar nightclub, spoke against the relocation request Tuesday. He urged commissioners to ensure moving the historic structure and tree would not introduce safety hazards, while challenging the proposal’s parking arrangements. DeGeorge characterized the plan as adding a 4,300-capacity venue with “zero additional parking spaces.” That is his assessment, rather than a parking determination independently established by the commission.

Crowbar [closed July 31 after 20 years](https://baynews9.com/fl/tampa/news/2026/07/31/ybor-city-s-last-independently-owned-music-venue--the-crowbar--hosting--goodbye--concert-tonight-as-staff-ready-to-close-its-doors?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), amid rising operating costs and pressures DeGeorge has described as increasingly difficult for small, independently run music rooms. Its closure preceded construction of the proposed Live Nation venue; it should not be attributed solely to that project.

DeGeorge has connected his local concerns to the national debate over concert promotion and ticketing. At a congressional forum in May, he [described the difficulty independent venues face when competing for tours](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/lawmakers-witnesses-dissect-corrupt-live-nation-settlement-press-for-breakup-after-ticketmaster-verdict/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) against a company with major positions in promotion, ticketing and venue operations. He warned that the impact accumulates “slowly, show by show, year by year.”

[TicketNews previously covered a petition](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/05/ybor-city-residents-create-petition-ln-new-proposal/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) opposing the project, which gathered support from residents worried about the neighborhood’s independent music identity. Live Nation, for its part, has maintained that the venue would complement existing clubs by bringing more concerts and visitors to the area, rather than replacing those businesses.

The local fight also unfolds after an April federal antitrust jury verdict against Live Nation and Ticketmaster on monopoly and tying claims. That legal proceeding and its disputed remedies are separate from the Barrio Latino Commission’s responsibility to assess whether development plans satisfy the historic district’s preservation and design requirements.

For now, Kettler has approval to pursue the contested relocations, but not to construct the venue. The proposed concert hall’s next stated review date is March 23, 2027, leaving the larger project subject to another commission decision.
