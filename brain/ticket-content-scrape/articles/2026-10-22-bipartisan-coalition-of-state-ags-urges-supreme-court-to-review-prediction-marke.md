# Bipartisan Coalition of State AGs Urges Supreme Court to Review Prediction-Market Dispute

## Summary

A bipartisan coalition of 39 state attorneys general plus D.C. filed an amicus brief urging the US Supreme Court to review a Third Circuit ruling on whether prediction-market operator Kalshi's sports-event contracts are exempt from state gambling law. This is a sports-prediction-market federalism dispute with no direct ticketing nexus.

### Key Points
- Brief filed Oct. 7 in *Flaherty v. KalshiEX LLC*, backing New Jersey's petition for Supreme Court review.
- Central question: whether CFTC-regulated sports-event contracts count as "swaps" under the Commodity Exchange Act, preempting state gambling law.
- Coalition frames the dispute as a "national turf war" dividing federal appellate courts and pitting states against federal regulators.
- No ticketing company or ticketing regulation is implicated; included here only because it came from a regulatory-alert feed also used for ticketing news.

A bipartisan coalition of 39 state attorneys general (AGs), joined by the District of Columbia, has asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review a closely watched dispute over whether federally regulated prediction markets may offer sports-event contracts without complying with state gambling laws.

In an amicus brief filed October 7 in *Flaherty v. KalshiEX LLC*, Ohio and the other participating jurisdictions, support New Jersey’s petition for review of a Third Circuit decision favoring prediction-market operator Kalshi. The coalition argues that the decision threatens states’ traditional authority to regulate gambling and conflicts with rulings from other federal appellate courts. The brief characterizes the growing litigation as a “national turf war” that has divided courts and pitted states against the federal government.

At the center of the case is whether sports-event contracts offered on a Commodity Futures Trading Commission-regulated exchange qualify as “swaps” subject to exclusive federal oversight under the Commodity Exchange Act. The AGs contend that Congress did not clearly displace the states’ longstanding police powers over gambling merely by amending federal derivatives law.

The breadth of the coalition gives the filing added significance. Rather than presenting the dispute as a narrow disagreement over statutory terminology, the AGs frame it as a consequential federalism question affecting states’ ability to enforce their licensing, consumer-protection, and public-safety laws. The coalition also emphasizes that conflicting decisions are producing different regulatory outcomes across the U.S.

The brief does not guarantee that the Supreme Court will hear the case. But the combination of a developing circuit split, coordinated bipartisan concern from nearly 40 states, and an asserted intrusion on traditional state authority strengthens New Jersey’s argument that the dispute warrants prompt national resolution. Kalshi’s response to the petition is currently due November 9.
