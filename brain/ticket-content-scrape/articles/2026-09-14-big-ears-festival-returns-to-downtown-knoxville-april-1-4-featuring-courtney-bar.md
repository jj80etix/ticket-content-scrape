# Big Ears Festival Returns To Downtown Knoxville April 1-4, Featuring Courtney Barnett, Daniel Lanois,  Norah Jones, Jeff Tweedy, Robert Glasper, Kronos Quartet, Many Others

## Summary

Knoxville's genre-spanning Big Ears Festival returns April 1-4 for its 14th year, with more than 250 performances across the city's historic downtown venues, featuring Courtney Barnett, Norah Jones, Jeff Tweedy and Kronos Quartet.

### Key Points
- Program includes a Bang On A Can All-Stars series (works by Philip Glass, Steve Reich, Ryuichi Sakamoto).
- Venues span historic theaters, churches, rock clubs, museums and converted warehouses across walkable downtown Knoxville.

In its 14th year, the eclectic, boundary-pushing Big Ears festival once again delivers a unique lineup and setting April 1-4 in downtown Knoxville, with a varied lineup and multiple locations somehow all coming together for the annual event.

Promising more than 250 concerts and performances, plus films, artist conversations, workshops, exhibitions and interactive experiences, Big Ears will present something for just about everyone in multiple settings, including historic theaters, churches, rock clubs, museums, event spaces, converted warehouses, galleries and still other surprises “within the very walkable footprint of Knoxville’s historic downtown,” according to the announcement.

Specific music highlights on the bill include The Bang On A Can All-Stars presenting six concerts, including Philip Glass’ Glassworks, Steve Reich’s masterpiece Music for 18 Musicians, and Ryuichi Sakamoto’s 1996. The Big Star Quintet – featuring Jon Auer, Mike Mills, Pat Sansone, Chris Stamey, and Jody Stephens – will the spirit of the legendary Memphis band to life. Evan Ziporyn and Maya Beiser are set to perform a reimagining of David Bowie’s final work, *Blackstar*, with the Knoxville Symphony Orchestra. Also for 2027 and building on its eight-hour-long Big Ears debut last year, Ragamala (A Garland of Ragas) will expand to 12 hours this year, a sequence of concerts featuring legends and masters of Indian classical traditions, according to the announcement.

Some of the more contemporary talent on the bill includes jazz favorites like Brad Mehldau and Bill Frisell to David Murray, Robert Glasper and Chucho Valdés; singer/songwriters Norah Jones, Jeff Tweedy, Natalie Merchant, Nathaniel Rateliff, Rosanne Cash, Gillian Welch and David Rawlings; influential electronic artists like Oneohtrix Point Never, Mouse on Mars, Tyondai Braxton, and Tristan Perich; acclaimed indie artists like Wednesday, Weyes Blood, Kevin Morby, and U.S. Girls; international legends like Gustavo Santaolalla, Yasuaki Shimizu, and Midori Takada; and newer voices like Yilian Canizares, Fabiano do Nascimento, Tim Bernardes, and El Khat; and sonic explorers like Kali Malone, Pamela Z, and Valentina Magaletti.

Started by AC Entertainment, Big Ears in 2016 incorporated as a nonprofit organization and has been independently produced since 2022 after the sale of AC Entertainment to Live Nation. AC Entertainment founder Ashley Capps remains executive & artistic director for Big Ears in his hometown of Knoxville.
