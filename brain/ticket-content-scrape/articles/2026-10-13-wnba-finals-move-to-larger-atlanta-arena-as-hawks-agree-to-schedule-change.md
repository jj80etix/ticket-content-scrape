# WNBA Finals Move to Larger Atlanta Arena as Hawks Agree to Schedule Change

## Summary

The WNBA moved Games 3 and 4 of the 2026 Finals to the larger State Farm Arena after the NBA's Atlanta Hawks agreed to shift their own home-opener start time, resolving a scheduling conflict that would have forced the Dream into a much smaller venue. The switch raises arena capacity from roughly 3,500 to 17,000, opening more than 13,000 additional seats per game.

### Key Points
- WNBA Finals Games 3-4 move from Gateway Center Arena (3,500 capacity) to State Farm Arena (17,000).
- Change required the Atlanta Hawks to shift their 2026-27 NBA season home opener.
- Adds over 13,000 potential additional attendees per game.
- Announced by the WNBA on Tuesday, October 13.

State Farm Arena

The Atlanta Dream will host Games 3 and 4 of the 2026 WNBA Finals at State Farm Arena after the WNBA adjusted its championship schedule and the NBA’s Atlanta Hawks agreed to move the start time of their home opener, clearing the way for thousands more fans to attend the games.

The [WNBA announced the agreement Tuesday, October 13](https://www.wnba.com/news/wnba-finals-games3-and-4-to-be-played-at-state-farm-arena?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), resolving scheduling conflicts that threatened to force the Dream to play their championship games against the Golden State Valkyries at the much smaller Gateway Center Arena in College Park.

State Farm Arena, home of the Hawks, accommodates approximately 17,000 basketball fans, compared with just 3,500 at Gateway Center Arena. The change increases potential attendance by nearly five times, creating an opportunity for more than 13,000 additional spectators at each game.

The arrangement required changes to two WNBA Finals games and the Hawks’ first home game of the 2026-27 NBA season.

## Growing Demand Creates Venue Challenges

The scheduling agreement comes as the Dream prepare to move permanently into State Farm Arena beginning with the 2027 season.

The team [announced a multiyear agreement with the venue on October 6](https://dream.wnba.com/news/atlanta-dream-announces-multi-year-agreement-to-play-all-home-games-at-state-farm-arena?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), following several seasons of increasing attendance and ticket demand.

According to the franchise, season ticket memberships have increased more than 400% over the past five years. The Dream have sold out every game at Gateway Center Arena over the last three seasons and have also recorded eight sellouts at State Farm Arena, including several games drawing more than 17,000 fans.

Earlier this year, [TicketNews reported that the Dream had sold out their available 2026 full-season and half-season ticket memberships](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/04/wnbas-las-vegas-aces-and-atlanta-dream-sell-out-2026-season-tickets/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

The franchise also hosted five regular-season games and its first three home playoff games at State Farm Arena this season.

The uncertainty surrounding the Finals highlighted a logistical challenge accompanying the WNBA’s growing popularity. Although larger NBA arenas can accommodate significantly more spectators, their calendars are frequently committed months in advance to professional basketball games, concerts and other events.

The situation also reflects a broader trend across the league. In 2025, [TicketNews documented six WNBA teams moving games against the Indiana Fever to larger venues](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/03/six-wnba-teams-move-fever-games-to-larger-venues/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) to accommodate increased demand.

For Atlanta, the agreement allows the Dream to bring their championship games to the building that will become their permanent home next season, rather than limiting attendance to the smallest arena in the WNBA.

The larger venue also creates additional ticket inventory and potential revenue opportunities for the franchise, although actual attendance and ticket sales will depend on demand.

## Game 6 Venue Remains Unresolved

While the agreement addresses Games 3 and 4, questions remain about where Atlanta would host a potential Game 6.

That game is tentatively scheduled for Thursday, October 29, when the Hawks are scheduled to host the Cleveland Cavaliers at State Farm Arena.

The WNBA’s announcement did not address the potential Game 6 conflict or identify an alternative venue if the championship series extends beyond five games.

The best-of-seven Finals begin Saturday, October 17, at Chase Center in San Francisco, with the Valkyries hosting the opening two games.

Tickets for Atlanta’s Games 3 and 4 will go on sale to the general public Friday, October 16, at 10 a.m. ET through [the Atlanta Dream’s official website](https://dream.wnba.com/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

Game 3 will air on USA Network and CNBC and stream on Peacock, while Game 4 will air on NBC and stream on Peacock.
