# Live Nation Moves to Refinance $1.2 Billion in Pandemic-Era Debt

## Summary

Live Nation is refinancing $1.2 billion of pandemic-era debt, launching a private placement of $840 million in dollar notes and €500 million in euro notes, both due 2032, to redeem its existing 6.5% senior secured notes due 2027. The original debt was raised in May 2020 to keep the company liquid through the COVID-19 concert shutdown; this transaction would push that maturity out five years, though the new interest rate isn't yet set. Live Nation still carries $9.28 billion in total debt principal, including another $950 million of unrelated notes also due 2027.

### Key Points
- New notes: $840M (USD) + €500M, both maturing 2032, sold via private placement (Rule 144A / Regulation S).
- Proceeds primarily redeem the existing $1.2B, 6.5% senior secured notes due May 2027, raised during the 2020 COVID shutdown.
- Total Live Nation debt principal as of June 30: $9.28 billion; a separate $950M, 4.75% note tranche also matures in 2027 and is untouched by this deal.
- Follows an October 2025 $1.3B convertible-notes offering that already refinanced other near-term maturities.
- Final economics (new interest rate vs. the 6.5% being retired) not yet disclosed; deal still subject to pricing and market conditions.

Live Nation Entertainment is moving to refinance $1.2 billion in debt it raised during the depths of the pandemic, launching a new dollar- and euro-denominated notes offering that would push the maturity of that borrowing from 2027 to 2032.

The company said Tuesday it plans to privately place $840 million of senior notes due 2032 alongside €500 million of senior notes carrying the same maturity. Live Nation intends to use the proceeds primarily to redeem all of its outstanding 6.5% senior secured notes due 2027, according to its [announcement of the offering](https://news.livenationentertainment.com/news/live-nation-entertainment-announces-launch-of-private-senior-notes-offering?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content).

Remaining proceeds would cover transaction expenses and could be used for general corporate purposes, including repayment or repurchase of other Live Nation debt.

The transaction remains subject to pricing and market conditions. Live Nation has not yet disclosed the interest rates it expects to pay on the new 2032 notes, making it too early to determine how the refinancing will affect the company’s annual interest expense.

## Replacing Debt Raised During the Concert Shutdown

The debt targeted for redemption dates directly to the COVID-19 shutdown of the concert business.

In May 2020, as concerts around the world were canceled or postponed and Live Nation worked to preserve liquidity through an indefinite shutdown, the company raised $1.2 billion through 6.5% senior secured notes scheduled to mature May 15, 2027.

The financing had initially been announced at $800 million before Live Nation increased it to $1.2 billion, citing strong investor demand. [TicketNews reported at the time](https://www.ticketnews.com/2020/05/live-nation-ocesa-legal-steps/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) that the borrowing was part of a broader effort to give the promoter enough liquidity to continue operating even under an extended period without major concerts.

Live Nation CEO Michael Rapino described the financing at the time as providing an “extra cushion to withstand any scenario well into 2021” while leaving the company positioned to ramp its business back up when concerts returned.

Six years later, the proposed transaction would effectively extend that major pandemic-era maturity by another five years. The final economics will depend on how the new notes are priced relative to the 6.5% rate Live Nation has been paying on the existing debt.

## Live Nation Still Has Other 2027 Debt

The offering would remove one of Live Nation’s largest near-term maturities, but it would not eliminate all of the company’s debt due in 2027.

Live Nation reported $9.28 billion in total debt principal as of June 30. That included the $1.2 billion of 6.5% senior secured notes targeted by the new offering as well as another $950 million of 4.75% senior notes due in 2027.

The company’s quarterly filing classified the entire $1.2 billion pandemic-era issue as current because it matures in May 2027.

Live Nation has been actively reshaping its debt structure ahead of upcoming maturities. In October 2025, the company [launched a separate $1.3 billion convertible senior notes offering](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/10/live-nation-plans-billion-convertible-notes-offering/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) due 2031 as part of a refinancing that also involved new senior secured credit facilities. Proceeds from that transaction were slated to retire notes due in 2026 and repay existing term-loan and revolving-credit borrowings.

The new 2032 notes will be sold through a private placement rather than a public retail offering. Live Nation said the U.S. portion will be offered to qualified institutional buyers under Rule 144A, with notes also offered outside the United States under Regulation S.

Pricing of the new debt will provide the next important piece of the transaction: whether Live Nation is able to extend the maturity of its pandemic-era borrowing while also reducing the 6.5% interest rate attached to the notes it plans to retire.
