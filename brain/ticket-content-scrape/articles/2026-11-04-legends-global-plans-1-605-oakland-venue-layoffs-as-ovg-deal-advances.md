# Legends Global Plans 1,605 Oakland Venue Layoffs as OVG Deal Advances

## Summary

Legends Global filed WARN notices for 1,605 Oakland Arena/Coliseum employees as its venue-management contract expires Dec. 31 amid a pending sale that would transfer the arena to rival operator Oak View Group for at least $100 million, while Oakland and Alameda County retain the Coliseum stadium site. OVG and deal backers say they intend to rehire unionized staff, but reapplication is required and no clear plan exists for nonunion workers or the related 181 Levy concession-staff layoffs.

### Key Points
- 1,605 employees at Oakland Arena/Coliseum face layoffs effective Dec. 31 as Legends' 2012 operating agreement expires.
- Pending sale: Oakland Acquisition Company (OAC) would keep the Coliseum stadium land; Oak View Group would acquire the Arena for $100M+.
- OAC/OVG say unionized workers will be offered rehire but must reapply; nonunion staff plans are unclear.
- A separate WARN filing covers 181 Levy concessions employees in a planned permanent closure, also effective Dec. 31.
- County-level litigation over an earlier A's-affiliated agreement could delay the sale closing past the Dec. 31 target.

### Highlights
- 1,605 Legends/AEG Management Oakland jobs affected; OVG to acquire Oakland Arena for $100M+ while OAC retains the Coliseum stadium.
- Oakland's prior sale-of-interest deal gives the city 6% of gross ticket sales at both venues going forward.

### Industry Problem
Venue-operator consolidation (OVG displacing Legends) creates workforce and continuity risk around venue transitions, and illustrates how M&A in venue management can disrupt ticketing/event operations mid-transition.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor — the OVG/Legends/OAC transition and its 6%-of-gross-ticket-sales revenue-share structure are a data point on how municipalities are now negotiating ticketing-revenue stakes into venue deals.

Legends Global plans to lay off 1,605 workers at Oakland Arena and the neighboring Oakland Coliseum as its management contract approaches expiration and a proposed sale would put the arena in the hands of rival venue operator Oak View Group.

The [California layoff listing](https://moneytrackerdaily.com/trackers/layoffs/california/company/aeg-management-oakland?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) identifies 1,605 affected employees under AEG Management Oakland LLC, the operating entity named in [Oakland Arena’s visitor information](https://www.theoaklandarena.com/oakland-a-z?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). The notice lists Dec. 31, 2026, as the effective date. The layoffs are planned, not job losses that have already taken effect.

Legends said it will continue operating both venues with its current employees through the end of the year. The [San Francisco Chronicle, which first reported the layoffs Nov. 3](https://www.sfchronicle.com/eastbay/article/oakland-coliseum-layoffs-22509681.php?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), said the company’s operating agreement dates to 2012 and has not been renewed. Its term runs through Dec. 31 or until the city or county no longer owns the complex, whichever occurs first.

The employment notices come as Oakland and Alameda County work to sell their interests in the 112-acre property to Oakland Acquisition Company (OAC), which is affiliated with the African American Sports and Entertainment Group. Under the proposed structure, OAC would retain the Coliseum stadium and surrounding land while selling Oakland Arena to Oak View Group for at least $100 million.

OAC spokesperson Ray Bobbitt told the Chronicle that Oak View Group had met with labor representatives and intended to rehire unionized workers affected by the transition. “They’ll transition from Legends to Oak View Group,” he said. OAC investor Susan Cronin said employees would have to reapply, but added that “the intention is to hire them back.”

Those assurances leave significant questions unresolved. They describe plans for unionized employees rather than a guarantee covering every worker, and rehiring would require a new application. Neither the number of available positions nor the arrangements for nonunion staff have been clearly established.

The split ownership structure creates an additional complication. Oakland Arena has [concerts and other events scheduled for 2027](https://www.theoaklandarena.com/upcoming-events?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), including Kenshi Yonezu in February and Calvin Harris in May. The Chronicle reported that the Coliseum stadium has no events on its 2027 calendar. With OVG slated to control the arena but OAC retaining the stadium, it remains unclear where all displaced employees would work. The Chronicle said OVG did not provide a clear explanation of its hiring plans.

A separate workforce notice suggests that the transition extends beyond the venue management company. [California WARN records for Levy](https://employeesfirstlaborlaw.com/california-layoffs/company/levy/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content) identify 181 employees at 7000 S. Coliseum Way in Oakland in a planned permanent closure effective Dec. 31. That filing is separate from the notice covering AEG Management Oakland’s 1,605 employees; the records do not establish how many Levy workers might be employed by a successor concessionaire.

Levy has operated Oakland Arena’s food and beverage concessions. In [April 2026 Coliseum Authority documents](https://www.theoaklandarena.com/assets/doc/Board-Package-4.17.26-48eee779c2.pdf?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), officials extended its contract through Dec. 31 at the latest and addressed responsibility for penalties if insufficient notice of early termination prevented Levy from meeting layoff-notification obligations. The provision does not establish that the newly reported closure will necessarily result in permanent unemployment for those workers.

The looming change in operators follows an unsuccessful attempt by Legends to take ownership of the arena itself. As [TicketNews reported in June](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/06/oakland-arena-sale-draws-potential-bidding-fight/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), Legends proposed a $102 million investment covering the arena purchase and renovations, setting up a contest with OVG. The OAC-OVG proposal ultimately advanced instead.

Oakland’s City Council subsequently approved the sale of the city’s interest for $125 million, including payments tied to future development, as [TicketNews reported in July](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/oakland-coliseum-deal-advances-plans-for-major-music-investment/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content). The agreement also gives Oakland a share equal to 6% of gross ticket sales at the arena and stadium. OVG has outlined plans for arena improvements intended to bolster Oakland’s position as a live-music destination.

The takeover is not yet complete. According to [city redevelopment documents](https://www.oaklandca.gov/Government/Departments/City-Administrator/Oakland-Coliseum-Redevelopment-Project?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), the county must address an earlier agreement involving an Oakland Athletics-affiliated entity before transferring its share of the property. The Chronicle reported that litigation has delayed that process. OAC hopes to close by Dec. 31, the same date on which Legends expects its operating role to end, leaving both the timing of the handover and the future of the affected workers unsettled.
