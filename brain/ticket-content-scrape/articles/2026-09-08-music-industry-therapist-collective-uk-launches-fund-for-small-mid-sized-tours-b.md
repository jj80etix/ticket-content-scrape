# Music Industry Therapist Collective UK Launches Fund For Small & Mid-Sized Tours, Backed By AEG, Live Nation, The Team & Others

## Summary

The Music Industry Therapist Collective (MITC) launched the Pay It Forward Fund, an industry-funded, free-at-source occupational-health program bringing mental-health support (confidential "Tour Check-Ins," welfare consultations, workshops) to small- and mid-scale touring teams that typically can't budget for it. $370,000 has been raised to support 50 tour teams annually over a three-year pilot, backed by AEG International, Live Nation, d&b audiotechnik, The Team, Lockton, and Carmela Wallace's Live Free 999 foundation, among others.

### Key Points
- Initial funding: $370,000, targeting 50 tour teams/year over a 3-year pilot.
- Founding funders include AEG International, Live Nation, d&b audiotechnik, Fly By Nite, The Team, Universal Pixels, Lockton, Radiohead's Philip Selway, and Live Free 999 (founded by Carmela Wallace, Juice WRLD's mother).
- Services: 24/7 multilingual confidential "Tour Check-Ins," a 90-minute occupational-health consultation, "Healthy Touring" workshops, and an audiobook resource.
- First application round is open for tours beginning Oct–Dec 2026, closing Sept. 30, 2026.
- Framed explicitly as closing a resource gap between big-budget tours (which already build in welfare support) and smaller, leaner touring teams.

The Music Industry Therapist Collective (MITC) has launched the Pay It Forward Fund, which it described as “an industry-funded, free at source, occupational health program for artists and crew on small and mid-scale tours.”

Touring professionals are at an elevated risk of stress, anxiety and burnout, as well as clinical depression and suicidality, and a growing number of larger tours now provide welfare or pastoral support as part of the production.

On smaller tours those pressures can be compounded by tighter budgets, leaner teams and more challenging work and travel conditions. However, these teams rarely have the budget to offer people the same resources or opportunities to talk, reflect and reset.

Pay It Forward is designed to address a practical gap in touring infrastructure by making specialist mental health support accessible to smaller teams. An initial $370,000 has been raised to support 50 tour teams annually over a three year pilot period.

Contributors to the fund include leading organizations from across the world of live entertainment, including AEG International, d&b audiotechnik, Fly By Nite, Live Nation, The Team and Universal Pixels, as well as Radiohead’s Philip Selway, insurance brokerage Lockton, and Live Free 999 – the charitable foundation set up by Carmela Wallace, mother of the late artist Juice WRLD.

MITC’s “Tour Health” packages include Tour Check-Ins, which are “confidential conversations with qualified mental health practitioners who understand the realities of music and touring,” and which are available online 24/7 in several languages, according to a press release from MITC announcing the launch of Pay It Forward.

It continues, “Tour Check-Ins can be used by any member of the touring team to decompress after a difficult day, think through a decision, talk about work or relationships, deal with tension in the team, reflect upon coping habits, or plan what comes next. No diagnosis, referral or previous experience of therapy is required.”

Also included in each package is a 90 minute occupational health consultation with MITC’s Touring Welfare Officer Sascha Heeney (who supported Dua Lipa’s Radical Optimism tour team); Healthy Touring workshops, resources to support performance, communication and collaboration; and access to MITC’s audiobook ‘Touring and Mental Health: the Music Industry Manual’ (featured in Rough Trade’s Books of the Year 2023).

Tamsin Embleton, founder of the Music Industry Therapist Collective.

Tamsin Embleton, founder of MITC, said: “People who tour are often highly skilled and resourceful. They’re used to managing lots of moving parts, adapting quickly and solving problems on the fly. Pressure itself isn’t necessarily the problem; touring is demanding by nature, and that’s part of what can make it rewarding. The difficulty comes when stress becomes chronic, without enough support to buffer its effects or enough opportunity to recover.

“The Pay It Forward Fund is about making specialist support accessible to tours working within tight budgets, but it’s also about moving beyond the idea that support should only be sought when crisis hits. We want to help forward-thinking teams develop the skills to manage pressure, recognise when something is beginning to take a toll, and intervene effectively before things start to snowball.”

Alongside providing funded access, the campaign’s wider aim is to establish occupational health on tour as part of normal professional practice: “Just as touring professionals maintain their trucks and buses, equipment and systems before they fail,” adds Laura Newton, MITC’s Operations Director, “The people who keep the show moving should benefit from the same foresight and planning. You wouldn’t send a rigger 50 ft into the air without a harness, and the same applies to health and wellbeing on tour. We believe that this should be the norm, rather than an exception or perk once tours reach a certain level.”

A first round of applications is now open to eligible tour teams anywhere in the world for tours that begin between October and December 2026, and closes Sept. 30. MITC’s leadership team will oversee funding allocation, with plans to carry out regular impact assessments to refine and develop the program in response to feedback.

Eligible artists, crew and tour teams can find out more and apply at https://www.musicindustrytherapists.com/payitforward/apply.

Organizations and individuals interested in funding future sessions can visit https://www.musicindustrytherapists.com/payitforward/support or contact info@musicindustrytherapists.com.

MITC is a collective of specialist music industry therapists based across the UK, US, Europe and India. Over the past eight years, it has supported artists and touring teams including Radiohead, Dua Lipa, The Last Dinner Party, Joji and The Script, as well as AEG International, Warner Music Group, Kobalt and Believe.

Comments from Pay It Forward’s Founding Funders:

“On Radiohead’s European tour last autumn, we had a support network in place, offering confidential access to the therapists of MITC. Even though we’re a cohesive, well-resourced and very experienced touring party, we knew that you can still never completely guard against the trip hazards of touring. Knowing that this support was there was hugely reassuring.
