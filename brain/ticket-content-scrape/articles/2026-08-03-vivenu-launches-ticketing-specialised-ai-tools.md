# Vivenu launches ticketing-specialised AI tools

## Summary

Vivenu, an event-ticketing platform for organisers, launched a suite of AI-powered features including a new "Vivenu AI Assistant" that answers natural-language questions about ticket sales, revenue, and customer behavior directly from live data. The assistant can also build customer segments and draft campaign copy for organiser review, and the platform now exposes a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server so customers can connect their own external AI tools to their first-party ticketing data. Stanford Athletics is cited as an early production adopter.

### Key Points
- Vivenu AI Assistant answers natural-language questions about ticket sales, revenue, and customer behavior from live data.
- Platform is API-first and exposes an MCP (Model Context Protocol) server, letting customers wire external AI tools directly to their ticketing data.
- AI Assistant is trained on Vivenu's proprietary knowledge base to answer product questions and surface feature recommendations.
- Can auto-build customer segments and draft campaign copy/subject lines, handed back to organisers for review and approval.
- Stanford Athletics (Spenser Ayres, associate athletics director) named as an early adopter, citing faster data-to-decision turnaround.
- CEO Simon Hennes frames the tools as removing manual execution work so organisers can focus on fan relationships.

### Highlights
- MCP server exposure is a concrete, shippable AI-integration pattern, not just a chatbot bolt-on.
- Natural-language analytics plus auto-drafted campaigns are live features, not roadmap talk.
- Stanford Athletics named as a production reference customer.

### Industry Problem
Organisers spend significant manual effort turning raw ticketing and sales data into decisions and building multi-step marketing campaigns; slow, technical reporting workflows delay revenue-generating action.

### Proposed Solution
Vivenu addresses this with an embedded, natural-language AI assistant plus an open MCP server for external AI integration. Etix Assistant should benchmark against this pattern — conversational analytics, campaign drafting, and open API/MCP access — as an emerging baseline for AI-in-ticketing-ops among peer platforms.

TheTicketingBusiness News

**Vivenu has launched a suite of AI-powered features, including the new Vivenu AI assistant.**

The new capabilities give event organisers the ability to answer complex data insight questions, create and analyse multi-faceted, time-consuming reports, and build and execute campaigns to drive better fan experiences and greater revenue growth.

Vivenu’s platform was built for first-party data ownership, giving full control to organisers. With these new capabilities, Vivenu said clients can quickly and effectively perform real-time data analysis to discover insights, create new customer segments, and build and automate personalised campaigns, send logic, and reports.

With Stanford Athletics an early adopter of the new AI tools, Spenser Ayres, associate athletics director, ticket sales and operations, said: “With the Vivenu AI Assistant, I can ask questions in plain language about ticket sales, revenue, and customer behaviour, and get answers pulled directly from our live data in seconds. It has changed how quickly I can turn data into decisions.”

**API-first**

Vivenu built is AI stack to be API-first, enabling every capability to run on open APIs that integrate directly into organisers’ existing workflows. Users can also connect their existing AI tools directly to their ticketing data using Vivenu’s Model Context Protocol (MCP) server.

Because of Vivenu’s first-party data ownership model, customers have instant access to their own ticketing intelligence meaning they are able to act quickly and with confidence.

Vivenu’s AI Assistant has been trained on the company’s proprietary ticketing knowledge base, meaning it has the ability to answer product questions, guide feature use, and surface recommendations from the organiser’s own data.

With analytics-focussed AI, organisers can query sales and customer data in natural language to discover insights that manual analysis may not find.

Organisers can also describe a campaign to the Vivenu AI Assistant, which then builds the customer segment, drafts the copy and subject lines, before handing everything back for review and approval. Vivenu AI Assistant sits within the larger Vivenu platform and can access live data, using the same enterprise-grade security standards.

“Today’s headlines are full of AI stories about technology replacing human contact. With our new AI capabilities, we’re using that same technology to do the opposite: make live entertainment, one of the last truly social experiences left, even more powerful. That’s the paradox at the heart of what we’ve built,” said Simon Hennes, co-founder and chief executive of Vivenu.

“Our new AI capabilities take the execution work off organisers’ plates so they can focus on what only humans can do: authentically understand what actually motivates a fan.”
