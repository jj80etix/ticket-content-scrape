# Ibiza officials mulling island-wide ban on future club and venue licenses

## Summary

Ibiza's island council (Consell d'Eivissa) is drafting a Tourism Areas Intervention Plan (PIAT), expected to take effect in 2027, that would formalize an island-wide freeze on new club and venue licenses. The freeze already operates informally in many districts; the new plan would extend it island-wide, with a carve-out for the Sant Josep area (home to Platja d'en Bossa). Existing licensed venues would be unaffected, but no new nightlife venues would be permitted, a move officials frame as containing an already oversaturated nightlife economy amid rising enforcement against illegal/unlicensed parties.

### Key Points
- Proposed PIAT plan would ban new club/venue licenses island-wide, formalizing a freeze already in effect in most districts; Sant Josep (Platja d'en Bossa) is the notable exception.
- Plan is expected for approval in the coming weeks, ahead of the current tourism-regulation framework expiring at the end of 2026; the new plan takes effect in 2027.
- Existing licensed venues are unaffected — officials explicitly defend current clubs as economically and culturally valuable, generating jobs and differentiation for the island.
- Measure follows growing enforcement against unauthorized/illegal parties, including a recent police shutdown of an unlicensed event near Sant Miquel.
- Backed by the PSOE party locally, whose spokesperson describes the current nightlife-driven tourism model as "spiralled out of control" and incompatible with family-friendly tourism.
- Plan also bundles unrelated tourism-management measures, such as expanding designated smokeless beaches.

### Highlights
- A blanket, island-wide freeze on new venue/club licenses is a hard supply-side cap: total addressable nightlife-venue inventory in a major European live-event market is fixed by regulation, not demand.
- Sant Josep/Platja d'en Bossa is carved out as the one area still able to add venues, meaning any future venue (and associated ticketing) growth on the island concentrates there.
- Regulatory tightening is explicitly tied to enforcement crackdowns on unlicensed/illegal events, signaling the same authorities are also squeezing the informal/unticketed side of the market.

### Industry Problem
For ticketing and event-management companies operating in saturated destination-nightlife markets, regulatory license caps put a hard ceiling on organic growth from new venues — peers serving Ibiza-style markets can no longer count on expanding client rosters via new licensed rooms, and the addressable market effectively becomes a fixed pool of existing operators.

### Proposed Solution
The article describes a regulatory response (the license freeze), not a vendor solution, so the posture here is watch-and-adapt: Etix and peers should monitor whether similar venue-cap/anti-oversaturation rules spread to other saturated destination markets (Balearics-wide, other Mediterranean nightlife hubs), since that would shift competitive focus for ticketing vendors from winning new venues toward deepening wallet share and retention within an unchanging pool of existing licensed operators, and would raise the stakes of any consolidation among that fixed set of venues.

Ibizan authorities have proposed plans for an island-wide ban on new venue licenses as part of a new Tourism Areas Intervention Plan (PIAT).

The plan is set for approval in the coming weeks as the existing framework for the island’s tourism regulations expires at the end of 2026. The ban would formalise a policy of freezing venue and club licenses, which is already effectively in place in many of the nightlife destination’s districts, across the whole island, with a notable exception being Sant Josep, which is home to nightlife resort Platja d’en Bossa.

This comes after the first vice president of the island’s Consell d’Eivissa, Mariano Juan, tabled the measure in a meeting of the legislative body in late June.

“The proposal aims to contain existing nightlife. We believe there is already enough nightlife in certain areas and no more licenses should be granted,” Juan said to the Consell, via *Periodico de Ibiza.*

> “We believe there is already enough nightlife in certain areas and no more licenses should be granted”

Juan said the proposed ban would not affect existing club licenses, saying that these venues “ position us, differentiate us, complement our existing offerings, and provide thousands of jobs”**.**

“There’s nothing shameful about them; they’re not a problem. They’re part of Ibiza, and we should be proud of them,” he added, according to *Periodico.*

Other proposals tabled by the council in the plan include expanding the number of designated smokeless beaches to Figueretes and Bossa Beach, which members say aims to “protect health, the environment and offer more healthy spaces”.

The momentum behind the proposal follows mounting tensions over illegal parties on the island, with police having shut down unauthorised event at a villa near Sant Miquel this week in the latest enforcement action against the phenomenon.

The proposal has received support from the socialist PSOE party, whose spokesperson in the Consell, Elena López, said Ibiza “has become one enormous party venue; this model has gotten out of hand, and there’s no room for families in it”.

Voicing support for the measure, López said: “Stop this tourism economy model that has spiralled out of control, listen to the public, and take on that control and leadership when it comes to being tough with vendors — tough enough not to allow a single new leisure venue, not one more nightclub on our island, and there’s nothing wrong with saying that out loud, and tough enough to properly control all the venues currently operating so they follow the rules and don’t affect our residents.”
