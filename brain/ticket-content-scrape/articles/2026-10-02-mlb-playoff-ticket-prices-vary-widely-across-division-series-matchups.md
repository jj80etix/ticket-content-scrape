# MLB Playoff Ticket Prices Vary Widely Across Division Series Matchups

## Summary

Ticket Club resale-market data shows wide price variance across the four 2026 MLB Division Series matchups as the postseason opens. White Sox-Guardians carries the round's highest median asking price, Yankees-Rays combines moderate pricing with the deepest active market, and Dodgers-Braves and Brewers-Padres anchor the low end.

### Key Points
- White Sox-Guardians: $581 median across the first three guaranteed games, with highs near $1,300; Chicago's Game 3 spikes from a $135 get-in to a $617 median — the round's highest.
- Yankees-Rays: the round's broadest/deepest market, $112 get-in, $308 median, highs above $1,200.
- Dodgers-Braves offers the round's lowest get-in price; Brewers-Padres sits at the low end on median pricing.
- Data covers the 12 already-guaranteed Division Series games, supplied by Ticket Club.

### Highlights
- White Sox-Guardians carries the postseason's highest median resale price ($581), spiking to $617 for Chicago's home Game 3 off just a $135 get-in.
- Yankees-Rays is the deepest active market of the round: $112 get-in, $308 median, highs above $1,200.
- Dodgers-Braves and Brewers-Padres anchor the low end of current postseason resale pricing.

### Industry Problem
Secondary-market pricing for high-demand postseason games is highly volatile and matchup-dependent, swinging several-fold within a single round based on team performance, rivalry narratives, and market thinness — a reminder of how unpredictable demand signals complicate pricing and inventory strategy for resale and primary platforms alike.

### Proposed Solution
The article itself is market reporting with no platform response proposed. For an independent primary platform like Etix, the takeaway is to monitor these swings as a signal of where dynamic-pricing and demand-forecasting tooling matter most for high-demand onsales, rather than an immediate action item.

## Yankees-Rays Pairs a Deep Ticket Market With an AL East Rivalry

The series also matches the two best regular-season teams in the American League. ## Dodgers-Braves Has the Lowest Division Series Get-In

The cheapest entry point among the guaranteed Division Series games belongs to Dodgers-Braves.

Game 1 at Dodger Stadium carries a $70 get-in, while Game 2 begins at $89.

Those numbers, however, tell only part of the story. Game 1’s median stands at $356, with high-end asking prices around $647. Game 2 carries an even higher $399 median and high-end pricing around $653.

That makes Los Angeles one of the clearest examples of why a playoff get-in price does not necessarily describe the broader ticket market. A limited number of lower-priced seats creates an attractive entry point, but typical available asking prices are substantially higher.

The baseball matchup is just as interesting. Atlanta went 5-1 against Los Angeles during the regular season and outscored the Dodgers 26-15, making the Braves one of the few clubs able to claim sustained success against the reigning back-to-back champions.

The teams also have substantial October history. This will be their sixth postseason meeting. Atlanta beat Los Angeles in the 2021 NLCS before winning the World Series, while the Dodgers won their 2020 NLCS meeting on the way to a championship. Los Angeles holds a 3-2 edge across their five previous postseason series.

The Dodgers enter with the rest advantage and a formidable rotation. Yoshinobu Yamamoto is the likely Game 1 starter, with Tarik Skubal, Blake Snell and Tyler Glasnow also giving Los Angeles multiple high-end options. Atlanta, meanwhile, had to use its pitching to get through a three-game Wild Card Series against Philadelphia. Chris Sale will not be available until at least Game 2, while Tyler Mahle cannot return before Game 3.

Los Angeles also has some health questions in its star-studded lineup. Shohei Ohtani recently returned from right biceps and left knee injuries, while Freddie Freeman has dealt with right knee tendinitis. Mookie Betts enters October in better form after producing a 1.112 OPS in September.

The ticket market changes considerably when the series moves east. Game 3 at Truist Park has a $106 get-in but only a $169 median, with high-end pricing around $244 — substantially below the first two games at Dodger Stadium.

For Los Angeles, the postseason also carries a larger historical pursuit. The Dodgers are attempting to become the first MLB team to win three consecutive World Series since the Yankees from 1998 through 2000, and the first National League club ever to complete a World Series three-peat.

## Brewers-Padres Has the Lowest Series Median

Brewers-Padres currently presents the lowest overall median asking price among the four Division Series matchups.

The series begins with a $97 get-in and carries a $288 median. High-end asking prices reach roughly $442, with premium listings stretching higher.

The first two games at American Family Field are particularly affordable relative to much of the Division Series. Game 1 has a $97 get-in, $233 median and high-end price around $375. Game 2 starts at $99 with a $257 median and high-end asking prices around $386.

The matchup itself pits baseball’s best regular-season team against one of its hottest clubs entering October.

Milwaukee won a franchise-record 103 games, claimed MLB’s No. 1 overall postseason seed and became the first Brewers team to win four consecutive NL Central titles. The Brewers also went 18-6 during September.

San Diego matched that 18-6 September record, then swept the Cubs in the Wild Card Series. The Padres have already shown they can cause Milwaukee trouble: when the Brewers visited Petco Park in August, San Diego swept all three games and held Milwaukee to 3-for-28 with runners in scoring position.

Milwaukee will hand the ball in Game 1 to one of the season’s breakout stars. Jacob Misiorowski finished 16-5 with a 1.80 ERA and MLB-best 252 strikeouts, while opponents hit only.157 against him. San Diego had not announced its Game 1 starter as of Friday, with Walker Buehler and Robbie Ray among the leading possibilities.

The matchup also presents a contrast in offensive style. Milwaukee ranked second in MLB in runs scored despite being near the bottom of the league in home runs. The Brewers instead led the majors in batting average and on-base percentage while ranking fifth in stolen bases. San Diego counters with stars Fernando Tatis Jr. and Manny Machado and one of the strongest late-inning bullpens in the postseason, anchored by Mason Miller.

The ticket market becomes more expensive when the series moves to San Diego. Game 3 at Petco Park has a $141 get-in, $368 median and high-end asking prices around $572.

That difference makes Brewers-Padres one of the clearest examples of how much the host market can affect playoff pricing even within the same series.

## Potential Game 5 Prices Show No Universal Deciding-Game Premium

The conditional Game 5 markets also show that sellers are not applying a uniform premium to the possibility of a winner-take-all game.

A potential Dodgers-Braves Game 5 at Dodger Stadium carries a $150 get-in, $563 median and high-end pricing around $902, substantially above the first two Los Angeles games at the middle of the market.

A possible Brewers-Padres Game 5 in Milwaukee starts at $139 with a $405 median and high-end pricing around $599.

The American League markets look quite different. A potential Yankees-Rays Game 5 in Tampa currently carries a $126 get-in and $193 median, while a possible White Sox-Guardians finale in Cleveland starts at $102 with a $148 median.

That variation is another reason to treat conditional postseason listings differently from confirmed games. Their value can change rapidly once the actual series results determine whether the event will take place — and what would be at stake if it does.

## World Series Listings Already Occupy a Different Price Tier

Advance World Series listings move into an entirely different range.

Across the contingent World Series market in the supplied data, the median asking price stands at $3,551. High-end pricing reaches roughly $5,615 at the 75th percentile and climbs above $10,500 further up the market.

Those figures should be interpreted cautiously. The matchups are not yet known, and many advance World Series markets remain thin, meaning individual premium listings can have an outsized effect.

Even so, the listings illustrate how aggressively the market values the possibility of championship baseball.
