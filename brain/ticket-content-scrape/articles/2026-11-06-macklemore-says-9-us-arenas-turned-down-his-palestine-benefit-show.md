# Macklemore Says 9 US Arenas Turned Down His Palestine Benefit Show

## Summary

Macklemore says nine major U.S. arenas — including Madison Square Garden, Barclays Center, and Crypto.com Arena — declined to book his "Free Palestine" benefit tour after initially offering dates, following successful European stops in Dublin, Paris, and London. He says venues cited the shows' nature as a benefit for Palestine as the reason dates fell through; a Madison Square Garden Entertainment spokesperson offered a different account.

### Key Points
- Nine U.S. arenas declined: MSG, Prudential Center, Barclays Center, UBS Arena, Intuit Dome, Kia Forum, Crypto.com Arena, Chase Center, Climate Pledge Arena.
- Tour had successful stops in Dublin, Paris, and London before the U.S. push.
- Macklemore says available dates were pulled once venues learned the shows benefited Palestine.
- MSG Entertainment's spokesperson disputed the characterization in a statement to Pollstar, citing ticket-sales concerns as a possible alternate reason.

## Transcript

Macklemore claims that nine U.S. arenas refused to have him host his “Free Palestine” tour.

The American rapper posted a statement on Instagram informing fans that he was turned down by nine U.S. venues: New York’s Madison Square Garden, Prudential Center in Newark, Brooklyn’s Barclays Center, UBS Arena in Elmont, Intuit Dome and Kia Forum in Inglewood, Los Angeles’ Crypto.com Arena, San Francisco’s Chase Center and Climate Pledge Arena in Macklemore’s hometown of Seattle.

Following successful showings at Dublin’s 3Arena, Accor Arena in Paris and London’s OVO Arena Wembley, Macklemore was hoping to bring the benefit tour to the States.

“Thank you to everyone who shared in those nights of joy, love and resistance,” he wrote. “I left with a renewed sense of what’s possible when people come together for something bigger than themselves. From the beginning, our plan was to bring this tour home to the U.S. But after weeks of reaching out, nine major American arenas have turned us down.”

According to Macklemore, his team received available dates through the end of the year, but those dates were “suddenly off the table” when venues learned it was a benefit concert for Palestine.

“The doors were closed. The conversations were all different, but the result was the same,” he said in his statement. “The shows were not going to be allowed to happen in these venues. In over twenty years of touring, I’ve never experienced anything like this.”

Venue operators, however, may pass on a show simply because they believe it won’t sell enough tickets. In a statement sent to *Pollstar*, a Madison Square Garden Entertainment spokesperson responded to Macklemore’s accusation by saying, “We have some of the busiest venues in the world with extremely limited availability and he’s not an arena level act.”

*Pollstar* reached out to the other eight arenas for comment.

Macklemore performed in Europe and the U.K. after [controversially being removed as an opener for Ed Sheeran’s stadium tour](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/09/14/macklemore-dropped-from-ed-sheerans-stadium-tour-following-onstage-free-palestine-comments/) in September following remarks he made during a Sept. 4 show at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey.

He was removed from supporting duties after venue owners told promoter Messina Touring Group that they wouldn’t move forward with any shows if Macklemore remained on the bill.

[New England Patriots owner Robert Kraft confirmed he barred the Seattle rapper](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/09/15/ed-sheeran-responds-to-macklemores-exclusion-from-tour-aaron-rowe-drops-out-as-opener/) from performing at Gillette Stadium because he said his venue provides “a welcoming environment for all guests” and ensures that events don’t “provide a platform for hate speech.”

Macklemore donated his $1 million earnings from Ed Sheeran’s “Loop Tour” to six organizations supporting Palestinian relief efforts. He said he plans to put on more shows for Palestine.

“We’ll keep working with other artists and organizers, continuing where we left off in Europe. We’ll probably just take that shit outside where it belonged in the first place,” he wrote. “Our commitment to raising funds and standing in solidarity with the Palestinian people remains the same.

“There comes a point when the world changes, not because those in power give us permission, but because the people stop asking for it,” he added. “We will build our own stages. They may own the arenas, but they do not own the people who fill them. And they never will.”
