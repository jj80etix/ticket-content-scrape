# Maryland Sphere Faces County Financing Vote

## Summary

A proposed $1.4 billion, 6,000-seat Sphere venue in National Harbor, Maryland, faces a critical county financing vote this week, with the Prince George's County Council weighing $130 million in tax-increment bonds, a $40 million parking garage, and $13.5 million in state funding alongside $1.2 billion in private investment. Local residents and labor leaders have raised concerns about infrastructure impact and labor agreements.

### Key Points
- County weighing up to $215 million in special obligation bonds, repaid via captured tax revenue rather than the general fund.
- Bonds would also refinance pre-existing National Harbor debt.
- A county fiscal analysis flagged risks including revenue shortfalls and cost/schedule overruns.
- September public hearings raised concerns about infrastructure strain and local labor commitments.

## Transcript

The proposed 6,000-seat Sphere in National Harbor, Maryland — just outside Washington, D.C. — faces a critical financing vote this week.

The Prince George’s County Council will meet Tuesday to approve the county portion of the financing proposal. While $1.2 billion of the $1.4 billion price tag is being covered by private investment, the plan calls for $130 million in county tax increment financing bonds, a $40 million county-owned parking garage and $13.5 million in state funding.

During public hearings in September, concerns were raised by local residents, labor leaders and legislators about infrastructure and road improvements around the project and labor agreements that would provide jobs to locals.

Under the plan, the county will authorize up to $215 million in special obligation bonds to be repaid through captured tax revenue at National Harbor rather than through the county general fund; in addition to the Sphere costs, those bonds will refinance broader pre-existing debt at National Harbor. A county policy and fiscal analysis said risks to the continue include lower-than-projected revenues and cost and schedule overruns.
