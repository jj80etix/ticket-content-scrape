# House Oversight Democrat Expands Inquiry Into StubHub CEO’s Ticket Resale Fund

## Summary

House Oversight ranking Democrat Rep. Robert Garcia expanded his inquiry into StubHub CEO Eric Baker's ties to professional reseller Andro Capital, sending an Oct. 22 letter directly to Baker as Andro's managing director (rather than as StubHub CEO) seeking financial records, marketplace agreements and SEC registration details by Nov. 5. A newer StubHub SEC filing shows the company earned ~$600K in Q2 2026 fees from a related entity (Colloquy Capital) and that Colloquy held $10.8M in seller-proceeds security interests as of June 30, up from $7.9M at year-end 2025.

### Key Points
- Garcia's letter (eight question sets) seeks Baker's ownership stakes in Andro and Colloquy Capital, related-company lists, API/data-feed access terms, and SEC investment-adviser registration or exemption documentation.
- Letters are requests for information, not subpoenas; no finding of wrongdoing has been made, and this is a Democratic-committee-member inquiry, not a full committee action.
- StubHub's August SEC filing (Q2 2026) discloses a renewed Colloquy services agreement (since June 25, 2026) generating ~$600,000 in fees that quarter, plus a seller-financing program where Colloquy held $10.8M in security interests as of June 30 (vs. $7.9M at 2025 year-end).
- StubHub reported zero seller fees from Andro-sold tickets in H1 2025 or H1 2026, despite Garcia's letter citing an earlier $100K balance owed to Andro that the newer filing doesn't confirm.
- A related customer class action was moved to individual arbitration in September; StubHub previously called the Baker-Andro relationship "a small fraction" of total revenue.

### Highlights
- Congressional inquiry into StubHub CEO's financial ties to a professional reseller widens, now targeting the reseller entity directly, with an SEC-filing paper trail showing ~$600K/quarter in related-party fees — situational awareness, no action required yet (response deadline Nov. 5).

### Industry Problem
The inquiry probes whether a primary resale-platform executive's personal financial stake in a bulk-ticket reseller creates preferential marketplace access unavailable to other sellers — a structural transparency question for the secondary market that could inform how resale platforms disclose related-party seller relationships.

### Proposed Solution
No action needed from Etix; monitor for the Nov. 5 response and any resulting disclosure requirements, since stricter related-party transparency rules for resale marketplaces could eventually extend to primary-ticketing/resale integration disclosures generally.

Rep. Robert Garcia is expanding his congressional inquiry into StubHub CEO Eric Baker’s financial ties to professional ticket reseller Andro Capital, this time seeking detailed records directly from the resale operation about its ownership, financing arrangements and business dealings with StubHub.

Garcia, the ranking Democrat on the House Oversight and Government Reform Committee, sent a [new three-page letter to Baker on Thursday, Oct. 22](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/imo/media/doc/2026-10-22garciatoericbakerandrorestubhub.pdf?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), requesting written responses and supporting documents by Nov. 5.

Unlike Garcia’s initial inquiry in July, which addressed Baker in his capacity as StubHub CEO, the new letter is directed to Baker as managing director of Andro Capital Management. The change shifts the focus toward the reseller’s own financial arrangements and operations, including how it purchases tickets, finances inventory and conducts business through StubHub and potentially other secondary marketplaces.

The latest request follows [TicketNews’ July reporting on Garcia’s original investigation](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/stubhub-ceo-ticket-resale-fund-house-scrutiny/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), which raised questions about whether Baker’s overlapping business interests could provide Andro and its affiliates with advantages unavailable to other professional ticket sellers.

## Garcia Seeks Andro Financial Records, Marketplace Agreements

Garcia’s latest letter contains eight sets of questions and document requests, seeking a fuller picture of Andro’s operations and its commercial relationship with StubHub.

Among other information, Garcia is asking Baker to identify his ownership interests in Andro, Colloquy Capital and any other businesses with financial relationships to StubHub. He also wants a complete list of related companies and funds affiliated with Andro.

The requests extend to Andro’s bulk-ticket purchasing activities, financing arrangements, ticket sales and the financial terms governing its interactions with StubHub.

Garcia is seeking information about any specialized application programming interfaces, data feeds, memberships or fee structures available to Andro through StubHub or other ticketing marketplaces. He also requested communications and contracts among Andro, StubHub and Colloquy, along with Andro’s internet domains and SEC investment adviser registration documents or an explanation of any applicable exemption.

In his letter, Garcia argues that Baker’s overlapping positions raise concerns about possible preferential treatment and whether those relationships could disadvantage ticket buyers, artists or smaller sellers.

The inquiry also reflects Garcia’s broader criticism of professional ticket brokers, whom he argues can make popular events more expensive by purchasing tickets in bulk for resale.

However, the existence of professional resellers and bulk-ticket purchasing does not itself establish that consumers were overcharged or that a particular seller received improper marketplace advantages. The question of preferential treatment depends on the actual commercial arrangements and whether they differ materially from those available to comparable sellers.

## Newer SEC Filing Details Colloquy Agreement and Financial Relationships

Garcia’s October letter cites earlier StubHub securities disclosures, but a more recent quarterly filing provides additional details about the company’s continuing dealings with Andro and its affiliate, Colloquy Capital.

In its [August SEC filing covering the quarter ended June 30, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1337634/000162828026056402/stub-20260630.htm?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), StubHub disclosed that it entered into a new services agreement with Colloquy on June 25, 2026.

The arrangement has substantially similar terms to a previous services agreement that ended Nov. 1, 2025. Under the agreement, StubHub facilitates the sale and servicing of tickets owned by Colloquy in exchange for a percentage-based fee.

StubHub reported generating approximately $600,000 in fees associated with the Colloquy services agreement during the second quarter of 2026.

The filing also describes a separate seller-financing program established in July 2024, through which StubHub refers selected professional sellers to Colloquy for potential short-term financing based on their current or anticipated ticket-sale proceeds.

As of June 30, Colloquy held security interests in $10.8 million of seller proceeds under that program, compared with $7.9 million at the end of 2025. Those figures represent proceeds securing financing arrangements, not Colloquy’s profits or fees.

StubHub additionally reported that $3.5 million was due to Colloquy as of June 30, reflecting ticket-sale proceeds and disbursements under the financing program.

The filing also states that StubHub generated no seller fees from tickets sold by Andro during the first six months of either 2025 or 2026. The disclosures do not explain whether that result reflects transaction activity, negotiated commercial terms or other factors.

Although Garcia’s letter references a $100,000 outstanding balance owed to Andro in an earlier disclosure, the newer quarterly filing reported no proceeds due to Andro as of June 30.

These reported transactions establish ongoing business relationships among the companies, but they do not independently demonstrate marketplace manipulation, improper preferential treatment or inflated consumer ticket prices.

## StubHub Previously Defended Relationship

When Garcia first raised questions about the arrangements in July, StubHub told TicketNews that Baker’s investment in Andro had been publicly disclosed and that Andro operates as a separate company.

The company described Andro as one of numerous vendors it works with to provide services and said the relationship represents “only a small fraction of StubHub’s total revenue.”

The controversy has also been the subject of private litigation. A customer filed a proposed class action against StubHub and Baker in July, alleging that consumers were not adequately informed about the relationship between the marketplace and Andro.

As [TicketNews reported in September](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/09/stubhub-arbitration-ceo-ticket-fund-class-action/?utm_source=TicketNews&utm_medium=content), a federal judge granted StubHub’s request to move the customer’s claims into individual arbitration, staying the court case without deciding the underlying allegations. The ruling did not determine whether StubHub or Baker engaged in improper conduct.

TicketNews contacted StubHub on Thursday seeking comment on Garcia’s latest inquiry, including whether the company responded to the original July request, the terms of Andro’s seller-fee arrangements, the renewed Colloquy services agreement and the company’s oversight of related-party transactions. StubHub declined to comment on the new questions, instead referring TicketNews to the statement it provided following Garcia’s initial inquiry in July.

Garcia’s new letter does not indicate whether StubHub responded to his original July inquiry, which carried an Aug. 6 deadline. It also does not announce a hearing or formal enforcement action.

The next deadline is Nov. 5, when Garcia has asked Baker to provide Andro’s responses and supporting records. Whether those materials will become public, and whether they provide additional insight into the business relationships at issue, remains to be seen.
