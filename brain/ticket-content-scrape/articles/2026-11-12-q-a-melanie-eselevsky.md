# Q&A: Melanie Eselevsky

## Summary

In an interview with IQ, Move Concerts Argentina's Melanie Eselevsky describes a maturing Latin American touring market: three years of currency stability now let shows be booked two to three years out, international tours (including K-pop acts like BTS) are expanding into more markets per run, and average ticket prices have risen sharply since 2022. She also notes a cooling in post-pandemic buying urgency, with fans more deliberate and price-sensitive again, especially for mid-sized acts, amid a glut of show supply.

### Key Points
- Argentina has had three consecutive years of currency stability, enabling tours to be booked 2-3 years in advance.
- K-pop acts (e.g., BTS) now tour 5 Latin American markets per run, versus 1-2 previously.
- Average regional ticket prices have risen significantly since 2022, enabling bigger artist guarantees.
- Mid-sized acts are struggling and price sensitivity is rising again amid a glut of show supply ("too many tickets available").
- Ticket-purchasing timing is reverting to pre-pandemic patterns, with fans buying later and more selectively.

### Highlights
- Three years of currency stability in Argentina has shifted booking lead times to 2-3 years.
- Rising average ticket prices since 2022 are funding bigger, multi-market regional tours.
- Oversupply of shows is softening demand urgency and raising price sensitivity, particularly for mid-sized acts.

### Industry Problem
Promoters and organizers (Q1 clients) face a market where show supply has outpaced demand growth, making price sensitivity and purchase-timing unpredictability a recurring planning risk even as top-tier international demand stays strong.

### Proposed Solution
The interview doesn't propose a platform fix directly; for a primary ticketing platform this is a signal to track regional demand/price-elasticity by market and consider flexible pricing and earlier demand-signal tooling for organizer clients navigating oversupplied markets, watching for softening sell-through as the trigger to act.

## Transcript

## “Latin America is seeing a return towards pre-pandemic purchasing patterns with ambitious regional tours”

![Melanie Eselevsky](https://www.iqmagazine.com/wp-content/smush-webp/2026/11/Melanie-Eselevsky-931x582.png.webp)

Seven years on from being named one of IQ's New Bosses, Move Concerts' Eselevsky is never far from backstage

**In October 2019, *IQ* named Melanie Eselevsky one of its New Bosses. At 24, she was the youngest on that year’s list, splitting her days between law school at the University of Buenos Aires and a talent-booking desk at Move Concerts Argentina, where she had started two years earlier as a production assistant on a three-month probation.**

At the time, she described a market where the exchange rate could swing by the minute in the run-up to a presidential election. The picture has since changed. *IQ* ’s Global Promoters Report 2026 describes an Argentine live business that has entered a stage of maturity, with three consecutive years of currency stability and shows now being closed two or three years in advance. Here is how she views the market’s progression.

### After the post-pandemic recovery boom, how have Latin American markets evolved over the past year? Are you seeing a stabilisation, or is growth continuing?

It’s a combination of both growth and stabilisation. On one hand, we are seeing more concerts, more major international artists performing in secondary markets, and more domestic acts taking on larger shows.

Niche acts have also performed particularly well lately, very specific content targeting relatively small audiences, but at higher price points, with a clear understanding of the size and spending power of their local fan bases.

At the same time, mid-sized acts have been struggling, and price sensitivity is becoming an issue again.

With such a significant increase in the overall supply of concerts, it is still unclear whether the “blue dot fever” means demand has genuinely withdrawn, or simply that there are too many tickets available in the market.

### How is the way tours are planned across Latin America changing? Are tours increasingly incorporating more cities and markets outside the traditional circuits?

Yes, definitely. We are seeing tours expand beyond the traditional Latin American circuit, both in terms of the number of markets and the number of shows per market.

A good example is K-pop. This is the first time we have seen two major K-pop acts building tours that include multiple dates across more than one or two Latin American markets.

> “t he Latin American touring map is becoming more diverse”

BTS, for example, previously played Chile in 2017 and Brazil in 2019, but we are now seeing them plan multiple shows across five markets. Of course, the growth and consolidation of the fan bases is a major factor, but there is also an economic component. Average ticket prices across the region have increased significantly since 2022, which has made it possible to support guarantees at this level and, consequently, to build more ambitious regional tours.

There is still a lot of room for these markets to develop, particularly in terms of infrastructure and audience depth, but the fact that international artists are increasingly willing to perform there-and to trust us as their partners in those markets-is a very positive sign. I think the Latin American touring map is becoming more diverse, and artists are increasingly looking at the region as a collection of individual opportunities rather than simply a handful of traditional stops.

### What changes have you seen in audience behavior over the past year? Has the way people buy tickets, purchase timing, or interest in certain artists and genres shifted?

Purchasing timing seems to be moving back toward pre-pandemic patterns. While there are still blockbuster shows that sell out almost immediately, in general fans seem to be more cautious and deliberate with their purchases. The urgency we saw coming out of the pandemic, when people were eager to secure tickets to anything and everything, has clearly softened.

Part of that may be related to the sheer volume of shows now being announced. There is a certain anxiety around committing too early, knowing that something bigger—or simply something they might prefer—could be announced a few weeks later. With consumers having to make choices about where to spend their entertainment budget, they are becoming more selective.

> “ The bar has been raised, and the competition is becoming much more even”

### What were the main challenges for promoters during this period? Is there a challenge that has become particularly significant at the regional level?

Avails! And infrastructure. They are really two sides of the same coin.

On one hand, there are only a handful of proper arenas across the continent, and some of our main markets don’t even have one. Rosalía recently played Farmasi Arena in Rio and skipped São Paulo, which is a good example of how infrastructure—or the lack of it—can directly impact routing decisions.

On the other hand, stadium dates are always subject to the football calendar, which is rarely planned as far in advance as international touring cycles.

So we have this interesting situation where markets are expanding, domestic and international artists are willing to play them, and audiences are there, but we have a relatively small number of appropriate venues-and some of them are incredibly difficult to secure.

### How do major international tours currently coexist with the growth of local and regional artists? What role does Latin American talent play in programming today?

Domestic talent has grown tremendously since the pandemic, and that growth has not slowed down. But production standards have also increased significantly, so audiences have realised that local artists are not only worth paying to see, but can offer an experience that is just as valuable as that of many international acts.

The bar has been raised, and the competition is becoming much more even. In Argentina, for example, we have at least 15 artists who are capable of performing multiple stadium shows, including Lali, Airbag, Tan Biónica and Abel Pintos, among many others. This is no longer simply a competition for the audience’s wallet, it is also a competition for their time. There are more shows, more entertainment options and more artists demanding attention than ever before.

We are also seeing a stronger internationalisation of Latin American artists. Rawayana is a great example of an act that has grown enormously in recent years and is now selling out arenas across the continent and internationally. The global success of artists such as Shakira, Bad Bunny, and Karol G has also helped change the perception of audiences toward Latin American and Spanish-speaking talent more broadly. They have demonstrated that artists from the region can build truly global careers, which in turn raises the expectations, and the opportunities, for the next generation of talent.
