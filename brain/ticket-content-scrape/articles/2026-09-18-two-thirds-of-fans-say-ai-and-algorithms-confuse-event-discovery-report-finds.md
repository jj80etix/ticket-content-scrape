# Two thirds of fans say AI and algorithms confuse event discovery, report finds

## Summary

A new Tixel report, "The Future of Ticketing – The Live Equation 2030," finds 68% of ticket buyers say rising AI and algorithmic recommendations make it harder to know which event suggestions to trust, even as major ticketers increasingly build AI-driven discovery and personalization features. Tixel frames the finding as evidence that event discovery still runs largely through people rather than platforms.

### Key Points
- 68% of surveyed ticket purchasers say AI/algorithm-driven recommendations make event discovery harder to trust.
- Report published by ticketer Tixel as part of a 2030-outlook study.
- Comes as large ticketing platforms expand AI recommendation and personalization features.
- (Article paywalled after this point; full survey methodology and remaining stats not available in the fetched content.)

### Highlights
- 68% distrust figure is a direct patron-facing signal against unchecked AI-recommendation rollout in ticketing.
- Source (Tixel) is itself a ticketing platform positioning against algorithm-led discovery, a competitive framing worth tracking.

### Industry Problem
Patrons increasingly distrust AI/algorithmic event recommendations, creating a discovery-trust gap even as platforms invest in AI personalization — a direct pain point for how buyers find and choose events.

### Proposed Solution
The report implies platforms should keep human/social signals central to discovery rather than leading with opaque AI recommendations; Etix should watch for more data on what buyers *do* trust in AI-assisted discovery before investing further in algorithmic recommendation features, and consider transparency/explainability if such features are built.
