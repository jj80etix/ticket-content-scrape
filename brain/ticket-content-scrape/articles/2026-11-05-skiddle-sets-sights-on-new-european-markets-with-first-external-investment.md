# Skiddle sets sights on new European markets with first external investment

## Summary

UK independent ticketer Skiddle has taken its first outside investment in 25 years, a minority stake from Soho Square Capital, to fund expansion into new European markets and deepen its lifestyle-events vertical, while founders Ben Sebborn and Richard Dyer retain control. Skiddle processed £178 million in face-value sales across 180,000 events in 2025 and already operates in Ireland, Spain, and Malta.

### Key Points
- Soho Square Capital takes an undisclosed minority stake; founders retain control of the company.
- Growth targets: new European territories beyond existing Ireland/Spain/Malta presence, plus expansion into lifestyle events beyond music/festivals/nightlife.
- Skiddle did £178M in face-value sales across 180,000 events in 2025.
- Funds a 2025 partnership model (AIF membership funding/marketing support) that Skiddle has used to build independent-promoter relationships.

### Highlights
- First external capital in Skiddle's 25-year history; minority stake, founder control retained.
- 2025 scale: £178M face-value sales, 180,000 events.

### Industry Problem
Independent Tier-1-adjacent primary ticketers need capital to compete on international expansion and adjacent verticals (lifestyle events) without ceding control to larger consolidators.

### Proposed Solution
Monitor/Build: Skiddle's minority-stake model (growth capital without loss of control) is a template worth tracking as a competitive-financing signal in the independent primary-ticketing segment; watch for its lifestyle-events-vertical product moves as a Q12 competitive benchmark.

UK ticketing platform Skiddle has taken on its first external investment in its 25-year history, which it says will fund expansion across Europe and a deepening of its lifestyle events vertical.

The investment of an undisclosed amount comes from Soho Square Capital and will see the capita investment company take a minority stake in Skiddle. Founders Ben Sebborn and Richard Dyer will retain control of the company.

In the medium-term, the firm is targeting expansion into “new European territories”, saying it is aiming to build on existing market presence in Ireland, Spain, and Malta.

The investment will also support Skiddle’s expansion beyond its position in music, festivals, and nightlife, with lifestyle events representing an immediate area of focus, the company said.

Skiddle is one of the UK’s biggest independent ticketers by volume, having turned over £178 million in face value sales across 180,000 events in 2025.

Its recent work with the independent live music sector in the UK includes a 2025 partnership with the Association of Independent Festivals, through which Skiddle funds AIF memberships and provides marketing support for festivals.

> “Taking investment for the first time was a big decision”

“That growth has been built on close relationships with independent promoters, a deep understanding of the events industry and a team committed to helping organisers grow their events while connecting audiences with new experiences,” the company said.

Skiddle co-founders Ben Sebborn and Richard Dyer said: “Taking investment for the first time was a big decision. Finding a partner who understood Skiddle, our independence and the relationships we’ve built with promoters and fans was essential. Soho Square’s approach means we keep control of the business we’ve spent 25 years building, with the support to take it further.”

They added: “We started Skiddle because we wanted to help people find out what was on. Twenty-five years later, that’s still at theheart of what we do, but the opportunity ahead of us is bigger than ever.

“We want to help more people discover brilliant events, give more independent organisers the tools and support to grow, and take Skiddle into new markets. This investment gives us the backing to move faster across Europe, build our lifestyle offering and keep improving our technology.”
