# Marlins Man Ticket Dispute Adds to StubHub’s Mounting Consumer Scrutiny

## Summary

StubHub says it has resolved a high-profile public dispute with superstar sports fan Laurence "Marlins Man" Leavy, who publicly accused the marketplace of failing to honor its FanProtect Guarantee after receiving mismatched seats and initially being refused a refund or comparable replacement. The episode is the latest in a string of 2026 consumer-facing controversies for StubHub, including consolidated World Cup ticket-delivery class actions, a suit over CEO Eric Baker's ties to professional resellers, and an earlier $10M FTC settlement over deceptive fee disclosures.

### Key Points
- Leavy, a self-described $500K+/year StubHub spender, publicly accused the company of fraud after receiving different seats than ordered and being denied a refund, replacement, or comparable seats.
- StubHub confirmed a private resolution directly with Leavy but did not disclose terms.
- Context: two U.S. World Cup ticket-delivery class actions have been consolidated in federal court in New York; a separate Canadian class action and a U.S. suit over CEO Eric Baker's resale-fund ties are also pending.
- StubHub separately paid $10M earlier in 2026 to settle FTC deceptive-pricing-disclosure charges.
- StubHub attributes some World Cup fulfillment issues to FIFA's own ticket-transfer infrastructure rather than systemic misconduct; all litigation allegations remain unproven.

### Industry Problem
Even an isolated, resolved dispute like this adds to a visible pattern of patron-facing fulfillment and refund friction at a major secondary marketplace, eroding buyer trust in "guarantee"-branded protections and raising the platform's regulatory/litigation exposure.

### Proposed Solution
Treat this as reinforcing evidence for auditing any comparable guarantee/refund language Etix or its resale partners use — the gap between guarantee wording and lived buyer experience (wrong seats, refusal to refund) is exactly the kind of pain point that invites both viral backlash and regulatory attention; watch whether the pending World Cup litigation surfaces broader systemic fulfillment findings.

StubHub says it has resolved a high-profile ticket dispute with Laurence Leavy, better known as “Marlins Man,” after the prominent sports fan publicly accused the resale marketplace of failing to honor its FanProtect Guarantee.

Leavy, who has become famous for his regular appearance at high profile sporting events like the World Series and Super Bowl wearing bright orange Miami Marlins gear, lodged a series of public complaints against the resale giant over the weekend. Leavy, who is an attorney beyond his sports fandom fame, said an order on the marketplace resulted in the ticket seller sending tickets that weren’t the ones promised, and the marketplace would not provide the tickets he ordered, comparable replacement seats or a refund.

He said he had purchased seats in Section C, Row B – in a prime on-camera area of the stadium as is his preference – and claimed the tickets he ultimately received did not match that order.

“I was DEFRAUDED BY @StubHub,” Leavy wrote, adding that he spends more than $500,000 annually through the marketplace.

StubHub told TicketNews Tuesday that the company had since spoken directly with Leavy and reached a resolution.

“We connected directly with Marlins Man yesterday and reached a resolution together,” a StubHub spokesperson said. “He can speak to his own experience, but very glad it’s settled.”

The company also publicly responded to Leavy Tuesday morning, writing that it was “glad we could work through it together and land on a resolution.” StubHub did not disclose the terms of that resolution.

TicketNews contacted Leavy through X seeking additional details about the resolution but had not received a response as of press time.

StubHub’s FanProtect Guarantee says buyers will receive tickets that are the same as or comparable to those ordered. When that does not happen, the company says it can provide comparable or better tickets, a refund or credit, subject to its terms. StubHub reiterated those protections in an August 20 article describing comparable replacement tickets when available and a full refund as a backstop.

The dispute comes during an unusually turbulent stretch for the resale giant, even if Leavy’s individual complaint has now apparently been settled.

[TicketNews reported this summer on proposed class-action litigation](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/stubhub-sued-in-proposed-class-action-over-world-cup-ticket-delivery-failures/) from World Cup buyers who alleged StubHub failed to deliver tickets despite its FanProtect promises. Two U.S. cases involving World Cup ticket fulfillment have since been consolidated in federal court in New York.

StubHub has separately faced a proposed Canadian class action over undelivered World Cup tickets and another U.S. lawsuit challenging the [marketplace’s ties to professional ticket sellers connected to CEO Eric Baker](https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/07/stubhubs-world-cup-pr-crisis-deepens-with-new-class-action-over-ceos-ticket-resale-fund/). Earlier this year, the company also agreed to pay $10 million to settle Federal Trade Commission charges involving allegedly deceptive ticket-price disclosures.

The allegations in those lawsuits remain unproven, and StubHub has disputed claims that broader World Cup fulfillment problems reflected systemic misconduct, attributing many of those issues to FIFA’s ticket-transfer infrastructure.

Leavy has not announced that he filed any lawsuit against StubHub, and the company’s latest statement indicates his individual dispute has been resolved.
