# Secure Venues Summit Kicks Off In Seattle With Opening Remarks From Prevent Advisors’ David Born

## Summary

The inaugural Secure Venues Summit, hosted by Oak View Group and Prevent Advisors, opened in Seattle alongside the VenuesNow Conference, bringing venue operators together for panels on venue security trends. Opening remarks from Prevent Advisors' David Born emphasized that effective security work is meant to go unnoticed by attendees.

### Key Points
- First-ever Secure Venues Summit, held at the Grand Sheraton Seattle, tied to the VenuesNow Conference (Sept. 29-30).
- Opening remarks from David Born (Prevent Advisors) and Jim Dermody (Climate Pledge Arena director of security).
- Discussion themes included the evolving technology and threat landscape venue security teams must adapt to.
- Framed as a peer-networking event for venue security professionals rather than a product/vendor showcase.

## Transcript

![untitled 358](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/untitled-358.jpg)

David Born, senior director of business services at Prevent Advisors, kicked off the first-ever Secure Venues Summit hosted by Oak View Group and Prevent Advisors.

Brief remarks from security and event management veteran David Born Monday morning kicked off the first-ever Secure Venues Summit, hosted by Oak View Group and Prevent Advisors, at the Grand Sheraton Seattle, where venue operators gathered to hear discussions and panels on the latest trends and strategies for keeping their buildings safe.

He opened the inaugural event, tied to the VenuesNow Conference taking place in the same location Sept. 29 and 30, saying the best security isn’t the highlight of a venue and often goes unnoticed by most guests.

“Success for us is often going unnoticed,” said Born, senior director of business services at Prevent Advisors. “People come. They spend money. … They sit in their seat and enjoy a great concert, and then they leave. Security is not a highlight. Security is not a highlight. Security is not something they’re thinking of when they get home. That is success for us.”

Born touched on some of the topics to be discussed across various panels, including the evolution of technology and how operators have to adapt to the ever-changing security landscape.

Some advice from the security expert: you can’t do it all alone.

“Some of you may have a big title. That doesn’t mean you have all the answers,” said Born, who then encouraged people to speak to each other so that they can learn from each other and expand their contact list should they host an event similar to one that someone else from the conference had.

![untitled 468](https://static.pollstar.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/untitled-468.jpg)

Jim Dermody, director of security at Seattle’s Climate Pledge Arena, spoke about the importance of having an event like the Secure Venues Summit.

“Getting those relationships and maintaining them is something we all look forward to in our field,” Born said. “That’s going to help us in the long run.”

Jim Dermody, Climate Pledge Arena’s director of security, joined Born onstage to emphasize the importance of hosting the Secure Venues Summit.

“We’re all responsible for much more than security,” Dermody said. “We’re responsible for creating an environment where thousands and sometimes tens of thousands … even hundreds of thousands like our World Cup cities experienced this past summer come together and enjoy an event, conduct business, active partnerships and then return home safely. That responsibility has never been more complex. Today’s venue security leaders are navigating evolving threats. … That’s why this conference matters. The most valuable ideas in our industry often come from the people doing the work every day.”
